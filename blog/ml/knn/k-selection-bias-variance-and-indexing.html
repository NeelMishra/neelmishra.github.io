    Choosing K, Bias-Variance, the Curse of Dimensionality & Spatial Indexing

    October 2026 · 13 min read

    In linear models, adding polynomial terms increases model flexibility. In $K$-Nearest Neighbors, the effective degrees of freedom are approximately $n / K$: smaller $K$ yields a more complex model, while larger $K$ yields a simpler, more heavily regularized model.

    1. Exact Bias–Variance Decomposition of KNN Regression

    Suppose observations obey $y_i = f(x_i) + \varepsilon_i$ with fixed design points $x_i$ and independent zero-mean noise $\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i) = \sigma^2$. At a query point $q$, the uniform $K$-NN prediction is $\hat f_K(q) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{i \in \mathcal N_K(q)} y_i$. Conditioning on the training inputs, the expected squared prediction error at $q$ decomposes as:

    $$\mathbb E\!\left[\bigl(Y - \hat f_K(q)\bigr)^2 \;\middle|\; X = q\right] = \underbrace{\sigma^2}_{\text{Irreducible Noise}} + \underbrace{\left(f(q) - \frac{1}{K}\sum_{i \in \mathcal N_K(q)} f(x_i)\right)^2}_{\operatorname{Bias}^2(\hat f_K(q))} + \underbrace{\frac{\sigma^2}{K}}_{\operatorname{Var}(\hat f_K(q))}.$$
    • Variance shrinks as $O(1/K)$: Averaging $K$ independent noise terms $\varepsilon_i$ divides the variance by $K$. At $K = 1$, $\operatorname{Var} = \sigma^2$; at $K = n$, $\operatorname{Var} = \sigma^2/n$.
    • Squared bias grows with $K$: As $K$ increases, the neighborhood radius $r_K(q) \propto (K/n)^{1/p}$ expands, pulling in points $x_i$ farther from $q$ where $f(x_i)$ differs systematically from $f(q)$ (proportional to $(K/n)^{4/p}$ for smooth functions via Taylor expansion).

    Balancing squared bias $\propto (K/n)^{4/p}$ against variance $\propto 1/K$ yields the theoretical optimal scaling $K^* \asymp n^{4/(p+4)}$ and minimax MSE rate $O(n^{-4/(p+4)})$.

    2. The Curse of Dimensionality: Why Distances Concentrate

    Notice the exponent $4/(p+4)$ above: when $p = 1$, the MSE decays at $n^{-4/5}$, but when $p = 36$, it crawls at $n^{-1/10}$. Why does locality break down as dimension $p$ grows?

    Consider $n$ points distributed uniformly in the $p$-dimensional unit hypercube $[0, 1]^p$. To capture a small fraction $\alpha = K/n$ of the training data inside an axis-aligned subcube around $q$, each side length must be:

    $$\ell_p(\alpha) = \alpha^{1/p}.$$

    To capture just $\alpha = 1\%$ ($0.01$) of the data:

    • In $p = 1$ dimension: $\ell_1(0.01) = 0.01$ ($1\%$ of the coordinate range — truly local).
    • In $p = 10$ dimensions: $\ell_{10}(0.01) = 0.01^{1/10} \approx \mathbf{0.631}$ ($63\%$ of every coordinate's entire range!).
    • In $p = 50$ dimensions: $\ell_{50}(0.01) = 0.01^{1/50} \approx \mathbf{0.912}$ ($91\%$ of every coordinate's range!).

    At the same time, for i.i.d. coordinates, the relative contrast between the farthest and nearest distances vanishes as $p \to \infty$ (Beyer et al., 1999):

    $$\frac{d_{\max}(q) - d_{\min}(q)}{d_{\min}(q)} \xrightarrow{p \to \infty} 0.$$

    When all training points become nearly equidistant from $q$, reduce dimensionality first using PCA, Fisher LDA, or learned embeddings (Behavioral Embeddings).

    3. Accelerating Exact and Approximate KNN Queries

    Brute-force search computes all $n$ distances in $O(np)$ time and selects the top $K$ using a size-$K$ max-heap in $O(n \log K)$ (or std::nth_element / quickselect in $O(n)$).
