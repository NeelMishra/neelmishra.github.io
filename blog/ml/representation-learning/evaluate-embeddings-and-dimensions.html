    Evaluate Embeddings and Choose Their Dimension

    September 2026 · 4 min read

    Before you start: Behavioral skip-gram embeddings.

    A list of plausible nearest neighbors is a useful smoke test. It is not enough to select an embedding model. You can usually find a few attractive examples in a bad model, and popularity can make those examples look convincing. Define the neighborhood’s intended meaning and evaluate it on held-out cases.

    Build an intrinsic test with difficult negatives

    For brand similarity, annotate pairs or ranked candidates under a precise rubric. Two brands can be similar in price, style, category, or audience without being interchangeable. Include hard negatives that share one property but differ in the intended one. Keep train-time co-occurrence out of the test labels when it would make evaluation circular.

    Measure agreement between predicted similarities and graded judgments, or rank the annotated candidates and use nDCG. Inspect tail brands, new items, and low-frequency tokens separately. A vector learned from three occurrences has different support from one learned from three million. Store counts with the embeddings to make that distinction visible.

    Evaluate the task that consumes the vectors

    Freeze the candidate universe, query set, filters, and scoring function. Use exact search first to compare representation quality, then add the ANN index to measure approximation loss. If embeddings feed a ranking model as features, evaluate the full downstream model too: a better intrinsic neighborhood need not improve that consumer.

    Use Recall@K, MAP, and MRR where their task assumptions fit, and nDCG for graded ranking. Report catalog coverage and popularity concentration separately. A model that retrieves only popular items can look strong on head-heavy traffic while offering little personalization.

    Turn dimension into a controlled experiment

    Train or fit candidate representations with dimensions such as 16, 32, 64, and 128 while keeping the data and evaluation protocol fixed. Tune on validation and reserve a final test. Plot quality against dimension, memory, and latency. An elbow is a visual hint of diminishing returns; it is not proof that a particular dimension is optimal.

    If a smaller model is within a predeclared acceptable quality loss and substantially cheaper, it may be preferable. Use paired uncertainty estimates because a small apparent difference can be noise. Report the chosen operating point rather than implying that an elbow at a visually appealing dimension guarantees no information loss.

    Retraining, projection, and truncation are different

    Training a 32-dimensional embedding optimizes in 32 dimensions. Applying PCA to a 128-dimensional embedding preserves selected variance directions from the fitted reference set. Arbitrarily taking the first 32 coordinates has no general preservation guarantee. A learned representation’s axes can rotate without changing its pairwise geometry, so coordinate order usually has no intrinsic importance.

    Fit projection transforms on training data, apply them consistently to query and item vectors, and re-evaluate normalization and the ANN index. Reducing dimension may change norms and nearest-neighbor order. If an embedding is explicitly trained for prefix truncation, follow that method’s contract; do not assume ordinary Word2Vec has it.

    Neighbors can move even when quality stays stable

    Different seeds can rotate the embedding space or choose different but similarly useful neighbors. Compare task scores and neighborhoods, not raw coordinate equality. For model migration, never combine old query vectors with new item vectors unless compatibility was established. Build a new index and switch the encoder/index pair together.

    A useful report includes corpus and vocabulary versions, frequency thresholds, vector export choice, similarity function, dimension, exact-search quality, ANN loss, memory, latency, and head/tail slices. Add failure examples that explain the trade-off. This gives a dimension choice an evidence trail.

    Check your understanding: Can you safely reduce ordinary embeddings by taking their first 22 coordinates?

    Not by default. The coordinate order need not encode importance. Retrain at that dimension or fit a justified projection, then validate the intended retrieval task.
