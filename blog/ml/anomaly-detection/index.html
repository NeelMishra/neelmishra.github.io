    Anomaly Detection: Reading Guide

    September 2026 · 4 min read

    Isolation Forest isolates observations through random axis-aligned cuts: points separated in fewer cuts receive higher anomaly scores. Turning that score into a reliable production workflow requires three pieces: a well-defined telemetry feature row, a calibrated forest score, and a grouped review queue.

    Three artifacts make the workflow reviewable

    Three chapters, three questions

    01
    How do cuts become an anomaly score?

    Compare first-cut intervals, follow queries through a reproducible three-tree forest, and calculate corrected path lengths $h_t(x)$ and normalized scores $s(x) = 2^{-\bar{h}(x)/c(\psi)}$.

    02
    Which behavior should count as unusual?

    Switch between app and app–tenant rows, apply Empirical-Bayes exposure smoothing $r_{\mathrm{smooth}} = \frac{c + k r_0}{n + k}$, derive $\operatorname{log1p}$ cut mappings, and separate observed zeros from collection gaps.

    03
    Which cases should reviewers receive?

    Compare score cutoffs and capacity caps across days, group rows into cases before applying a budget, and decompose single-feature attributions in path vs. score space.
