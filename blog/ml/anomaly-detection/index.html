    Anomaly Detection: Reading Guide

    September 2026 · 3 min read

    Isolation Forest finds observations that random tree cuts separate quickly. Turning that ranking into useful decisions requires two more choices: which observations to compare and what to do with the highest scores.

    Three chapters, three questions

    01
    Why does an isolated point score highly?

    Follow one point through random cuts, then connect average path length to the score. The normalization derivation is optional.

     02
    What counts as ordinary behavior?

    Define one row, compare counts with exposure, and keep missing observations separate from genuine zeros.

     03
    Which cases deserve review?

    Choose a cutoff or budget, read precision and recall, and preserve the evidence behind each case.

    Stop after the question you came to answer

    Read chapter 1 to understand the algorithm. Continue through chapters 2–3 to design a scoring and investigation workflow. Each starts with a small example; the controls let you change that example after its terms have been introduced.

    The examples illustrate calculations, not measured production performance. An isolation score measures unusualness relative to the training sample. Deciding whether a case is harmful requires further evidence.

    Connect this to the tree notes

    A supervised tree chooses splits to improve predictions; an isolation tree uses random splits. Compare the split-selection explanation with chapter 1. For feature attributions, read TreeSHAP and check which output the explainer reconstructs.

    The Isolation Forest paper explains the algorithm, and the scikit-learn reference documents its scoring API.
