    Bagging & Boosting: Interview Practice

    September 2026 · 8 min read

    Pick one section for a short practice session. Answer aloud before opening the explanation. Give the mechanism, one example, and one limitation; follow a derivation link only when that step needs work.

    Explain the mechanism

    How do bagging and boosting organize the next tree?

    Bagging fits trees on resamples and combines their predictions. A forest also randomizes candidate features. Boosting chooses the next correction using the current ensemble. Separate bagging trees can train concurrently; boosting rounds depend on earlier rounds. See the comparison table.

    Why can an ensemble still lose to one model?

    Shared errors survive averaging. Poor features, an unsuitable objective, noisy labels, or extra capacity can outweigh the benefit of combining models. Compare with a simple baseline on valid held-out data.

    How do random forests, Extra Trees, and stacking differ?

    Forests vary rows and candidate features. Extra Trees additionally randomize thresholds, with bootstrap use depending on configuration. Stacking learns a separate combination model, typically from out-of-fold base predictions. It is a different mechanism from either resampling or sequential correction.

    Reveal the reasoning

    B and D are out of bag: neither appears in the drawn sample. There are five draws, three distinct in-bag rows, and two omitted original rows. This tree can contribute an OOB prediction for B or D; it cannot contribute one for A because A was used to fit it.

    For A’s OOB estimate, combine only trees whose training resample omitted A. Different rows can therefore use different subsets of trees. If too few trees omit a row, its estimate can be unstable or unavailable; software needs a policy for that case.

    The omitted count is random. Drawing A five times would leave out four original rows, while drawing A, B, C, D and E would leave out none. The large-sample 37% rule below is an expectation, not a constraint on each resample. At $n=5$, the exact expected omission fraction is $(4/5)^5=0.32768$.

    Now change the setting: A and B are visits by the same person. Even when A is omitted, training on B can share person-specific information. OOB omission alone does not create an unseen-person or future-time evaluation. State the intended deployment population before interpreting the score.

    Work one calculation at a time

    Why are roughly 37% of the original rows absent from a bootstrap resample?

    For $n$ original rows and $n$ independent uniform draws with replacement, a particular row is absent with probability $(1-1/n)^n$. As $n$ grows, this approaches $e^{-1}\approx0.368$. By averaging the row indicators, this is also the expected fraction of distinct original rows left out; the realized fraction varies from tree to tree. It is not 37% of the drawn sample, and it does not define a permanent validation subset. Calculation and assumptions.

    Why does averaging hit a correlation floor?

    Expand the variance of the sum into individual variance and pairwise covariance terms, then account for dividing the prediction by the tree count. Shared fluctuations do not cancel. Numeric example and optional proof.

    What are AdaBoost’s two kinds of weights?

    Example weights determine which training mistakes matter now. Learner votes determine how much each fitted classifier contributes to the final score. The votes come from minimizing exponential loss for a fixed new classifier. Two rounds and vote derivation.

    Why does classification boosting fit a regression tree?

    The next targets are continuous negative loss derivatives with respect to the current score. For binary log loss, a positive label at probability 0.2 gets target +0.8. Tree outputs change scores; sigmoid converts scores to probabilities. Score-scale example.

    How would you derive a regularized leaf value?

    Approximate the loss change by a quadratic in one shared leaf correction. Sum slopes and curvatures, add the leaf penalty, and set the derivative to zero. Compare the optimized cost of a parent with its two children for split gain. Worked leaf and split.

    Diagnose a concrete failure

    Forest training accuracy is 99%; validation accuracy is 70%. Add trees?

    More trees may stabilize the average without fixing overly specific leaves or a distribution mismatch. Check the split, feature availability, and metric first; then compare leaf size, depth, and feature/sample fractions.

    Boosting training loss falls while validation loss rises. What next?

    Use the best validation iteration. Test smaller corrections or less tree capacity, but do not react to a single noisy round. The early-stopping example separates the best round from the stopping round.

    OOB is excellent, but next month’s performance collapses. Why?

    Random row omission can share users, duplicate events, or future information across training and evaluation. Check chronological and entity boundaries, train-only preprocessing, and feature availability at prediction time. Measure distribution drift separately.

    Fraud accuracy is 99.5%. Is that enough?

    Compare with the always-legitimate baseline. Evaluate precision and recall at the investigation capacity, or a cost-based metric. Choose thresholds on validation data and assess probabilities on the expected deployment prevalence. Review-queue evaluation.

    Do feature importance and good ranking prove reliable probabilities?

    No. Importance describes model behavior, not causality. Ranking and calibration are different properties. Check probability reliability and proper scoring rules on held-out predictions, and choose an explanation method for the actual question.

    Should every feature be standardized or one-hot encoded?

    Exact numeric tree splits mostly depend on row order, so standardization is usually unnecessary. Finite precision and histogram bins can matter. Category IDs need an appropriate encoding or a configured native categorical algorithm. Fit learned preprocessing within training folds.

    Implement one learner you can check

    For a weighted classification stump, sort a feature, move equal-valued groups together, and evaluate each valid boundary using weighted-majority predictions on each side. Include a constant prediction for unsplittable data and a deterministic tie rule.

    Then choose one exercise:

    • AdaBoost: reproduce the five-row weight table, check normalization, and handle a perfect learner without infinity.
    • Squared-error boosting: reproduce the four-row update with MSE 3.25. At inference, include the initial mean and every scaled correction.

    Make model comparisons defensible

    1. Choose a decision-relevant metric and an evaluation split that matches deployment.
    2. Fit preprocessing inside training folds; reserve final test data.
    3. Compare capacity and training controls under a comparable tuning budget.
    4. Evaluate the selected model’s important subgroups, calibration when needed, and serving cost.
