    The Lagrangian Dual, KKT Complementary Slackness & Support Vector Sparsity

    November 2026 · 15 min read

    In Part 1 and Part 2, we saw visually that points sitting comfortably behind the margin street exert zero pull on an SVM's decision boundary. Now we prove why—and unlock a second, even bigger prize along the way. By rewriting the primal problem in $(w, b, \xi) \in \mathbb R^{d + 1 + n}$ as its Lagrangian Dual over one bounded multiplier $\alpha_i \in [0, C]$ per training observation, two structural miracles emerge at once:

    1. Exact Sparsity via KKT Complementary Slackness: The optimal weight vector is $w^* = \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i^* y_i x_i$, where $\alpha_i^*$ is provably exact zero for every point strictly outside the margin street.
    2. Dot-Product-Only Geometry: Feature vectors $x_i$ disappear everywhere except inside pairwise inner products $x_i^\top x_j$, allowing Part 4 to operate in thousand- or infinite-dimensional feature spaces without ever building $w^*$.

    1. From the Soft-Margin Primal to the Box-Constrained Dual

    Recall the Soft-Margin Primal QP for $n$ training pairs $\{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ with $x_i \in \mathbb R^d$, $y_i \in \{-1, +1\}$, and regularization trade-off $C > 0$. To follow standard convex optimization sign conventions ($g_i \le 0$), we write every inequality with $\le 0$ on the right-hand side:

    $$\min_{w \in \mathbb R^d,\; b \in \mathbb R,\; \xi \in \mathbb R^n} \;\frac{1}{2}\|w\|_2^2 + C \sum_{i=1}^n \xi_i \qquad \text{s.t.} \quad 1 - \xi_i - y_i(w^\top x_i + b) \le 0, \quad -\xi_i \le 0 \quad \forall i.$$

    We introduce two vectors of non-negative Lagrange multipliers (dual variables):

    • $\alpha_i \ge 0$ for each margin inequality $1 - \xi_i - y_i(w^\top x_i + b) \le 0$, and
    • $\mu_i \ge 0$ for each slack non-negativity inequality $-\xi_i \le 0$.

    Adding each constraint weighted by its non-negative multiplier gives the Lagrangian function $\mathcal L(w, b, \xi, \alpha, \mu)$:

    $$\mathcal L(w, b, \xi, \alpha, \mu) = \frac{1}{2}\|w\|_2^2 + C \sum_{i=1}^n \xi_i + \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i \bigl(1 - \xi_i - y_i(w^\top x_i + b)\bigr) - \sum_{i=1}^n \mu_i \xi_i.$$

    Why does maximizing the dual give the exact same optimal value as minimizing the primal (strong duality, zero duality gap)? Because the primal objective is convex and all $2n$ inequality constraints are affine, Slater's condition holds trivially for every $C > 0$: setting $w = 0$, $b = 0$, and $\xi_i = 2$ makes $1 - 2 - 0 = -1 < 0$ and $-2 < 0$ strictly satisfied everywhere.

    Step 1: Minimize $\mathcal L$ Over Primal Variables $(w, b, \xi)$ (Stationarity)

    Fixing $(\alpha, \mu) \ge 0$, the inner problem $\theta_D(\alpha, \mu) = \inf_{w, b, \xi} \mathcal L(w, b, \xi, \alpha, \mu)$ is an unconstrained convex function of $(w, b, \xi)$, so its minimum occurs where all three partial derivatives vanish:

    1. With respect to $w \in \mathbb R^d$: $$\nabla_w \mathcal L = w - \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i y_i x_i = 0 \quad \implies \quad \mathbf{w = \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i y_i x_i}.$$ Think of a rigid plank floating between positive and negative pegs: each peg $x_i$ pushes on the plank with force magnitude $\alpha_i$, and the normal vector $w$ is a linear combination of the training examples!
    2. With respect to scalar bias $b \in \mathbb R$: $$\frac{\partial \mathcal L}{\partial b} = -\sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i y_i = 0 \quad \implies \quad \mathbf{\sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i y_i = 0}.$$ Mechanically, this is force balance (zero net push): the total multiplier weight on Class $+1$ must equal the total multiplier weight on Class $-1$, $\sum_{y_i=+1}\alpha_i = \sum_{y_i=-1}\alpha_i$. (If this sum were non-zero, sending $b \to \pm\infty$ would drive $\mathcal L \to -\infty$.)
    3. With respect to each slack $\xi_i \in \mathbb R$: $$\frac{\partial \mathcal L}{\partial \xi_i} = C - \alpha_i - \mu_i = 0 \quad \implies \quad \mathbf{\mu_i = C - \alpha_i}.$$ Look at what $\mu_i \ge 0$ and $\alpha_i \ge 0$ imply together: $\alpha_i \ge 0$ and $C - \alpha_i = \mu_i \ge 0 \iff \mathbf{0 \le \alpha_i \le C}$! Eliminating $\mu_i$ turns the soft-margin penalty $C$ into a simple upper cap—a box constraint—on every $\alpha_i$.

    Step 2: Substitute Back to Obtain the Wolfe Dual

    Group the $\xi_i$ terms in $\mathcal L$: $\sum_{i=1}^n (C - \alpha_i - \mu_i)\xi_i = 0$. Group the $b$ term: $-b\sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i y_i = 0$. All that survives is:

    $$\theta_D(\alpha) = \frac{1}{2} w^\top w + \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i - w^\top \underbrace{\left(\sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i y_i x_i\right)}_{w} = \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i - \frac{1}{2}\|w\|_2^2.$$

    Expanding $\|w\|_2^2 = \left(\sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i y_i x_i\right)^\top \left(\sum_{j=1}^n \alpha_j y_j x_j\right) = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n \alpha_i \alpha_j y_i y_j (x_i^\top x_j)$ delivers the celebrated Soft-Margin Dual Quadratic Program:

    $$\max_{\alpha \in \mathbb R^n} \; W(\alpha) = \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i - \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n \alpha_i \alpha_j y_i y_j (x_i^\top x_j) \qquad \text{subject to} \quad 0 \le \alpha_i \le C \;\;\forall i, \quad \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i y_i = 0.$$

    2. The KKT Complementary Slackness Trichotomy

    Because strong duality holds, a primal-dual pair $(w^*, b^*, \xi^*)$ and $(\alpha^*, \mu^*)$ is globally optimal if and only if it satisfies primal feasibility, dual feasibility, stationarity, and the two Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) complementary slackness equations (each non-negative multiplier times its non-positive constraint must equal zero):

    $$\alpha_i^* \bigl(1 - \xi_i^* - y_i f(x_i)\bigr) = 0, \qquad \mu_i^* \xi_i^* = (C - \alpha_i^*)\,\xi_i^* = 0 \qquad \forall i \in \{1, \ldots, n\},$$

    where $f(x_i) = w^{*\top} x_i + b^*$. Inspecting where $\alpha_i^*$ lands inside the box $[0, C]$ partitions every training point into an exact three-way geometric trichotomy:

    The KKT complementary slackness trichotomy for Soft-Margin SVMs ($0 \le \alpha_i^* \le C$).
    Multiplier Value Implied Slack $\xi_i^*$ Signed Score $y_i f(x_i)$ Geometric Position & Practical Role
    $\alpha_i^* = 0$ $\xi_i^* = 0$
    (since $C - \alpha_i^* = C > 0$)    		 $y_i f(x_i) \ge 1$ Non-Support Vector: Lies outside (or degenerately touches) the street. Contributes zero to $w^*$ and can be deleted after training without changing predictions.
    $0 < \alpha_i^* < C$ $\xi_i^* = 0$
    (since $C - \alpha_i^* > 0$)    		 $y_i f(x_i) = 1$
    (exact equality!)    		 Free (Margin) Support Vector: Pinned precisely on the $\pm 1$ margin gutter. Used to recover the exact scalar intercept $b^*$.
    $\alpha_i^* = C$ $\xi_i^* \ge 0$
    ($C - \alpha_i^* = 0$ frees $\xi_i^*$)    		 $y_i f(x_i) = 1 - \xi_i^* \le 1$ Bounded (Violating) Support Vector: Inside the street ($0 < \xi_i^* < 1$), on the boundary ($\xi_i^* = 1$), or misclassified ($\xi_i^* > 1$). Its influence on $w^*$ is capped at $C$!

    Recovering $b^*$ and Predicting with Support Vector Sparsity

    Notice that $b$ disappeared completely from the dual objective $W(\alpha)$! Once the dual solver finds $\alpha^*$, let $\mathcal S = \{i : \alpha_i^* > 0\}$ be the sparse set of support vectors and let $\mathcal M = \{i : 0 < \alpha_i^* < C\} \subseteq \mathcal S$ be the free margin support vectors. For any $k \in \mathcal M$, the KKT table guarantees $y_k(w^{*\top} x_k + b^*) = 1$. Multiplying both sides by $y_k$ (using $y_k^2 = 1$) gives $w^{*\top} x_k + b^* = y_k$, and averaging over all $k \in \mathcal M$ to suppress floating-point solver jitter recovers $b^*$:

    $$b^* = \frac{1}{|\mathcal M|} \sum_{k \in \mathcal M} \left(y_k - \sum_{j \in \mathcal S} \alpha_j^* y_j (x_j^\top x_k)\right).$$

    At test time, evaluating a new query $q \in \mathbb R^d$ requires dot products only with support vectors $i \in \mathcal S$:

    $$f(q) = w^{*\top} q + b^* = \sum_{i \in \mathcal S} \alpha_i^* y_i (x_i^\top q) + b^*.$$

    Unlike KNN, which must retain and scan every training row $i = 1, \ldots, n$, an SVM discards every interior point with $\alpha_i^* = 0$.

    3. How Solvers Actually Optimize the Dual: SMO Pair Updates

    Generic interior-point QP solvers factor dense $n \times n$ matrices at $O(n^3)$ cost per step, which becomes unusable past a few thousand rows. In 1998, John Platt introduced Sequential Minimal Optimization (SMO)—the engine inside LIBSVM and sklearn.svm.SVC.

    Why not update one variable $\alpha_i$ at a time via ordinary coordinate ascent? Look at the equality constraint $\sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_k y_k = 0$: if you freeze all $n - 1$ other multipliers, then $\alpha_i = -y_i \sum_{k \ne i} \alpha_k y_k$ is completely locked and cannot move! The smallest possible feasible move must update two multipliers $(\alpha_i, \alpha_j)$ simultaneously so that $\alpha_i y_i + \alpha_j y_j = \zeta$ stays constant:

    • Expressing $\alpha_i = y_i(\zeta - \alpha_j y_j)$ turns $W(\alpha)$ into a univariate concave parabola in $\alpha_j$ alone, whose unconstrained peak $\alpha_j^{\text{unc}}$ has a 3-line closed-form formula!
    • Intersecting $0 \le \alpha_i \le C$ and $0 \le \alpha_j \le C$ with the diagonal line $\alpha_i y_i + \alpha_j y_j = \zeta$ restricts $\alpha_j$ to a closed segment $[L, H] \subseteq [0, C]$, so the exact optimal pair update simply clips $\alpha_j^{\text{new}} = \operatorname{clip}(\alpha_j^{\text{unc}}, L, H)$ with zero matrix inversions!

    4. Complete Worked Dual Solve & KKT Verification

    Let us solve a 3-point dual problem completely by hand with $C = 1.0$, then verify both our hand-solved numbers and a 5-point dataset exhibiting all three KKT regimes simultaneously against scipy.optimize.minimize and sklearn.svm.SVC.

    3-point dataset in $\mathbb R^2$ solved in the dual with $C = 1.0$.

    $3$$(4, 4)^\top$$+1$ $[12,\; 20,\; 32]$ $0.0$ (omitted—not an SV) Non-SV ($\alpha_3^* = 0$, $y_3 f = 2 > 1$)

    Exact Algebraic Solution

    1. Eliminate $\alpha_1$ via zero-sum balance: $\sum_{i=1}^3 \alpha_i y_i = -\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 + \alpha_3 = 0 \implies \alpha_1 = \alpha_2 + \alpha_3$.
    2. Express $w(\alpha)$ and $W(\alpha_2, \alpha_3)$ cleanly: $$w(\alpha) = -(\alpha_2 + \alpha_3)\begin{bmatrix}1\\2\end{bmatrix} + \alpha_2\begin{bmatrix}3\\2\end{bmatrix} + \alpha_3\begin{bmatrix}4\\4\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}2\alpha_2 + 3\alpha_3\\2\alpha_3\end{bmatrix}.$$ Hence $\frac{1}{2}\|w(\alpha)\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{2}\bigl((2\alpha_2 + 3\alpha_3)^2 + 4\alpha_3^2\bigr) = 2\alpha_2^2 + 6\alpha_2 \alpha_3 + \frac{13}{2}\alpha_3^2$, while $\sum_{i=1}^3 \alpha_i = 2\alpha_2 + 2\alpha_3$, giving: $$W(\alpha_2, \alpha_3) = 2\alpha_2 + 2\alpha_3 - 2\alpha_2^2 - 6\alpha_2 \alpha_3 - \frac{13}{2}\alpha_3^2.$$
    3. Maximize over $\alpha_2, \alpha_3 \ge 0$: Setting $\frac{\partial W}{\partial \alpha_2} = 2 - 4\alpha_2 - 6\alpha_3 = 0$ gives $2\alpha_2 + 3\alpha_3 = 1$. Substituting that into $\frac{\partial W}{\partial \alpha_3}$ gives: $$\frac{\partial W}{\partial \alpha_3} = 2 - 6\alpha_2 - 13\alpha_3 = 2 - 3(2\alpha_2 + 3\alpha_3) - 4\alpha_3 = 2 - 3(1) - 4\alpha_3 = -1 - 4\alpha_3 < 0 \quad \forall \alpha_3 \ge 0!$$ Because $W$ strictly decreases whenever $\alpha_3$ increases above zero, the boundary $\alpha_3^* = 0$ is optimal! Then $2\alpha_2^* = 1 \implies \alpha_2^* = 0.5$, and $\alpha_1^* = \alpha_2^* + \alpha_3^* = 0.5$ (both strictly inside $[0, 1]$).
    4. Recover $w^*$, $b^*$, and verify duality gap $= 0$: $$w^* = -0.5\begin{bmatrix}1\\2\end{bmatrix} + 0.5\begin{bmatrix}3\\2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}, \qquad b^* = y_2 - w^{*\top} x_2 = 1 - (1\cdot 3 + 0\cdot 2) = -2.$$ Notice that primal objective $\frac{1}{2}\|w^*\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{2}(1^2 + 0^2) = \mathbf{0.5}$ matches dual objective $W(0.5, 0) = 2(0.5) - 2(0.5)^2 = \mathbf{0.5}$ to exact equality, and $x_3 = (4, 4)^\top$ has $y_3(w^{*\top} x_3 + b^*) = +1(4 - 2) = 2 > 1$, confirming $\alpha_3^* = 0$!
    Show the complete Python verification 
    import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import minimize
from sklearn.svm import SVC

# 1. Verify our 3-point exact dual solve against SciPy SLSQP and sklearn SVC
X = np.array([[1.0, 2.0], [3.0, 2.0], [4.0, 4.0]])
y = np.array([-1.0, 1.0, 1.0])
C = 1.0

Q = np.outer(y, y) * (X @ X.T)
res = minimize(
    fun=lambda a: 0.5 * a @ Q @ a - np.sum(a),
    x0=np.zeros(3),
    bounds=[(0.0, C)] * 3,
    constraints={"type": "eq", "fun": lambda a: np.dot(a, y)},
    method="SLSQP",
    options={"ftol": 1e-12},
)
alpha_scipy = res.x
clf = SVC(kernel="linear", C=C, tol=1e-10).fit(X, y)

print("SciPy optimal alpha*  :", np.round(alpha_scipy, 6))   # [0.5 0.5 0. ]
print("SVC support_ indices  :", clf.support_)               # [0 1]
print("SVC dual_coef_ (y*a*) :", clf.dual_coef_)             # [[-0.5  0.5]]
print("SVC w*, b*            :", clf.coef_[0], clf.intercept_[0])  # [1. 0.] -2.0
assert np.allclose(alpha_scipy, [0.5, 0.5, 0.0], atol=1e-6)

# 2. Verify all 3 KKT regimes simultaneously on 5 collinear 2D points at C = 0.3
X5 = np.array([[-1., -1.], [0., 0.], [1., 1.], [2., 2.], [4., 4.]])
y5 = np.array([-1., -1., 1., 1., 1.])
clf5 = SVC(kernel="linear", C=0.3, tol=1e-10).fit(X5, y5)
a5 = np.zeros(5)
a5[clf5.support_] = np.abs(clf5.dual_coef_[0])
yf5 = y5 * (X5 @ clf5.coef_[0] + clf5.intercept_[0])
for i in range(5):
    print(f"Point {i+1}: alpha*={a5[i]:.6f}, y*f(x)={yf5[i]:.6f}")
# Point 1 & 4: 0 < alpha*=0.011111 < 0.3 -> y*f = 1.000000 (Free margin SVs)
# Point 2 & 3: alpha* = 0.300000 = C     -> y*f = 0.333333 (Bounded street SVs)
# Point 5:     alpha* = 0.000000         -> y*f = 2.333333 (Non-SV)

    Look back at the dual objective $W(\alpha)$ and prediction formula $f(q)$: nowhere do we ever touch an individual coordinate of $x_i$ outside a dot product $x_i^\top x_j$ or $x_i^\top q$. In Part 4, we replace $x_i^\top x_j$ with a kernel function $K(x_i, x_j)$—turning our linear street builder into a nonlinear classifier capable of carving ellipses, XOR hyperbolas, and smooth radial islands.
