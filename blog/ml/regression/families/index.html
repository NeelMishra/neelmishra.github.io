    Regression Types for Interviews

    July 2026 · 17 min read

    In technical interviews and real modeling reviews, candidates are rarely handed a formula and asked to invert a matrix. Instead, they are given a messy domain problem—hospital readmission counts over varying stays, noisy regional conversion rates with five rows in small markets, wide sensor arrays where columns overlap, or subscription churn where active customers have not yet canceled—and asked which regression family fits and why.

    Answering cleanly requires separating two concepts before naming an algorithm: the support of the outcome $Y$ (whether $Y$ is unrestricted real, binary, integer count, strictly positive, or right-censored) and the estimand (which property of $Y \mid X$ matters: conditional mean, conditional quantile, event rate, or survival probability). Many conditional-mean models share a single unifying equation,

    $$g\big(E[Y \mid X]\big) = \eta(X),$$

    where $\eta(X)$ is a linear predictor built from raw or basis-expanded features and $g(\cdot)$ is a monotone link function. Basis expansions and splines enrich $\eta(X)$; Generalized Linear Models (GLMs) change $g(\cdot)$ and the conditional variance law; Weighted and Generalized Least Squares (WLS/GLS) reweight correlated or unequal-variance errors; Bayesian hierarchies pool noisy group parameters; PCR and PLS compress collinear predictors; and quantile or survival models step outside conditional means altogether to target $Q_\tau(Y\mid X)$ or hazard $h(t\mid X)$.

    Five Orthogonal Axes of a Regression Model

    Treat model design as five independent levers rather than a flat list of buzzwords. Changing one lever leaves the others open:

    Decomposing regression families into five independent modeling decisions.
    Design axisPlain-English questionConcrete mechanisms
    1. Outcome support & estimandWhat values can $Y$ take, and what summary of $Y\mid X$ drives the decision?Mean $E[Y\mid X]$, probability $P(Y=1\mid X)$, exposure rate $E[Y\mid X]/T$, quantile $Q_\tau(Y\mid X)$, survival $S(t\mid X)$
    2. Predictor representation $\eta(X)$Is the signal straight, curved, local, interactive, or low-dimensional?Raw columns, interactions $x_1 x_2$, orthogonal polynomials, B-splines/natural splines, PCA or PLS latent scores
    3. Objective / likelihoodWhat discrepancy or probability law defines a good fit?Squared loss, inverse-variance weighted squares, exponential-family deviance, pinball loss, Cox partial likelihood
    4. Error covariance $\Omega$Do observations have unequal variance or share shocks across space/time/groups?Spherical $\sigma^2 I_n$, diagonal weights $W = \operatorname{diag}(1/\sigma_i^2)$, AR(1)/spatial GLS $\Omega^{-1}$, cluster-robust sandwich SEs
    5. Complexity & pooling controlHow do we restrain overfitting, collinearity, or tiny-group noise?Ridge ($L_2$), Lasso ($L_1$), roughness penalty $\lambda \int (f''(x))^2 dx$, hierarchical normal partial pooling

    Notice why this decomposition prevents common category errors: fitting $y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_i + \beta_2 x_i^2 + \varepsilon_i$ modifies Axis 2 (predictor basis) while keeping Axis 3 as ordinary least squares because $\eta(x)$ remains linear in $(\beta_0, \beta_1, \beta_2)$.

    Two Exact Worked Contrasts Frequently Tested in Interviews

    1. No Pooling, Complete Common-Mean Pooling, and Hierarchical Partial Pooling

    Suppose two regional cohorts have sample means $\bar y_1 = 2.0$ (noisy small group with sampling variance $v_1 = \sigma^2 / n_1 = 4.0$, precision $w_1 = 1/v_1 = 0.25$) and $\bar y_2 = 8.0$ (larger group with sampling variance $v_2 = \sigma^2 / n_2 = 1.0$, precision $w_2 = 1.00$). Candidates often confuse inverse-variance pooling of a single common mean with hierarchical partial pooling:

    Distinguishing separate estimation, common-mean inverse-variance pooling, and hierarchical Bayesian partial pooling ($\theta_g \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \tau^2)$ with $\mu_0 = 5.0$, $\tau^2 = 2.0$).
    StrategyStructural assumptionGroup 1 estimate ($v_1 = 4$)Group 2 estimate ($v_2 = 1$)Key tradeoff
    No poolingSeparate unrelated parameters $\theta_1, \theta_2$$2.00$$8.00$Unbiased per group, high variance in small Group 1
    Complete poolingSingle shared parameter $\theta_1 = \theta_2 = \theta$$6.80$$6.80$Precision-weighted $\frac{0.25(2)+1(8)}{1.25}=6.80$; erases real group differences
    Partial pooling (Hierarchical)$\theta_g \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \tau^2)$ ($\mu_0=5, \tau^2=2$)$4.00$$7.00$Reliability $\frac{\tau^2}{\tau^2+v_g}$ shrinks noisy Group 1 by $2.0$ and precise Group 2 by $1.0$

    Under the conjugate normal-normal hierarchy $\bar y_g \mid \theta_g \sim \mathcal{N}(\theta_g, v_g)$ and $\theta_g \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \tau^2)$, each posterior mean is a convex combination $\hat\theta_g^{\text{post}} = \frac{\tau^2}{\tau^2 + v_g}\bar y_g + \frac{v_g}{\tau^2 + v_g}\mu_0$. When $\tau^2 \to \infty$ we recover no pooling; when $\tau^2 \to 0$ we recover complete common-mean pooling.

    2. Unsupervised Variance (PCR) Versus Supervised Covariance (PLS)

    Consider the exact six-row centered training design and four-row test set analyzed in the PCR and PLS chapter, where the true response depends solely on the low-variance second coordinate, $y = 2x_2$:

    Six centered training rows and four held-out test rows where predictor spread lies in $x_1$ ($96.55\%$) while all predictive signal lies in $x_2$.
    Split & rows $(x_1, x_2)$True $y = 2x_2$PCR (1 component)PLS (1 component)PCR (2 components = OLS)
    Train (6 rows): $(-6,1), (-4,-1), (-2,0), (2,0), (4,-1), (6,1)$$(2, -2, 0, 0, -2, 2)$Selects $x_1$ ($X^\top X = \operatorname{diag}(112, 4)$); slope $0$Selects $x_2$ ($X^\top y = (0, 8)^\top$); slope $2$Retains both $(x_1, x_2)$; recovers $2x_2$
    Test (4 rows): $(-5,-0.5), (-1,1), (1,-1), (5,0.5)$$(-1, 2, -2, 1)$Predicts $(0, 0, 0, 0)$
    Held-out MSE $= 2.50$    		Predicts $(-1, 2, -2, 1)$
    Held-out MSE $= 0.00$    		Predicts $(-1, 2, -2, 1)$
    Held-out MSE $= 0.00$

    Principal Component Regression orders directions solely by $X^\top X$: PC1 captures $112 / 116 \approx 96.55\%$ of predictor variation along $x_1$, yet $x_1^\top y = 0$, so one-component PCR predicts zero everywhere and incurs held-out $\text{MSE} = \frac{1 + 4 + 4 + 1}{4} = 2.50$. Partial Least Squares aligns its first direction with $X^\top y = (0, 8)^\top$, immediately isolating $x_2$ and achieving zero held-out error in one component (matching two-component PCR).

    Diagnostic Selection Matrix for Interviews

    Wide collinear predictors ($p$ large or correlated sensors/spectra)Ridge, PLS, or PCRStandardize inside CV folds; high $X$-variance does not imply $y$-relevance

    A Six-Step Template for Strong Interview Answers

    1. State the estimand and support: Name $E[Y\mid X]$, $P(Y=1\mid X)$, rate $E[Y\mid X]/T$, quantile $Q_\tau(Y\mid X)$, or hazard $h(t\mid X)$, and state the legal range of $Y$.
    2. Specify the systematic predictor $\eta(X)$ and link $g$: Explain why identity, logit, or log link matches the domain and whether splines or interactions are needed.
    3. Name the objective and variance structure: Contrast least squares, weighted squares, exponential-family deviance, or partial likelihood, and state how $\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X)$ behaves.
    4. Specify regularization or pooling: Explain how collinearity, high-dimensional $p$, or sparse group counts are stabilized.
    5. Design leak-free validation: Match random, grouped, or chronological splits to deployment and keep scaling/basis/component tuning strictly inside training folds.
    6. Name the primary failure mode: State what you would inspect first after fitting (e.g., overdispersion $Var > \mu$ in Poisson, proportional-hazards Schoenfeld residuals in Cox, calibration under prior shift in logistic).
    One upgrade never repairs an unrelated layer. Replacing a straight line with a cubic spline fixes Axis 2 (curvature) while leaving Axis 4 (clustered errors), Axis 1 (right-censoring bias), and pipeline leakage completely untouched. Match the remedy to the broken axis.

    Focused Series Roadmap

    Polynomial and Basis Regression

    Linearity in parameters, Vandermonde ill-conditioning, Legendre/orthogonal bases, bias-variance degree tuning, and boundary Runge oscillation.

    Weighted and Generalized Least Squares

    Heteroscedasticity, correlated errors, Cholesky whitening $\Omega^{-1/2}$, Aitken's Gauss-Markov theorem, feasible GLS, and sandwich SEs.

    Generalized Linear Models

    Exponential-family canonical links, score and Fisher scoring / IRLS working responses, deviance, offsets, and Poisson overdispersion.

    Splines and GAMs

    Truncated power and B-spline bases, natural boundary linearity, second-derivative smoothing penalties, effective degrees of freedom $\operatorname{tr}(S_\lambda)$, and backfitting.

    Bayesian Regression

    Conjugate Gaussian-linear updates, Ridge MAP equivalence, posterior predictive variance decomposition, and hierarchical partial pooling.

    PCR and PLS

    Unsupervised SVD scores versus supervised covariance directions, unit-scaling sensitivity, cross-validated component count, and fold-safe projection.

    Censored and Survival Regression

    Survival $S(t)$ and hazard $h(t)$, right-censoring likelihoods, Kaplan-Meier product-limit curves, Cox partial likelihood, and Accelerated Failure Time.

    Foundation and Implementation Links

    The linear regression estimand chapter and OLS projection chapter provide the geometric foundation behind basis expansions, WLS whitening, and PCR.     Reproduce the Worked Family Contrasts

    The script below checks the synced PCR/PLS counterexample ($\text{MSE} = 2.50$ vs $0.00$), the hierarchical partial-pooling vs common-mean pooling formulas, WLS vs OLS, and the Kaplan-Meier risk-set calculation.

    Show the complete numerical check 
    import numpy as np

# 1. Synced PCR vs PLS six-row training and four-row test counterexample
X_tr = np.array([[-6., 1.], [-4., -1.], [-2., 0.], [2., 0.], [4., -1.], [6., 1.]])
y_tr = 2.0 * X_tr[:, 1]
X_te = np.array([[-5., -0.5], [-1., 1.], [1., -1.], [5., 0.5]])
y_te = 2.0 * X_te[:, 1]

assert np.allclose(X_tr.T @ X_tr, [[112.0, 0.0], [0.0, 4.0]])
assert np.allclose(X_tr.T @ y_tr, [0.0, 8.0])

# PCR1 projects onto e1 = (1, 0), slope = 0 -> predicts 0
pred_pcr1 = np.zeros_like(y_te)
# PLS1 aligns with X_tr.T @ y_tr -> e2 = (0, 1), slope = (8 / 4) = 2
pred_pls1 = 2.0 * X_te[:, 1]
# PCR2 uses full column space = OLS
pred_pcr2 = X_te @ np.linalg.lstsq(X_tr, y_tr, rcond=None)[0]

assert np.isclose(np.mean((y_te - pred_pcr1) ** 2), 2.5)
assert np.isclose(np.mean((y_te - pred_pls1) ** 2), 0.0)
assert np.isclose(np.mean((y_te - pred_pcr2) ** 2), 0.0)
print("MSE -> PCR1:", np.mean((y_te - pred_pcr1) ** 2), "PLS1:", 0.0, "PCR2:", 0.0)

# 2. No pooling vs Complete common-mean pooling vs Hierarchical partial pooling
y_bar = np.array([2.0, 8.0])
v = np.array([4.0, 1.0])
mu0, tau2 = 5.0, 2.0
common_mean = np.sum(y_bar / v) / np.sum(1.0 / v)
partial_pool = (tau2 / (tau2 + v)) * y_bar + (v / (tau2 + v)) * mu0
assert np.isclose(common_mean, 6.8)
assert np.allclose(partial_pool, [4.0, 7.0])
print("Common mean:", common_mean, "| Partial pooling:", partial_pool)

# 3. WLS vs OLS on unequal-variance 4-row sample
xw = np.array([1., 2., 3., 4.])
yw = np.array([2., 3., 4., 9.])
w = np.array([4., 4., 4., 0.25])
Xw = np.column_stack([np.ones(4), xw])
b_ols = np.linalg.lstsq(Xw, yw, rcond=None)[0]
b_wls = np.linalg.solve(Xw.T @ (w[:, None] * Xw), Xw.T @ (w * yw))
assert np.allclose(b_ols, [-1.0, 2.2])
assert np.allclose(b_wls, [7.0 / 11.0, 67.0 / 55.0])
print("OLS:", b_ols, "| WLS:", np.round(b_wls, 4))
