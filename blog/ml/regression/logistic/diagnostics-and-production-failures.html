    Diagnostics and Production Failure Modes

    July 2026 · 21 min read

    How can a production logistic regression model report a healthy $0.71$ ROC-AUC on Tuesday morning while actively losing more money than a trivial coin flip? Because logistic models fail in two distinct phases:

    1. Pre-deployment statistical misspecification and fragility: forcing a straight log-odds line onto a U-shaped clinical risk curve, letting one high-leverage row dictate $\hat\beta$, or ignoring quasi-separating sparse categories.
    2. Post-deployment distribution shift and pipeline decay: upstream unit mismatches (train-serve skew), delayed-label drift that ruins probability calibration while leaving rank order mostly intact, and selective-label feedback loops where blocking risky transactions censors the very outcomes needed for retraining.

    Deviance, McFadden's Pseudo-$R^2$, and Likelihood-Ratio Tests

    Let $\ell(\beta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \big[y_i\ln\hat p_i + (1-y_i)\ln(1-\hat p_i)\big] = -n\,J(\beta)$ denote the sample log-likelihood. Generalized linear models measure lack of fit via residual deviance relative to a saturated model that assigns each observation its own parameter:

    $$D(\beta) = -2\big[\ell(\beta) - \ell_{\mathrm{sat}}\big].$$

    For ungrouped binary outcomes $y_i \in \{0,1\}$, setting $\hat p_{i,\mathrm{sat}} = y_i$ gives $\ell_{\mathrm{sat}} = 0$ (using $0\ln 0 = 0$), so $D(\beta) = -2\ell(\beta) = 2n\,J(\beta)$ (whereas for grouped binomial counts $k_g \sim \operatorname{Bin}(m_g, p_g)$, $\ell_{\mathrm{sat}} \ne 0$). Comparing $\ell(\hat\beta)$ against the intercept-only null model $\ell(\hat\beta_{\mathrm{null}}) = n\big[\bar y\ln\bar y + (1-\bar y)\ln(1-\bar y)\big]$ defines McFadden's (1974) pseudo-$R^2$:

    $$R^2_{\mathrm{McF}} = 1 - \frac{\ell(\hat\beta)}{\ell(\hat\beta_{\mathrm{null}})}.$$
    When is $R^2_{\mathrm{McF}} \in [0,1)$, and when can it turn strictly negative? First, $R^2_{\mathrm{McF}}$ measures relative reduction in negative log-likelihood, not variance explained (McFadden noted $0.20\text{--}0.40$ already indicates strong discrete-choice fit). Second, on unweighted training data fitted by unpenalized MLE with an intercept, nesting guarantees $\ell(\hat\beta_{\mathrm{null}}) \le \ell(\hat\beta) < 0$, so $R^2_{\mathrm{McF}} \in [0,1)$. However, $R^2_{\mathrm{McF}}$ can go negative ($R^2_{\mathrm{McF}} < 0$) whenever $\ell(\hat\beta) < \ell(\hat\beta_{\mathrm{null}})$—notably on a held-out or drifted deployment cohort (see our worked audit below where $R^2_{\mathrm{McF,dep}} = -0.1437$), when the intercept is omitted or heavily penalized, or under mismatched sample weights!

    To test $q$ added parameters between nested models ($\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{red}} \subset \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{full}}$) under standard interior-MLE regularity (no separation or boundary constraints), Wilks' (1938) likelihood-ratio statistic satisfies $\Lambda = 2\big[\ell(\hat\beta_{\mathrm{full}}) - \ell(\hat\beta_{\mathrm{red}})\big] = D_{\mathrm{red}} - D_{\mathrm{full}} \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_q$. For non-nested feature sets, compare $\mathrm{AIC} = -2\ell(\hat\beta) + 2p$ or $\mathrm{BIC} = -2\ell(\hat\beta) + p\ln n$ alongside out-of-sample log-loss.

    Residuals, Pregibon Leverage, and One-Step Deletion Influence

    Because $y_i \in \{0,1\}$ is binary, raw residuals $e_i = y_i - \hat p_i$ lie on two deterministic curves ($1 - \hat p_i$ and $-\hat p_i$). Standardizing by Bernoulli variance or signed deviance contribution yields Pearson residuals $r_i$ and deviance residuals $d_i$:

    $$r_i = \frac{y_i - \hat p_i}{\sqrt{\hat p_i(1 - \hat p_i)}}, \qquad d_i = \operatorname{sign}(y_i - \hat p_i)\sqrt{-2\big[y_i\ln\hat p_i + (1-y_i)\ln(1-\hat p_i)\big]}, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{n} d_i^2 = D(\hat\beta).$$

    Plot binned or spline-smoothed residuals against each continuous predictor $x_j$ to catch nonlinearities in log-odds (e.g., restricted cubic splines or $x_j\ln x_j$ Box–Tidwell terms for $x_j > 0$) rather than relying on the Hosmer–Lemeshow decile test, whose $p$-value fluctuates with arbitrary bin cutpoints and sample size.

    Which single rows dominate $\hat\beta$? From the converged IRLS working weights $W = \operatorname{diag}(w_i)$ with $w_i = \hat p_i(1-\hat p_i)$, Pregibon (1981) derived the generalized hat matrix and diagonal leverage $h_{ii}$:

    $$H = W^{1/2}X\big(X^\top W X\big)^{-1}X^\top W^{1/2}, \qquad h_{ii} = w_i\,\tilde x_i^\top\big(X^\top W X\big)^{-1}\tilde x_i \in [0, 1], \qquad \operatorname{tr}(H) = p.$$

    Notice a crucial subtlety absent in OLS: $h_{ii}$ multiplies geometric Mahalanobis distance $\tilde x_i^\top(X^\top W X)^{-1}\tilde x_i$ by $w_i = \hat p_i(1-\hat p_i)$! An extreme covariate row deep inside a confident region ($\hat p_i \approx 0.999 \implies w_i \approx 0.001$) carries tiny Fisher-information leverage, whereas an unusual row near the decision boundary ($\hat p_i \approx 0.50$) paired with a surprising label exerts massive pull, quantified without refitting $n$ models via Pregibon's one-step Newton deletion shift and Cook-like influence $C_i$:

    $$\Delta\hat\beta_{(-i)}^{1} = \hat\beta - \hat\beta_{(-i)}^{1} = \frac{\big(X^\top W X\big)^{-1}\tilde x_i\,(y_i - \hat p_i)}{1 - h_{ii}}, \qquad C_i = \frac{r_i^2\,h_{ii}}{p\,(1 - h_{ii})^2}.$$

    Covariate, Label, and Concept Drift & PSI as Jeffreys Divergence

    In production, joint data shifts $P_{\mathrm{dep}}(X,Y) \ne P_{\mathrm{train}}(X,Y)$ factor into three distinct mechanisms requiring completely different engineering remedies:

    Shift RegimeFactorization ChangeEffect on Logistic Model & Correct Remedy
    Covariate shift$P(X)$ changes, $P(Y\mid X)$ invariantIf the linear logit is well-specified on the new support, $\Pr(Y{=}1\mid X)$ stays valid; under misspecification or extrapolation into unseen feature regions, refit with importance weights $w(x) = P_{\mathrm{dep}}(x)/P_{\mathrm{train}}(x)$ or richer basis terms.
    Prior / label shift$P(Y)$ changes, $P(X\mid Y)$ invariantRankings ($\mathrm{AUC}$) stay invariant, but probabilities shift systematically. Exact fix: add $\Delta\beta_0 = \operatorname{logit}(\pi_{\mathrm{dep}}) - \operatorname{logit}(\pi_{\mathrm{train}})$ to the intercept (no full refit needed).
    Concept drift$P(Y\mid X)$ changesFeature-to-outcome physics changed (e.g., fraudsters adapt tactics or inflation shifts credit meaning). Intercept/Platt patches decay; collect fresh labels and refit $\hat\beta$.

    Before delayed labels arrive, teams monitor feature and score histograms across $B$ fixed reference quantiles using the Population Stability Index (PSI). Denoting reference bin proportions by $r_b > 0$ and current deployment proportions by $c_b > 0$ ($\sum_b r_b = \sum_b c_b = 1$, applying Laplace smoothing $\tilde c_b = \frac{n_{c,b}+\epsilon}{N_c + B\epsilon}$ if a bin is empty), PSI is literally Jeffreys' (1946) symmetric KL divergence:

    $$\mathrm{PSI} = \sum_{b=1}^{B}(c_b - r_b)\ln\frac{c_b}{r_b} = D_{\mathrm{KL}}(c\,\|\,r) + D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r\,\|\,c) \ge 0.$$

    Worked Delayed-Label Audit: Healthy AUC ($0.7143$) Yet Negative $R^2_{\mathrm{McF}}$ ($-0.1437$)

    An unlabeled alert ($\mathrm{PSI} = 0.1482$) proves the score distribution moved, but cannot tell whether predictions are still accurate! Suppose delayed outcomes $y_1,\dots,y_{10}$ arrive for $10$ representative deployment cases below. Look at what a joint Cox diagnostic uncovers:

    • Ranking looks acceptable: $15$ of $7\times 3 = 21$ positive-negative pairs are ordered correctly ($\mathrm{ROC\text{-}AUC} = 15/21 = 0.7143$).
    • Probabilities are worse than predicting a constant! Because actual prevalence surged to $\bar y_{\mathrm{dep}} = 0.7000$ while $\bar p_{\mathrm{dep}} = 0.4970$ and several low logits fired positive ($y_2=y_3=1$), deployment log-likelihood $\ell_{\mathrm{dep}}(\hat\beta) = -6.9862$ falls below the deployment null log-likelihood $\ell_{\mathrm{dep}}(\bar y_{\mathrm{dep}}) = -6.1086$, driving $R^2_{\mathrm{McF,dep}} = 1 - \frac{-6.9862}{-6.1086} = \mathbf{-0.1437 < 0}$!
    • Offset vs Cox slope separates prior shift from concept drift: fitting $\gamma = 1$ gives $\hat\alpha_{\mathrm{offset}} = +1.2762$, whereas joint Cox regression gives $\hat\alpha = +0.9951$ and $\hat\gamma = 0.5656 < 1$—diagnosing both prevalence shift and slope attenuation.

    Train-Serve Skew, Target Leakage, and Selective-Label Feedback Loops

    • Train-serve skew: offline SQL backfills and online microservice feature extractors silently disagree on missing-value defaults (0 vs NaN median), currency/unit scaling, UTC window boundaries, or out-of-vocabulary category hashes. Guard against skew by sharing one versioned feature specification and asserting byte-identical predictions on a pinned golden record suite before every rollout.
    • Target leakage: an offline backfill joins a table updated after the outcome (e.g., collection-agency flags or post-discharge lab codes). Offline AUC jumps to $0.99$; live production receives zeros or nulls and collapses on day one. Enforce strict point-in-time event timestamps on every join.
    • Selective labels and censored feedback loops (Lakkaraju et al., 2017): a credit or fraud classifier that rejects applications with $\hat p_i \ge \tau$ never observes what $y_i$ would have been among rejected applicants! Naive retraining exclusively on approved traffic learns on a truncated support and progressively blinds the model. Maintain a small randomized exploration / holdout slice (e.g., $1\%\text{--}2\%$ with inverse-propensity weighting $1/\pi_{\mathrm{obs}}(x)$) whenever safety constraints permit, and version your feature pipeline, $\hat\beta$, calibrator $g$, and threshold $\tau^\star$ as a single atomic artifact.
    Show the complete numerical check 
    import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import minimize
from sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score

# 1. Exact 5-bin PSI (Jeffreys symmetric KL divergence)
ref = np.array([0.30, 0.25, 0.20, 0.15, 0.10])
cur = np.array([0.18, 0.20, 0.22, 0.22, 0.18])
psi = np.sum((cur - ref) * np.log(cur / ref))
kl_cr = np.sum(cur * np.log(cur / ref))
kl_rc = np.sum(ref * np.log(ref / cur))
assert np.isclose(psi, kl_cr + kl_rc) and np.isclose(psi, 0.14819485)

# 2. Delayed-label deployment audit: negative McFadden R^2 + Cox diagnosis
p_dep = np.array([0.08, 0.14, 0.22, 0.30, 0.42, 0.55, 0.68, 0.78, 0.86, 0.94])
y_dep = np.array([0, 1, 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 1, 1, 1], dtype=float)
z_dep = np.log(p_dep / (1 - p_dep))

ll_mod = np.sum(y_dep * np.log(p_dep) + (1 - y_dep) * np.log(1 - p_dep))
ybar = y_dep.mean()
ll_null = np.sum(y_dep * np.log(ybar) + (1 - y_dep) * np.log(1 - ybar))
r2_mcf_dep = 1.0 - ll_mod / ll_null
auc_dep = roc_auc_score(y_dep, p_dep)
assert r2_mcf_dep < 0 and np.isclose(r2_mcf_dep, -0.14366294) and np.isclose(auc_dep, 15 / 21)

a_off = minimize(lambda a: np.sum(np.logaddexp(0, a[0] + z_dep) - y_dep * (a[0] + z_dep)), [0.0]).x[0]
a_cox, g_cox = minimize(lambda t: np.sum(np.logaddexp(0, t[0] + t[1] * z_dep) - y_dep * (t[0] + t[1] * z_dep)), [0.0, 1.0]).x
d_res = np.where(y_dep > p_dep, 1, -1) * np.sqrt(-2 * (y_dep * np.log(p_dep) + (1 - y_dep) * np.log(1 - p_dep)))
assert np.isclose(np.sum(d_res ** 2), -2 * ll_mod)
print(f"PSI={psi:.4f}, AUC={auc_dep:.4f}, R2_McF_dep={r2_mcf_dep:.4f}, a_off={a_off:+.4f}, Cox=({a_cox:+.4f}, {g_cox:.4f})")
