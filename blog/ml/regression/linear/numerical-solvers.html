    Solving Least Squares Reliably: Cholesky, QR, and SVD

    July 2026 · 17 min read

    On a chalkboard with infinite-precision real numbers, $(X^\top X)^{-1} X^\top y$, $R^{-1} Q^\top y$, and $V \Sigma^{-1} U^\top y$ produce the exact same least-squares vector $\hat\beta$. Inside IEEE-754 double-precision floating point ($\epsilon_{\text{mach}} = 2^{-53} \approx 2.22 \times 10^{-16}$, roughly $15.9$ decimal digits), explicitly forming the Gram matrix $X^\top X$ can destroy every single digit of $\hat\beta$ while Householder QR and SVD still recover the exact answer to $16$ digits! Understanding why separates production numerical software (LAPACK dgels, dgelsd, numpy.linalg.lstsq) from naive textbook translations.

    Proof That Forming $X^\top X$ Squares the Condition Number

    Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}$ ($n \ge p$, where $p$ here denotes total columns in $X$) have full column rank $p$ and thin singular value decomposition

    $$X = U \Sigma V^\top, \qquad U \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}\;(U^\top U = I_p), \qquad \Sigma = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_p), \qquad V \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times p}\;(V^\top V = I_p),$$

    ordered $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \cdots \ge \sigma_p > 0$. The 2-norm condition number of $X$ is $\kappa_2(X) := \|X\|_2 \|X^+\|_2 = \frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_p}$. Now form the $p \times p$ Gram matrix:

    $$X^\top X = (V \Sigma U^\top)(U \Sigma V^\top) = V \Sigma^2 V^\top.$$

    Because $V$ is orthogonal, the eigenvalues of symmetric positive-definite $X^\top X$ are $\sigma_1^2 \ge \cdots \ge \sigma_p^2 > 0$, giving

    $$\kappa_2(X^\top X) = \frac{\lambda_{\max}(X^\top X)}{\lambda_{\min}(X^\top X)} = \frac{\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_p^2} = \big(\kappa_2(X)\big)^2.$$

    Residual-dependent sensitivity of least squares (Wedin / Wilkinson / Higham)

    Keep problem conditioning separate from algorithm stability. For small normwise perturbations $\|\Delta X\|_2 \le \epsilon \|X\|_2$ and $\|\Delta y\|_2 \le \epsilon \|y\|_2$ on a full-rank problem with nonzero fitted vector $\hat y = X\hat\beta$ and residual $e = y - \hat y$, classical perturbation analysis (Wedin, 1973; Higham, 2002, Theorem 20.3) bounds the intrinsic relative change in $\hat\beta$ by

    $$\frac{\|\Delta \hat\beta\|_2}{\|\hat\beta\|_2} \;\lesssim\; \epsilon \left(\kappa_2(X) + \kappa_2(X)^2\,\frac{\|e\|_2}{\|\hat y\|_2}\right).$$

    Read both terms carefully:

    • Small-residual / well-fitting regime ($\|e\|_2 / \|\hat y\|_2 \ll 1$): Intrinsic sensitivity is $O(\epsilon\,\kappa_2(X))$. Backward-stable Householder QR and SVD achieve forward error $O(\epsilon_{\text{mach}}\,\kappa_2(X))$, whereas normal-equation Cholesky unconditionally suffers $O(\epsilon_{\text{mach}}\,\kappa_2(X)^2)$ just from rounding $X^\top X$!
    • Large-residual regime ($\|e\|_2 / \|\hat y\|_2 = O(1)$): Even exact mathematics has sensitivity proportional to $\epsilon\,\kappa_2(X)^2$. Even here, however, QR and SVD avoid premature breakdown when $\kappa_2(X) \ge \epsilon_{\text{mach}}^{-1/2} \approx 10^8$, where rounded $\operatorname{fl}(X^\top X)$ ceases to be positive definite.

    Worked Float64 Failure: The Läuchli Matrix

    Läuchli (1961) introduced a $3 \times 2$ design matrix whose columns differ only in tiny entries $\delta > 0$:

    $$X(\delta) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ \delta & 0 \\ 0 & \delta \end{bmatrix}, \quad \beta_{\text{true}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad y = X(\delta)\beta_{\text{true}} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ \delta \\ 2\delta \end{bmatrix}, \quad X(\delta)^\top X(\delta) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 + \delta^2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 + \delta^2 \end{bmatrix}.$$

    Here $\sigma_1 = \sqrt{2 + \delta^2}$, $\sigma_2 = \delta$, $\kappa_2(X) = \frac{\sqrt{2 + \delta^2}}{\delta} \approx \frac{1.414}{\delta}$, and $\|e\|_2 = 0$. At $\delta = 10^{-8}$, $\delta^2 = 10^{-16} < \frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\text{mach}}$, so in IEEE-754 float64, 1.0 + 1e-16 rounds to exact 1.0! Forming $X^\top X$ wipes $\delta$ from memory completely, turning $X^\top X$ into the singular matrix $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$, while QR and SVD operate directly on $X(\delta)$ where $\delta = 10^{-8}$ is $10^8$ times larger than underflow/rounding:

    Comparing Direct and Iterative Least-Squares Solvers

    Householder QR factors $X = QR$ ($Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}$ orthonormal columns, $R \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times p}$ upper triangular) via orthogonal reflections and solves $R\hat\beta = Q^\top y$ by back-substitution ($\kappa_2(R) = \kappa_2(X)$). SVD computes the truncated pseudoinverse expansion

    $$\hat\beta^+ = X^+ y = \sum_{\sigma_j > t} \frac{u_j^\top y}{\sigma_j}\,v_j, \qquad t = \epsilon_{\text{mach}}\max(n, p)\,\sigma_1,$$

    returning the unique minimum-Euclidean-norm least-squares solution $\|\hat\beta^+\|_2$ whenever $\operatorname{rank}(X) < p$ or $n < p$.

    Column scaling changes singular values! Recording area in square millimeters instead of square meters multiplies that column by $10^6$ and artificially inflates $\kappa_2(X)$ by up to $10^6$, which can push genuine predictors below an unscaled SVD cutoff $t = \epsilon_{\text{mach}}\max(n,p)\sigma_1$. Always equilibrate or standardize column norms before diagnosing numerical rank or running iterative solvers (LSQR/LSMR).

    Reproduce Every Number on This Page

    Show the complete numerical check 
    import numpy as np

# 1. Verify kappa_2(X^T X) == kappa_2(X)^2 and Lauchli float64 benchmark
b_true = np.array([1.0, 2.0])
for delta in [1e-4, 1e-7, 1e-8]:
    X = np.array([[1.0, 1.0], [delta, 0.0], [0.0, delta]], dtype=np.float64)
    y = X @ b_true
    s = np.linalg.svd(X, compute_uv=False)
    assert np.isclose(s[0] / s[1], np.sqrt(2.0 + delta ** 2) / delta)

    Q, R = np.linalg.qr(X, mode="reduced")
    b_qr = np.linalg.solve(R, Q.T @ y)
    b_svd = np.linalg.lstsq(X, y, rcond=None)[0]
    assert np.linalg.norm(b_qr - b_true) / np.linalg.norm(b_true) < 1e-15
    assert np.linalg.norm(b_svd - b_true) / np.linalg.norm(b_true) < 1e-15

    if delta == 1e-7:
        L = np.linalg.cholesky(X.T @ X)
        b_chol = np.linalg.solve(L.T, np.linalg.solve(L, X.T @ y))
        rel_err = np.linalg.norm(b_chol - b_true) / np.linalg.norm(b_true)
        assert 0.01 < rel_err < 0.02
    elif delta == 1e-8:
        # 1.0 + (1e-8)^2 rounds to exact 1.0 in IEEE-754 float64!
        assert (X.T @ X)[0, 0] == 1.0
        failed = False
        try:
            np.linalg.cholesky(X.T @ X)
        except np.linalg.LinAlgError:
            failed = True
        assert failed

# 2. Verify kappa_2(X^T X) == kappa_2(X)^2 on a well-conditioned matrix
X_well = np.array([[1.0, 1.0], [1e-3, 0.0], [0.0, 1e-3]])
assert np.isclose(np.linalg.cond(X_well.T @ X_well), np.linalg.cond(X_well) ** 2, rtol=1e-6)
print("Lauchli float64 Cholesky failure vs QR/SVD machine-precision recovery verified.")
