    Model Specification, Transformations, and Interactions

    July 2026 · 18 min read

    In linear regression, the word linear restricts how the unknown parameter vector $\beta$ enters $X\beta$, never how raw measurements enter $X$. Placing $x^2$, $\log x$, spline basis functions $B_m(x)$, categorical indicators, or product interactions $x_1 x_2$ inside the columns of $X$ leaves the normal equations $X^\top X \hat\beta = X^\top y$ completely intact while letting the fitted surface bend, saturate, or change slope across subgroups. By contrast, a model such as $y = \beta_0 e^{\beta_1 x} + \varepsilon$ is nonlinear in $\beta_1$ because $\partial(X(\beta))/\partial\beta_1$ still depends on $\beta_1$.

    Exact Finite-Sample and Population Omitted-Variable Bias

    Suppose a long design matrix $X = [X_1, X_2]$ has full column rank, with full OLS fit $y = X_1 \hat\beta_1 + X_2 \hat\beta_2 + e$ ($X_1^\top e = 0$). What happens in the exact same sample if we omit $X_2$ and compute $\hat\beta_1^{\text{short}} = (X_1^\top X_1)^{-1} X_1^\top y$? Substituting $y$ and using $X_1^\top e = 0$ gives an exact finite-sample algebraic identity that requires zero probabilistic assumptions:

    $$\hat\beta_1^{\text{short}} = (X_1^\top X_1)^{-1}X_1^\top\big(X_1\hat\beta_1 + X_2\hat\beta_2 + e\big) = \hat\beta_1 + \underbrace{(X_1^\top X_1)^{-1}X_1^\top X_2}_{\hat\Delta_{12}}\,\hat\beta_2.$$

    Here $\hat\Delta_{12}$ is the matrix of sample auxiliary OLS slopes from regressing each omitted column in $X_2$ on the included columns $X_1$. Now add the conditional mean assumption $E[y \mid X_1, X_2] = X_1 \beta_1 + X_2 \beta_2$: taking conditional expectations yields $E[\hat\beta_1^{\text{short}} \mid X_1, X_2] = \beta_1 + \hat\Delta_{12}\beta_2$, and under i.i.d. sampling with invertible second moments, $\operatorname{plim}_{n \to \infty} \hat\beta_1^{\text{short}} = \beta_1 + \Delta_{12}\beta_2$ where $\Delta_{12} = (E[X_1 X_1^\top])^{-1} E[X_1 X_2^\top]$.

    Worked Seven-Row Quadratic Example: Missing Curvature as OVB

    Missing nonlinear terms are simply omitted variables! Consider seven rows generated near $y \approx 2 + 4.5x - 0.5x^2$ across $x \in \{1, \ldots, 7\}$ ($\bar y = 10.0$, $\text{SST} = 27.7850$). On the symmetric grid $x = 1..7$, regressing $x^2$ on $(1, x)$ has exact auxiliary slope $\hat\delta_1 = 8.0000$ because $\sum_{x=1}^7 (x - 4)(x^2 - 8x) = 0$:

    Interactions, Marginal-Effect Uncertainty, and the Hierarchy Principle

    In the interaction model $E[Y \mid x, z] = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x + \beta_2 z + \beta_3 x z$, the marginal effect of $x$ depends on $z$, and its standard error depends on the covariance between $\hat\beta_1$ and $\hat\beta_3$:

    $$\frac{\partial E[Y \mid x, z]}{\partial x} = \beta_1 + \beta_3 z, \qquad \operatorname{se}\!\left(\hat\beta_1 + z\hat\beta_3\right) = \sqrt{\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat\beta_1) + z^2 \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat\beta_3) + 2z\,\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(\hat\beta_1, \hat\beta_3)}.$$

    Crucially, $\beta_1$ alone is the slope of $x$ only at $z = 0$ (which is why centering continuous predictors $x_c = x - \bar x$, $z_c = z - \bar z$ makes main effects interpretable at the sample average rather than an impossible zero like $0\text{ kg}$ weight). Under the marginal-hierarchy principle, any model containing $x z$ must retain both main effects $x$ and $z$; dropping $x$ forces all $z$-slices to have zero $x$-slope at $z = 0$ and destroys invariance of fitted values under shifting $z \mapsto z + c$.

    Log-Target Retransformation: Jensen's Inequality vs. Duan's Smearing

    When positive skewed outcomes (prices, incomes, hospital stays) are modeled on the natural log scale as $\log Y = x^\top \beta + \varepsilon$ with $E[\varepsilon \mid x] = 0$, exponentiating $x^\top \hat\beta$ does not estimate $E[Y \mid x]$! Because $\exp(\cdot)$ is strictly convex, Jensen's inequality gives

    $$E[Y \mid x] = E\big[\exp(x^\top \beta + \varepsilon) \mid x\big] = \exp(x^\top \beta)\,E[e^\varepsilon \mid x] > \exp(x^\top \beta) \exp(E[\varepsilon \mid x]) = \exp(x^\top \beta).$$

    Specifically, $\exp(x^\top \beta)$ estimates the conditional geometric mean (and conditional median if $\operatorname{Med}(\varepsilon \mid x) = 0$). To recover $E[Y \mid x]$ on the raw dollar/unit scale:

    • Homoscedastic Gaussian correction: If $\varepsilon \mid x \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$, the moment-generating function gives $E[e^\varepsilon] = \exp(\sigma^2 / 2)$, motivating $\hat y_{\text{Gauss}}(x) = \exp(x^\top \hat\beta + s^2 / 2)$. However, if $\varepsilon$ is skewed or heavy-tailed, $\exp(s^2 / 2)$ can severely over- or under-shoot.
    • Duan's (1983) nonparametric smearing estimator: Under i.i.d. homoscedastic errors $\varepsilon_i$ of arbitrary distribution with finite $E[e^\varepsilon]$, the empirical average $\hat\phi_{\text{Duan}} := \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \exp(e_i)$ consistently estimates $E[e^\varepsilon]$, giving $\hat y_{\text{Duan}}(x) = \hat\phi_{\text{Duan}} \exp(x^\top \hat\beta)$. (Warning: if $\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon \mid x)$ depends on $x$, $E[e^\varepsilon \mid x]$ also varies with $x$—fit a log-link Gamma or Poisson quasi-MLE GLM directly on raw $Y$ instead!)

    Categorical Coding, Missingness, and Functional-Form Checks

    A $K$-level categorical feature requires $K - 1$ indicator columns when an intercept is present (omitting one baseline category avoids the $\sum_{c=1}^K \mathbf{1}\{g_i = c\} = \mathbf{1}_n$ dummy-variable trap; switching the baseline shifts $\hat\beta$ labels while leaving $\mathcal{C}(X)$ and $\hat y$ identical). For missing feature values, complete-case deletion is unbiased when missingness depends only on observed $X$ ($P(R = 1 \mid X, Y) = P(R = 1 \mid X)$), whereas naive constant/mean imputation shrinks variance and distorts multivariate correlations unless paired with missingness mechanisms learned strictly on training folds. Always inspect $e_i$ versus $\hat y_i$ and each $x_{ij}$ (and consider Ramsey's RESET test adding $\hat y^2, \hat y^3$); the C++ diagnostics guide implements residual and specification metrics.

    Reproduce Every Number on This Page

    Show the complete numerical check 
    import numpy as np

# 1. Seven-row quadratic dataset and exact finite-sample OVB identity
x7 = np.arange(1., 8.)
y7 = np.array([6.2, 8.7, 11.25, 11.8, 12.3, 10.75, 9.0])
sst7 = np.sum((y7 - y7.mean()) ** 2)
X1 = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(x7), x7])
X_quad = np.column_stack([X1, x7 ** 2])

b_short = np.linalg.lstsq(X1, y7, rcond=None)[0]
b_quad = np.linalg.lstsq(X_quad, y7, rcond=None)[0]
delta_12 = np.linalg.lstsq(X1, x7 ** 2, rcond=None)[0]  # [-12, 8]
assert np.allclose(b_short, b_quad[:2] + delta_12 * b_quad[2])
assert np.isclose(sst7, 27.7850)
assert np.isclose(np.sum((y7 - X1 @ b_short) ** 2), 21.2278, atol=1e-4)
assert np.isclose(np.sum((y7 - X_quad @ b_quad) ** 2), 0.3775, atol=1e-4)

# 2. Ten-row interaction dataset
x10 = np.tile(np.arange(1., 6.), 2)
d10 = np.repeat([0., 1.], 5)
y10 = np.array([3.2, 3.8, 5.1, 5.8, 7.1, 4.8, 7.8, 9.9, 12.7, 14.8])
X_add = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(x10), x10, d10])
X_int = np.column_stack([X_add, x10 * d10])
b_add = np.linalg.lstsq(X_add, y10, rcond=None)[0]
b_int = np.linalg.lstsq(X_int, y10, rcond=None)[0]
assert np.allclose(b_add, [-0.205, 1.735, 5.0])
assert np.allclose(b_int, [2.06, 0.98, 0.47, 1.51])
assert np.isclose(np.sum((y10 - X_add @ b_add) ** 2), 11.7555)
assert np.isclose(np.sum((y10 - X_int @ b_int) ** 2), 0.3550)

# 3. Five-row log-target retransformation (Naive vs Gaussian vs Duan smearing)
xl = np.arange(1., 6.)
logy = np.array([1.2, 2.3, 2.1, 3.4, 3.0])
y_raw = np.exp(logy)
Xl = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(xl), xl])
bl = np.linalg.lstsq(Xl, logy, rcond=None)[0]
mu_hat = Xl @ bl
el = logy - mu_hat
s2 = np.sum(el ** 2) / 3.0
naive = np.exp(mu_hat)
gauss_fac = np.exp(s2 / 2.0)
duan_fac = np.mean(np.exp(el))

assert np.allclose(bl, [0.99, 0.47])
assert np.isclose(y_raw.mean(), 14.3020, atol=1e-4)
assert np.isclose(naive.mean(), 13.6150, atol=1e-4)
assert np.isclose(gauss_fac, 1.1221, atol=1e-4)
assert np.isclose(duan_fac, 1.0741, atol=1e-4)
assert np.isclose((duan_fac * naive).mean(), 14.6233, atol=1e-4)
print("All quadratic OVB, interaction, and Duan smearing calculations verified.")
