    Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) & Expectation–Maximization (EM)

    November 2026 · 15 min read

    In Part 1, we watched $K$-Means chop tilted cigar-shaped clusters in half and force a rigid $100\%$-or-$0\%$ label onto ambiguous points sitting halfway between two centers. Both flaws trace back to the same missing ingredient: $K$-Means has no notion of cluster shape (covariance) or probabilistic uncertainty.

    A Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) models the unlabeled data cloud as a weighted superposition of $K$ multivariate Gaussian distributions—each with its own prior prevalence $\pi_k$, center $\mu_k$, and full ellipsoidal covariance matrix $\Sigma_k$. Instead of hard binary indicators $r_{ik} \in \{0, 1\}$, every observation $x_i$ receives a calibrated vector of soft responsibilities $\gamma_{ik} = P(Z_i = k \mid x_i) \in [0, 1]$ summing to $1$ across $k = 1, \ldots, K$.

    1. The Latent-Variable Generative Model & Why Direct MLE Fails

    Imagine generating a synthetic observation $x_i \in \mathbb R^d$ in two steps: first roll a $K$-sided weighted die to pick a hidden (latent) cluster identity $Z_i \in \{1, \ldots, K\}$ with mixing probability $P(Z_i = k) = \pi_k$ ($\pi_k \ge 0$, $\sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k = 1$); then draw $x_i$ from component $k$'s $d$-dimensional Gaussian density:

    $$\mathcal N(x \mid \mu_k, \Sigma_k) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2} |\Sigma_k|^{1/2}} \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}(x - \mu_k)^\top \Sigma_k^{-1} (x - \mu_k)\right),$$

    where $\mu_k \in \mathbb R^d$ is component $k$'s mean vector, $\Sigma_k \in \mathbb R^{d \times d}$ is its symmetric positive-definite covariance matrix, and $|\Sigma_k| = \det(\Sigma_k) > 0$. Marginalizing (summing) over the unobserved die roll $Z_i$ yields the mixture density $p(x_i \mid \theta) = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k \mathcal N(x_i \mid \mu_k, \Sigma_k)$ with parameters $\theta = \{\pi_k, \mu_k, \Sigma_k\}_{k=1}^K$, and the observed-data log-likelihood across $n$ i.i.d. points is:

    $$\ell(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ln p(x_i \mid \theta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ln\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k \mathcal N(x_i \mid \mu_k, \Sigma_k)\right).$$

    Why That Sum Inside the Logarithm Blocks Closed-Form MLE

    When fitting a single Gaussian ($K = 1$, or supervised Gaussian Bayes where $Z_i$ is observed), the $\ln(\cdot)$ hits $\exp(\cdot)$ directly and turns the exponent into a clean quadratic whose derivative solves for $\hat\mu$ and $\hat\Sigma$ in one line. In a mixture ($K \ge 2$), the sum $\sum_{k=1}^K$ sits trapped inside $\ln(\cdot)$! Taking $\nabla_{\mu_k} \ell(\theta) = 0$ leaves every component coupled through posterior weights that themselves depend on all $(\pi_j, \mu_j, \Sigma_j)$, preventing any closed-form algebraic solution.

    How Jensen's Inequality & the ELBO Motivate EM

    Introduce any auxiliary probability distribution $q_i(k) \ge 0$ ($\sum_{k=1}^K q_i(k) = 1$) over the latent cluster of point $i$. Multiply and divide inside the logarithm by $q_i(k)$, then apply Jensen's inequality ($\ln \mathbb E[U] \ge \mathbb E[\ln U]$ since $\ln$ is concave):

    $$\ell(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ln\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^K q_i(k) \frac{\pi_k \mathcal N(x_i \mid \mu_k, \Sigma_k)}{q_i(k)}\right) \;\ge\; \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{k=1}^K q_i(k) \ln \frac{\pi_k \mathcal N(x_i \mid \mu_k, \Sigma_k)}{q_i(k)} \;\triangleq\; \mathcal F(q, \theta).$$

    Look at $\mathcal F(q, \theta)$—the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO): the logarithm is now inside the sum, hitting $\pi_k \mathcal N(x_i \mid \mu_k, \Sigma_k)$ directly! Moreover, the gap $\ell(\theta) - \mathcal F(q, \theta) = \sum_{i=1}^n D_{\text{KL}}\!\bigl(q_i(\cdot) \,\|\, P(Z_i = \cdot \mid x_i, \theta)\bigr) \ge 0$ vanishes to exact zero when $q_i(k)$ equals the Bayes posterior responsibility $\gamma_{ik} = P(Z_i = k \mid x_i, \theta)$. Expectation–Maximization (EM) (Dempster, Laird & Rubin, 1977) simply alternates two coordinate-ascent steps on $\mathcal F(q, \theta)$, guaranteeing $\ell(\theta^{(t+1)}) \ge \ell(\theta^{(t)})$ at every iteration!

    2. Complete E-Step & M-Step Equations, Covariance Tying & the K-Means Limit

    Starting from initialized parameters $\theta^{(0)} = \{\pi_k^{(0)}, \mu_k^{(0)}, \Sigma_k^{(0)}\}_{k=1}^K$ (typically seeded by running rapid $K$-Means++ first!), EM repeats two closed-form steps until $|\ell(\theta^{(t+1)}) - \ell(\theta^{(t)})| < \text{tol}$:

    E-Step (Compute Posterior Responsibilities $\gamma_{ik}$)

    Holding parameters $\theta^{(t)}$ fixed, apply Bayes' rule to compute the soft probability $\gamma_{ik} \in [0, 1]$ that component $k$ generated point $x_i$:

    $$\gamma_{ik} = P(Z_i = k \mid x_i, \theta^{(t)}) = \frac{\pi_k^{(t)}\,\mathcal N\bigl(x_i \mid \mu_k^{(t)}, \Sigma_k^{(t)}\bigr)}{\sum_{j=1}^K \pi_j^{(t)}\,\mathcal N\bigl(x_i \mid \mu_j^{(t)}, \Sigma_j^{(t)}\bigr)}.$$

    M-Step (Re-Estimate Weighted Gaussian Parameters)

    Holding responsibilities $\gamma_{ik}$ fixed, maximize $\mathcal F(\gamma, \theta)$ over $(\pi_k, \mu_k, \Sigma_k)$. Define the effective point count assigned to component $k$ as $N_k = \sum_{i=1}^n \gamma_{ik}$ (notice $\sum_{k=1}^K N_k = n$). Each parameter updates via responsibility-weighted sample moments:

    $$\pi_k^{\text{new}} = \frac{N_k}{n}, \qquad \mu_k^{\text{new}} = \frac{1}{N_k}\sum_{i=1}^n \gamma_{ik} x_i, \qquad \Sigma_k^{\text{new}} = \frac{1}{N_k}\sum_{i=1}^n \gamma_{ik}\bigl(x_i - \mu_k^{\text{new}}\bigr)\bigl(x_i - \mu_k^{\text{new}}\bigr)^\top.$$
    Covariance tying options in GaussianMixture(covariance_type=...) on $\mathbb R^d$ with $K$ components (plus $K-1$ weights and $Kd$ mean parameters).
    covariance_type Constraint on $\Sigma_k$ Covariance Params Geometric Contour Shape
    "spherical" $\Sigma_k = \sigma_k^2 I_d$ $K$ Isotropic spheres of different radii $\sigma_k$ per cluster.
    "diag" $\Sigma_k = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{k1}^2, \ldots, \sigma_{kd}^2)$ $K d$ Axis-aligned ellipsoids (scales linearly with dimension $d$).
    "tied" $\Sigma_1 = \cdots = \Sigma_K = \Sigma$ $\dfrac{d(d+1)}{2}$ Tilted ellipsoids sharing the same orientation and spread (unsupervised cousin of LDA).
    "full" Unconstrained $\Sigma_k \succ 0$ $K\,\dfrac{d(d+1)}{2}$ Arbitrarily tilted, stretched ellipsoids per cluster; needs $N_k \gg d$ to avoid overfitting.

    Proof That K-Means Is the $\sigma^2 \to 0$ Zero-Temperature Limit of Isotropic GMM

    Freeze equal mixing weights $\pi_k = \frac{1}{K}$ and shared isotropic spherical covariances $\Sigma_k = \sigma^2 I_d$ across all $K$ components. In the E-step, the normalizing prefactors $\frac{1}{K}(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-d/2}$ cancel out between numerator and denominator, leaving a softmax with temperature $2\sigma^2$:

    $$\gamma_{ik} = \frac{\exp\!\bigl(-\|x_i - \mu_k\|_2^2 / (2\sigma^2)\bigr)}{\sum_{j=1}^K \exp\!\bigl(-\|x_i - \mu_j\|_2^2 / (2\sigma^2)\bigr)}.$$

    Now let $\sigma^2 \to 0^+$. Let $k^*(i) = \arg\min_j \|x_i - \mu_j\|_2^2$ be the unique closest centroid. Dividing numerator and denominator by $\exp\bigl(-\|x_i - \mu_{k^*(i)}\|_2^2 / (2\sigma^2)\bigr)$ sends every term with $j \ne k^*(i)$ to $\exp(-\Delta / (2\sigma^2)) \to 0$, so $\gamma_{ik} \to \mathbb I(k = k^*(i)) = r_{ik}$ (Lloyd's hard Voronoi assignment!), and the M-step $\mu_k^{\text{new}} = \frac{\sum_i \gamma_{ik} x_i}{\sum_i \gamma_{ik}}$ collapses identically into Lloyd's centroid mean!

    The Covariance Singularity Trap & Model Selection via AIC / BIC

    Unlike $K$-Means (whose WCSS is bounded below by $0$), unconstrained GMM log-likelihood $\ell(\theta)$ is unbounded above ($+\infty$)! Suppose component $k$ parks its mean directly on a single training point, $\mu_k = x_1$, and shrinks its variance $\sigma_k^2 \to 0^+$. While the other $K - 1$ Gaussians keep $p(x_i) > 0$ finite on $i \ge 2$, the spike at $x_1$ explodes:

    $$\pi_k \mathcal N(x_1 \mid x_1, \sigma_k^2) = \frac{\pi_k}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_k} \xrightarrow{\sigma_k \to 0^+} +\infty \quad \implies \quad \ell(\theta) \to +\infty!$$

    Production implementations prevent covariance collapse by adding a small diagonal ridge $\Sigma_k \leftarrow \Sigma_k + \epsilon I_d$ at every M-step (reg_covar=1e-6 in scikit-learn) or placing an Inverse-Wishart Bayesian prior on $\Sigma_k$.

    How do we choose $K$ without labels? Because $\ell(\hat\theta)$ rises monotonically with $K$, we penalize the total number of free parameters $p(K)$ using the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) or Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) (lower is better for both):

    $$\operatorname{AIC} = -2\ell(\hat\theta) + 2p, \qquad \operatorname{BIC} = -2\ell(\hat\theta) + p\ln n.$$

    For any sample size $n \ge 8$, $\ln n > 2$, so BIC penalizes redundant components more strongly than AIC and is statistically consistent for recovering the true mixture order $K^*$.

    Show the complete Python verification 
    import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import norm
from sklearn.mixture import GaussianMixture

# 1. Exact manual Iteration 1 E-step & M-step on x = [1.0, 1.5, 5.0, 6.0]
x = np.array([1.0, 1.5, 5.0, 6.0])
pi0, mu0, var0 = np.array([0.5, 0.5]), np.array([2.0, 4.5]), np.array([4.0, 4.0])

p1 = pi0[0] * norm.pdf(x, mu0[0], np.sqrt(var0[0]))
p2 = pi0[1] * norm.pdf(x, mu0[1], np.sqrt(var0[1]))
gamma1 = p1 / (p1 + p2)
gamma2 = p2 / (p1 + p2)
print("Iter 1 gamma_i1:", np.round(gamma1, 4))  # [0.8032 0.7491 0.2509 0.152 ]

N1, N2 = gamma1.sum(), gamma2.sum()
mu1_new = np.sum(gamma1 * x) / N1
mu2_new = np.sum(gamma2 * x) / N2
print(f"Iter 1 M-step: N=[{N1:.4f}, {N2:.4f}], mu=[{mu1_new:.4f}, {mu2_new:.4f}]")

# 2. Verify converged GMM parameters against scikit-learn
gmm = GaussianMixture(
    n_components=2,
    weights_init=[0.5, 0.5],
    means_init=[[2.0], [4.5]],
    precisions_init=[[[0.25]], [[0.25]]],
    reg_covar=1e-6,
    tol=1e-10,
).fit(x.reshape(-1, 1))
print("Converged means :", np.round(gmm.means_.ravel(), 4))        # [1.25 5.5 ]
print("Converged vars  :", np.round(gmm.covariances_.ravel(), 4))  # [0.0625 0.25  ]
print("Converged log-L :", round(gmm.score(x.reshape(-1, 1)) * len(x), 4))  # -4.2895

# 3. Verify BIC recovers K* = 2 on a 120-point two-Gaussian sample
rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
X_sim = np.concatenate([rng.normal(1.25, 0.25, 60), rng.normal(5.5, 0.5, 60)])[:, None]
bics = {k: round(GaussianMixture(k, random_state=42).fit(X_sim).bic(X_sim), 2) for k in [1, 2, 3, 4]}
print("BIC by K:", bics, "-> selected K* =", min(bics, key=bics.get))
