    Entropy from KL Divergence

    September 2026 · 4 min read

    The handwritten derivation compares a node’s class proportions with uniform probabilities. This gives another interpretation of entropy. For a first introduction to the logarithms, use entropy as average prediction loss.

    Use an equally likely reference

    Let $p$ describe the class proportions in a node. For $K$ possible classes, a uniform reference $u$ assigns probability $1/K$ to each. In the two-class case, that is 0.5 and 0.5.

    Entropy is largest at this balanced mixture. It measures uncertainty in the labels, not whether the node is useful in every possible task.

    Lower entropy means greater KL from uniform

    Write entropy as $H(p)$ and KL divergence from $p$ to the uniform reference as $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|u)$. With base-2 logarithms, their relationship is:

    $$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|u)=\log_2 K-H(p).$$

    For two classes, the first term is 1. A balanced node has entropy 1 and KL 0; a pure node has entropy 0 and KL 1. The animation moves between mixtures and calculates both. Its Gini curve is a separate impurity measure, on a different scale.

    ▶ Impurity Curves and Distance from Uniform

    For two classes with proportion $p$: entropy $H(p)$ and Gini $2p(1-p)$ both peak at $p=\tfrac12$ (uniform, most impure) and vanish at the pure ends. The KL divergence from uniform, $1-H(p)$, does the opposite.

    Separate the three quantities

    For outcomes occurring with probabilities $p$ and predictions supplied by $q$, the terms in the scan mean:

    QuantityMeaning
    Entropy, $H(p)$Average log loss when predictions match $p$
    Cross-entropy, $H(p,q)$Average log loss when predictions use $q$
    KL, $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)$The second quantity minus the first

    KL is nonnegative but not a symmetric distance. All three must use the same logarithm base for this identity.

    Distinguish the parent from the split’s remaining impurity

    The expression “left fraction × left entropy + right fraction × right entropy” is the average impurity after a split. It need not equal the parent’s entropy before the split. Their difference is information gain.

    For example, splitting a 50/50 parent into two pure children changes entropy from 1 bit to 0, gaining 1 bit. Keep this distinction when reading the scan’s compact notation $H(S)$. The worked split calculation writes the two quantities separately.
