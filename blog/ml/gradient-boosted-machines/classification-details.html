    4. Classification: The Derivation

    September 2026 · Lecture 4 / 4 · 5 min read

    Notes on StatQuest with Josh Starmer · Gradient Boost Part 4 · 37:00 video. Explanations and redrawn figures follow the lecture’s worked example.

    This lecture derives the classification steps from binary log loss, including the leaf formula used in Lecture 3. Keep three quantities separate: the observed label $y$, the additive score $F$, and the probability $p=\operatorname{sigmoid}(F)$.

    1. Write the classification loss

    Video 2:00 ↗

    If $y=1$, the model assigns probability $p$ to the observed outcome. If $y=0$, it assigns $1-p$. Taking the negative log gives a loss that is small when the observed outcome receives high probability:

    $$L(y,p)=-\bigl[y\log p+(1-y)\log(1-p)\bigr].$$

    This is $-\log p$ for a “yes” row and $-\log(1-p)$ for a “no” row. Boosting changes the log-odds score, so express the same loss in terms of $F$.

    $$\log p=F-\log(1+e^F),\qquad\log(1-p)=-\log(1+e^F).$$
    $$L(y,F)=-yF+\log(1+e^F).$$

    Substitution combines the two $\log(1+e^F)$ coefficients into $y+(1-y)=1$. This is the same loss, now written in the variable we will update.

    2. Differentiate: the negative gradient is y − p

    Video 9:18 ↗

    Just as in regression, differentiating the total loss with respect to row $i$’s individual log-odds score $F(x_i)$ drops every other row $k \ne i$ from the sum:

    $$\frac{\partial L(y_i,F)}{\partial F}=-y_i+\frac{e^F}{1+e^F}=p_i-y_i.$$

    Negating that derivative gives the pseudo-residual:

    $$r_{im}=-\frac{\partial L(y_i,F)}{\partial F}=y_i-p_i.$$

    This is the negative gradient with respect to the log-odds score $F$, even though its simplified formula is written in terms of the probability $p_i$.

    3. Derive the starting score

    Video 10:59 ↗

    The lecture’s smaller data set has three people, with labels $[1,1,0]$. Choose one shared score $c$ for everyone and minimize total loss. Its derivative is

    $$\frac{d}{dc}\sum_{i=1}^{n} L(y_i,c)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p-y_i)=np-\sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i=0.$$

    Solving gives $p_0=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i y_i=2/3$. Convert to the log-odds score we store:

    $$F_0=\log\frac{p_0}{1-p_0}=\log2.$$
    Lecture 4 uses these three rows. We retain p = 2/3 rather than rounding to 0.67 during calculations.

    4. Fit a tree to the pseudo-residuals

    Video 14:09 ↗

    The lecture splits on “likes popcorn.” Row 1 goes to one leaf; rows 2 and 3 share the other. These leaves define the regions $R_{jm}$. Next, choose a log-odds correction $\gamma$ for each region.

    5. Derive the leaf correction

    Video 16:43 ↗

    For one leaf $R$, the old scores $F_i$ are fixed. Adding a shared correction $\gamma$ to every row in that leaf gives total loss

    $$Q(\gamma)=\sum_{i:x_i\in R}L(y_i,F_i+\gamma).$$

    Approximate this loss near $\gamma=0$ with a second-order Taylor polynomial:

    $$Q(\gamma)\approx Q(0)+G\gamma+\frac12H\gamma^2.$$

    Here $G$ is the sum of the rows’ first derivatives and $H$ is the sum of their second derivatives. Since $L'(y,F)=p-y$ and $y$ is fixed, the second derivative is the derivative of sigmoid:

    $$L''(y,F)=\frac{dp}{dF}=\frac{e^F}{(1+e^F)^2}=p(1-p).$$

    Therefore $G=\sum_{i:x_i\in R}(p_i-y_i)$ and $H=\sum_{i:x_i\in R} p_i(1-p_i)$. Differentiate the quadratic with respect to the shared addition $\gamma$ and set to zero:

    $$Q'(\gamma)\approx G+H\gamma=0.$$
    $$\boxed{\gamma=-\frac{G}{H}=\frac{\sum_{i:x_i\in R}(y_i-p_i)}{\sum_{i:x_i\in R} p_i(1-p_i)}}.$$

    The numerator supplies the direction; the denominator scales the step by the loss’s curvature (one Newton step).

    6. Calculate the two leaf outputs

    Video 24:53 ↗

    Every row starts with $p=2/3$, so its curvature contribution is $p(1-p)=2/9$:

    $$\gamma_{\text{popcorn}}=\frac{1/3}{2/9}=1.5,\qquad \gamma_{\text{no popcorn}}=\frac{1/3-2/3}{2/9+2/9}=-0.75.$$

    7. Update scores, then probabilities, then residuals

    $$F_m(x)=F_{m-1}(x)+\nu\sum_{j=1}^{J_m}\gamma_{jm}\mathbf1\{x\in R_{jm}\},\qquad p_m(x)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-F_m(x)}}.$$
