    SHAP & LIME: Local Explainability

    September 2026 · 3 min read

    A trained model produces a prediction. LIME and SHAP help inspect how its inputs relate to that output, but they ask different questions: what simple model fits nearby behavior, or how should we allocate the difference from a baseline?

    Keep the predictor fixed

    An explainer queries the existing predictor on modified inputs; it does not retrain the predictor. LIME generates nearby inputs. A SHAP method evaluates what the output would be when some features are revealed and the others are averaged over a declared reference rule.

    A local explanation concerns one row. For example, it can describe why this row’s model score differs from a background average. It does not establish why the real-world outcome occurred.

    ▶ One Prediction, Two Explanation Views

    The highlighted point represents one row. LIME approximates nearby model behavior; SHAP allocates a prediction difference among features. This is a workflow sketch; the next chapters calculate examples.
