    Choosing SHAP, LIME, and Other Explainers

    September 2026 · 4 min read

    Choose an explainer by the question you need answered. A local approximation, a contribution to one prediction, and the effect of shuffling a feature on test performance are different quantities.

    Match the question to a method

    Your questionStarting pointWhat to specify
    How does the model behave near this row?LIMEPerturbations, distance weights, and surrogate quality
    How should this prediction difference be allocated?SHAPBackground, hidden-feature rule, and output units
    How can I compute those values for a supported tree ensemble?TreeSHAPModel support and exact explanation settings
    Which features does this model rely on for held-out performance?Permutation importanceEvaluation dataset, metric, and repeated shuffles

    Permutation importance measures how a chosen performance metric changes when one feature is shuffled. It needs labels and a useful predictive model. SHAP contribution magnitudes can be computed without labels, but they summarize the model’s outputs, not its accuracy. The permutation-importance guide explains that distinction.

    Inspect the assumptions that can change the answer

    Correlated features: another feature may substitute for a shuffled one, while SHAP attribution depends on the hidden-feature rule. A small score does not establish that the underlying information is useless.

    Unrealistic inputs: perturbations or background replacements may create combinations absent from real data. Check the comparison rows, not just the final plot.

    Instability: repeat sampled explanations with another seed or budget. For LIME, also inspect the local fitting error. For SHAP, check reconstruction in the specified output units and deliberately test another plausible background.

    None of these methods alone establishes what will happen after changing a feature in the real world. Use them to inspect model behavior and formulate questions that the data can support.

    Start with one inspectable case

    Pick a row whose inputs and prediction you can verify. Record the model, preprocessing, output, background or neighborhood, and explainer settings. Read the worked explanation before scaling to a dashboard.
