    RAG Evaluation: Find the Evidence, Use It, and Cite It

    September 2026 · 16 min read

    Before you start: pipeline-stage diagnosis and RAG fundamentals.

    A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipeline can retrieve the exact policy document it needs and accidentally slice off the crucial exception sentence during context packing. It can state the right answer while attaching a citation link to an unrelated paragraph. Or it can faithfully summarize an obsolete 2023 policy PDF that should have been retired. Collapsing all of those into a single 1–5 “answer quality” LLM-judge score hides which engineering team needs to act.

    Follow one concrete store-policy query across every boundary: “What are the return windows for standard and sale items, and can I return a sale item after 20 days?” Under current store rules, standard items have a 30-day window (Passage G) and sale items have a 14-day window (Passage E), so at 20 days the correct decision is No.

    Boundary 1: Is the supplied context sufficient and clean?

    Annotate required evidence at the level of atomic required facts rather than specific chunk IDs (so duplicate copies of Passage G do not double-count coverage). Define the symbols for question $q$:

    • $\mathcal{F}_q$: the set of annotated required facts needed to answer $q$ ($|\mathcal{F}_q| = 2$ here: standard window + sale window).
    • $C_q$: the ordered list of chunks actually sent in the generator’s prompt ($|C_q| \ge 1$).
    • $\operatorname{cov}(C_q) \subseteq \mathcal{F}_q$: the subset of required facts entailed by the passages in $C_q$.
    • $U(C_q) \subseteq C_q$: the subset of chunks in $C_q$ that provide non-redundant support for at least one fact in $\mathcal{F}_q$.
    $$\operatorname{ReqRecall}(q) = \frac{|\operatorname{cov}(C_q)|}{|\mathcal{F}_q|}, \qquad \operatorname{ChunkPrec}(q) = \frac{|U(C_q)|}{|C_q|}.$$

    Boundary 2: Separate task correctness, context faithfulness, and citation support

    Decompose the generated answer into $S \ge 1$ atomic statements $s_1, \dots, s_S$ (here $S = 3$: standard window, sale window, and the 20-day purchase decision), where statement $s_m$ carries attached citation set $\Gamma_m \subseteq C_q$. Score three distinct binary indicators on every claim $m \in \{1, \dots, S\}$:

    • $y_m^{\text{corr}} \in \{0, 1\}$ (Task correctness): $1$ iff $s_m$ agrees with current authoritative reference facts.
    • $y_m^{\text{faith}} \in \{0, 1\}$ (Context faithfulness): $1$ iff $s_m$ is logically entailed by the supplied context $C_q$ plus facts given in the user’s prompt.
    • $y_m^{\text{cite}} \in \{0, 1\}$ (Cited support / ALCE citation recall): $1$ iff $\Gamma_m$ is non-empty, resolves to valid spans in $C_q$, and the cited spans $\Gamma_m$ alone entail $s_m$.
    $$\operatorname{Correctness} = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{m=1}^{S} y_m^{\text{corr}}, \qquad \operatorname{Faithfulness} = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{m=1}^{S} y_m^{\text{faith}}, \qquad \operatorname{CitedSupport} = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{m=1}^{S} y_m^{\text{cite}}.$$

    Boundary 3: Evaluate selective abstention with visible denominators

    Every RAG test set needs unanswerable or insufficient-evidence queries so a model that guesses fluently is penalized. Over $N$ total evaluation queries ($N_{\text{suff}}$ with sufficient context, $N_{\text{insuff}}$ without), let $\hat{p}_j \in [0, 1]$ be the system’s answerability score on query $j$, let $z_j \in \{0, 1\}$ indicate whether its candidate answer is correct, and let $A(\tau) = \{j \in \{1,\dots,N\} : \hat{p}_j \ge \tau\}$ be the emitted set at threshold $\tau$:

    $$\operatorname{Cov}(\tau) = \frac{|A(\tau)|}{N}, \qquad \operatorname{SelAcc}(\tau) = \frac{1}{|A(\tau)|}\sum_{j \in A(\tau)} z_j \quad (\text{defined when } |A(\tau)| > 0).$$

    Audit LLM judges against human annotations

    Automated frameworks like RAGAS and ARES scale claim-level checks, yet LLM judges exhibit verbosity and position biases (Zheng et al. 2023). Pass only the actual supplied context $C_q$ to a faithfulness judge, pass only the resolved cited span $\Gamma_m$ to a citation judge, blind system names, randomize pairwise order, and calibrate automated labels against a stratified human-annotated gold subset:

    A compact JSON record for auditing one atomic claim
    {
  "question_id": "returns-sale-20-days",
  "claim": "Sale items have a 14-day return window.",
  "supplied_context_ids": ["G-current", "E-current"],
  "cited_span_id": "G-current:standard-window",
  "correct_against_current_rules": true,
  "supported_by_supplied_context": true,
  "supported_by_attached_citation": false,
  "reason": "E states 14 days; the cited G span concerns standard items."
}

    References and further reading

    • Es et al. (2023): RAGAS — automated reference-free evaluation of faithfulness, answer relevance, and context precision.
    • Gao et al. (2023): ALCE — statement-level NLI citation recall and citation precision.
    • Saad-Falcon et al. (2024): ARES — prediction-powered confidence intervals combining LLM judges with small human-labeled gold sets.
    • Zheng et al. (2023): Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena — position, verbosity, and self-enhancement biases in LLM evaluators.
