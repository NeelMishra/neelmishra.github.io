    nDCG: How Much Does the Order of Useful Results Matter?

    September 2026 · 4 min read

    Before you start: Precision, recall, AP, and reciprocal rank.

    A waterproof hiking boot, an ordinary hiking shoe, and a fashion sneaker should not necessarily receive the same credit. Binary relevance discards degrees of usefulness. nDCG lets you assign more gain to stronger matches and discount useful results that appear farther down the list.

    Calculate one list from scratch

    Use gain $g(r)=2^r-1$ and discount $1/\log_2(i+1)$ at rank $i$. Suppose the top three grades are [2, 0, 3], and the catalog contains grades [3, 2, 1, 0].

    $$\operatorname{DCG}@K=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{2^{r_i}-1}{\log_2(i+1)}.$$
    DCG@3 = 6.5. A grade-three result is valuable even at rank three, but it would receive more credit at rank one.
    RankGradeGainDiscounted gain
    1233 / log₂2 = 3
    2000 / log₂3 = 0
    3377 / log₂4 = 3.5

    To compute ideal DCG, sort all eligible judged documents by grade and take the best three: [3,2,1]. The ideal value is 7+3/log₂3+1/log₂4≈9.3928. Therefore nDCG@3=6.5/9.3928≈0.6920.

    $$\operatorname{nDCG}@K=\frac{\operatorname{DCG}@K}{\operatorname{IDCG}@K}.$$

    The denominator comes from the collection’s judgments, not merely the items your system happened to retrieve. If you normalize against only your returned items, you can give a perfect score to a system that missed the best documents. That becomes a conditional ordering metric and should be labeled as such.

    The gain function encodes a value judgment

    Exponential gain makes grade 3 worth seven units and grade 1 worth one. Linear gain makes them worth three and one. Neither follows automatically from the label names. Ask what the grades mean: is an excellent result seven times as useful as a marginal one? Often the scale is a practical ranking convention, not measured economic utility. Record it and avoid treating a 0.05 nDCG increase as a 5% revenue prediction.

    Many libraries accept graded labels but use different defaults. The ordering can also change if a rubric changes what counts as grade 2. Store grade definitions and gain mapping alongside the scores. If the system returns tied scores, use a deterministic secondary ordering or an evaluator’s documented tie policy.

    What happens when nothing is relevant?

    If every eligible document has zero gain, IDCG is zero. There is no meaningful ratio. As with AP, our lab marks this query as undefined for nDCG and reports the excluded count. Evaluate no-result detection or abstention separately. If an external benchmark defines nDCG=0 for such cases, follow its convention and label the difference when comparing reports.

    A perfect relevance order can still make a poor page

    Imagine ten nearly identical black shoes occupy the entire first page. Their relevance grades may be high, yet the page offers little variety. nDCG alone does not measure brand coverage, duplicate suppression, novelty, price diversity, or user fatigue. Add those measures only where they express a real product requirement; a navigational query for a precise model number may benefit from a narrow result set.

    Keep relevance, variety, and outcomes separate so a change has an interpretable explanation.
    QuestionCompanion measureInterpretation
    Did we expose enough distinct items?Catalog coverageDistinct served items / eligible catalog size over a defined window
    Are all results near duplicates?Duplicate rate or intra-list similarityRequires a declared duplicate rule or embedding model
    Did smaller slices improve?Per-slice nDCG and query countsExpose language, intent, cold-start, and hard-filter differences
    Did users succeed?Task completion or downstream outcomeValidate in an online experiment

    Use the ranking explorer in the previous chapter to move a grade-three item below a grade-one item. Precision may remain unchanged while nDCG falls. That is the point of graded evaluation: credit depends on which useful results are prominent.

    For a final report, include the cutoff, gain mapping, zero-IDCG rule, query weighting, judgment coverage, and uncertainty interval. Then inspect examples from the largest wins and losses. Aggregate nDCG tells you whether an ordering improved under your rubric; the examples explain why.

    Check your understanding: Can a reranker recover nDCG lost because the relevant item never entered its candidate set?

    No. A reranker can only reorder its candidates. Measure an oracle ranking within those candidates against the full collection ideal to expose the retrieval ceiling.
