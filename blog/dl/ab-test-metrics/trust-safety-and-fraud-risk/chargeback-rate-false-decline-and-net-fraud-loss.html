    Payment Chargeback Rate (bps), False Decline (Customer Insult Rate) & Net Risk-Adjusted Margin

    October 2026 · 11 min read

    Every real-time payment fraud model walks a tightrope: approve a stolen credit card and you lose $100\%$ of the transaction principal plus a $\$15\text{–}\$25$ card-network dispute fee; falsely decline a legitimate customer ("Customer Insult") and you lose their immediate purchase margin plus up to $30\%$ of their future Customer Lifetime Value ($\text{LTV}$). Learn how to measure Chargeback Rate in basis points ($\text{bps}$), overcome selective-label censoring using an Exploration Pass-Through Slice ($\epsilon$), and optimize Net Risk-Adjusted Contribution Margin.

    1. Payment Chargeback Rate (in Basis Points) & 30-to-90 Day Maturation Lag

    Let $\mathcal{A}$ denote the set of $N_{\text{app}} = |\mathcal{A}|$ approved transactions in an experiment arm, where transaction $i \in \mathcal{A}$ has dollar amount $d_i > 0$ and binary indicator $C_i \in \{0, 1\}$ equal to $1$ if the cardholder's issuing bank files a fraud chargeback (e.g., Visa Reason Code 10.4 / Mastercard 4837). Because fraud rates on approved traffic are small (typically $0.05\%\text{–}0.60\%$), production teams express both Transaction-Count Chargeback Rate and Dollar-Volume Chargeback Rate in basis points ($\text{bps}$, where $1\text{ bps} = 0.01\% = 10^{-4}$):

    $$\operatorname{CBR}_{\text{count}}\ (\text{bps}) = 10^4 \times \frac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} C_i}{N_{\text{app}}},\qquad \operatorname{CBR}_{\$}\ (\text{bps}) = 10^4 \times \frac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} d_i\,C_i}{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} d_i}.$$
    • Why You Must Track Both Count and Dollar $\text{CBR}$: Business profitability depends on $\operatorname{CBR}_{\$}$ (losing one $\$2{,}000$ laptop hurts $200\times$ more than losing a $\$10$ digital gift card), whereas Visa (VDMP) and Mastercard (ECMP) compliance monitoring thresholds penalize merchants based on Transaction-Count $\operatorname{CBR}_{\text{count}}$ (typically triggering fines and potential payment-rail termination if $\operatorname{CBR}_{\text{count}} \ge 65\text{–}90\text{ bps}$!).
    • The 30-to-90 Day Chargeback Maturation Lag: Cardholders only notice unauthorized charges when they review their monthly billing statement, so roughly $40\%$ of fraud chargebacks arrive within $14\text{ days}$, $75\%$ within $30\text{ days}$, and $98\%$ within $90\text{ days}$. To evaluate a 14-day online A/B test without waiting 90 days, fraud experimentation platforms combine:
      1. Early Issuer Fraud Warnings (Visa TC40 / Mastercard SAFE notifications): Electronic fraud reports transmitted by issuing banks within $2\text{–}7\text{ days}$ prior to formal chargeback settlement.
      2. Maturation-Adjusted Kaplan–Meier / Chain-Ladder Estimator: If an experiment transaction $i$ has been observed for $a_i$ days and historical fraud maturation CDF is $F_{\text{lag}}(a_i) = \Pr(\text{Report Delay} \le a_i \mid \text{Fraud})$, the completion-weighted chargeback estimate is $\hat{C}_i^{\text{mature}} = C_i(a_i) / F_{\text{lag}}(a_i)$ (blended with a 7-day post-transaction account-takeover / manual review audit model).

    2. False Decline (Customer Insult Rate) & The Selective Labels Problem

    Suppose a fraud model predicts fraud probability $\hat{p}_i = \hat{P}(\text{Fraud} \mid x_i)$ and declines any transaction where $\hat{p}_i \ge \tau$. What fraction of declined transactions $\mathcal{D} = \{i : \hat{p}_i \ge \tau\}$ were actually legitimate customers ($Y_i = 0$, where $Y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ is true fraud status)? This false discovery rate of the decline rule is called the False Decline Rate (or Customer Insult Rate):

    $$\operatorname{False\ Decline\ (Insult)\ Rate} = \Pr(Y_i = 0 \mid \hat{p}_i \ge \tau),\qquad \operatorname{Good\ User\ Insult\ Rate} = \Pr(\hat{p}_i \ge \tau \mid Y_i = 0).$$

    The Counterfactual Censoring Problem: When your system declines transaction $i \in \mathcal{D}$, the charge is never sent to the payment processor, so no chargeback can ever occur! You never observe $Y_i$ on declined transactions.

    Production Solution — Random Exploration Pass-Through Slice ($\epsilon$-Greedy Horvitz–Thompson Auditing): Leading payment platforms (Stripe Radar, Adyen, Uber, Airbnb) allocate a small, risk-bounded random exploration budget $\epsilon(s) \in [0.005, 0.05]$ (e.g., $1\%\text{–}2\%$ of model-declined transactions whose score lies between the decision threshold $\tau$ and a hard extreme-fraud ceiling $\tau_{\max}$, say $\hat{p}_i \in [\tau, 0.40]$) and approve them anyway (or route them through 3D-Secure / biometric step-up verification).

    Let $E_i \in \{0, 1\}$ indicate that declined-band transaction $i \in \mathcal{D}$ was randomly selected for exploration pass-through with known propensity $\epsilon(x_i) = \Pr(E_i = 1 \mid x_i) > 0$. Then the unbiased Horvitz–Thompson estimator for the False Decline Rate and missed good-user GMV in the declined band is:

    $$\widehat{\operatorname{FDR}}_{\text{decline}} = \frac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}} \frac{E_i}{\epsilon(x_i)}\,\bigl(1 - C_i\bigr)}{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}} \frac{E_i}{\epsilon(x_i)}},\qquad \widehat{\text{Insulted Good GMV}} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}} \frac{E_i}{\epsilon(x_i)}\,d_i\,\bigl(1 - C_i\bigr).$$

    3. Net Risk-Adjusted Contribution Margin & Bayes-Optimal Threshold $\tau^*$

    To unite approval conversion, take-rate revenue, chargeback principal losses, dispute fees, and long-term customer insult damage into a single dollar OEC, let:

    • $r \in (0, 1]$ be the platform's net contribution margin rate on a legitimate transaction of amount $d_i$ (e.g., $r = 12\%$ marketplace take rate, or $r = 85\%$ digital goods margin).
    • $\ell_{\text{fraud}}(d_i) = \alpha_{\text{liab}}\,d_i + f_{\text{cb}}$ be the dollar loss when an approved transaction is fraudulent ($C_i = 1$), where $\alpha_{\text{liab}} \in [0, 1]$ is the platform's principal liability share ($1.0$ if the platform eats the full商品/payout cost) and $f_{\text{cb}} \approx \$15$ is the non-refundable card-network chargeback fee.
    • $\ell_{\text{insult}}(u_i, d_i) = \gamma_{\text{abandon}}\,r\,d_i + \Delta\operatorname{LTV}_{\text{churn}}(u_i)$ be the expected LTV loss from insulting a legitimate user $u_i$ (empirical studies show $30\%\text{–}45\%$ of falsely declined buyers abandon the purchase and reduce future spend).

    Then the Net Risk-Adjusted Contribution Margin across all attempted transactions $i \in \{1, \dots, M\}$ (where $A_i \in \{0, 1\}$ is the approval indicator) is:

    $$\operatorname{Net\ Risk-Adjusted\ Margin} = \sum_{i=1}^M A_i\Bigl[r\,d_i\,(1 - Y_i) - \ell_{\text{fraud}}(d_i)\,Y_i\Bigr] - \sum_{i=1}^M (1 - A_i)\,\ell_{\text{insult}}(u_i, d_i)\,(1 - Y_i).$$

    Setting the expected marginal value of approving transaction $i$ equal to declining it yields the exact Bayes-Optimal Fraud Decision Threshold $\tau^*(u_i, d_i)$:

    $$\hat{p}_i \ge \tau^*(u_i, d_i) = \frac{r\,d_i + \ell_{\text{insult}}(u_i, d_i)}{r\,d_i + \ell_{\text{insult}}(u_i, d_i) + \alpha_{\text{liab}}\,d_i + f_{\text{cb}}}.$$

    Notice the profound implication for ML System Design: the optimal fraud block threshold $\tau^*$ is never a single static constant across all users! A trusted 3-year repeat subscriber with high $\ell_{\text{insult}}(u_i)$ should have a much higher fraud tolerance threshold $\tau^*$ than an anonymous guest checkout buying a high-resale gift card!

    Worked numerical example: Aggressive Fraud Blocking Destroying Net Profit

    Suppose a marketplace processes $100{,}000$ checkout attempts of $d = \$100$ each ($\$10\text{M}$ attempted GMV), with platform margin $r = 15\%$ ($\$15$ margin per good order), full principal fraud liability $\alpha_{\text{liab}} = 1.0$, chargeback fee $f_{\text{cb}} = \$15$ ($\ell_{\text{fraud}} = \$115$), and repeat-customer insult churn penalty $\Delta\text{LTV}_{\text{churn}} = \$20$:

    • Control ($C$, balanced threshold): Approves $97{,}000$ transactions ($3{,}000$ declined). Among approved, $200$ are fraud ($\operatorname{CBR}_C = 20.6\text{ bps}$). Exploration pass-through on the $3{,}000$ declines shows $50\%$ ($1{,}500$) were legitimate customers.
      • Good Approved Margin: $96{,}800 \times \$15 = +\$1{,}452{,}000$.
      • Fraud Chargeback Loss: $200 \times \$115 = -\$23{,}000$.
      • Insult LTV Churn Penalty: $1{,}500 \times \$20 = -\$30{,}000$.
      • $\text{Net Risk-Adjusted Margin}_C = \$1{,}452{,}000 - \$23{,}000 - \$30{,}000 = \mathbf{\$1{,}399{,}000}$.
    • Treatment ($T$, overly aggressive threshold lowered to cut chargebacks): Approves $94{,}000$ transactions ($6{,}000$ declined). Fraud drops to $120$ cases ($\operatorname{CBR}_T = 12.8\text{ bps}$, saving $80$ frauds!), but $2{,}920$ of the $3{,}000$ additional declines are legitimate customers ($4{,}420$ total insulted good orders!).
      • Good Approved Margin: $93{,}880 \times \$15 = +\$1{,}408{,}200$ ($-\$43{,}800$ lost immediate margin!).
      • Fraud Chargeback Loss: $120 \times \$115 = -\$13{,}800$ ($+\$9{,}200$ saved in fraud).
      • Insult LTV Churn Penalty: $4{,}420 \times \$20 = -\$88{,}400$ ($-\$58{,}400$ extra LTV destruction!).
      • $\text{Net Risk-Adjusted Margin}_T = \$1{,}408{,}200 - \$13{,}800 - \$88{,}400 = \mathbf{\$1{,}306{,}000}$ (a $-\$93{,}000$ net loss from over-blocking!).

    4. Python Implementation: Horvitz–Thompson False Decline Rate & Risk-Adjusted Margin

    import numpy as np

def evaluate_fraud_ab_with_exploration(
    amount: np.ndarray, p_fraud_hat: np.ndarray, threshold: float,
    explored_flag: np.ndarray, explore_propensity: np.ndarray,
    observed_chargeback: np.ndarray,
    take_rate: float = 0.15, cb_fee: float = 15.0, insult_ltv_loss: float = 20.0
):
    """
    Computes Approval Rate, Chargeback Rate (bps count & dollar),
    Horvitz-Thompson False Decline (Insult) Rate from the exploration pass-through slice,
    and Net Risk-Adjusted Contribution Margin.
    """
    approved = (p_fraud_hat < threshold)
    declined = ~approved

    # 1. Chargeback metrics on model-approved traffic
    app_amounts = amount[approved]
    app_cbs = observed_chargeback[approved]
    cbr_count_bps = 10_000.0 * np.mean(app_cbs)
    cbr_dollar_bps = 10_000.0 * np.sum(app_amounts * app_cbs) / np.sum(app_amounts)

    # 2. Horvitz-Thompson False Decline Rate on model-declined traffic via exploration slice
    dec_exp = explored_flag[declined]
    dec_prop = explore_propensity[declined]
    dec_cbs = observed_chargeback[declined]
    dec_amt = amount[declined]

    ht_weights = dec_exp / np.clip(dec_prop, 1e-5, 1.0)
    false_decline_rate = float(np.sum(ht_weights * (1 - dec_cbs)) / np.maximum(np.sum(ht_weights), 1e-9))
    est_insulted_good_orders = float(np.sum(ht_weights * (1 - dec_cbs)))
    est_insulted_good_gmv = float(np.sum(ht_weights * dec_amt * (1 - dec_cbs)))

    # 3. Net Risk-Adjusted Margin
    good_margin = float(np.sum(take_rate * app_amounts * (1 - app_cbs)))
    fraud_loss = float(np.sum((app_amounts + cb_fee) * app_cbs))
    insult_loss = est_insulted_good_orders * insult_ltv_loss
    net_margin = good_margin - fraud_loss - insult_loss

    return {
        "Approval_Rate_%": round(float(100 * np.mean(approved)), 2),
        "CBR_Count_bps": round(float(cbr_count_bps), 2),
        "CBR_Dollar_bps": round(float(cbr_dollar_bps), 2),
        "HT_False_Decline_Rate_%": round(100 * false_decline_rate, 2),
        "Est_Insulted_Good_GMV_$": round(est_insulted_good_gmv, 2),
        "Net_Risk_Adjusted_Margin_$": round(net_margin, 2),
    }

if __name__ == "__main__":
    rng = np.random.default_rng(808)
    M = 100_000
    amt = rng.lognormal(4.4, 0.5, M)
    p_true = rng.beta(0.3, 35.0, M)
    y_fraud = rng.binomial(1, p_true)
    p_hat = np.clip(p_true + rng.normal(0, 0.015, M), 0.0001, 0.999)
    prop = np.full(M, 0.02)  # 2% random exploration slice on declined band
    exp_flag = rng.binomial(1, prop)
    print("Balanced Threshold (0.12):", evaluate_fraud_ab_with_exploration(
        amt, p_hat, 0.12, exp_flag, prop, y_fraud
    ))
    print("Over-Strict Threshold (0.04):", evaluate_fraud_ab_with_exploration(
        amt, p_hat, 0.04, exp_flag, prop, y_fraud
    ))

    5. Self-Check Questions

    Why should fraud A/B tests randomize at the User / Account / Device-Graph Component level rather than the individual Transaction level?

    First, when a transaction is declined, the user (or fraudster) immediately retries 30 seconds later with the same or a backup card; transaction-level randomization would route Retry #1 to Treatment and Retry #2 to Control, contaminating both arms. Second, measuring the post-decline Customer Insult LTV penalty requires tracking the user's repeat purchasing behavior over the subsequent 30–90 days, which is only well-defined under user-level (or connected-account-component) randomization.

    Instead of hard-declining borderline risky transactions ($\hat{p}_i \in [\tau_{\text{challenge}}, \tau_{\text{block}}]$), modern payment systems trigger a step-up challenge (3DS2 / SMS OTP / FaceID). What metric evaluates the step-up tier?

    Track the Step-Up Challenge Completion Rate among Legitimate Users (friction drop-off rate) alongside Liability Shift Coverage (since successful 3DS authentication shifts fraud chargeback liability from the merchant to the card-issuing bank).
