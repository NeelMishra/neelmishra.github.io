    Click-Through Rate (CTR), Position Bias & Clicks Over Expected Clicks (COEC)

    October 2026 · ML System Design · Ad & Feed Click Evaluation

    Click-Through Rate ($\text{CTR}$) is the most ubiquitous metric in online ML, yet it is also one of the easiest metrics to misinterpret in an A/B test. If a treatment removes low-CTR bottom-of-page ad slots, overall $\text{CTR}$ jumps by $+15\%$ while total clicks and revenue drop! Conversely, if a better ranking model encourages users to scroll deeper into slots $5\text{–}10$, overall $\text{CTR}$ can fall even though CTR increased at every single position (Simpson's Paradox). Here we formalize Served vs. Viewable $\text{CTR}$, the Position Examination Hypothesis, and Clicks Over Expected Clicks ($\text{COEC}$).

    1. Served CTR vs. Viewable CTR vs. Qualified CTR

    Not all "impressions" in system logs represent a human eye seeing an ad. Production pipelines track three distinct denominators:

    $$\text{Served CTR} = \frac{N_{\text{clicks}}}{N_{\text{rendered}}}, \qquad \text{Viewable CTR (vCTR)} = \frac{N_{\text{clicks}}}{N_{\text{viewable}}}, \qquad \text{Qualified CTR (qCTR)} = \frac{N_{\text{clicks with dwell} \ge \tau}}{N_{\text{viewable}}}$$
    • Served Impression ($N_{\text{rendered}}$): Logged when the server returns the ad payload or the client DOM mounts the element (even below the fold). Highly sensitive to prefetching/caching changes!
    • Viewable Impression ($N_{\text{viewable}}$, MRC/IAB Standard): Logged only when $\ge 50\%$ of the ad's pixels remain in the active viewport for $\ge 1.0\text{ continuous second}$ ($\ge 2.0\text{ seconds}$ for video ads). Always prefer $\text{vCTR}$ over Served $\text{CTR}$ in A/B tests.
    • Qualified / Long-Click CTR ($\text{qCTR}$): Counts only clicks where the user stays on the advertiser landing page for $\ge \tau$ seconds (e.g., $\tau = 5\text{s}$ or $10\text{s}$), filtering out accidental fat-finger taps and misleading clickbait creatives.

    2. The Examination Hypothesis & Position Bias

    Under the Richardson–Craswell Position-Based Examination Model (PBM), a user clicks an ad $a$ displayed at slot position $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ if and only if they both examine slot $k$ ($E_k = 1$) and find ad $a$ relevant ($R_{u,a} = 1$):

    $$P(C = 1 \mid u, a, k) = \underbrace{P(E = 1 \mid k)}_{\gamma_k \text{ (Position Propensity)}} \cdot \underbrace{P(R = 1 \mid u, a)}_{\mu_{u,a} \text{ (Intrinsic Relevance)}}$$

    In sponsored search, $\gamma_1 \approx 1.0$, $\gamma_2 \approx 0.60$, $\gamma_3 \approx 0.38$, and $\gamma_4 \approx 0.22$. Because $\gamma_k$ drops steeply with rank $k$, raw $\text{CTR}$ conflates model ranking quality ($\mu_{u,a}$) with where ads were shown ($\gamma_k$).

    3. Clicks Over Expected Clicks ($\text{COEC}$): Solving Simpson's Paradox

    To evaluate whether Treatment $T$ surfaces higher-relevance ads than Control $C$ without being confounded by shifts in the position distribution (or page-type / device distribution), we compute Clicks Over Expected Clicks ($\text{COEC}$). First, estimate the baseline average CTR at each position $k$ (and optionally surface $s$) using Control traffic:

    $$\overline{\text{CTR}}_{k,C} = \frac{C_{k,C}}{I_{k,C}}$$

    Then, for any arm (Control or Treatment) with impression counts $I_k$ and observed clicks $C_k$ at slot $k$, the Expected Clicks under the baseline position curve is $\sum_{k=1}^K I_k \cdot \overline{\text{CTR}}_{k,C}$, and $\text{COEC}$ is the ratio of observed to expected clicks:

    $$\boxed{\text{COEC} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K} C_k}{\sum_{k=1}^{K} I_k \cdot \overline{\text{CTR}}_{k,C}}}$$

    By construction, $\text{COEC}_C = 1.000$. If $\text{COEC}_T = 1.035$, then Treatment generated $+3.5\%$ more clicks than expected given the exact slots where its impressions occurred!

    4. Worked Numerical Example: Simpson's Paradox in Raw CTR vs. COEC

    Suppose a search engine has two ad slots: Slot 1 (Top of Page) with baseline $\overline{\text{CTR}}_1 = 10.0\%$ and Slot 2 (Bottom of Page) with baseline $\overline{\text{CTR}}_2 = 2.0\%$. In Treatment, our ranking model improves CTR at both slots by $+10\%$ relative ($\text{CTR}_{1,T} = 11.0\%$, $\text{CTR}_{2,T} = 2.2\%$), and also fills Slot 2 on more queries:

    Arm Slot 1 ($I_1, C_1, \text{CTR}_1$) Slot 2 ($I_2, C_2, \text{CTR}_2$) Global Raw $\text{CTR} = \frac{C_1+C_2}{I_1+I_2}$ Position-Normalized $\text{COEC}$
    Control ($C$) $10{,}000 \text{ imp}, \; 1{,}000 \text{ clk} \; (10.0\%)$ $2{,}000 \text{ imp}, \; 40 \text{ clk} \; (2.0\%)$ $\frac{1{,}040}{12{,}000} = \mathbf{8.67\%}$ $\frac{1{,}040}{10{,}000(0.10) + 2{,}000(0.02)} = \mathbf{1.000}$
    Treatment ($T$) $10{,}000 \text{ imp}, \; 1{,}100 \text{ clk} \; (11.0\%)$ $8{,}000 \text{ imp}, \; 176 \text{ clk} \; (2.2\%)$ $\frac{1{,}276}{18{,}000} = \mathbf{7.09\%} \;\;(-18.2\%\text{!})$ $\frac{1{,}276}{10{,}000(0.10) + 8{,}000(0.02)} = \frac{1{,}276}{1{,}160} = \mathbf{1.100} \;\;(+10.0\%)$

    Look at the paradox: Treatment increased CTR by $+10\%$ in Slot 1, increased CTR by $+10\%$ in Slot 2, and generated $+22.7\%$ more total clicks ($1{,}276$ vs. $1{,}040$)—yet **Global Raw CTR dropped by $-18.2\%$** ($7.09\%$ vs. $8.67\%$) solely because Slot 2 impressions expanded! $\text{COEC} = 1.100$ immediately recovers the exact $+10.0\%$ intrinsic relevance lift.

    5. Production Python Implementation: User-Clustered COEC A/B Test

    import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

def coec_ab_test(
    clicks_c: np.ndarray, imps_c: np.ndarray,
    clicks_t: np.ndarray, imps_t: np.ndarray,
) -> dict:
    """
    Computes Position-Normalized COEC and its Delta-Method p-value.
    clicks_c, imps_c: shape (N_users_c, K_slots)
    clicks_t, imps_t: shape (N_users_t, K_slots)
    """
    # Step 1: Baseline CTR per slot k from Control
    base_ctr_k = clicks_c.sum(axis=0) / np.maximum(imps_c.sum(axis=0), 1)

    # Step 2: Per-user observed clicks Y_u and expected clicks E_u
    y_c = clicks_c.sum(axis=1)
    e_c = imps_c @ base_ctr_k

    y_t = clicks_t.sum(axis=1)
    e_t = imps_t @ base_ctr_k

    # Step 3: Ratio estimator COEC = sum(Y_u) / sum(E_u) via Delta Method
    def _ratio_var(y, e):
        n = len(y)
        r = y.sum() / e.sum()
        mu_e = e.mean()
        var_r = (y.var(ddof=1) - 2 * r * np.cov(y, e, ddof=1)[0, 1] + (r ** 2) * e.var(ddof=1)) / (n * mu_e ** 2)
        return r, var_r

    coec_c, var_c = _ratio_var(y_c, e_c)
    coec_t, var_t = _ratio_var(y_t, e_t)
    se = np.sqrt(var_c + var_t)
    z = (coec_t - coec_c) / se
    p_val = 2.0 * (1.0 - stats.norm.cdf(abs(z)))

    raw_ctr_c = y_c.sum() / imps_c.sum()
    raw_ctr_t = y_t.sum() / imps_t.sum()

    return {
        "raw_ctr_control": round(float(raw_ctr_c), 5),
        "raw_ctr_treatment": round(float(raw_ctr_t), 5),
        "coec_control": round(float(coec_c), 5),
        "coec_treatment": round(float(coec_t), 5),
        "coec_lift_pct": round(float((coec_t - coec_c) * 100), 3),
        "z_stat": round(float(z), 3),
        "p_value": float(p_val),
    }

if __name__ == "__main__":
    rng = np.random.default_rng(10)
    # 2 slots: Control mostly slot 1, Treatment expands slot 2 but has +10% CTR in both
    ic = np.column_stack([rng.poisson(10, 5000), rng.poisson(2, 5000)])
    cc = rng.binomial(ic, [0.10, 0.02])
    it = np.column_stack([rng.poisson(10, 5000), rng.poisson(8, 5000)])
    ct = rng.binomial(it, [0.11, 0.022])
    print(coec_ab_test(cc, ic, ct, it))

    6. Self-Check & Interview Exercises

    Exercise 1 — How Can a Ranking Model Game Both CTR and COEC?

    Suppose a new visual ad ranker achieves $+6\%$ $\text{COEC}$ by ranking high-curiosity clickbait ads at Slot 1. Which two guardrail metrics immediately expose this failure mode?

    Check solution

    (1) Short-Click Bounce Rate (percentage of ad clicks where the user bounces back from the advertiser landing page in $< 5\text{ seconds}$) and (2) Post-Click Conversion Rate ($\text{pcCVR}$). If $\text{COEC}$ rises by $+6\%$ while $\text{pcCVR}$ drops by $-10\%$ and short-click bounces spike, the model is exploiting accidental or misleading clicks rather than true user intent.
