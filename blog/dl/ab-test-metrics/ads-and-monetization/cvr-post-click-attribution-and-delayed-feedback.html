    Conversion Rate (CVR), Multi-Touch Attribution & Delayed Feedback Bias

    October 2026 · ML System Design · Conversion Modeling & Right-Censored Labels

    While a click happens within seconds of an ad impression, a downstream conversion (purchasing a laptop, installing an app and subscribing, or applying for a mortgage) can occur hours or weeks later. This creates three fundamental challenges in ML System Design and online A/B testing: (1) conditioning bias when evaluating $\text{CVR}$ per click vs. per impression ($\text{CTCVR}$), (2) attribution windows ($1\text{-day click}$ vs. $7\text{-day click} / 1\text{-day view}$), and (3) Delayed Feedback right-censoring, where conversions triggered near the end of an A/B test have not yet arrived when the experiment is analyzed.

    1. Post-Click CVR vs. CTCVR vs. View-Through CVR

    Let $I \in \{0,1\}$ denote an ad impression, $C \in \{0,1\}$ a click, and $Y \in \{0,1\}$ a downstream conversion within attribution window $W$:

    $$\text{Post-Click CVR (pcCVR)} = P(Y = 1 \mid C = 1) = \frac{N_{\text{post-click conversions}}}{N_{\text{clicks}}}$$ $$\text{Click-Through Conversion Rate (CTCVR)} = P(C = 1, Y = 1 \mid I = 1) = \text{CTR} \times \text{pcCVR} = \frac{N_{\text{post-click conversions}}}{N_{\text{impressions}}}$$ $$\text{View-Through CVR (vtCVR)} = P(Y = 1 \mid I = 1, C = 0) = \frac{N_{\text{view-only conversions}}}{N_{\text{non-clicked impressions}}}$$
    The Selection Bias Trap of Evaluating $\text{pcCVR}$ in Isolation: In an online A/B test, the set of clicked impressions $\{i : C_i = 1\}$ is endogenous to the treatment model! Suppose Treatment lowers the click threshold and generates $+20\%$ more clicks from medium-intent users who convert at a slightly lower per-click rate. Then $\text{pcCVR} = N_{\text{conv}} / N_{\text{clicks}}$ will decrease even though total conversions per impression ($\text{CTCVR}$) increased by $+12\%$! Always evaluate $\text{CTCVR}$ (conversions per impression or per active user) alongside $\text{pcCVR}$ to avoid post-click selection bias.

    2. Attribution Windows & Multi-Touch Credit Assignment

    In production ad platforms (Meta Ads, Google Ads, Apple SKAdNetwork), a conversion event is matched to prior ad interactions using standardized Attribution Windows:

    • Standard Windows: $1\text{-Day Click}$ ($W_c = 24\text{h}$), $7\text{-Day Click}$ ($W_c = 168\text{h}$), or $7\text{-Day Click} + 1\text{-Day View}$ ($W_c = 168\text{h}, W_v = 24\text{h}$).
    • Fixed-Cohort Maturation Rule for A/B Tests: If your metric is $7\text{-Day Click CVR}$, any click occurring on Day $t$ requires waiting until Day $t + 7$ to observe its full label. In a 14-day experiment, either restrict the numerator/denominator to clicks from Days $1\text{–}7$ with a 7-day maturation bake, or use importance-weighted survival correction.
    • Multi-Touch Attribution (MTA): When a user clicks multiple ads $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_m$ before converting, credit $w_j$ ($\sum_{j=1}^m w_j = 1$) is assigned via:
      • Last-Touch: $w_m = 1$, $w_j = 0$ for $j < m$.
      • Time-Decay (Half-life $\tau_{1/2}$): $w_j \propto 2^{-(T_{\text{conv}} - t_j)/\tau_{1/2}}$.
      • Shapley Value Attribution: $w_j = \sum_{S \subseteq \mathcal{A} \setminus \{j\}} \frac{|S|!(|\mathcal{A}| - |S| - 1)!}{|\mathcal{A}|!}\left[v(S \cup \{j\}) - v(S)\right]$, measuring each touchpoint's marginal contribution to conversion probability across all coalition orderings.

    3. Mathematical Model of Delayed Feedback (Chapelle 2014)

    Let $Y \in \{0, 1\}$ be the true eventual conversion indicator (whether the user will ever convert within window $W$), and let $D \ge 0$ be the conversion delay ($T_{\text{conv}} - T_{\text{click}}$) given $Y = 1$. Suppose we evaluate the experiment at elapsed time $E$ since the click occurred. We only observe $Y^{\text{obs}} = 1$ if $Y = 1$ and $D \le E$.

    Assuming conversion delay follows an exponential distribution $P(D \le E \mid Y = 1, \mathbf{x}) = 1 - \exp(-\lambda(\mathbf{x}) E)$ with delay hazard rate $\lambda(\mathbf{x}) > 0$, the probability of observing a conversion by elapsed age $E$ is:

    $$P(Y^{\text{obs}} = 1 \mid \mathbf{x}, E) = P(Y = 1 \mid \mathbf{x}) \cdot \left(1 - e^{-\lambda(\mathbf{x}) E}\right)$$

    And the posterior probability that an currently unconverted click ($Y^{\text{obs}} = 0$ at age $E$) will still convert later is given by Bayes' rule:

    $$P(Y = 1 \mid Y^{\text{obs}} = 0, \mathbf{x}, E) = \frac{p(\mathbf{x})\, e^{-\lambda(\mathbf{x}) E}}{1 - p(\mathbf{x})\left(1 - e^{-\lambda(\mathbf{x}) E}\right)}$$

    In online A/B evaluation, we can form an Inverse-Probability-of-Censoring Weighted (IPCW) estimator for clicks with elapsed age $E_i \ge E_{\min}$:

    $$\widehat{\text{CVR}}_{\text{IPCW}} = \frac{1}{N_{\text{clicks}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{clicks}}} \frac{Y_i^{\text{obs}}}{1 - \exp(-\hat{\lambda}_i E_i)}$$

    which removes right-censoring downward bias without waiting an extra 7 days after stopping traffic!

    4. Worked Numerical Example: Right-Censored CVR Bias

    Suppose an advertiser's true $7\text{-day}$ conversion rate is $p = 5.0\%$, and the median conversion delay is $24\text{ hours}$ ($\lambda = \ln(2)/1.0 = 0.693\text{ day}^{-1}$). For clicks that occurred $E = 12\text{ hours}$ ($0.5\text{ days}$) before the A/B test snapshot, only $1 - e^{-0.693 \times 0.5} = 29.3\%$ of true conversions have arrived, so naïve observed CVR is $5.0\% \times 0.293 = 1.46\%$. Dividing observed conversions by the completion factor $F(0.5) = 0.293$ restores the unbiased estimate $\widehat{\text{CVR}}_{\text{IPCW}} = 1.46\% / 0.293 = 5.00\%$.

    5. Production Python Implementation: IPCW Delayed-Feedback CVR Estimator

    import numpy as np

def ipcw_delayed_cvr(
    y_obs: np.ndarray,
    elapsed_days: np.ndarray,
    half_life_days: float = 1.0,
    min_elapsed_days: float = 0.25,
) -> dict:
    """
    Computes naive observed CVR vs. Inverse-Probability-of-Censoring Weighted (IPCW)
    CVR correcting for exponential conversion delay right-censoring.
    """
    lam = np.log(2.0) / half_life_days
    # Clip minimum elapsed time to avoid exploding weights on brand-new clicks
    eff_elapsed = np.maximum(elapsed_days, min_elapsed_days)
    completion_prob = 1.0 - np.exp(-lam * eff_elapsed)

    naive_cvr = y_obs.mean()
    ipcw_scores = y_obs / completion_prob
    ipcw_cvr = ipcw_scores.mean()
    ipcw_se = ipcw_scores.std(ddof=1) / np.sqrt(len(y_obs))

    return {
        "naive_observed_cvr": round(float(naive_cvr), 5),
        "ipcw_debiased_cvr": round(float(ipcw_cvr), 5),
        "ipcw_se": round(float(ipcw_se), 5),
        "mean_completion_factor": round(float(completion_prob.mean()), 4),
    }

if __name__ == "__main__":
    rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
    N = 100_000
    true_y = rng.binomial(1, 0.05, size=N)
    # Clicks occurred uniformly over a 7-day test window
    elapsed = rng.uniform(0.25, 7.0, size=N)
    # Exponential delay with half-life = 1.0 day (lambda = ln(2))
    delays = rng.exponential(scale=1.0 / np.log(2.0), size=N)
    y_observed = (true_y == 1) & (delays <= elapsed)
    print(ipcw_delayed_cvr(y_observed.astype(float), elapsed, half_life_days=1.0))

    6. Self-Check & Interview Exercises

    Exercise 1 — Why Can Retraining Hourly on Raw Unmatured Logs Hurt a CVR Model?

    Suppose you switch a production $\text{pCVR}$ model from batch training (with a 7-day attribution wait) to streaming hourly training where any click without a conversion so far is labeled $y=0$. What happens to the model's predictions and auction bids?

    Check solution

    Clicks from the last few hours haven't had time to convert yet, so labeling them $y=0$ injects massive false negatives into recent training data. The model learns to systematically under-predict $\hat{p}(\text{CVR})$ by $30\%\text{–}60\%$ (destroying $\text{COPC}$ calibration and causing the ad auction to under-bid on CPA campaigns), and furthermore biases predictions toward impulse low-price items (which convert in 5 minutes) over high-ticket considered purchases (which take 2 days to convert). Production streaming $\text{pCVR}$ pipelines must use Delayed Feedback Loss (Chapelle 2014) or Fake-Negative Importance Weighting (Yasui et al., KDD 2020).
