    Normalized Cross-Entropy (NE), Calibration (COPC) & Ad Fatigue Guardrails

    October 2026 · ML System Design · Probability Calibration & User Experience Protection

    Suppose you multiply every predicted $\hat{p}_{\text{CTR}}$ in your ad ranking model by $2.0$. What happens to $\text{ROC-AUC}$? Nothing at all—$\text{ROC-AUC}$ only depends on relative rank order! What happens to your live ad auction? CPC bids now beat CPM bids and reserve prices twice as often, advertisers are over-charged relative to true value, and budget pacing collapses. That is why production ad systems track Normalized Cross-Entropy ($\text{NE}$), Clicks-Over-Predicted-Clicks ($\text{COPC}$), and strict Ad Fatigue Guardrails in every online A/B test.

    1. Normalized Cross-Entropy (NE / RIG)

    Introduced in Meta's seminal paper Practical Lessons from Predicting Clicks on Ads at Facebook (He et al., KDD 2014), Normalized Cross-Entropy ($\text{NE}$) divides the model's average binary log-loss by the entropy of a trivial constant predictor that always outputs the empirical background click rate $\bar{y} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N y_i$:

    $$\boxed{\text{NE} = \frac{-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[y_i \ln \hat{p}_i + (1 - y_i)\ln(1 - \hat{p}_i)\right]}{-\left[\bar{y}\ln \bar{y} + (1 - \bar{y})\ln(1 - \bar{y})\right]}}$$

    Why is $\text{NE}$ far better than raw Log-Loss for online monitoring across days and surfaces?

    • Invariance to Background Base Rate $\bar{y}$: Raw Log-Loss is mechanically lower on surfaces with lower CTR ($\bar{y} = 0.5\%$ has entropy $0.0315$, whereas $\bar{y} = 5.0\%$ has entropy $0.1985$). If an A/B test shifts impression mix toward lower-CTR slots, raw Log-Loss drops even if the model got worse! Normalizing by background entropy removes this base-rate confounding.
    • Relative Information Gain ($\text{RIG}$): $\text{RIG} = 1 - \text{NE}$ measures the percentage of label entropy explained by the model. In large-scale ad systems, even a $-0.1\%$ ($-10\text{ bps}$) reduction in $\text{NE}$ is considered a major launchable win.

    2. Calibration Metrics: COPC & Slice-Level Calibration

    Global and slice-level probability calibration is monitored online via Clicks-Over-Predicted-Clicks ($\text{COPC}$) (or its reciprocal $\text{PCOC} = \frac{\sum_i \hat{p}_i}{\sum_i y_i}$):

    $$\boxed{\text{COPC}_{\mathcal{S}} = \frac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}} y_i}{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}} \hat{p}_i}}$$
    • $\text{COPC} = 1.00$: Perfectly calibrated in aggregate on slice $\mathcal{S}$.
    • $\text{COPC} < 1.00$ (e.g., $0.85$): The model over-predicts CTR by $+17.6\%$ ($\hat{p} > y$), causing the auction to over-value CPC/oCPM ads against CPM ads and over-clear reserve prices.
    • $\text{COPC} > 1.00$ (e.g., $1.15$): The model under-predicts CTR, starving performance campaigns of impressions.

    Because global $\text{COPC} = 1.00$ can mask over-prediction on iOS (+15%) canceling out under-prediction on Android (-15%), online scorecards track Expected Calibration Error ($\text{ECE}$) across quantile bins $B_m$ and **Slice-Weighted $|\text{COPC}_{\mathcal{S}} - 1|$** across device OS, placement, campaign objective, and advertiser size:

    $$\text{ECE} = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \frac{|B_m|}{N} \left|\bar{y}(B_m) - \bar{p}(B_m)\right|$$
    Correcting Negative-Downsampling Shift in Calibration: During training, if negative examples ($y=0$) are downsampled by keeping only fraction $w \in (0, 1]$ (e.g., $w = 0.10$) to save compute, the raw model logit $\hat{z}$ predicts the biased rate $q = \frac{p}{p + w(1-p)}$. Before entering the auction, every prediction MUST be recalibrated in closed form: $$\hat{p}_{\text{calibrated}} = \frac{q}{q + \frac{1 - q}{w}} = \sigma\!\left(\hat{z} + \ln w\right)$$

    3. Ad Load, Creative Fatigue & User Distress Guardrails

    Every ad ranking A/B test pairs $\text{RPM} / \text{eCPM}$ with strict user-side and ecosystem guardrails:

    Guardrail Metric Mathematical Definition What It Protects Against
    Ad Load / Ad Density $\frac{N_{\text{ad impressions}}}{N_{\text{organic}} + N_{\text{ad impressions}}}$ Prevents dynamic-slot algorithms from flooding feeds with ads to juice short-term RPM
    Ad Hide / Report / X-Out Rate $\frac{N_{\text{hide}} + w_{\text{rep}} N_{\text{report}}}{N_{\text{viewable ad imps}}}$ Catches offensive, repetitive, or intrusive creatives (often penalized directly in auction score via $-\lambda_{\text{hide}}\hat{p}_{\text{hide}}$)
    Creative Frequency & Decay Slope $\frac{\text{CTR}(f \ge 4)}{\text{CTR}(f = 1)}$ per user-creative pair Detects ad fatigue when a small pool of high-bid ads is shown repeatedly to the same user
    Organic Engagement Cannibalization $\Delta \text{Organic Session Time}$ & $\Delta \text{Organic Clicks}$ Measures the shadow cost of ads displacing organic content or organic search answers

    4. Production Python Implementation: NE, Slice COPC & Downsampling Recalibration

    import numpy as np

def compute_ne_and_copc(y_true: np.ndarray, p_pred: np.ndarray, eps: float = 1e-12) -> dict:
    """Computes Normalized Cross-Entropy (NE), Relative Information Gain (RIG), COPC, and ECE."""
    y = np.asarray(y_true, dtype=float)
    p = np.clip(np.asarray(p_pred, dtype=float), eps, 1.0 - eps)

    # Model Log-Loss
    logloss = -np.mean(y * np.log(p) + (1.0 - y) * np.log(1.0 - p))

    # Background entropy of empirical mean CTR
    y_bar = np.clip(y.mean(), eps, 1.0 - eps)
    bg_entropy = -(y_bar * np.log(y_bar) + (1.0 - y_bar) * np.log(1.0 - y_bar))

    ne = logloss / bg_entropy
    copc = y.sum() / p.sum()

    # 10-bin quantile ECE
    quantiles = np.linspace(0, 100, 11)
    edges = np.percentile(p, quantiles)
    ece = 0.0
    for m in range(10):
        mask = (p >= edges[m]) & (p <= edges[m + 1] if m == 9 else p < edges[m + 1])
        if mask.any():
            ece += (mask.sum() / len(y)) * abs(y[mask].mean() - p[mask].mean())

    return {
        "log_loss": round(float(logloss), 6),
        "background_entropy": round(float(bg_entropy), 6),
        "normalized_cross_entropy_NE": round(float(ne), 5),
        "rig_pct": round(float((1.0 - ne) * 100), 3),
        "copc": round(float(copc), 4),
        "ece": round(float(ece), 5),
    }

if __name__ == "__main__":
    rng = np.random.default_rng(21)
    true_p = rng.beta(1.5, 48.5, size=50_000)  # ~3% average CTR
    y = rng.binomial(1, true_p)
    # Well-calibrated model vs 15% over-predicting model
    print("Calibrated:", compute_ne_and_copc(y, true_p))
    print("Miscalibrated (1.15x):", compute_ne_and_copc(y, np.clip(true_p * 1.15, 1e-6, 0.99)))

    5. Self-Check & Interview Exercises

    Exercise 1 — Why Can $\text{NE}$ Degrade Even When $\text{ROC-AUC}$ Improves?

    In an offline/online evaluation of a new Transformer ad ranker trained with aggressive focal loss or label smoothing, you observe $\text{ROC-AUC}$ increased by $+0.40\%$, but $\text{NE}$ worsened (increased) by $+1.20\%$ and $\text{COPC} = 0.78$. Why did this happen, and how do you fix it before launching to the auction?

    Check solution

    Label smoothing and focal loss distort the log-odds scale of the output probabilities (pushing rare $3\%$ click probabilities toward $0.05$ or $0.10$), which leaves the relative ranking order ($\text{ROC-AUC}$) intact or improved while severely inflating calibration error ($\text{COPC} = 0.78 \implies 28\%$ over-prediction), which penalizes log-loss and $\text{NE}$. Applying post-hoc Isotonic Regression or Platt / Temperature Scaling per surface slice on a held-out calibration window restores $\text{COPC} \approx 1.00$ and turns the $+0.40\%$ AUC gain into a true $\text{NE}$ reduction.
