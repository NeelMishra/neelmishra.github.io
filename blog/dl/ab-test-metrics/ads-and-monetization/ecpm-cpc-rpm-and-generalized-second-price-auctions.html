    eCPM, CPC, RPM & Generalized Second-Price (GSP / VCG) Auction Pricing

    October 2026 · ML System Design · Ad Auction Mechanics & Revenue Metrics

    How does an ad platform compare a brand advertiser bidding $\$12.00$ per $1{,}000$ impressions ($\text{CPM}$), a performance advertiser bidding $\$1.50$ per click ($\text{CPC}$), and an e-commerce advertiser bidding $\$25.00$ per purchase ($\text{oCPM} / \text{CPA}$)? The ML ranking system translates every bid into a single universal currency—Expected Cost Per Mille ($\text{eCPM}$)—and clears the auction via Generalized Second-Price ($\text{GSP}$) or Vickrey–Clarke–Groves ($\text{VCG}$) pricing. Understanding how model predictions interact with auction clearing prices is essential for Ads ML System Design interviews.

    1. The Core Monetization Identities: eCPM, CPC & RPM

    For an ad $a$ shown to user $u$, let $\hat{p}_{\text{CTR}} = \hat{P}(\text{Click} \mid u, a)$ and $\hat{p}_{\text{CVR}} = \hat{P}(\text{Conv} \mid u, a, \text{Click})$ be the calibrated ML predictions. The expected revenue per $1{,}000$ impressions ($\text{eCPM}$) is:

    $$\text{eCPM}(u, a) = \begin{cases} b_{\text{CPM}} & \text{(CPM Bid)} \\[4pt] 1000 \cdot \hat{p}_{\text{CTR}}(u,a) \cdot b_{\text{CPC}} & \text{(CPC Bid)} \\[4pt] 1000 \cdot \hat{p}_{\text{CTR}}(u,a) \cdot \hat{p}_{\text{CVR}}(u,a) \cdot b_{\text{CPA}} & \text{(oCPM / CPA Target Bid)} \end{cases}$$

    In empirical A/B test evaluation over observed impressions $N_{\text{imp}}$, clicks $N_{\text{clk}}$, searches/sessions $N_{\text{req}}$, and realized revenue $R_{\text{total}}$:

    $$\text{Realized eCPM} = \frac{R_{\text{total}}}{N_{\text{imp}}} \times 1000 = 1000 \times \text{CTR} \times \text{CPC}, \qquad \text{Average CPC} = \frac{R_{\text{total}}}{N_{\text{clk}}}$$ $$\text{RPM (Revenue per Thousand Requests/Searches)} = \frac{R_{\text{total}}}{N_{\text{req}}} \times 1000 = \text{Ad Load}\!\left(\frac{N_{\text{imp}}}{N_{\text{req}}}\right) \times \text{eCPM}$$

    Notice the critical decomposition of $\text{RPM}$:

    $$\boxed{\text{RPM} = 1000 \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{N_{\text{imp}}}{N_{\text{req}}}\right)}_{\text{Ad Depth / Fill Rate}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{N_{\text{clk}}}{N_{\text{imp}}}\right)}_{\text{CTR}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{R_{\text{total}}}{N_{\text{clk}}}\right)}_{\text{Clearing CPC}}}$$

    2. Generalized Second-Price (GSP) vs. VCG Auction Mechanics

    In a quality-adjusted Generalized Second-Price (GSP) auction (used in search ads), ads are ranked in descending order of their Total Ad Rank Score $S_a$:

    $$S_a = \hat{p}_{\text{CTR}}(u,a) \cdot b_a + \gamma \, q(u, a)$$

    where $b_a$ is the advertiser's maximum CPC bid and $\gamma \, q(u, a)$ is an additive user-experience quality/organic utility term (which can be negative if the ad is low quality or high bounce). If ad $a_{(k)}$ wins slot $k$ directly above runner-up ad $a_{(k+1)}$ (with score $S_{(k+1)}$), the minimum CPC that $a_{(k)}$ needed to bid just to beat $S_{(k+1)}$ (or the slot reserve score $R_{\min}$) is:

    $$\hat{p}_{\text{CTR},(k)} \cdot \text{CPC}_{(k)} + \gamma q_{(k)} = \max\!\left(S_{(k+1)}, R_{\min}\right) \implies \boxed{\text{CPC}_{(k)} = \frac{\max\!\left(S_{(k+1)}, R_{\min}\right) - \gamma q_{(k)}}{\hat{p}_{\text{CTR},(k)}} + \$0.01}$$
    Why Improving ONLY the Top Ad's $\hat{p}_{\text{CTR}}$ Can Lower Short-Term CPC Revenue! Look at the denominator of $\text{CPC}_{(k)}$: $\hat{p}_{\text{CTR},(k)}$! If your new ML model increases $\hat{p}_{\text{CTR},(1)}$ of the already-winning #1 ad without changing who is in #2 ($S_{(2)}$), then the clearing price $\text{CPC}_{(1)} = S_{(2)} / \hat{p}_{\text{CTR},(1)}$ decreases! How does a better ML ranker actually increase auction revenue? By re-ordering candidates—promoting a relevant candidate with higher $\hat{p}_{\text{CTR}} \cdot b$ from slot 4 to slot 1 or 2, which raises the runner-up score $S_{(2)}$ and thickens auction competition!

    VCG (Vickrey–Clarke–Groves) Pricing in Multi-Slot Feeds

    In Meta/social feed auctions, VCG charges each winning advertiser the exact externality (social welfare loss) their presence imposes on all other advertisers and the user. If showing ad $a_{(1)}$ in slot 1 pushes ad $a_{(2)}$ from slot 1 (position examination $\gamma_1$) down to slot 2 ($\gamma_2$) and pushes ad $a_{(K+1)}$ out of the page entirely ($\gamma_{K+1}=0$):

    $$\text{Expected Payment}_{\text{VCG}}(a_{(k)}) = \sum_{j = k+1}^{K+1} \left(\gamma_{j-1} - \gamma_j\right) S_{(j)}$$

    Unlike GSP, VCG is dominant-strategy incentive compatible (DSIC)—truthful bidding ($b_a = v_a$) is the mathematically optimal strategy for every advertiser.

    3. Worked Numerical Example: GSP Auction & Model Re-Ranking Lift

    Consider 3 ads competing for 1 top slot with $\gamma = 0$ and reserve $R_{\min} = \$0.10$ per impression ($\text{eCPM} = \$100$):

    • Ad A: Bid $b_A = \$4.00$, True $\text{CTR}_A = 4.0\%$, Old Model $\hat{p}_A = 4.0\% \implies S_A^{\text{old}} = \$0.160$.
    • Ad B: Bid $b_B = \$3.00$, True $\text{CTR}_B = 7.0\%$, Old Model $\hat{p}_B = 4.5\% \implies S_B^{\text{old}} = \$0.135$.

    Under the Old Model: Ad A wins ($S_A = 0.160 > S_B = 0.135$). It pays $\text{CPC}_A = \frac{0.135}{0.040} = \$3.375$ whenever clicked. Because Ad A's true CTR is $4.0\%$, realized $\text{eCPM}^{\text{old}} = 1000 \times 0.040 \times \$3.375 = \mathbf{\$135.00}$.

    Under the New Model (which accurately recognizes $\hat{p}_B = 7.0\%$): Now $S_B^{\text{new}} = 0.070 \times \$3.00 = \$0.210 > S_A^{\text{new}} = \$0.160$. Ad B wins the auction! Ad B pays $\text{CPC}_B = \frac{0.160}{0.070} = \$2.286$ per click. Because Ad B's true CTR is $7.0\%$, realized $\text{eCPM}^{\text{new}} = 1000 \times 0.070 \times \$2.286 = \mathbf{\$160.00}$ (a $+18.5\%$ revenue lift and $+75\%$ click lift, even though average CPC dropped from $\$3.38$ to $\$2.29$!).

    4. Production Python Implementation: GSP vs. VCG Multi-Slot Simulator

    import numpy as np

def run_gsp_and_vcg_auction(
    bids: np.ndarray,
    pctr: np.ndarray,
    true_ctr: np.ndarray,
    slot_gammas: np.ndarray,
    reserve_ecpm: float = 10.0,
) -> dict:
    """
    Simulates a K-slot ad auction under both GSP and VCG pricing rules.
    slot_gammas: position examination probabilities [gamma_1, ..., gamma_K].
    """
    K = len(slot_gammas)
    scores = pctr * bids  # expected value per examination (in dollars)
    reserve_score = reserve_ecpm / 1000.0

    # Filter eligible ads above reserve and sort descending
    eligible = np.where(scores >= reserve_score)[0]
    order = eligible[np.argsort(-scores[eligible])]
    winners = order[:K]

    gsp_expected_rev = 0.0
    vcg_expected_rev = 0.0
    gsp_cpcs = []

    for k in range(len(winners)):
        w_idx = winners[k]
        next_score = scores[order[k + 1]] if (k + 1) < len(order) else reserve_score
        next_score = max(next_score, reserve_score)

        # GSP clearing CPC and realized revenue using true_ctr * slot_gamma
        cpc_k = next_score / pctr[w_idx]
        gsp_cpcs.append(round(float(cpc_k), 4))
        gsp_expected_rev += slot_gammas[k] * true_ctr[w_idx] * cpc_k

        # VCG externality payment
        externality = 0.0
        for j in range(k + 1, len(winners) + 1):
            gamma_prev = slot_gammas[j - 1]
            gamma_curr = slot_gammas[j] if j < K else 0.0
            s_j = scores[order[j]] if j < len(order) else reserve_score
            externality += (gamma_prev - gamma_curr) * max(s_j, reserve_score)
        vcg_expected_rev += externality * (true_ctr[w_idx] / pctr[w_idx])

    return {
        "winning_ads": winners.tolist(),
        "gsp_cpcs": gsp_cpcs,
        "realized_gsp_rpm": round(float(gsp_expected_rev * 1000.0), 2),
        "realized_vcg_rpm": round(float(vcg_expected_rev * 1000.0), 2),
    }

if __name__ == "__main__":
    bids = np.array([4.0, 3.0, 2.5, 1.8])
    pctr = np.array([0.04, 0.07, 0.05, 0.04])
    true_ctr = np.array([0.04, 0.07, 0.05, 0.04])
    gammas = np.array([1.0, 0.6, 0.35])
    print(run_gsp_and_vcg_auction(bids, pctr, true_ctr, gammas))

    5. Self-Check & Interview Exercises

    Exercise 1 — Reserve Price Tuning in Thin vs. Thick Auctions

    In which auctions does raising the Reserve Price $R_{\min}$ produce the largest $\text{RPM}$ lift: auctions with 25 competing advertisers or niche long-tail queries with only 1 eligible advertiser? What is the guardrail risk of setting $R_{\min}$ too high?

    Check solution

    In thin auctions with 1 advertiser (or 1 dominant advertiser far ahead of #2), the runner-up score $S_{(2)}$ is zero or near-zero, so the winner pays the reserve price $R_{\min} / \hat{p}_{(1)}$. Raising $R_{\min}$ directly increases CPC on every thin auction where $S_{(1)} \ge R_{\min}$. However, if $R_{\min}$ exceeds $S_{(1)}$, the auction goes unfilled (zero revenue), and over time advertisers whose ROI drops below their target will reduce budgets or churn—so Ad Fill Rate and Advertiser ROI / Budget Retention are mandatory guardrails.
