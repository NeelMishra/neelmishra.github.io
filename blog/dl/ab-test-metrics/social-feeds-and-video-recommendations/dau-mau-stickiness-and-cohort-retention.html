    DAU/MAU Stickiness Ratio, $L_7/L_{28}$ Active Days & $D_1 / D_7 / D_{28}$ Cohort Retention

    October 2026 · Habit Formation, Active-Day Histograms & Surrogate Index Estimation

    Within-session consumption metrics like Watch Time per Session can be inflated in the short term by dark patterns (autoplay loops, cliffhanger clickbait, aggressive push notifications) that burn out users over subsequent weeks. The ultimate North Star of any consumer feed or streaming recommendation system is Habit Formation & Long-Term Retention: does the ranking model make the user's experience so rewarding today that they voluntarily open the app again tomorrow, next week, and next month? Here we derive the exact mathematics of $\text{DAU}/\text{MAU}$ Stickiness, $L_7 / L_{28}$ Active-Day Distributions, Session Frequency vs. Length Decomposition, Cohort Retention ($D_k$), and Athey–Chetty–Imbens Surrogate Index Models.

    1. DAU/MAU Stickiness Ratio & $L_7 / L_{28}$ Active-Day Histograms

    At the macro product level, Stickiness is defined as the ratio of Daily Active Users ($\text{DAU}$) to Monthly ($28$-Day) Active Users ($\text{MAU}_{28}$):

    $$\text{Stickiness} = \frac{\overline{\text{DAU}}}{\text{MAU}_{28}} \in \left[\frac{1}{28},\, 1.0\right]$$

    How do we translate macro $\text{DAU}/\text{MAU}_{28}$ into a user-level random variable that can be tested cleanly in a randomized A/B experiment? Let $A_{u,d} \in \{0, 1\}$ indicate whether user $u$ logged at least one qualified active session on day $d$. Over a window of $W \in \{7, 14, 28\}$ days, user $u$'s Active-Day Count ($L_W(u)$) is:

    $$L_W(u) = \sum_{d=1}^{W} A_{u,d} \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots, W\}$$

    Taking the expectation over all monthly active users ($L_{28}(u) \ge 1$) yields an exact algebraic identity connecting user-level $L_{28}$ to macro $\text{DAU}/\text{MAU}_{28}$:

    $$\frac{\overline{\text{DAU}}}{\text{MAU}_{28}} = \frac{\frac{1}{28}\sum_{d=1}^{28} \sum_{u=1}^N A_{u,d}}{\sum_{u=1}^N \mathbb{I}[L_{28}(u) \ge 1]} = \frac{1}{28}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[L_{28}(u) \;\big|\; L_{28}(u) \ge 1\bigr]$$

    In an A/B test where $N_C$ and $N_T$ users are randomized into Control and Treatment, we evaluate Unconditional $L_7(u)$ or $L_{14}(u)$ per Randomized User (including $L_W(u) = 0$ for users who become inactive during the window) so the denominator $N$ is fixed by randomization:

    $$\bar{L}_7 = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{u=1}^N L_7(u), \qquad L_7^{\text{CUPED}}(u) = L_7(u) - \theta\bigl(L_{7,\text{pre}}(u) - \bar{L}_{7,\text{pre}}\bigr)$$

    Because pre-experiment $L_{7,\text{pre}}(u)$ has an extraordinarily high correlation ($\rho \approx 0.85\text{–}0.92$) with in-experiment $L_7(u)$, CUPED reduces the standard error of $\bar{L}_7$ by $45\%\text{–}65\%$, making $\Delta \bar{L}_7$ one of the most statistically powerful retention-sensitive metrics in all of consumer ML!

    Distributional Shifts on the $L_7 / L_{28}$ Power-User Curve

    Rather than inspecting only the mean $\bar{L}_7$, mature experimentation platforms compute the treatment effect across bins of the $L_7$ histogram:

    • Rescuing At-Risk / Casual Users ($\Pr(L_7(u) \ge 1) - \Pr(L_7(u) = 0)$): Measures whether the model prevents casual users from churning into $L_7 = 0$.
    • Power-User Graduation ($\Pr(L_7(u) \in \{6, 7\})$): Measures whether regular users ($3\text{–}5\text{ days/week}$) form a daily habit ($6\text{–}7\text{ days/week}$).

    2. Decomposing Watch Time: Session Frequency vs. Session Length

    For any user $u$ over a $W$-day test window, Total Watch Time $T_u$ factors multiplicatively into three behavioral components:

    $$T_u = \underbrace{L_W(u)}_{\text{Active Days}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{S_u}{L_W(u)}\right)}_{\text{Sessions per Active Day}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{T_u}{S_u}\right)}_{\text{Mean Duration per Session}}$$

    Taking logarithmic relative changes ($\frac{\Delta Y}{Y} \approx \Delta \ln Y$) shows that relative Watch-Time lift decomposes additively into:

    $$\frac{\Delta \bar{T}}{\bar{T}} \approx \underbrace{\frac{\Delta \bar{L}_W}{\bar{L}_W}}_{\text{Daily Return Lift}} + \underbrace{\frac{\Delta (\text{Sessions/Day})}{\text{Sessions/Day}}}_{\text{Intraday Habit Lift}} + \underbrace{\frac{\Delta (\text{Min/Session})}{\text{Min/Session}}}_{\text{Within-Session Depth Lift}}$$
    Why Session Frequency (Inter-Session Return Time) Matters More Than Session Length:

    Empirical meta-analyses across thousands of A/B tests at major video and social platforms consistently show that a $+1\%$ increase in Session Frequency (or decrease in Inter-Session Return Time $\Delta t_{\text{return}}$) produces $2.5\times\text{–}4\times$ larger long-term $D_{60}$ retention lift than a $+1\%$ increase in single-session length! Why? Because session length only measures how hard it is to stop scrolling once the app is already open, whereas initiating a new session $4$ hours later proves the user remembered the previous session fondly enough to actively tap the app icon again.

    3. Cohort Retention ($D_1, D_7, D_{28}$) & The Sensitivity Challenge

    For a cohort of users $\mathcal{C}_0$ who enter the experiment (or install the app) on reference day $t_0$, Exact Day-$k$ Retention ($D_k$) and Rolling Window Retention ($R_{[k_1, k_2]}$) are defined as:

    $$D_k = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_0|}\sum_{u \in \mathcal{C}_0} \mathbb{I}\bigl[\text{Active}(u, t_0 + k) = 1\bigr], \qquad R_{[8, 14]} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_0|}\sum_{u \in \mathcal{C}_0} \mathbb{I}\!\left[\sum_{k=8}^{14} A_{u, t_0+k} \ge 1\right]$$

    Why Raw $D_{28}$ Retention Has Low Sensitivity in Standard 2-Week A/B Tests

    Suppose baseline $D_{14}$ retention for existing active users is $p_C = 0.80$, so the Bernoulli variance is $p_C(1-p_C) = 0.16$ ($\sigma = 0.40$). Even a genuinely outstanding ranking algorithm lift typically moves $D_{14}$ retention by only $\Delta p = +0.15\text{ percentage points}$ ($+0.0015$). Using the two-sample sample-size formula for $\alpha = 0.05$ ($z_{0.975}=1.96$) and $80\%$ power ($z_{0.80}=0.84$):

    $$N_{\text{per arm}} = \frac{2(z_{1-\alpha/2} + z_{1-\beta})^2 \sigma^2}{(\Delta p)^2} = \frac{2(1.96 + 0.84)^2 (0.16)}{(0.0015)^2} = \frac{2 \times 7.84 \times 0.16}{2.25 \times 10^{-6}} \approx 1{,}115{,}022 \text{ users per arm!}$$

    Furthermore, waiting $28$ or $60$ days to observe $D_{28}$ or $D_{60}$ slows engineering velocity to a crawl. How do top ML teams solve both the time horizon problem and the statistical power problem?

    4. Constructing a Short-Term Surrogate Index for Long-Term Retention

    Following the econometric framework of Athey, Chetty, Imbens, and Kang (The Surrogate Index: Combining Short-Term Proxies to Estimate Long-Term Treatment Effects), let $Y_u^{\text{long}} = L_{[31, 60]}(u)$ be the long-term outcome (active days in Month 2), and let $\mathbf{S}_u \in \mathbb{R}^d$ be the vector of short-term behaviors observed during Days $1\text{–}14$ of the A/B test:

    $$\mathbf{S}_u = \Bigl(L_{14}(u),\; \text{Sessions}_{1:14}(u),\; \text{Follows}_{1:14}(u),\; \text{Shares}_{1:14}(u),\; \text{Saves}_{1:14}(u),\; \text{WatchMin}_{1:14}(u),\; \text{Hides}_{1:14}(u)\Bigr)^\top$$

    Using historical longitudinal logs (where both Days $1\text{–}14$ and Days $31\text{–}60$ are observed), we train a calibrated regression model $h(\mathbf{S}_u, \mathbf{X}_{u,\text{pre}}) = \widehat{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[Y_u^{\text{long}} \mid \mathbf{S}_u, \mathbf{X}_{u,\text{pre}}\bigr]$. Then, at Day $14$ of any new A/B test, we evaluate the treatment effect on the Surrogate Index $\widehat{Y}_u^{\text{surr}} = h(\mathbf{S}_u, \mathbf{X}_{u,\text{pre}})$:

    $$\widehat{\tau}_{\text{surr}} = \frac{1}{N_T}\sum_{u \in \mathcal{T}} h(\mathbf{S}_u, \mathbf{X}_{u,\text{pre}}) - \frac{1}{N_C}\sum_{u \in \mathcal{C}} h(\mathbf{S}_u, \mathbf{X}_{u,\text{pre}})$$

    Under the Surrogacy Assumption ($Y_u^{\text{long}} \perp\!\!\!\perp W_u \mid \mathbf{S}_u, \mathbf{X}_{u,\text{pre}}$, i.e., the ranking change affects Month-2 retention through the comprehensive vector of Month-1 habits, social graph edges, saves, and satisfaction signals captured in $\mathbf{S}_u$), $\widehat{\tau}_{\text{surr}}$ is an unbiased, lower-variance estimator of the 60-day retention effect available on Day 14!

    5. Worked Numerical Example: Comparing Two Ranking Algorithms via Decomposition & Surrogates

    Suppose you test two candidate ranking models against Control over $14$ days ($N = 500{,}000$ users/arm):

    • Model A (Autoplay Loop / Long-Video Boost): Increases total watch time by $+2.8\%$, driven by $+5.1\%$ longer session duration, but Sessions/Day drops by $-1.4\%$ and $L_{14}$ Active Days drops by $-0.8\%$.
    • Model B (Creator Discovery + Diversity + Freshness Boost): Increases total watch time by $+1.9\%$, driven by $+1.1\%$ lift in $L_{14}$ Active Days, $+1.5\%$ lift in Sessions/Day, and $+8.4\%$ lift in Creator Follows, with $-0.7\%$ shorter average session duration.
    Metric (Days 1–14) Control Baseline Model A (Binge Boost) Model B (Habit & Follow Boost)
    Total Watch Time / User $420.0\text{ min}$ $431.8\text{ min}$ ($+2.8\%$) $428.0\text{ min}$ ($+1.9\%$)
    ├─ $L_{14}$ Active Days / User $8.40\text{ days}$ $8.33\text{ days}$ ($-0.8\%$) $8.49\text{ days}$ ($+1.1\%$)
    ├─ Sessions / Active Day $2.50$ $2.465$ ($-1.4\%$) $2.538$ ($+1.5\%$)
    └─ Mean Session Duration $20.00\text{ min}$ $21.02\text{ min}$ ($+5.1\%$) $19.86\text{ min}$ ($-0.7\%$)
    New Creator Follows / User $1.20$ $1.05$ ($-12.5\%$) $1.30$ ($+8.3\%$)
    Surrogate Predicted $\Delta L_{[31,60]}$ $17.50\text{ days}$ $-0.14\text{ days}$ (Churn risk!) $+0.26\text{ days}$ (Durable growth!)

    Decision: Ship Model B, reject Model A. Even though Model A has a higher raw watch-time lift ($+2.8\%$ vs $+1.9\%$), its multiplicative decomposition reveals that users are returning on fewer days ($-0.8\%$) and opening fewer sessions ($-1.4\%$), causing the 60-day Surrogate Retention Index to go negative. Model B builds durable return habits ($L_{14} \uparrow$, Sessions/Day $\uparrow$, Follows $\uparrow$).

    6. Production Python Implementation: CUPED $L_7$, Decomposition & Surrogate Index

    import numpy as np
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Dict
from scipy import stats

@dataclass
class UserRetentionTelemetry:
    pre_l14: np.ndarray          # Active days in 14 days before experiment
    in_l14: np.ndarray           # Active days during 14-day experiment
    in_sessions: np.ndarray      # Total sessions in 14-day experiment
    in_watch_min: np.ndarray     # Total watch time (minutes) in 14-day experiment
    in_follows: np.ndarray       # New creator follows in 14-day experiment
    in_saves_shares: np.ndarray  # Explicit saves + shares in 14-day experiment
    in_hides: np.ndarray         # Explicit hides / not-interested in 14-day experiment

def evaluate_retention_and_surrogate_index(
    ctrl: UserRetentionTelemetry,
    trt: UserRetentionTelemetry,
    surrogate_weights: Dict[str, float] = None,
) -> Dict[str, Dict[str, float]]:
    """
    Computes CUPED-adjusted L14 Active Days, Session Frequency, and a calibrated
    Multi-Signal Surrogate Index forecasting Month-2 Active Days L_[31,60].
    """
    if surrogate_weights is None:
        # Weights learned via historical ridge regression predicting L_[31,60]
        surrogate_weights = {
            "in_l14": 1.65,
            "in_sessions": 0.08,
            "in_watch_min": 0.002,
            "in_follows": 0.35,
            "in_saves_shares": 0.18,
            "in_hides": -0.45,
        }

    def cuped_test(y_c, x_c, y_t, x_t):
        x_pool = np.concatenate([x_c, x_t])
        y_pool = np.concatenate([y_c, y_t])
        theta = np.cov(y_pool, x_pool, ddof=1)[0, 1] / np.var(x_pool, ddof=1)
        xm = x_pool.mean()
        yc_adj = y_c - theta * (x_c - xm)
        yt_adj = y_t - theta * (x_t - xm)
        se = np.sqrt(np.var(yc_adj, ddof=1) / len(yc_adj) + np.var(yt_adj, ddof=1) / len(yt_adj))
        diff = float(yt_adj.mean() - yc_adj.mean())
        z = diff / se if se > 0 else 0.0
        p = float(2.0 * (1.0 - stats.norm.cdf(abs(z))))
        return {
            "control": float(yc_adj.mean()),
            "treatment": float(yt_adj.mean()),
            "abs_diff": diff,
            "rel_lift_pct": float(100.0 * diff / yc_adj.mean()),
            "se": float(se),
            "p_value": p,
        }

    def compute_surrogate(tel: UserRetentionTelemetry) -> np.ndarray:
        return (
            surrogate_weights["in_l14"] * tel.in_l14
            + surrogate_weights["in_sessions"] * tel.in_sessions
            + surrogate_weights["in_watch_min"] * tel.in_watch_min
            + surrogate_weights["in_follows"] * tel.in_follows
            + surrogate_weights["in_saves_shares"] * tel.in_saves_shares
            + surrogate_weights["in_hides"] * tel.in_hides
        )

    surr_c = compute_surrogate(ctrl)
    surr_t = compute_surrogate(trt)

    return {
        "CUPED_L14_Active_Days": cuped_test(ctrl.in_l14, ctrl.pre_l14, trt.in_l14, trt.pre_l14),
        "CUPED_Sessions_Per_User": cuped_test(ctrl.in_sessions, ctrl.pre_l14, trt.in_sessions, trt.pre_l14),
        "CUPED_Surrogate_L31_60_Index": cuped_test(surr_c, ctrl.pre_l14, surr_t, trt.pre_l14),
    }

    7. Self-Check & ML System Design Interview Exercises

    1. Why does Goodhart's Law break a Surrogate Index if you directly put the surrogate linear combination into your real-time feed ranker's scoring function?

    The Surrogacy Assumption ($Y^{\text{long}} \perp\!\!\!\perp W \mid \mathbf{S}$) relies on the historical structural relationship between short-term signals $\mathbf{S}$ (e.g., Creator Follows) and long-term retention $Y^{\text{long}}$. If you heavily boost $\hat{p}_{\text{follow}}$ in the feed ranker solely because "Follows have a $+0.35$ weight in the surrogate model," the ranker may start surfacing aggressive "Follow for Part 2!" bait creators. Those low-quality follows will have a much lower marginal effect on 60-day retention than organic follows did historically! To prevent Goodhart distortion, regularly validate surrogate predictions against a Long-Term Holdout ($1\%\text{–}2\%$ of traffic held back for a full quarter).

    2. Why is $L_7$ Active Days typically much more statistically sensitive than binary $D_7$ Retention in an A/B test on existing users?

    Binary $D_7$ retention only observes a single slice in time (Day $7$): a user who was active on Days $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$ but happened to be busy on Day $7$ logs $D_7 = 0$, whereas a user who only opened the app once on Day $7$ logs $D_7 = 1$. By summing across all $7$ days, $L_7(u) = \sum_{d=1}^7 A_{u,d} \in \{0, \dots, 7\}$ captures 7 times as many daily coin flips per user and enjoys a much higher pre-experiment autocorrelation ($\rho \approx 0.88$ for $L_7$ vs. $\rho \approx 0.45$ for single-day $D_7$), yielding dramatically smaller relative standard errors after CUPED adjustment.

    3. How do you detect and separate a "Novelty Effect" from a true habit-formation gain when evaluating Session Frequency over a 14-day test?

    Segment the treatment effect by Day-Since-First-Exposure ($k = 1, 2, \dots, 14$) or compare Week-1 lift ($\Delta L_{[1,7]}$) against Week-2 lift ($\Delta L_{[8,14]}$). A UI novelty effect spikes on Days $1\text{–}3$ and decays monotonically toward zero by Days $10\text{–}14$. Conversely, a personalization or recommendation quality improvement (e.g., better interest embeddings or creator follows) exhibits a compounding learning curve where Week-2 lift $\Delta L_{[8,14]}$ is larger than Week-1 lift $\Delta L_{[1,7]}$ because the recommender accumulates richer positive feedback for the user over time.
