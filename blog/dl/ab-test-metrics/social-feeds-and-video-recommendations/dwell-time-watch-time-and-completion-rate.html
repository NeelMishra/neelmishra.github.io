    Dwell Time, Total Watch Time, Video Completion Rate (VCR) & Skip-Before-3s Rate

    October 2026 · Passive Consumption Metrics · Duration Debiasing & Heavy-Tail Statistics

    In modern infinite-scroll feeds (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn, X), users rarely click a hyperlink to open a new page—consumption happens directly inside the viewport as the user scrolls or swipes. Consequently, continuous time-in-viewport metrics—Post Dwell Time, Total Video Watch Time, Video Completion Rate ($\text{VCR}$), Loop Rate, and Skip-Before-$3\text{s}$ Bounce Rate—serve as the primary implicit feedback signals for both model training and online A/B evaluation. Yet raw Watch Time and raw $\text{VCR}$ suffer from severe, opposing Video Duration Biases that can distort an entire recommendation ecosystem if left unnormalized.

    1. Qualified Feed Impressions & Static Post Dwell Time

    In an infinite-scroll feed (such as LinkedIn, X/Twitter, Reddit, or Instagram mixed media), what counts as an impression? If a user flicks their thumb rapidly down the screen at $3{,}000\text{ pixels/sec}$, ten posts may pass through the viewport in $400\text{ms}$ without the user's eye ever fixating on them. Dividing engagement or dwell time by raw rendered DOM nodes makes fast-scrollers look like they have terrible engagement rates.

    Production feed telemetry therefore enforces the MRC / Industry Qualified Viewport Impression Standard: an item $i$ logs a qualified impression for user $u$ if at least $\alpha_{\text{px}} = 50\%$ of the card's pixel area remains inside the active foreground viewport for a continuous duration of at least $\tau_{\text{imp}} = 1.0\text{s}$ (or $2.0\text{s}$ for video view counts).

    Viewport Dwell Time on Image & Text Cards

    For non-video feed items (text posts, carousels, news articles, image cards), let $d_{u,i}$ be the total time card $i$ spends with $\ge 50\%$ viewport visibility while scroll velocity is below the reading threshold ($|v_{\text{scroll}}| < v_{\max}$). Because reading speed and card length $L_i$ (word count or carousel slide count) vary widely, we track two metrics:

    1. Qualified Read Rate ($\text{QRR}_{\text{feed}}$): Fraction of qualified impressions where viewport dwell exceeds a length-adjusted reading threshold $\tau_{\text{read}}(L_i) = \max\bigl(3.0\text{s},\, \min(15.0\text{s}, 1.5\text{s} + 0.15\text{s}\cdot \text{words}_i)\bigr)$:
      $$\text{QRR}_{\text{feed}} = \frac{\sum_{u=1}^N \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_u} \mathbb{I}\bigl[d_{u,i} \ge \tau_{\text{read}}(L_i)\bigr]}{\sum_{u=1}^N |\mathcal{I}_u|}$$
    2. Log-Dwell Time per Impression: $\ln(1 + \min(d_{u,i}, 120\text{s}))$, which compresses idle-tab outliers when a user leaves their phone unlocked on a single post.

    2. Total Watch Time per DAU & Heavy-Tail Variance Control

    For video platforms (YouTube, TikTok, Reels, Netflix), Daily Watch Time per Active User (or per randomized user in the experiment cohort) is the flagship consumption driver metric. Let $T_{u,d}$ be the total foreground playback time (in minutes) accumulated by user $u$ on day $d \in \{1, \dots, D_{\text{exp}}\}$:

    $$W_u = \frac{1}{D_{\text{exp}}}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{\text{exp}}} T_{u,d}$$

    Notice that $W_u$ is already aggregated to the randomization unit (user $u$)! If a user does not open the app on day $d$, $T_{u,d} = 0$ is included in $W_u$.

    Why You Must Include $T_{u,d} = 0$ on Inactive Days (Unconditional Watch Time per User):

    If you compute Watch Time per Daily Active User by dividing only by the users who opened the app on day $d$, you create survivorship / composition bias! Suppose Treatment sends a helpful notification or improves the feed so that marginal light users (who watch $8\text{ min/day}$) return on day $d$, whereas in Control only heavy users (who watch $65\text{ min/day}$) opened the app. Adding light returning users to Treatment lowers average Watch Time per DAU on day $d$, even though total watch time increased for every single user! Always use Total Watch Time per Randomized User ($\bar{W} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{u=1}^N W_u$) as the primary test statistic, and report conditional Watch Time per DAU alongside $\text{DAU}$ lift for diagnostic decomposition.

    Winsorization & CUPED for Watch Time

    User watch time $W_u$ is heavy-tailed: the top $0.5\%$ of accounts include smart TVs left playing $24\text{ hours/day}$ in lobbies or bedrooms ($1{,}440\text{ min/day}$, vs. a median of $35\text{ min/day}$). Two standard techniques stabilize $\bar{W}$ in every production A/B pipeline:

    1. Daily Winsorization (Capping at $p_{99}$ or $p_{99.5}$): Cap daily playback per user at $\tau_{\text{day}}$ (e.g., $\tau_{\text{day}} = 360\text{ minutes} = 6\text{ hours/day}$):
      $$\widetilde{W}_u = \frac{1}{D_{\text{exp}}}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{\text{exp}}} \min(T_{u,d},\, \tau_{\text{day}})$$
    2. Pre-Experiment CUPED Adjustment: Because a user's watch time during the $14$ days prior to the experiment ($W_{u,\text{pre}}$) typically has correlation $\rho \approx 0.75\text{–}0.85$ with in-experiment watch time $\widetilde{W}_u$, applying CUPED:
      $$\widetilde{W}_u^{\text{CUPED}} = \widetilde{W}_u - \theta\bigl(W_{u,\text{pre}} - \bar{W}_{\text{pre}}\bigr), \qquad \theta = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde{W}, W_{\text{pre}})}{\operatorname{Var}(W_{\text{pre}})}$$
      reduces variance by $1 - \rho^2 \approx 56\%\text{–}72\%$, equivalent to multiplying your A/B test sample size by $2.3\times\text{–}3.5\times$!

    3. Video Completion Rate (VCR) & Removing Duration Bias

    For a video impression $i$ of duration $D_i > 0$ and watched duration $T_i \ge 0$ (which can exceed $D_i$ if the user loops/re-watches a short video), the raw Video Completion Rate ($\text{VCR}_i$) and Binary Finish Indicator ($F_i$) are:

    $$\text{VCR}_i = \frac{\min(T_i,\, c_{\max} D_i)}{D_i}, \qquad F_i = \mathbb{I}\bigl[T_i \ge \eta D_i\bigr]$$

    where $c_{\max} \in \{1.0, 2.0\}$ caps multi-loop inflation and $\eta \in [0.85, 0.98]$ accounts for users swiping past the final $1\text{s}$ outro screen.

    Mathematical Derivation of Duration Bias

    Why do raw $T_i$ and raw $\text{VCR}_i$ pull the recommender in opposite directions? Suppose a user's boredom/exit hazard rate during video playback is a constant $\lambda > 0$ (so the user's attention survival time is $S \sim \operatorname{Exp}(\lambda)$), and actual watch time is right-censored by the video's duration $D_i$: $T_i = \min(S, D_i)$. Then:

    $$\mathbb{E}[T_i \mid D_i] = \int_0^{D_i} \Pr(S > t)\,dt = \int_0^{D_i} e^{-\lambda t}\,dt = \frac{1 - e^{-\lambda D_i}}{\lambda}$$

    And consequently, the expected Video Completion Rate for a video of length $D_i$ is:

    $$\mathbb{E}[\text{VCR}_i \mid D_i] = \frac{\mathbb{E}[T_i \mid D_i]}{D_i} = \frac{1 - e^{-\lambda D_i}}{\lambda D_i}$$

    Look at the derivatives with respect to video duration $D_i$:

    • $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial D_i}\mathbb{E}[T_i \mid D_i] = e^{-\lambda D_i} > 0$ for all $D_i$: Expected raw watch time strictly increases with video length $D_i$, even when content quality $\lambda$ is identical!
    • $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial D_i}\mathbb{E}[\text{VCR}_i \mid D_i] < 0$ for all $D_i$: Expected completion rate strictly decreases with video length $D_i$, even when content quality $\lambda$ is identical!

    Three Production Solutions to Duration Bias

    To evaluate and train video rankers without distorting the duration mix of the feed, production systems (e.g., Zhan et al., Deconfounding Duration Bias in Watch-time Prediction for Video Recommendation, KDD; Covington et al., YouTube DNN) use three duration-debiased metrics:

    1. Duration-Stratified Watch-Time Quantile ($q\text{-VCR}$ / Percentile Rank): Partition video durations into fine buckets $B_1, B_2, \dots, B_M$ (e.g. $[0, 10\text{s}), [10\text{s}, 20\text{s}), [20\text{s}, 40\text{s}), [40\text{s}, 90\text{s}), [90\text{s}, 180\text{s}), \dots$). For any impression $i$ with $D_i \in B_b$, define its Watch Quantile Score $q_i \in [0, 1]$ using the empirical CDF $\widehat{F}_{T \mid B_b}$ of watch times within bucket $B_b$:
      $$q_i = \widehat{F}_{T \mid D \in B_b}(T_i) = \Pr\bigl(T \le T_i \;\big|\; D \in B_b\bigr) \sim \operatorname{Uniform}(0, 1)$$
      By construction, $\mathbb{E}[q_i \mid D_i \in B_b] = 0.50$ for every duration bucket $B_b$! A $120\text{s}$ video watched for $85\text{s}$ ($q_i = 0.92$) now scores much higher than a $6\text{s}$ video watched for $4.5\text{s}$ ($q_i = 0.48$).
    2. Duration-Normalized Watch Z-Score ($\text{WTZ}_i$): Standardize watch time by the conditional mean $\mu(D_i)$ and standard deviation $\sigma(D_i)$ for duration $D_i$:
      $$\text{WTZ}_i = \frac{T_i - \mu(D_i)}{\sigma(D_i)}$$
    3. Effective Watch Count / Engaged View Rate ($\text{EVR}$): Count a video view as an "Engaged View" if $T_i \ge \tau_{\text{eff}}(D_i)$, where $\tau_{\text{eff}}(D_i)$ is a concave threshold curve (e.g., $\tau_{\text{eff}}(D_i) = \min(0.8 D_i,\, 10\text{s} + 0.15 D_i)$), requiring near-completion on $8\text{s}$ videos ($6.4\text{s}$) and a solid $28\text{s}$ watch on $120\text{s}$ videos.

    4. Skip-Before-3s Rate (Fast-Swipe Bounce Guardrail)

    In vertical short-form video feeds (TikTok, Reels, Shorts), autoplay begins the instant a video snaps into the viewport. When a user swipes past a video within the first $\tau_{\text{skip}} = 2.0\text{s}\text{–}3.0\text{s}$, it is the video equivalent of a search pogo-stick bounce:

    $$\text{Skip-}3\text{s-Rate} = \frac{\sum_{u=1}^N \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{u,\text{video}}} \mathbb{I}\bigl[T_{u,i} < 3.0\text{s}\bigr]}{\sum_{u=1}^N |\mathcal{I}_{u,\text{video}}|}$$

    Why is $\text{Skip-}3\text{s-Rate}$ an indispensable guardrail alongside Total Watch Time? Suppose an exploration or candidate-generation experiment injects $15\%$ more out-of-distribution videos into the feed, and one out of ten hits is a $5$-minute video that keeps the user watching, while the other nine are instantly swiped away in $1.2\text{s}$. Total Watch Time might look flat or slightly positive, but $\text{Skip-}3\text{s-Rate}$ will spike by $+8\text{ pp}$, revealing severe feed friction and user thumb fatigue.

    6. Production Python Implementation: Duration-Debiased Watch Metrics & CUPED

    import numpy as np
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import List, Dict
from scipy import stats

@dataclass
class VideoImpression:
    user_id: int
    duration_sec: float
    watched_sec: float

def compute_duration_quantiles(
    impressions: List[VideoImpression],
    bucket_edges: List[float] = [0, 10, 20, 40, 90, 180, 600, np.inf]
) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Computes Duration-Stratified Watch-Time Quantiles q_i = F(T_i | D_i in B_b)
    so that every duration bucket has mean quantile 0.5.
    """
    durations = np.array([imp.duration_sec for imp in impressions], dtype=float)
    watched = np.array([imp.watched_sec for imp in impressions], dtype=float)
    bucket_ids = np.digitize(durations, bucket_edges) - 1
    quantiles = np.zeros(len(impressions), dtype=float)

    for b in np.unique(bucket_ids):
        mask = (bucket_ids == b)
        w_bucket = watched[mask]
        if len(w_bucket) <= 1:
            quantiles[mask] = 0.5
        else:
            # Mid-rank empirical CDF percentile in (0, 1)
            ranks = stats.rankdata(w_bucket, method="average")
            quantiles[mask] = (ranks - 0.5) / len(w_bucket)

    return quantiles

def cuped_user_watch_time_test(
    pre_watch_c: np.ndarray, in_watch_c: np.ndarray,
    pre_watch_t: np.ndarray, in_watch_t: np.ndarray,
    daily_cap_min: float = 360.0
) -> Dict[str, float]:
    """
    Winsorizes daily user watch time and applies pooled pre-experiment CUPED
    variance reduction: Y_adj = Y - theta * (X - mean(X_pooled)).
    """
    y_c = np.minimum(in_watch_c, daily_cap_min)
    y_t = np.minimum(in_watch_t, daily_cap_min)
    x_c = np.minimum(pre_watch_c, daily_cap_min)
    x_t = np.minimum(pre_watch_t, daily_cap_min)

    x_pool = np.concatenate([x_c, x_t])
    y_pool = np.concatenate([y_c, y_t])
    theta = np.cov(y_pool, x_pool, ddof=1)[0, 1] / np.var(x_pool, ddof=1)
    x_mean = x_pool.mean()

    y_c_adj = y_c - theta * (x_c - x_mean)
    y_t_adj = y_t - theta * (x_t - x_mean)

    raw_se = np.sqrt(np.var(y_c, ddof=1) / len(y_c) + np.var(y_t, ddof=1) / len(y_t))
    cuped_se = np.sqrt(np.var(y_c_adj, ddof=1) / len(y_c_adj) + np.var(y_t_adj, ddof=1) / len(y_t_adj))

    diff = float(y_t_adj.mean() - y_c_adj.mean())
    z_stat = diff / cuped_se if cuped_se > 0 else 0.0
    p_val = float(2.0 * (1.0 - stats.norm.cdf(abs(z_stat))))

    return {
        "control_mean_adj": float(y_c_adj.mean()),
        "treatment_mean_adj": float(y_t_adj.mean()),
        "abs_diff": diff,
        "rel_lift_pct": float(100.0 * diff / y_c_adj.mean()),
        "raw_se": float(raw_se),
        "cuped_se": float(cuped_se),
        "variance_reduction_pct": float(100.0 * (1.0 - (cuped_se / raw_se) ** 2)),
        "p_value": p_val,
    }

    7. Self-Check & ML System Design Interview Exercises

    1. In YouTube's seminal Deep Neural Network paper (Covington et al., RecSys 2016), why did they train the final logistic regression ranking layer using weighted logistic regression with weight $T_i$ on positive clicked impressions and weight $1$ on unclicked impressions?

    In weighted logistic regression where positive examples (clicked videos) receive weight $w_i = T_i$ (watched duration) and negative examples (unclicked impressions) receive weight $1$, the learned odds ratio of the logistic unit is: $$\text{Odds}(u, i) = \frac{\sum_{i \in \text{Pos}} T_i}{\sum_{i \in \text{Neg}} 1} = \frac{N_{\text{pos}} \cdot \mathbb{E}[T_i \mid \text{Click}]}{N_{\text{imp}} - N_{\text{pos}}} = \frac{p_{\text{click}}}{1 - p_{\text{click}}}\mathbb{E}[T_i \mid \text{Click}] \approx p_{\text{click}}\cdot \mathbb{E}[T_i \mid \text{Click}] = \mathbb{E}[T_i]$$ whenever $p_{\text{click}} \ll 1$. Thus, evaluating $\exp(\mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{h})$ at serving time directly estimates unconditional expected watch time $\mathbb{E}[T_i \mid u, i]$ in a single forward pass! However, on modern autoplay feeds where $p_{\text{view}} \approx 1$, $1 - p_{\text{view}}$ is near zero, so weighted logistic regression breaks down—modern feeds instead use multi-bucket ordinal classification ($P(T_i > \tau_1), \dots, P(T_i > \tau_B)$) or duration-conditioned quantile regression ($q\text{-VCR}$).

    2. A creator uploads a $7$-second video with a dense block of text on screen so viewers are forced to let the video loop $3$ times ($21\text{s}$ watched, $\text{VCR} = 300\%$) just to finish reading the sentence. How do your metrics and ranking model defend against this "Loop-Bait" hack?

    Three defenses prevent loop-bait from dominating the feed:
    1. Cap $\text{VCR}$ and Loop Credit: Cap per-impression completion credit at $c_{\max} = 1.0$ or $1.2$ unless accompanied by an explicit positive action (Like, Share, Save, Audio-Use).
    2. Duration-Stratified Quantiles ($q\text{-VCR}$): Because $q\text{-VCR}$ compares the $7\text{s}$ video only against other $[5\text{s}, 10\text{s})$ videos—and caps maximum credit—a $7\text{s}$ loop-bait clip cannot outscore a genuinely engaging $60\text{s}$ storytelling video.
    3. Post-Impression Fast-Swipe / Negative-Feedback Penalty: Users tricked by text-wall loop-bait rarely Like or Share the video and have elevated "Not Interested" / Hide rates on subsequent videos from that creator.

    3. If an experiment increases Total Daily Watch Time per User by $+1.5\%$ ($p < 0.001$), why should you still check "Number of Videos Watched per Session" and "Average Video Duration Served"?

    Total Watch Time per Session factors as: $$\text{Watch Time / Session} = \bigl(\text{Videos Served / Session}\bigr) \times \bigl(\text{Mean Video Duration } \bar{D}\bigr) \times \bigl(\text{Effective Completion Ratio}\bigr)$$ If $\bar{D}$ increased by $+25\%$ while Videos Watched per Session dropped by $-18\%$, the model is serving fewer, longer videos—which reduces ad-slot inventory between videos (hurting monetization in in-feed ad systems) and gives the exploration algorithm fewer bandit pulls per session to learn the user's evolving mood.
