    Explicit Engagement Value Models, Negative Feedback Guardrails & Creator Ecosystem Fairness

    October 2026 · Multi-Task Ranking · Negative Guardrails & Two-Sided Creator Markets

    Passive watch time alone cannot distinguish between a user zoning out in front of mindless content and a user discovering a transformative tutorial they bookmark, share with three friends, and subscribe to follow. Furthermore, a social platform is a two-sided ecosystem: if the ranking algorithm directs $98\%$ of impressions to the top $0.1\%$ of celebrity creators, emerging creators receive zero feedback, stop publishing, and starve future content supply. This note covers the three remaining pillars of feed experimentation: Multi-Task Explicit Engagement Value Models, Negative Feedback Guardrails, and Creator Ecosystem & Exposure Fairness Metrics.

    1. Multi-Task Explicit Engagement Rates & Composite Value Models

    For every qualified feed impression $(u, i)$, let $e_{u,i,k} \in \{0, 1\}$ denote whether user $u$ performed explicit action $k \in \mathcal{E}_+ = \{\text{Like}, \text{Share}, \text{Save}, \text{Comment}, \text{Follow}, \text{ProfileClick}\}$. We track each action at two levels of aggregation:

    1. Action Volume per Daily Active User ($\text{Actions}_k / \text{DAU}$):
      $$V_k = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{u=1}^N \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_u} e_{u,i,k}$$
      Measures total ecosystem engagement volume produced per user.
    2. Action Rate per Qualified Impression ($\text{Action-CTR}_k$):
      $$R_k = \frac{\sum_{u=1}^N \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_u} e_{u,i,k}}{\sum_{u=1}^N |\mathcal{I}_u|}$$
      Measures the precision with which the ranker matches action-worthy items to receptive users (evaluated with the Delta Method across user clusters).

    How Are Multi-Task Weights $w_k$ Calibrated in the Value Model?

    Because an A/B test on the multi-task ranker often trades off one action against another (e.g., increasing Shares by $+4\%$ while Likes dip by $-0.5\%$), platforms define a scalar Explicit Engagement Value per User ($\text{EEV}_u$):

    $$\text{EEV}_u = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_u} \sum_{k \in \mathcal{E}_+} w_k \, e_{u,i,k}$$

    Where do the weights $w_k$ come from? Rather than guessing arbitrary numbers, production ML teams calibrate $w_k$ using three empirical methods:

    • Inverse-Prevalence / Information Content Scaling: If $\bar{p}_{\text{like}} = 6.0\%$ per impression while $\bar{p}_{\text{share}} = 0.4\%$ per impression, giving both $w=1$ causes the Like head's gradient and variance to overwhelm the Share head by $15\times$. Normalization by baseline rate or square-root inverse prevalence ($w_k \propto \bar{p}_k^{-\gamma}$, $\gamma \in [0.5, 0.8]$) equalizes each head's influence on the top-$K$ ordering.
    • Downstream Retention Marginal Utility ($\partial L_{30} / \partial e_k$): Regress future 30-day user retention (and recipient resurrection for Shares!) against in-session action counts controlling for baseline user activity. Empirically:
      • External Share ($w_{\text{share}} \approx 10\text{–}15$): Highest value—signals strong endorsement and acquires or resurrects an external recipient via a viral loop.
      • Creator Follow / Subscribe ($w_{\text{follow}} \approx 8\text{–}12$): Creates a permanent interest-graph edge that improves future candidate generation for months.
      • Save / Bookmark / Collection Add ($w_{\text{save}} \approx 5\text{–}8$): High-intent utility signal (recipes, workouts, coding tips, travel itineraries) that frequently drives repeat app opens.
      • Substantive Comment ($w_{\text{comment}} \approx 3\text{–}5$): High engagement, conditional on non-toxic sentiment.
      • Like / Heart ($w_{\text{like}} = 1.0$): Baseline lightweight appreciation unit.
    • Bayesian Optimization / Pareto Frontier Grid Search: Run multi-arm online bandit or Gaussian-Process experiments over weight perturbations $(\mathbf{w} + \boldsymbol{\delta})$ to trace the Pareto frontier between Watch Time, $\text{EEV}_u$, and $L_{14}$ Active Days.

    2. Negative Feedback Guardrails & Rage-Bait Suppression

    Every feed ranking experiment must pass a strict battery of Dissatisfaction & Integrity Guardrails. Because explicit negative actions are relatively sparse ($10\text{–}150\text{ basis points}$ per $10{,}000$ impressions), we track both explicit negative rates and implicit toxicity ratios:

    $$\text{NFR} = \frac{\sum_{u=1}^N \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_u} \mathbb{I}\bigl[\text{Hide}_{u,i} \lor \text{NotInterested}_{u,i} \lor \text{MuteCreator}_{u,i} \lor \text{Report}_{u,i}\bigr]}{\sum_{u=1}^N |\mathcal{I}_u|} \times 10^4 \;\;(\text{in bps})$$

    Detecting Outrage / Clickbait via the Comment-to-Like Anomaly Ratio

    When a multi-task model over-weights raw $\hat{p}_{\text{comment}}$ and raw $\hat{T}_{\text{watch}}$, it rapidly discovers that inflammatory, divisive, or intentionally wrong posts ("rage-bait") generate massive comment threads where hundreds of users argue in the replies! How do we prevent rage-bait from masquerading as "high engagement"?

    1. The "Ratioed" Post Guardrail ($\text{Comment}/\text{Like}$ Skew): On healthy entertaining or educational posts, Likes outnumber Comments by roughly $15:1$ to $40:1$. When a post receives $500$ comments and only $40$ likes ($\text{Comments} / (\text{Likes} + 10) > \tau_{\text{ratio}}$) accompanied by elevated negative sentiment in comment text or elevated $\text{Hide}/\text{Report}$ rates, the post is discounted in the Value Model:
      $$w_{\text{comment, eff}}(u, i) = w_{\text{comment}} \cdot \bigl(1 - \hat{p}_{\text{toxic\_thread}}(i)\bigr) \cdot \min\!\left(1.0,\; \kappa \frac{\hat{p}_{\text{like}}(u,i)}{\hat{p}_{\text{comment}}(u,i) + \epsilon}\right)$$
    2. In-Feed Micro-Survey Satisfaction ("Worth Your Time" Rate): Sample $0.1\%$ of sessions to display a 1-tap inline survey card asking "How satisfied were you with the videos you just watched?" ($1\text{–}5$ stars). Models trained to predict survey satisfaction $P(\text{Survey} \ge 4 \mid u, i)$ act as an anchor against clickbait drift.

    3. Two-Sided Creator Ecosystem & Exposure Fairness Metrics

    In a user-randomized A/B test, you can easily miss supply-side degradation. Suppose a new collaborative-filtering ranker concentrates impressions on already-viral hits (popularity / Matthew effect bias). Consumers enjoy watching those viral hits for two weeks, but mid-tier and emerging creators see their views drop by $-40\%$ and stop uploading new videos! To protect the supply side of the marketplace, we track three creator-centric metrics:

    1. Creator Exposure Gini Coefficient ($G_{\text{exp}}$)

    Let $\mathcal{M} = \{1, 2, \dots, M\}$ be the set of active creators (or newly uploaded items), and let $E_c \ge 0$ be the total qualified impressions allocated to creator $c$ by the recommendation arm. Sorting creators in non-decreasing order of exposure $E_{(1)} \le E_{(2)} \le \dots \le E_{(M)}$, the Exposure Gini Coefficient is:

    $$G_{\text{exp}} = \frac{\sum_{c=1}^M (2c - M - 1)\, E_{(c)}}{M \sum_{c=1}^M E_{(c)}} = 1 - \frac{2}{M}\frac{\sum_{c=1}^M (M - c + 0.5)\, E_{(c)}}{\sum_{c=1}^M E_{(c)}} \in [0, 1]$$
    • $G_{\text{exp}} = 0$ indicates perfectly uniform exposure across all creators.
    • $G_{\text{exp}} \to 1$ indicates extreme monopoly concentration (one creator receives $100\%$ of impressions).
    • Alongside $G_{\text{exp}}$, platforms track Head-1% Impression Share (the percentage of total feed views captured by the top $1\%$ of creators) and Catalog Coverage @ $K_{\text{min}}$ (the fraction of newly uploaded videos that receive at least $K_{\text{min}} = 200$ qualified exploration impressions).

    2. Cold-Start First-$V$-Views Rate & Creator Publishing Frequency

    For new or emerging creators, receiving early feedback is the strongest predictor of whether they publish a second video:

    • Cold-Start Qualification Rate ($\text{CSQR}@V$): Fraction of newly uploaded valid videos that reach at least $V$ qualified views (typically $V = 100$ or $500$) within $24\text{ hours}$ of upload:
      $$\text{CSQR}@V = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}_{\text{new}}|}\sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{new}}} \mathbb{I}\bigl[\text{Views}_{24\text{h}}(v) \ge V\bigr]$$
    • Creator 7-Day Subsequent Upload Rate (Creator Feedback Loop): When a creator's upload receives views, likes, and comments, does that positive reinforcement cause the creator to upload again within $7$ days? Note that in a viewer-randomized A/B test, all viewers (Control + Treatment) pool their views onto the same creators, so creator-side feedback effects suffer from two-sided network interference! To measure true creator publishing lift, platforms use Creator-Side Randomization (splitting newly uploaded videos into Control vs. Treatment exploration policies) or Graph-Cluster / Ego-Cluster A/B Tests.

    4. Worked Numerical Example: Multi-Task Value vs. Creator Gini

    Suppose we compare two candidate-generation + ranking policies across a miniature ecosystem of $M = 5$ creators and $10{,}000$ total impressions:

    • Control Policy: Distributes impressions across the $5$ creators as $E_C = [200,\, 500,\, 1{,}000,\, 2{,}300,\, 6{,}000]$ (sum $= 10{,}000$).
    • Treatment Policy (Cold-Start Exploration Bandit + Share/Save Value Boost): Distributes impressions as $E_T = [600,\, 1{,}100,\, 1{,}600,\, 2{,}500,\, 4{,}200]$ (sum $= 10{,}000$).

    Step 1 — Compute Creator Exposure Gini Coefficient ($G_{\text{exp}}$):

    For $M = 5$, the weights $(2c - M - 1)$ for $c = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ are $[-4,\, -2,\, 0,\, +2,\, +4]$:

    • Control Gini: $$G_{\text{exp}, C} = \frac{(-4)(200) + (-2)(500) + (0)(1000) + (2)(2300) + (4)(6000)}{5 \times 10{,}000} = \frac{-800 - 1000 + 0 + 4600 + 24000}{50{,}000} = \frac{26{,}800}{50{,}000} = \mathbf{0.536}$$
    • Treatment Gini: $$G_{\text{exp}, T} = \frac{(-4)(600) + (-2)(1100) + (0)(1600) + (2)(2500) + (4)(4200)}{5 \times 10{,}000} = \frac{-2400 - 2200 + 0 + 5000 + 16800}{50{,}000} = \frac{17{,}200}{50{,}000} = \mathbf{0.344}$$

    Treatment reduces exposure inequality $G_{\text{exp}}$ from $0.536 \to 0.344$ ($-35.8\%$ reduction in concentration) and lifts the smallest creator from $200 \to 600$ views (crossing the $\text{CSQR}@500$ threshold so all $5/5 = 100\%$ of creators achieve $\ge 500$ views, vs. $4/5 = 80\%$ in Control).

    5. Production Python Implementation: Multi-Task Value, Guardrails & Creator Gini

    import numpy as np
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Dict

def exposure_gini_coefficient(exposures: np.ndarray) -> float:
    """
    Computes the exact Gini coefficient G in [0, 1] for non-negative creator
    or item exposure counts E_1, ..., E_M.
    """
    x = np.asarray(exposures, dtype=float)
    if x.size == 0 or np.all(x == 0):
        return 0.0
    x_sorted = np.sort(x)
    m = x_sorted.size
    idx = np.arange(1, m + 1, dtype=float)
    return float(np.sum((2.0 * idx - m - 1.0) * x_sorted) / (m * np.sum(x_sorted)))

@dataclass
class FeedExperimentSummary:
    user_impressions: np.ndarray
    user_likes: np.ndarray
    user_shares: np.ndarray
    user_saves: np.ndarray
    user_comments: np.ndarray
    user_follows: np.ndarray
    user_hides_reports: np.ndarray
    creator_exposures: np.ndarray

def evaluate_feed_value_and_ecosystem(
    ctrl: FeedExperimentSummary,
    trt: FeedExperimentSummary,
    weights: Dict[str, float] = None,
    cold_start_threshold: int = 500,
) -> Dict[str, float]:
    if weights is None:
        weights = {
            "like": 1.0,
            "comment": 3.5,
            "save": 6.0,
            "follow": 10.0,
            "share": 12.0,
            "negative": -20.0,
        }

    def user_value_score(s: FeedExperimentSummary) -> np.ndarray:
        return (
            weights["like"] * s.user_likes
            + weights["comment"] * s.user_comments
            + weights["save"] * s.user_saves
            + weights["follow"] * s.user_follows
            + weights["share"] * s.user_shares
            + weights["negative"] * s.user_hides_reports
        )

    eev_c = user_value_score(ctrl)
    eev_t = user_value_score(trt)

    nfr_bps_c = 1e4 * ctrl.user_hides_reports.sum() / ctrl.user_impressions.sum()
    nfr_bps_t = 1e4 * trt.user_hides_reports.sum() / trt.user_impressions.sum()

    gini_c = exposure_gini_coefficient(ctrl.creator_exposures)
    gini_t = exposure_gini_coefficient(trt.creator_exposures)

    csqr_c = float(np.mean(ctrl.creator_exposures >= cold_start_threshold))
    csqr_t = float(np.mean(trt.creator_exposures >= cold_start_threshold))

    return {
        "Control_EEV_per_User": float(eev_c.mean()),
        "Treatment_EEV_per_User": float(eev_t.mean()),
        "EEV_Rel_Lift_Pct": float(100.0 * (eev_t.mean() - eev_c.mean()) / eev_c.mean()),
        "Control_Negative_Feedback_bps": float(nfr_bps_c),
        "Treatment_Negative_Feedback_bps": float(nfr_bps_t),
        "Control_Creator_Gini": gini_c,
        "Treatment_Creator_Gini": gini_t,
        "Control_Cold_Start_Qual_Rate": csqr_c,
        "Treatment_Cold_Start_Qual_Rate": csqr_t,
    }

    6. Self-Check & ML System Design Interview Exercises

    1. Why does a viewer-randomized 50/50 A/B test fail to measure whether a new cold-start exploration algorithm increases Creator Publishing Retention?

    In a viewer-randomized 50/50 A/B test, every creator $c$ receives half of their impressions from Control viewers and half from Treatment viewers. Consequently, both Control-preferred creators and Treatment-preferred creators receive a blended mixture of traffic, violating the Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption (SUTVA) on the creator side! To measure how cold-start distribution impacts a creator's motivation to upload their next video, you must randomize at the Creator (or Newly Uploaded Video) level—or use bipartite graph cluster randomization—and allocate fixed impression budgets to each arm so cannibalization does not bias the estimate.

    2. Suppose you add an explicit $-\lambda_{\text{hide}} \hat{p}_{\text{hide}}(u, i)$ penalty term to the feed Value Model. In the A/B test, the Hide Rate drops by $-18\%$, but Total Watch Time also drops by $-2.5\%$. How do you diagnose why Watch Time dropped?

    Inspect the correlation and calibration of the $\hat{p}_{\text{hide}}$ model! Users can only click "Hide / Not Interested" on items they actually notice or watch for a few seconds, or users who are heavy engaged power-users may curate their feeds more actively by clicking "Not Interested". Furthermore, long-form or niche hobby videos sometimes have slightly higher Hide rates among non-hobbyists while generating massive watch time among hobbyists. If $\hat{p}_{\text{hide}}$ is uncalibrated or dominated by item-level popularity priors, a large linear penalty $-\lambda_{\text{hide}}\hat{p}_{\text{hide}}$ acts as a blunt penalty against bold or niche content in favor of bland, unobjectionable posts. Replacing the linear penalty with a thresholded guardrail ($\mathbb{I}[\hat{p}_{\text{hide}}(u,i) > \tau_{\text{max}}]$) or conditioning on $\Pr(\text{Hide} \mid \text{ShortWatch})$ eliminates the collateral watch-time loss.

    3. Why is the "Save / Bookmark Rate" often a stronger signal for long-term utility on platforms like Instagram, Pinterest, and TikTok than the "Like Rate"?

    A "Like" is a low-cost, reflexive social acknowledgment ($\bar{p}_{\text{like}} \approx 5\%\text{–}10\%$) that often reflects momentary amusement. A "Save / Bookmark" ($\bar{p}_{\text{save}} \approx 0.5\%\text{–}1.5\%$) indicates future utility: the user explicitly intends to return to the platform later to cook the recipe, try the workout, visit the restaurant, or reference the technical tutorial. Consequently, Saved items directly drive future user-initiated sessions and carry $5\times\text{–}8\times$ higher marginal correlation with 30-day retention per action.
