    Push & Email Notification CTR, Volume Efficiency & Unsubscribe / Fatigue Guardrails

    October 2026 · 10 min read

    Push notifications and lifecycle emails are the most powerful re-engagement lever in consumer tech—and the fastest way to permanently lose a user if abused. Understand why raw Notification Click-Through Rate ($\text{CTR}$) and Total Notification Clicks can both be gamed, how to measure Send Volume Efficiency ($\text{Incremental DAU per } 1{,}000\text{ Sends}$) against a holdout, and how LinkedIn, Pinterest, and Duolingo formulate notification frequency gating as a constrained Lagrangian optimization problem.

    1. Notification Funnel Metrics: Delivery, Open/Tap CTR & Downstream Session Value

    When a candidate notification $m$ is triggered for user $u$, it passes through four stages: (1) Candidate Trigger ($T_{\text{cand}}$) $\to$ (2) Delivered to Device / Inbox ($S_{\text{sent}}$) $\to$ (3) Opened / Tapped ($C_{\text{tap}}$) $\to$ (4) Qualified Downstream Session ($Q_{\text{sess}}$, e.g., $\ge 30\text{s}$ dwell or core action).

    $$\operatorname{Notification\ CTR} = \frac{\sum_{u=1}^N C_{\text{tap}, u}}{\sum_{u=1}^N S_{\text{sent}, u}},\qquad \operatorname{Qualified\ Tap\ Rate} = \frac{\sum_{u=1}^N Q_{\text{sess}, u}}{\sum_{u=1}^N S_{\text{sent}, u}}.$$
    • How Raw $\operatorname{CTR}$ Is Gamed by Volume Contraction: Because the denominator $\sum_u S_{\text{sent}, u}$ is controlled by the model's send threshold, a model that stops sending $80\%$ of notifications and only sends to the top $5\%$ most hyper-active users will skyrocket $\operatorname{CTR}$ by $+150\%$ while crashing total re-engaged DAU!
    • How Total Tap Count Is Gamed by Spamming: Conversely, optimizing unconditional taps per user $\frac{1}{N}\sum_u C_{\text{tap}, u}$ without a volume penalty encourages the model to send $10$ notifications per day, cannibalizing organic app opens and exhausting user tolerance.

    2. Send Volume Efficiency: Incremental DAU per 1,000 Notifications Sent

    Wait—if a user taps a push notification at 6:00 PM and opens the app, did that push notification cause an incremental Daily Active User ($\text{DAU}$)? Often no: habitual users who open the app $5$ times every evening would have opened the app organically at 6:15 PM even if no notification had been sent!

    To measure true causal re-engagement, experimentation platforms compare Treatment ($T$) against a Control arm ($C$, or a persistent $1\%\text{–}5\%$ Global Notification Holdout where all non-transactional notifications are suppressed). Let $\overline{\text{DAU}}_T, \overline{\text{DAU}}_C$ denote mean daily active days per user, and let $\bar{S}_T, \bar{S}_C$ denote mean notifications sent per user per day. The Marginal Send Volume Efficiency ($\text{SVE}$) is the ratio of incremental DAU lift to incremental thousand sends:

    $$\operatorname{SVE} = \text{Incremental DAU per } 1{,}000\text{ Sends} = 1{,}000 \times \frac{\overline{\text{DAU}}_T - \overline{\text{DAU}}_C}{\bar{S}_T - \bar{S}_C}.$$
    • Interpreting $\operatorname{SVE}$ across Experiments: If Baseline sends $\bar{S}_C = 2.0$ pushes/day and generates $+20$ incremental DAU per $1{,}000$ sends relative to the zero-push holdout, a new model is a relevance win if it holds send volume constant ($\bar{S}_T = \bar{S}_C = 2.0$) while lifting $\overline{\text{DAU}}_T > \overline{\text{DAU}}_C$, OR if it cuts notification volume by $-25\%$ ($\bar{S}_T = 1.5$) while keeping $\overline{\text{DAU}}_T \approx \overline{\text{DAU}}_C$ statistically non-inferior!

    3. Irreversible Fatigue Guardrails: Unsubscribe, OS Permission Revocation & App Uninstall

    In web ranking, showing a mediocre item on row 4 costs one wasted second of scrolling. In push and email channels, sending an annoying notification can trigger an irreversible channel death event: once a user disables iOS/Android push permissions or clicks "Unsubscribe" in an email footer, your future reach to that user drops to $0$ forever.

    Every notification experiment therefore enforces strict upper-bound guardrails on four negative signals (normalized per randomized user $N$, not per notification sent):

    1. Email Unsubscribe & Spam-Complaint Rate: $\frac{1}{N}\sum_{u=1}^N \mathbf{1}[\text{Unsub}_u \lor \text{SpamReport}_u]$. (Crucially, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender policies enforce a hard $0.30\%$ spam-complaint ceiling—exceeding $30\text{ bps}$ causes domain-wide inbox throttling into the Spam folder!)
    2. OS Push Permission Revocation / Channel Disable Rate: Tracked via daily silent push token reachability pings (APNs / FCM `Unregistered` or notification settings payload changes).
    3. App Uninstall Rate: Detected when silent background token checks transition to `NotRegistered`.
    4. Clickbait / Immediate Bounce Rate: Fraction of notification taps where the user closes the app within $<5\text{ seconds}$ without interacting—indicating the push copy ("Someone viewed your profile!") was deceptive clickbait.

    4. Constrained Optimization & Lagrangian Shadow Pricing of Notifications

    How do production notification systems (such as LinkedIn's Nearline Notification Scorer or Pinterest's John-Locke utility engine) decide whether to send candidate notification $m$ to user $u$? They formulate sending decisions $x_{u,m} \in \{0, 1\}$ as a constrained optimization problem maximizing incremental engagement subject to a global (or per-user) budget on unsubscribes/fatigue $\epsilon_{\text{unsub}}$ and total send volume $B_{\text{vol}}$:

    $$\max_{x_{u,m} \in \{0,1\}} \sum_{u, m} x_{u,m}\,\hat{\tau}_{\text{DAU}}(u, m) \quad \text{subject to} \quad \frac{1}{N}\sum_{u,m} x_{u,m}\,\hat{p}_{\text{unsub}}(u, m) \le \epsilon_{\text{unsub}}, \quad \frac{1}{N}\sum_{u,m} x_{u,m} \le B_{\text{vol}}.$$

    Introducing KKT dual multipliers (shadow prices) $\lambda_{\text{unsub}} \ge 0$ and $\lambda_{\text{vol}} \ge 0$, the optimal deterministic policy sends notification $m$ to user $u$ if and only if its net Lagrangian utility score is positive:

    $$\operatorname{Score}(u, m) = \hat{\tau}_{\text{DAU}}(u, m) + \alpha\,\hat{P}(\text{qualified tap} \mid u, m) - \lambda_{\text{unsub}}\,\hat{p}_{\text{unsub}}(u, m) - \lambda_{\text{vol}} > 0.$$

    Notice the elegance of this decision rule: a dormant user with high incremental DAU uplift $\hat{\tau}_{\text{DAU}}(u, m)$ and low unsubscribe risk passes the gate, whereas an active user who is already visiting the app daily ($\hat{\tau}_{\text{DAU}} \approx 0$) or a fatigued user who has ignored the last $5$ pushes (high $\hat{p}_{\text{unsub}}$) is automatically protected from spam!

    Worked numerical example: Evaluating a Lagrangian Volume-Reduction A/B Test

    Over $1{,}000{,}000$ users per arm for 14 days:

    • Zero-Notification Holdout ($H$): Mean $\text{DAU}_H = 40.00\%$, Sends $\bar{S}_H = 0$, Weekly Unsub Rate $= 0.00\%$.
    • Control Heuristic ($C$, fixed 3 pushes/day): Mean $\text{DAU}_C = 43.00\%$ ($+30.0\text{k}$ incremental DAU over Holdout), Sends $\bar{S}_C = 3.00/\text{day}$ ($3{,}000\text{k}$ daily sends), $\operatorname{CTR}_C = 3.2\%$, Weekly Unsub Rate $= 0.42\%$.
      • Baseline Send Volume Efficiency: $\operatorname{SVE}_C = 1{,}000 \times \frac{0.4300 - 0.4000}{3.00 - 0} = \mathbf{10.0\text{ incremental DAU per } 1{,}000\text{ sends}}$.
    • Treatment Lagrangian Scorer ($T$): Mean $\text{DAU}_T = 43.35\%$ ($+33.5\text{k}$ incremental DAU over Holdout, a $+0.35\%$ absolute DAU lift over Control!), while cutting daily sends by $-20\%$ to $\bar{S}_T = 2.40/\text{day}$, raising $\operatorname{CTR}_T = 4.5\%$, and cutting Weekly Unsub Rate by $-31\%$ to $0.29\%$.
      • Treatment Send Volume Efficiency: $\operatorname{SVE}_T = 1{,}000 \times \frac{0.4335 - 0.4000}{2.40 - 0} = \mathbf{13.96\text{ incremental DAU per } 1{,}000\text{ sends}}$ ($+39.6\%$ higher efficiency!).

    5. Python Implementation: Lagrangian Notification Gating & Dual Multiplier Solver

    import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import brentq

def solve_lagrangian_notification_policy(
    tau_dau: np.ndarray, p_tap: np.ndarray, p_unsub: np.ndarray,
    alpha_tap: float = 0.2, lambda_unsub: float = 15.0, target_sends_per_user: float = 2.0
):
    """
    Finds the shadow price lambda_vol that satisfies the average send budget B_vol
    under the Lagrangian utility Score(u, m) = tau_dau + alpha * p_tap - lambda_unsub * p_unsub - lambda_vol > 0.
    Inputs have shape (N_users, M_candidate_notifications).
    """
    net_utility_before_vol = tau_dau + alpha_tap * p_tap - lambda_unsub * p_unsub

    def excess_volume(l_vol):
        sends_per_user = np.mean(np.sum(net_utility_before_vol > l_vol, axis=1))
        return sends_per_user - target_sends_per_user

    lambda_vol_star = brentq(excess_volume, -1.0, 1.0)
    send_decisions = (net_utility_before_vol > lambda_vol_star)

    total_sends = np.sum(send_decisions)
    n_users = tau_dau.shape[0]
    inc_dau = np.sum(tau_dau * send_decisions) / n_users
    expected_taps = np.sum(p_tap * send_decisions)
    expected_unsubs = np.sum(p_unsub * send_decisions) / n_users

    return {
        "Shadow_Price_lambda_vol": round(float(lambda_vol_star), 5),
        "Sends_Per_User": round(float(total_sends / n_users), 3),
        "Expected_CTR_%": round(float(100 * expected_taps / total_sends), 2),
        "Incremental_DAU_Lift_%": round(float(100 * inc_dau), 3),
        "SVE_DAU_per_1k_Sends": round(float(1000 * inc_dau / (total_sends / n_users)), 2),
        "Expected_Unsub_Rate_%": round(float(100 * expected_unsubs), 4),
    }

if __name__ == "__main__":
    rng = np.random.default_rng(99)
    N, M = 10_000, 6
    tau_dau = rng.beta(1.5, 40.0, size=(N, M))
    p_tap = rng.beta(2.0, 45.0, size=(N, M))
    p_unsub = rng.beta(1.0, 1200.0, size=(N, M))
    print("Volume-Capped Lagrangian Policy:", solve_lagrangian_notification_policy(tau_dau, p_tap, p_unsub))

    6. Self-Check Questions

    Why must Unsubscribe Rate and OS Permission Revocation Rate always be normalized by the number of randomized users $N$ rather than by the number of notifications sent $\sum_u S_{\text{sent}, u}$?

    If a rogue treatment sends $3\times$ as many notifications per user and causes $2\times$ as many users to unsubscribe from the app, normalizing by notifications sent ($\frac{2\times}{3\times} = 0.67\times$) would make the Unsubscribe Rate per send look like it improved by $-33\%$ while twice as many users permanently blocked your app! Guardrails protecting user retention must always be evaluated per randomized user.

    When a notification model is deployed, why can an experiment show a $+1.5\%$ lift in Sessions Clicked from Notifications while showing $0.0\%$ lift in Total App Sessions?

    This is classic organic session cannibalization (time-shifting): the push notification merely prompted habitual users to enter the app by tapping the notification banner instead of tapping the home-screen app icon 20 minutes later. That is why Total App Sessions (or Incremental DAU) across all entry points—not notification-attributed sessions—must be the primary OEC.
