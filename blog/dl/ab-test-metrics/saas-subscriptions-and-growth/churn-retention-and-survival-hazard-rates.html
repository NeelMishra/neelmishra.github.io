    Logo Churn, Net Dollar Retention (NDR) & Kaplan–Meier Survival Hazard Rates

    October 2026 · 11 min read

    When evaluating a subscription product, a pricing tier experiment, or a payment-retry ML model, how you define "churn" determines what you optimize. Learn the exact accounting identities linking Logo Churn, Gross Dollar Retention ($\text{GDR}$), and Net Dollar Retention ($\text{NDR}$); separate Voluntary cancellation from Involuntary payment failure; and use Kaplan–Meier survival curves, discrete hazard rates, and Restricted Mean Survival Time ($\text{RMST}$) to analyze right-censored A/B tests without discarding staggered cohorts.

    1. Logo Churn vs. Gross Dollar Churn vs. Net Dollar Retention (NDR)

    Consider a cohort of $N_0$ active paying subscribers (or B2B accounts, called "logos") at the start of billing period $t=0$, where account $u \in \{1, \dots, N_0\}$ pays recurring revenue $r_{u,0} > 0$ (Monthly Recurring Revenue $\text{MRR}_0 = \sum_{u=1}^{N_0} r_{u,0}$ or Annual Recurring Revenue $\text{ARR}_0 = 12\,\text{MRR}_0$). At the end of the period ($t=1$), each starting subscriber $u$ has revenue $r_{u,1} \ge 0$, which decomposes into four mutually exclusive movements:

    • Full Churn ($\mathcal{C}$): $r_{u,1} = 0$, losing $\text{ARR}_{\text{churn}} = \sum_{u \in \mathcal{C}} r_{u,0}$.
    • Contraction / Downgrade ($\mathcal{D}$): $0 < r_{u,1} < r_{u,0}$, losing $\text{ARR}_{\text{contr}} = \sum_{u \in \mathcal{D}} (r_{u,0} - r_{u,1})$.
    • Flat Retention: $r_{u,1} = r_{u,0}$.
    • Expansion / Seat or Tier Upgrade ($\mathcal{E}$): $r_{u,1} > r_{u,0}$, adding $\text{ARR}_{\text{exp}} = \sum_{u \in \mathcal{E}} (r_{u,1} - r_{u,0})$. (Note: revenue from newly acquired logos after $t=0$ is excluded from cohort retention!)

    From these four buckets we define the three core subscription cohort metrics:

    $$\operatorname{Logo\ Churn\ Rate} = \frac{|\mathcal{C}|}{N_0},\qquad \operatorname{Gross\ Dollar\ Retention\ (GDR)} = \frac{\text{ARR}_0 - \text{ARR}_{\text{churn}} - \text{ARR}_{\text{contr}}}{\text{ARR}_0} \in [0, 1],$$
    $$\operatorname{Net\ Dollar\ Retention\ (NDR / NRR)} = \frac{\text{ARR}_0 + \text{ARR}_{\text{exp}} - \text{ARR}_{\text{contr}} - \text{ARR}_{\text{churn}}}{\text{ARR}_0} = \operatorname{GDR} + \frac{\text{ARR}_{\text{exp}}}{\text{ARR}_0}.$$
    • Why $\operatorname{GDR}$ is bounded at $100\%$ while $\operatorname{NDR}$ can exceed $100\%$: $\operatorname{GDR}$ clips each account's retained revenue at $\min(r_{u,1}, r_{u,0})$, measuring pure downside revenue preservation. Top enterprise SaaS and usage-based AI platforms achieve annual $\operatorname{NDR} \in [115\%, 140\%]$ because seat/consumption expansion among surviving accounts more than offsets churn and contraction.
    • Simpson's Paradox between Logo Churn and Dollar Churn: If a product has $9{,}000$ SMB accounts paying $\$20/\text{mo}$ and $1{,}000$ Enterprise accounts paying $\$1{,}000/\text{mo}$, losing $10\%$ of SMB accounts ($900$ logos) yields a $9.0\%$ Logo Churn Rate but only a $\frac{\$18{,}000}{\$1{,}180{,}000} = 1.53\%$ Gross Dollar Churn Rate.

    2. Voluntary vs. Involuntary Churn (Payment Dunning & Smart Retries)

    Total subscription churn is the sum of two fundamentally different causal mechanisms that must be tracked separately in every A/B experiment:

    $$c_{\text{total}} = c_{\text{voluntary}} + c_{\text{involuntary}}.$$
    1. Voluntary (Active) Churn ($c_{\text{voluntary}}$): The user explicitly clicks "Cancel Subscription" or turns off auto-renew due to price sensitivity, lack of product engagement, or switching to a competitor. Leading indicator: Auto-Renew Off Rate within 24 hours of signup/trial start.
    2. Involuntary (Passive / Payment Failure) Churn ($c_{\text{involuntary}}$): The user intended to stay subscribed, but their recurring renewal charge failed due to an expired credit card, insufficient funds on billing day, or issuer fraud false-positives, and was not recovered during the dunning grace period. In consumer subscriptions, involuntary churn accounts for $20\%\text{–}40\%$ of all churn! ML smart-retry models (optimizing the exact day-of-month, hour-of-day, and payment rail to retry soft declines) are evaluated on Dunning Recovery Rate $\frac{N_{\text{recovered}}}{N_{\text{initial soft declines}}}$ with a guardrail on network retry penalty fees.

    3. Time-to-Churn Survival Analysis: Hazard Rate, Kaplan–Meier & RMST

    Why is binary "30-day churn rate" statistically inefficient in online A/B tests? First, users enter the experiment on staggered calendar days, so at the end of a 28-day test, a user who joined on Day 15 has only been observed for $C_u = 13\text{ days}$ (right censoring). Second, binary churn treats a user who cancels on Day 2 identically to one who cancels on Day 29.

    Let $T_u > 0$ be user $u$'s true time-to-churn, $C_u$ be their censoring time (end of experiment window), $\tilde{T}_u = \min(T_u, C_u)$ be the observed tenure, and $\delta_u = \mathbf{1}[T_u \le C_u] \in \{0, 1\}$ indicate whether an actual churn event occurred ($\delta_u = 1$) or the user was right-censored still active ($\delta_u = 0$).

    • Discrete Hazard Rate ($h_t$) and Kaplan–Meier Survival Estimator ($\hat{S}(t)$): At discrete billing cycle or day $t \in \{1, 2, \dots\}$, let $n_t$ be the number of active uncensored users at risk just before time $t$, and let $d_t$ be the number of users who churn at time $t$. The empirical hazard rate is $\hat{h}_t = \frac{d_t}{n_t} = \widehat{\Pr}(T = t \mid T \ge t)$, and the Kaplan–Meier estimator chains the conditional survival probabilities:
      $$\hat{S}(t) = \widehat{\Pr}(T > t) = \prod_{k=1}^{t} \left(1 - \hat{h}_k\right) = \prod_{k=1}^{t} \left(1 - \frac{d_k}{n_k}\right).$$
      Its asymptotic variance is given by Greenwood's Formula:
      $$\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}\bigl(\hat{S}(t)\bigr) = \hat{S}(t)^2 \sum_{k=1}^{t} \frac{d_k}{n_k(n_k - d_k)}.$$
    • Restricted Mean Survival Time ($\text{RMST}(\tau)$) as an A/B Test OEC: The area under the survival curve up to horizon $\tau$ equals the expected active subscription duration within $[0, \tau]$:
      $$\operatorname{RMST}(\tau) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[\min(T, \tau)\bigr] = \int_0^\tau S(t)\,dt \approx \sum_{t=0}^{\tau-1} \hat{S}(t).$$
      Multiplying $\Delta\operatorname{RMST}(\tau) = \operatorname{RMST}_T(\tau) - \operatorname{RMST}_C(\tau)$ by daily/monthly subscription price directly translates survival curve separation into incremental retained revenue over horizon $\tau$, without requiring proportional hazards!
    • Log-Rank Hypothesis Test: To test $H_0: S_T(t) \equiv S_C(t)$ across all time points $t$, at each event time $k$ with pooled at-risk count $n_k = n_{k,T} + n_{k,C}$ and pooled churn count $d_k = d_{k,T} + d_{k,C}$, the expected churn count in Treatment under $H_0$ is $e_{k,T} = n_{k,T}\frac{d_k}{n_k}$ with hypergeometric variance $v_{k,T} = \frac{n_{k,T}n_{k,C}d_k(n_k - d_k)}{n_k^2(n_k - 1)}$, yielding the standard normal $Z$-statistic:
      $$Z_{\text{log-rank}} = \frac{\sum_{k} (d_{k,T} - e_{k,T})}{\sqrt{\sum_{k} v_{k,T}}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1).$$

    Worked numerical example: Kaplan–Meier Survival & RMST with Right Censoring

    Suppose $N = 100$ subscribers enter an experiment. In Month 1 ($n_1 = 100$), $d_1 = 10$ users churn ($\hat{h}_1 = 0.10 \implies \hat{S}(1) = 0.90$), and $10$ newly enrolled users reach the end of the experiment window still active (right-censored after Month 1).

    • Month 2 risk set: $n_2 = 100 - 10\text{ (churned)} - 10\text{ (censored)} = 80$ users at risk. Suppose $d_2 = 8$ users churn in Month 2: then $\hat{h}_2 = \frac{8}{80} = 0.10$, and $\hat{S}(2) = \hat{S}(1)(1 - \hat{h}_2) = 0.90 \times 0.90 = \mathbf{0.81}$.
    • Month 3 risk set: Suppose another $12$ active users are right-censored at the end of Month 2, leaving $n_3 = 80 - 8 - 12 = 60$ users at risk in Month 3, of whom $d_3 = 6$ churn ($\hat{h}_3 = \frac{6}{60} = 0.10 \implies \hat{S}(3) = 0.81 \times 0.90 = \mathbf{0.729}$).
    • Discrete 3-Month $\operatorname{RMST}(3)$: $\sum_{t=0}^2 \hat{S}(t) = 1.00 + 0.90 + 0.81 = \mathbf{2.71\text{ active billing months per user}}$. Notice how a naïve uncensored ratio $\frac{10+8+6}{100} = 24\%$ would have severely underestimated the true 3-month cumulative churn $1 - \hat{S}(3) = 27.1\%$ because $22$ users were censored early!

    4. Python Implementation: NDR, Kaplan–Meier, Greenwood SE, RMST & Log-Rank Test

    import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

def kaplan_meier_and_logrank(
    time_c: np.ndarray, event_c: np.ndarray,
    time_t: np.ndarray, event_t: np.ndarray,
    horizon: int
):
    """
    Computes discrete-time Kaplan-Meier survival S(t), Greenwood SE,
    Restricted Mean Survival Time RMST(horizon), and the two-sample Log-Rank test.
    """
    def km_curve(times, events, T):
        s = 1.0
        greenwood_sum = 0.0
        s_vals = [1.0]
        for t in range(1, T + 1):
            n_t = np.sum(times >= t)
            d_t = np.sum((times == t) & (events == 1))
            if n_t > 0:
                h_t = d_t / n_t
                s *= (1.0 - h_t)
                if n_t > d_t:
                    greenwood_sum += d_t / (n_t * (n_t - d_t))
            s_vals.append(s)
        se_end = s * np.sqrt(greenwood_sum)
        rmst = float(np.sum(s_vals[:-1]))
        return np.array(s_vals), float(s), float(se_end), rmst

    s_c_curve, s_c_end, se_c, rmst_c = km_curve(time_c, event_c, horizon)
    s_t_curve, s_t_end, se_t, rmst_t = km_curve(time_t, event_t, horizon)

    # Log-rank statistic across discrete event times 1..horizon
    obs_minus_exp = 0.0
    var_sum = 0.0
    for t in range(1, horizon + 1):
        n_c = float(np.sum(time_c >= t))
        n_t = float(np.sum(time_t >= t))
        d_c = float(np.sum((time_c == t) & (event_c == 1)))
        d_t = float(np.sum((time_t == t) & (event_t == 1)))
        n = n_c + n_t
        d = d_c + d_t
        if n > 1 and d > 0:
            e_t = n_t * (d / n)
            v_t = (n_t * n_c * d * (n - d)) / (n**2 * (n - 1.0))
            obs_minus_exp += (d_t - e_t)
            var_sum += v_t

    z_lr = obs_minus_exp / np.sqrt(var_sum + 1e-12)
    p_lr = 2 * (1 - stats.norm.cdf(abs(z_lr)))

    return {
        f"S({horizon})_Control": round(s_c_end, 4),
        f"S({horizon})_Treatment": round(s_t_end, 4),
        f"RMST({horizon})_Control": round(rmst_c, 4),
        f"RMST({horizon})_Treatment": round(rmst_t, 4),
        "LogRank_Z": round(float(z_lr), 3),
        "LogRank_p": round(float(p_lr), 4),
    }

if __name__ == "__main__":
    rng = np.random.default_rng(11)
    # Simulate 6-month subscription tenure with staggered right-censoring
    true_t_c = rng.geometric(p=0.10, size=2000)
    true_t_t = rng.geometric(p=0.075, size=2000)  # Treatment lowers monthly hazard to 7.5%
    censor_c = rng.integers(2, 7, size=2000)
    censor_t = rng.integers(2, 7, size=2000)
    obs_c, ev_c = np.minimum(true_t_c, censor_c), (true_t_c <= censor_c).astype(int)
    obs_t, ev_t = np.minimum(true_t_t, censor_t), (true_t_t <= censor_t).astype(int)
    print(kaplan_meier_and_logrank(obs_c, ev_c, obs_t, ev_t, horizon=6))

    5. Self-Check Questions

    Why does computing churn rate by filtering only to users who have already completed a full 30 days in the experiment ($\text{tenure potential} \ge 30\text{d}$) discard valuable statistical power compared to Kaplan–Meier $\hat{S}(30)$?

    Filtering to users enrolled during the first few days of the experiment throws away all early-tenure survival information from users who enrolled later (e.g., someone enrolled 15 days ago already provides valid observation of hazards $h_1, \dots, h_{15}$, where the steepest onboarding drop-off actually occurs!). Kaplan–Meier uses every enrolled user in the risk set $n_t$ up to their censoring time $C_u$, maximizing statistical power for early hazards while remaining mathematically unbiased under independent staggered entry.

    A checkout A/B test offers an aggressive $\$1$ first-month trial promo. First-month subscriber conversion jumps by $+40\%$, what metric must you check at Day 31 before declaring victory?

    Check the Trial-to-Paid Full-Price Renewal Rate at Month 2 ($\hat{S}(31)$) and the Day-1 Auto-Renew Cancellation Rate, plus Involuntary Payment Decline Rate on the first full-price charge. Low-friction $\$1$ promos routinely attract low-intent users (or prepaid virtual cards with only $\$2$ balances) whose Month-2 hazard rate $h_{31}$ exceeds $75\%\text{–}85\%$, yielding negative net LTV after CAC and content/serving costs.
