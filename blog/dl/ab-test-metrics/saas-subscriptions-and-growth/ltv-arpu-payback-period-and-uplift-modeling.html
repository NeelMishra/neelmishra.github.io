    LTV, ARPU, LTV:CAC Payback Period & Qini / AUUC Incrementality Uplift

    October 2026 · 11 min read

    Growth ML systems allocate hundreds of millions of dollars in retention discounts, referral bonuses, and onboarding incentives. To evaluate whether those incentives create net value, we need two mathematical frameworks: (1) Discounted Customer Lifetime Value ($\text{LTV}$), $\text{ARPU}$, and $\text{CAC}$ Payback Period; and (2) Causal Uplift Modeling ($\text{CATE}$ $\tau(x)$, Qini Curves, and $\text{AUUC}$) so we never waste promo budget subsidizing Sure Things or triggering cancellations among Sleeping Dogs.

    1. ARPU, ARPPU, and Discounted Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)

    In freemium and subscription products, Average Revenue Per User ($\text{ARPU}$) normalizes revenue over all active users $N_{\text{active}}$, whereas Average Revenue Per Paying User ($\text{ARPPU}$) conditions on paying subscribers $N_{\text{paying}}$:

    $$\operatorname{ARPU} = \frac{\text{Total Revenue}}{N_{\text{active}}} = \underbrace{\left(\frac{N_{\text{paying}}}{N_{\text{active}}}\right)}_{\text{Payer Conversion Rate}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{\text{Total Revenue}}{N_{\text{paying}}}\right)}_{\operatorname{ARPPU}}.$$

    A subscriber generates recurring gross margin $M_t = \operatorname{ARPPU}_t \times g_m$ (where $g_m \in (0, 1]$ is gross margin percentage after payment processing, royalties, or compute COGS) in each future billing period $t \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$ with survival probability $S(t) = \Pr(T > t)$. Discounting future cash flows at periodic cost of capital $d > 0$ gives the exact Discounted Customer Lifetime Value ($\text{LTV}$):

    $$\operatorname{LTV} = \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \frac{M_t\,S(t)}{(1 + d)^t}.$$

    In the special case of constant periodic margin $M_t \equiv M$ and constant memoryless churn hazard $c \in (0, 1)$ so that retention is $r = 1 - c$ and $S(t) = (1 - c)^t$, the geometric series with ratio $\frac{1-c}{1+d} < 1$ converges in closed form:

    $$\operatorname{LTV}_{\text{constant-hazard}} = M \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \left(\frac{1 - c}{1 + d}\right)^t = M\cdot\frac{1 + d}{c + d} \approx \frac{\operatorname{ARPPU}\cdot g_m}{c + d}.$$
    • Why the constant-hazard formula misleads in A/B tests (Declining Hazard Bias): Real subscription cohorts exhibit duration dependence (or heterogeneity mixing via the Beta-Geometric model): newly acquired users have high Month-1 churn ($c_1 \approx 12\%$), while 12-month veterans have low churn ($c_{12} \approx 2\%$). Plugging Month-1 churn $c_1$ into $\frac{M}{c_1 + d}$ severely underestimates $\text{LTV}$, whereas plugging blended population churn severely overestimates new-user $\text{LTV}$. Production LTV models therefore integrate a Weibull or Beta-Geometric survival curve $\hat{S}(t \mid x_u)$ over a finite horizon $H$ (e.g., $\text{LTV}_{12\text{m}}$ or $\text{LTV}_{24\text{m}}$).

    2. LTV:CAC Ratio & CAC Payback Period

    Comparing $\text{LTV}$ against Customer Acquisition Cost ($\text{CAC}$) (or retention promo cost) governs capital efficiency through two complementary metrics:

    $$\operatorname{LTV:CAC\ Ratio} = \frac{\operatorname{LTV}}{\operatorname{CAC}},\qquad T_{\text{payback}} = \min\left\{\tau \ge 0 : \sum_{t=0}^{\tau} M_t\,S(t) \ge \operatorname{CAC}\right\}.$$

    Under constant periodic margin $M$ and high early retention, $T_{\text{payback}} \approx \frac{\operatorname{CAC}}{\operatorname{ARPPU}\cdot g_m}$ months. Even if an acquisition or promo channel has a healthy $\text{LTV:CAC} = 3.5\times$, a payback period of $T_{\text{payback}} = 28\text{ months}$ creates a working-capital cash trough that can bankrupt a fast-growing product if early cohort churn shifts slightly upward.

    3. Causal Uplift Modeling: CATE, The 4 User Quadrants & Sleeping Dogs

    Suppose a growth ML team wants to target a $\$10$ retention coupon ($T \in \{0, 1\}$) to users at risk of churning ($Y=1$ means the user retains / renews; $Y=0$ means they churn). Under Rubin's Potential Outcomes framework, user $u$ with feature vector $X_u = x$ has potential outcomes $(Y_u(1), Y_u(0)) \in \{0, 1\}^2$. The Conditional Average Treatment Effect (CATE / Uplift) is:

    $$\tau(x) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[Y(1) - Y(0) \mid X = x\bigr] = \Pr(Y=1 \mid X=x, T=1) - \Pr(Y=1 \mid X=x, T=0).$$

    Every user belongs to one of four latent causal response types based on $(Y(1), Y(0))$:

    1. Persuadables ($Y(1)=1, Y(0)=0 \implies \tau > 0$): Users who renew if and only if given the promo. Target these users!
    2. Sure Things ($Y(1)=1, Y(0)=1 \implies \tau = 0$): Loyal users who would have renewed anyway without a promo. Sending them a $\$10$ coupon wastes $100\%$ of the discount margin (margin cannibalization).
    3. Lost Causes ($Y(1)=0, Y(0)=0 \implies \tau = 0$): Users who will churn regardless of the coupon.
    4. Sleeping Dogs / Do-Not-Disturbs ($Y(1)=0, Y(0)=1 \implies \tau < 0$): Users who were passively auto-renewing on autopilot ($Y(0)=1$), but receiving a "Wait! Don't cancel—here is $20\%$ off your subscription!" email reminds them they are still paying for the subscription, prompting them to log in and cancel ($Y(1)=0$)!

    Net Incremental Profit Objective: Let $v_{\text{retain}}$ be the incremental LTV of a retained user and $c_{\text{promo}}$ be the dollar cost of the coupon when redeemed ($Y(1)=1$). Sending the promo to user $x$ is profitable if and only if expected incremental revenue exceeds expected promo spend (which applies to both Persuadables and Sure Things who redeem the coupon!):

    $$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\Delta\text{Profit}(x)\bigr] = \underbrace{\hat{\tau}(x)\cdot v_{\text{retain}}}_{\text{Incremental Retention Value}} - \underbrace{\widehat{\Pr}(Y=1 \mid X=x, T=1)\cdot c_{\text{promo}}}_{\text{Coupon Cost Paid to Persuadables + Sure Things}} > 0.$$

    4. Evaluating Uplift Models Online: Qini Curve & Area Under the Uplift Curve (AUUC)

    Because we never observe both $Y_u(1)$ and $Y_u(0)$ for the same individual (the Fundamental Problem of Causal Inference), we evaluate an uplift model $\hat{\tau}(x)$ on a randomized A/B holdout ($N_T$ treated, $N_C$ control) by sorting all users in descending order of predicted uplift $\hat{\tau}(x_1) \ge \hat{\tau}(x_2) \ge \dots \ge \hat{\tau}(x_N)$.

    Let $q \in [0, 1]$ denote the top fraction of the sorted population. Within the top-$q$ prefix, let $N_T(q)$ and $N_C(q)$ be the number of treated and control users, and let $R_T(q) = \sum_{i \le qN, T_i=1} Y_i$ and $R_C(q) = \sum_{i \le qN, T_i=0} Y_i$ be their total positive outcomes.

    • Cumulative Uplift Curve $U(q)$ and Qini Curve $Q(q)$: The Qini curve (Radcliffe, 2007) measures the net incremental positive outcomes generated by treating the top $q$ fraction of users, scaled to the treated sample size $N_T$:
      $$Q(q) = R_T(q) - R_C(q)\,\frac{N_T(q)}{N_C(q)},\qquad U(q) = \frac{R_T(q)}{N_T(q)} - \frac{R_C(q)}{N_C(q)}\cdot q.$$
    • Qini Coefficient & $\operatorname{AUUC}$: A random targeting policy achieves a straight diagonal line from $(0, 0)$ to $(1, Q(1))$, enclosing area $\frac{1}{2}Q(1)$. The Area Under the Uplift Curve ($\operatorname{AUUC}$) integrates $U(q)$ over $q \in [0, 1]$, and the Qini Coefficient ($q_{\text{coef}}$) normalizes the area between the model's Qini curve $Q(q)$ and the random diagonal by the area of a theoretical perfect oracle uplift curve:
      $$\operatorname{AUUC}_{\text{net}} = \int_0^1 \bigl(Q(q) - q\,Q(1)\bigr)\,dq.$$

    Worked numerical example: Why Churn-Risk Targeting Loses Money vs. Uplift Targeting

    Suppose a retained subscription is worth $v_{\text{retain}} = \$100$ and a retention coupon costs $c_{\text{promo}} = \$20$ whenever a treated user stays subscribed ($Y(1)=1$). Consider two user segments of $1{,}000$ users each:

    • Segment A (High-Value Sure Things + Sleeping Dogs): $\Pr(Y=1\mid T=0) = 0.80$, $\Pr(Y=1\mid T=1) = 0.78$ ($\tau_A = -0.02$). Sending them the coupon loses $20$ subscribers ($\Delta\text{Rev} = -\$2{,}000$) and pays $780 \times \$20 = \$15{,}600$ in unnecessary discounts, for a net loss of $\mathbf{-\$17{,}600}$!
    • Segment B (True Persuadables): $\Pr(Y=1\mid T=0) = 0.40$, $\Pr(Y=1\mid T=1) = 0.55$ ($\tau_B = +0.15$). Sending them the coupon saves $150$ incremental subscribers ($+\$15{,}000$ value) while paying $550 \times \$20 = \$11{,}000$ in coupon redemptions, yielding a net incremental profit of $\mathbf{+\$4{,}000}$!

    5. Python Implementation: Qini Curve, AUUC & Profit-Optimal Promo Cutoff

    import numpy as np

def evaluate_uplift_qini_and_profit(
    y: np.ndarray, w: np.ndarray, tau_hat: np.ndarray, p_treat_hat: np.ndarray,
    v_retain: float = 100.0, c_promo: float = 20.0
):
    """
    Computes the Qini Curve, net Area Under the Uplift Curve (AUUC),
    and the profit-maximizing targeting fraction given v_retain and c_promo.
    w in {0, 1} is the randomized treatment indicator; y in {0, 1} is retention.
    """
    # 1. Rank users by expected incremental profit: tau_hat * v_retain - p_treat_hat * c_promo
    expected_profit = tau_hat * v_retain - p_treat_hat * c_promo
    order = np.argsort(-tau_hat)
    y_sorted, w_sorted = y[order], w[order]

    cum_t = np.cumsum(w_sorted == 1)
    cum_c = np.cumsum(w_sorted == 0)
    cum_rt = np.cumsum((w_sorted == 1) & (y_sorted == 1))
    cum_rc = np.cumsum((w_sorted == 0) & (y_sorted == 1))

    # Avoid division by zero in tiny prefixes
    ratio = np.where(cum_c > 0, cum_t / np.maximum(cum_c, 1), 1.0)
    qini_curve = cum_rt - cum_rc * ratio

    n = len(y)
    q_grid = np.arange(1, n + 1) / n
    random_line = q_grid * qini_curve[-1]
    net_auuc = float(np.trapz(qini_curve - random_line, q_grid))

    # 2. Realized incremental profit at profit-optimal threshold (expected_profit > 0)
    target_mask = expected_profit > 0
    t_sub = (w == 1) & target_mask
    c_sub = (w == 0) & target_mask
    inc_retain_rate = np.mean(y[t_sub]) - np.mean(y[c_sub])
    realized_profit_per_targeted = inc_retain_rate * v_retain - np.mean(y[t_sub]) * c_promo

    return {
        "Total_Pop_ATE_%": round(float(100 * (np.mean(y[w == 1]) - np.mean(y[w == 0]))), 2),
        "Net_Qini_AUUC": round(net_auuc, 2),
        "Optimal_Target_Share_%": round(float(100 * np.mean(target_mask)), 2),
        "Targeted_CATE_Lift_%": round(float(100 * inc_retain_rate), 2),
        "Net_Profit_Per_Targeted_$": round(float(realized_profit_per_targeted), 2),
    }

if __name__ == "__main__":
    rng = np.random.default_rng(21)
    N = 20_000
    x = rng.uniform(-1, 1, N)
    w = rng.binomial(1, 0.5, N)
    p0 = 0.50 + 0.20 * x
    true_tau = np.where(x < 0, 0.16, -0.03)  # Persuadables for x < 0, Sleeping Dogs for x >= 0
    p1 = np.clip(p0 + true_tau, 0.01, 0.99)
    y = rng.binomial(1, np.where(w == 1, p1, p0))
    print(evaluate_uplift_qini_and_profit(y, w, true_tau + rng.normal(0, 0.02, N), p1))

    6. Self-Check Questions

    Why does targeting a retention discount to the top decile of predicted churners ($\hat{P}(\text{churn}\mid X)$) frequently produce negative incremental ROI in online A/B tests?

    Because high predicted churn risk $\hat{P}(Y=0 \mid X, T=0)$ conflates Lost Causes (users whose credit card is dead, who moved abroad, or who already switched to a competitor and have $\tau(x) = 0$) with Persuadables ($\tau(x) > 0$). Meanwhile, moderate-risk users who redeem the coupon contain a high fraction of Sure Things ($\tau(x) = 0$) who happily accept the $\$20$ discount even though they were going to renew at full price anyway.

    When running a 2-week A/B test on a feature designed to improve 12-month $\text{LTV}$, how do you construct a valid short-term surrogate metric?

    Following Athey et al.'s Surrogate Index methodology, train a historical model predicting realized 12-month net revenue $\text{LTV}_{12\text{m}}$ from short-term 14-day intermediate outcomes $S_{1:14}$ (e.g., active days in Week 1 & Week 2, core feature adoption depth, payment method validity, and auto-renew toggle status). Under the surrogacy assumption ($\text{LTV}_{12\text{m}} \perp T \mid S_{1:14}$), the treatment effect on $\widehat{\text{LTV}}_{12\text{m}}(S_{1:14})$ is an unbiased, low-variance estimator of long-term LTV lift—which you then validate via a $5\%$ long-term holdback cohort.
