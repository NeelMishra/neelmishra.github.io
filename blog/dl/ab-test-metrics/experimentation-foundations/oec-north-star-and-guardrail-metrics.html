    OEC, North Star Decomposition & Guardrail Metrics

    October 2026 · ML System Design · Online Evaluation Architecture

    When an interviewer asks, "How would you evaluate your new ranking model in an online A/B test?", naming a single metric like $\text{CTR}$ or Revenue is a classic trap. Any single unconstrained metric can be gamed via Goodhart's Law—doubling ad load boosts short-term revenue while destroying user retention, and clickbait thumbnails inflate $\text{CTR}$ while collapsing watch time. Production experimentation relies on a three-tier architecture: a North Star Goal, a sensitive Overall Evaluation Criterion ($\text{OEC}$) / Driver Metric, and strict Guardrail Metrics.

    1. The Three-Tier Online Metric Hierarchy

    Every online ML system must reconcile two competing requirements: alignment with long-term business value (Directionality) and statistical sensitivity within a 1-to-2 week experiment window (Power).

    • Tier 1: North Star Metric (Long-Term Enterprise Value). Measures cumulative user and business value over quarters or years—such as $12\text{-Month Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)}$, $\text{Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR)}$, or $D_{90}\text{ Active User Retention}$. While perfectly aligned with business health, North Star metrics have terrible short-term sensitivity: a ranking model tweak rarely moves 90-day retention with statistical significance inside a 14-day A/B test.
    • Tier 2: Overall Evaluation Criterion (OEC) & Driver Metrics. A quantitative, short-term composite score or surrogate index measured during the A/B window that causally predicts long-term North Star movement. For example, in search ranking, $\text{Successful Sessions per User}$ (clicks with dwell $\ge 30\text{s}$ or instant answers) moves within hours and correlates strongly with long-term retention.
    • Tier 3: Guardrail Metrics (Invariants & Constraints). Metrics that the experiment is not trying to improve, but must not degrade beyond a pre-specified non-inferiority margin $\tau_k$. Guardrails protect system health ($p_{99}$ latency, OOM crash rate), ecosystem trust (hide/report rate, false declines), and adjacent business surfaces (organic search cannibalization).

    2. Mathematical Formulation of the OEC

    There are two rigorous ways to formalize an $\text{OEC}$ across $M$ positively-aligned driver metrics $Y_1, \dots, Y_M$ and $K$ guardrail metrics $G_1, \dots, G_K$.

    2.1 Standardized Linear Surrogate OEC

    Because raw metrics have incompatible units (e.g., milliseconds, dollars, clicks), we first normalize each user-level metric $Y_{u,m}$ by the control group's mean $\mu_{m,C}$ and standard deviation $\sigma_{m,C}$ (or baseline mean for relative lift), and combine them with weights $w_m \ge 0$ ($\sum_{m=1}^M w_m = 1$) estimated from historical long-term retention regressions:

    $$\text{OEC}_u = \sum_{m=1}^{M} w_m \left(\frac{Y_{u,m} - \mu_{m,C}}{\sigma_{m,C}}\right)$$

    Because $\text{OEC}_u$ is evaluated per randomization unit $u$ (the user), its variance is straightforward to compute via a standard two-sample $t$-test without cross-metric covariance headaches!

    2.2 Constrained Optimization & Lagrangian Dual OEC

    In production decision rules (and online multi-objective ranking), we formulate shipping a treatment $T$ over control $C$ as a constrained optimization problem with non-inferiority tolerances $\tau_k \ge 0$:

    $$\max_{\text{Model } \theta} \; \mathbb{E}_T[Y_{\text{primary}}] - \mathbb{E}_C[Y_{\text{primary}}] \quad \text{subject to} \quad \Delta G_k + z_{1-\alpha}\cdot \text{SE}(\Delta G_k) \le \tau_k \;\;\forall k \in \{1,\dots,K\}$$

    Note the direction of the confidence bound in the constraint: to prove non-inferiority on a guardrail $G_k$ (where higher is worse, such as latency or ad hide rate), the upper confidence bound of $\Delta G_k$ must lie below the tolerance threshold $\tau_k$—merely observing an insignificant $p > 0.05$ on an underpowered test is not proof of safety!

    When tuning ranking utility weights directly inside the scoring function, constraints are folded into a Lagrangian objective using shadow prices $\lambda_k \ge 0$:

    $$\mathcal{L}(\theta, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) = \Delta \mu_{\text{primary}}(\theta) - \sum_{k=1}^{K} \lambda_k \left(\Delta G_k(\theta) - \tau_k\right)$$
    ML System Design Rule of Thumb — How $\lambda_k$ Is Chosen: In ad-load or notification ranking, how do we pick the shadow price $\lambda$ trading off $\$1$ of ad revenue against $1$ user hide/unsubscribe? Teams run long-term degradation / holdout experiments (e.g., permanently increasing ad load by $+20\%$ on a $1\%$ user slice for 6 months) to measure the exact causal ratio $\lambda = \frac{\Delta \text{6-Month LTV}}{\Delta \text{Short-Term Hide Rate}}$.

    3. Taxonomy of Production Guardrail Metrics

    Guardrail Category Specific Metrics Tracked Failure Mode Caught
    1. Trustworthiness & Data Integrity Sample Ratio Mismatch ($\chi^2$ SRM $p < 0.001$), Missing Telemetry Rate, Event Duplication Rate Broken hash bucketing, crash-induced logging loss, bot asymmetry
    2. Systems & Serving Infrastructure $p_{50} / p_{95} / p_{99}$ End-to-End Latency, Client App Crash / ANR Rate, GPU/CPU Cost per 1k Requests, Timeout Fallback Rate Heavy cross-attention rankers causing tail-latency spikes that silently drop conversions ($+100\text{ms}$ latency often costs $-0.5\%$ to $-1\%$ GMV)
    3. User Distress & Negative Feedback Ad Hide / Block Rate, Content Report Rate, Notification Unsubscribe / OS Permission Revoke, Pogo-Sticking Rate Clickbait, sensory overload, spammy push frequency, or offensive recommendations
    4. Ecosystem & Cannibalization Organic Search Clicks (when testing Sponsored Ads), Creator Exposure Gini Coefficient, Seller Churn, Out-of-Stock Rate Sponsored ads stealing clicks from identical organic listings ($100\%$ cannibalization with zero incremental utility)

    4. Worked Numerical Example: Evaluating a Feed Ranking Experiment

    Suppose you test a new Cross-Network Deep Ranking model ($T$) against the production baseline ($C$) across $n_C = n_T = 500{,}000$ users over 14 days:

    • Primary Driver ($\text{Daily Qualified Watch Time}$): $\bar{Y}_C = 42.00\text{ min}$, $\bar{Y}_T = 42.84\text{ min}$ ($\Delta = +0.84\text{ min}$ or $+2.00\%$, $\text{SE} = 0.15\text{ min}$, $z = +5.60, p < 10^{-7}$).
    • Guardrail 1 ($p_{99}$ Feed Ranking Latency): Tolerance $\tau_{\text{lat}} = +15.0\text{ ms}$. Observed $\Delta G_{\text{lat}} = +11.2\text{ ms}$ with $\text{SE} = 1.4\text{ ms}$. Upper $95\%$ one-sided bound: $11.2 + 1.645(1.4) = +13.50\text{ ms} \le 15.0\text{ ms}$ (PASS).
    • Guardrail 2 (Creator Distress / "Not Interested" Rate): Baseline $1.20\%$, Tolerance $\tau_{\text{distress}} = +0.03\%$ ($+3\text{ bps}$). Observed $\Delta G_{\text{distress}} = +0.08\%$ ($+8\text{ bps}$) with $\text{SE} = 0.015\%$ (FAIL — Violates Guardrail).

    Ship Decision: Even though watch time increased by a highly significant $+2.00\%$, the experiment blocks launch because the model achieved that watch time by over-serving polarising/sensational videos that drove $+8\text{ bps}$ more explicit "Not Interested" taps. Adding a negative-feedback head $-\lambda_{\text{dislike}} \hat{p}(\text{not\_interested})$ to the multi-task ranking formula fixes the guardrail violation before re-testing.

    5. Production Python Implementation: Composite OEC & Non-Inferiority Gate

    import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

def evaluate_ab_with_guardrails(
    user_drivers_c: np.ndarray,
    user_drivers_t: np.ndarray,
    weights: np.ndarray,
    guardrails: list[dict],
    alpha: float = 0.05,
) -> dict:
    """
    Computes a standardized user-level composite OEC and runs non-inferiority
    one-sided upper-bound checks on all guardrail metrics.
    
    user_drivers_c, user_drivers_t: shape (N_users, M_drivers)
    weights: shape (M_drivers,), summing to 1.0
    guardrails: list of dicts with keys:
      {"name": str, "delta": float, "se": float, "max_allowed_increase": float}
    """
    weights = np.asarray(weights, dtype=float)
    weights = weights / weights.sum()

    # Standardize each driver metric using Control mean and std
    mu_c = user_drivers_c.mean(axis=0)
    sd_c = user_drivers_c.std(axis=0, ddof=1)

    z_c = (user_drivers_c - mu_c) / sd_c
    z_t = (user_drivers_t - mu_c) / sd_c

    # Per-user scalar OEC
    oec_c = z_c @ weights
    oec_t = z_t @ weights

    delta_oec = oec_t.mean() - oec_c.mean()
    se_oec = np.sqrt(oec_c.var(ddof=1) / len(oec_c) + oec_t.var(ddof=1) / len(oec_t))
    t_stat = delta_oec / se_oec
    p_val = 2.0 * (1.0 - stats.norm.cdf(abs(t_stat)))

    # Evaluate non-inferiority on guardrails (one-sided 1 - alpha upper bound)
    z_one_sided = stats.norm.ppf(1.0 - alpha)
    guardrail_results = []
    all_guardrails_passed = True

    for g in guardrails:
        upper_bound = g["delta"] + z_one_sided * g["se"]
        passed = bool(upper_bound <= g["max_allowed_increase"])
        if not passed:
            all_guardrails_passed = False
        guardrail_results.append({
            "name": g["name"],
            "delta": g["delta"],
            "upper_95_bound": round(upper_bound, 5),
            "tolerance": g["max_allowed_increase"],
            "passed": passed,
        })

    ship_decision = (delta_oec > 0) and (p_val < alpha) and all_guardrails_passed
    return {
        "delta_oec_sd": round(float(delta_oec), 5),
        "se_oec": round(float(se_oec), 5),
        "p_value": float(p_val),
        "guardrails": guardrail_results,
        "ship": bool(ship_decision),
    }

if __name__ == "__main__":
    rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
    # 2 driver metrics: [Successful Sessions, Watch Time]
    c_data = rng.normal(loc=[2.5, 42.0], scale=[1.2, 18.0], size=(50_000, 2))
    t_data = rng.normal(loc=[2.56, 42.84], scale=[1.2, 18.0], size=(50_000, 2))
    res = evaluate_ab_with_guardrails(
        c_data, t_data, weights=[0.6, 0.4],
        guardrails=[
            {"name": "p99_latency_ms", "delta": 11.2, "se": 1.4, "max_allowed_increase": 15.0},
            {"name": "not_interested_bps", "delta": 8.0, "se": 1.5, "max_allowed_increase": 3.0},
        ]
    )
    print(res)

    6. Self-Check & Interview Exercises

    Exercise 1 — Why "No Statistically Significant Drop" Is a Trap for Guardrails

    An engineer runs a small A/B test on $5{,}000$ users and reports: "Crash rate increased by $+0.40\%$ ($p = 0.18$). Since $p > 0.05$, the crash guardrail was not significantly hurt, so we can ship." Why is this statistically wrong, and what test should be used instead?

    Check solution

    Failing to reject $H_0: \Delta \text{Crash} = 0$ on a tiny sample simply means the test has low statistical power (wide confidence intervals), not that the treatment is safe! To protect a guardrail, you must run a non-inferiority test where the null hypothesis is that the treatment is unsafe ($H_0: \Delta G \ge \tau_{\max}$) and the alternative is safety ($H_1: \Delta G < \tau_{\max}$). You only pass the guardrail if the upper $1-\alpha$ confidence bound $\widehat{\Delta G} + z_{1-\alpha}\text{SE}$ falls strictly below $\tau_{\max}$.

    Exercise 2 — Designing an OEC for Sponsored Search Ads

    If you are asked in an ML System Design interview to define the OEC and guardrails for a Sponsored Search Ads ranking system, what metrics do you place in each tier?

    Check solution
    • Primary Driver / OEC: Incremental Revenue per Search ($\text{RPM}$) combined with Position-Normalized Ad Quality ($\text{COEC}$) and Post-Click Conversion Value.
    • Guardrails: (1) Ad Load (% of top slots occupied by ads), (2) Organic Search Click cannibalization & Session Success Rate, (3) Ad Hide / Bad-Ad Bounce Rate (dwell $< 5\text{s}$), (4) Calibration ($\text{COPC} \in [0.97, 1.03]$ so GSP auction pricing remains truthful), and (5) $p_{99}$ Auction Latency.
