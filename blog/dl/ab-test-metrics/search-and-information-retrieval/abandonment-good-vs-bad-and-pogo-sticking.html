    Search Abandonment (Good vs. Bad Abandonment) & Pogo-Sticking / Short-Dwell Bounces

    October 2026 · Implicit Satisfaction & Dissatisfaction Signals · Search Guardrails

    Two of the most misunderstood behavioral signals in search A/B testing are no-click queries (Abandonment) and multi-click queries (Pogo-Sticking). A query with zero clicks can represent a user delighted by an instant answer card or LLM summary—or a user who gave up in frustration after seeing ten irrelevant links. Conversely, a query with three clicks can represent deep comparative research—or a user repeatedly tricked by clickbait snippets who hits the browser Back button after $4$ seconds. This note formalizes the mathematical decomposition of Good vs. Bad Abandonment and Pogo-Sticking Rate.

    1. Decomposing Total Search Abandonment into Good vs. Bad

    Let $\mathcal{Q}$ be the set of all search queries issued by users in an experiment arm, and let $\mathcal{C}(q)$ be the set of clicks on query $q$ (excluding pagination or filter clicks that merely reload the SERP). A query $q$ is abandoned if $|\mathcal{C}(q)| = 0$. The Total Abandonment Rate ($\text{TAR}$) is:

    $$\text{TAR} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|}\sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} \mathbb{I}\bigl[|\mathcal{C}(q)| = 0\bigr] = 1 - \text{Query-CTR}$$

    In every search engine with rich SERP modules (instant answers, calculators, dictionary definitions, sports scores, product price comparisons, or LLM-generated summaries), $\text{TAR}$ is an exact additive sum of two opposing components:

    $$\underbrace{\text{TAR}}_{\text{Total Abandonment}} = \underbrace{\text{GAR}}_{\text{Good Abandonment Rate }(\uparrow\text{ good})} + \underbrace{\text{BAR}}_{\text{Bad Abandonment Rate }(\downarrow\text{ good})}$$

    Formal Criteria & Probabilistic Estimator for Good Abandonment

    How do we determine whether an abandoned query $q$ ($|\mathcal{C}(q)| = 0$) was a Good Abandonment ($G_q = 1$) or a Bad Abandonment ($B_q = 1 - G_q$)? We offers both a deterministic rule-based indicator (for standard dashboards) and a calibrated probabilistic classifier $\hat{p}_{\text{GA}}(q) \in [0, 1]$ (trained on human-raters + inline micro-surveys):

    $$G_q = \mathbb{I}\bigl[|\mathcal{C}(q)| = 0\bigr] \cdot \mathbb{I}\bigl[A_{\text{ans}}(q) = 1\bigr] \cdot \mathbb{I}\bigl[\tau_{\min,\text{read}} \le d_{\text{viewport}}(q) \le \tau_{\max,\text{read}}\bigr] \cdot \mathbb{I}\bigl[\text{Retry}(q, q_{\text{next}}) = 0\bigr]$$

    Let us inspect each of the four multiplicative conditions in $G_q$:

    1. Zero Organic Clicks ($|\mathcal{C}(q)| = 0$): The user did not click through to an external landing page (though micro-interactions inside an answer widget, such as toggling currency units or expanding an "Read more" accordion on the SERP, are strong positive signals).
    2. Answer-Bearing SERP Feature Present ($A_{\text{ans}}(q) = 1$): Either (a) a structured Instant Answer / Knowledge Panel / AI Overview rendered above the fold, or (b) the query is classified as a factoid/status lookup where the snippet text itself contains the extracted entity answer.
    3. Plausible Reading Viewport Dwell ($\tau_{\min,\text{read}} \le d_{\text{viewport}}(q) \le \tau_{\max,\text{read}}$): The answer module remained $\ge 50\%$ visible in the client viewport for at least $\tau_{\min,\text{read}} \approx 2.0\text{s}$ (enough time for human eye fixation and reading, ruling out accidental instant tab closures) and at most $\tau_{\max,\text{read}} \approx 60\text{s}$ (ruling out walk-away idle tabs). Additionally, the user did not frantically scroll past Rank $8$ hunting for alternatives.
    4. Absence of Frustrated Retry ($\text{Retry}(q, q_{\text{next}}) = 0$): The user does not issue a semantically similar reformulation $q_{\text{next}}$ within $\tau_w = 5\text{ minutes}$ (where $\text{sim}(q, q_{\text{next}}) \ge \gamma$). If a user sees an AI Overview for "how to reset airpods pro", reads it for $6\text{s}$, and immediately searches "airpods pro factory reset not working reddit", the first query was not a Good Abandonment!
    Continuous Expected Good Abandonment ($\mathbb{E}[\text{GAR}]$):

    Rather than a hard binary cutoff, production search teams at Google, Bing, and Amazon train a gradient-boosted or neural satisfaction model $f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_q) = \Pr(\text{Satisfied} \mid |\mathcal{C}(q)|=0, \mathbf{x}_q)$ using features $\mathbf{x}_q$ = (SERP module type, viewport dwell time, maximum scroll depth, mouse hover / text-highlight events on the answer card, time to next query, semantic similarity to next query). Then: $$\widehat{\text{GAR}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|}\sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} \mathbb{I}[|\mathcal{C}(q)|=0]\cdot f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_q), \qquad \widehat{\text{BAR}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|}\sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} \mathbb{I}[|\mathcal{C}(q)|=0]\cdot \bigl(1 - f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_q)\bigr)$$

    2. Pogo-Sticking Rate & Short-Dwell Bounce Metrics

    Suppose a user clicks Rank $1$, lands on the destination page, realizes within $5$ seconds that the article is outdated or behind a paywall or doesn't match the snippet, hits the browser Back button to return to the SERP, and clicks Rank $2$. This rapid back-and-forth movement between the SERP and result pages is called Pogo-Sticking.

    Calibrating Post-Click Dwell Thresholds ($\tau_{\text{short}}$ vs. $\tau_{\text{long}}$)

    For the $j$-th click $c_{q,j}$ on query $q$, let $d(c_{q,j}) = t_{\text{return}}(c_{q,j}) - t_{\text{click}}(c_{q,j})$ denote the time elapsed before the user returns to the SERP. Seminal log-analysis studies (Fox et al.; Kim et al., WSDM) show that log-dwell time $\ln d(c)$ is bimodal, mixtures of dissatisfied bounces and satisfied reads:

    • Short-Dwell Click (Bounce, $d(c_{q,j}) < \tau_{\text{short}}$): Typically $\tau_{\text{short}} \in [10\text{s}, 15\text{s}]$. Indicates that the landing page failed to satisfy the expectation set by the SERP title/snippet.
    • Medium-Dwell Click ($\tau_{\text{short}} \le d(c_{q,j}) < \tau_{\text{sat}}$): Typically $10\text{s}\text{–}30\text{s}$. Ambiguous zone—could be a quick fact check or a slower realization of irrelevance.
    • Long-Dwell / Satisfied Click ($d(c_{q,j}) \ge \tau_{\text{sat}}$ or Terminal Click): Typically $\tau_{\text{sat}} = 30\text{s}$ (or the final click of the session from which the user never returns to the SERP).

    Three Complementary Formulations of Pogo-Sticking

    Simply counting all clicks with $d(c) < 10\text{s}$ can falsely penalize fast factual lookups (e.g., clicking a restaurant's page just to check if it is "Open Now" in $7\text{s}$ and closing the browser satisfied). Therefore, true Pogo-Sticking requires both a short dwell $d(c_{q,j}) < \tau_{\text{short}}$ and a subsequent corrective action on the SERP (another click $c_{q,j+1}$ on the same SERP or a same-intent query reformulation $q_{\text{next}}$):

    $$\text{Pogo}(c_{q,j}) = \mathbb{I}\!\left[d(c_{q,j}) < \tau_{\text{short}} \;\land\; \bigl(\text{HasNextClick}(q, j) \;\lor\; \text{HasReformulation}(q)\bigr)\right]$$

    From this click-level indicator, we define three standard A/B metrics:

    1. Query-Level Pogo-Sticking Rate ($\text{PSR}_Q$): The fraction of all queries (or of all clicked queries) that experience at least one pogo-stick bounce:
      $$\text{PSR}_Q = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|}\sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} \mathbb{I}\!\left[\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{C}(q)|} \text{Pogo}(c_{q,j}) \ge 1\right]$$
    2. Click-Level Short-Dwell Bounce Share ($\text{SDBS}$): Among all organic clicks across the platform, what proportion are wasted pogo-stick clicks?
      $$\text{SDBS} = \frac{\sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} \sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{C}(q)|} \text{Pogo}(c_{q,j})}{\sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} |\mathcal{C}(q)|}$$
      $\text{SDBS}$ is the primary guardrail against clickbait ranking bias: if you add a raw click-prediction head $\hat{p}_{\text{click}}$ to your search ranker without dwell weighting, raw CTR will go up, but $\text{SDBS}$ will spike upwards because the model promotes sensational titles with poor landing-page utility.
    3. Session Pogo-Stick Intensity: The expected number of wasted short-dwell clicks per search session $\frac{\sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}} B_s}{|\mathcal{S}|}$.

    3. Worked Numerical Example: Evaluating an AI Overview / Instant Answer Launch

    Suppose your team runs an A/B test introducing an inline AI Summary Card at the top of informational queries. Each arm logs $10{,}000$ queries across $5{,}000$ users ($2.0$ queries/user on average). Look at how a naïve analyst vs. a seasoned Search ML engineer interprets the exact same numbers:

    Metric Control (Classic 10 Blue Links) Treatment (+ AI Summary Card) Relative / Absolute Shift
    Total Queries $|\mathcal{Q}|$ $10{,}000$ $10{,}000$ $0.0\%$
    Queries with $\ge 1$ Click ($|\mathcal{Q}_c|$) $6{,}800$ ($\text{CTR} = 68.0\%$) $5{,}900$ ($\text{CTR} = 59.0\%$) $-9.0\text{ pp}$ ($\text{CTR}\downarrow$)
    Total Abandonment Rate ($\text{TAR}$) $3{,}200$ ($32.0\%$) $4{,}100$ ($41.0\%$) $+9.0\text{ pp}$ ($\text{TAR}\uparrow$)
    ├─ Good Abandonment ($\text{GAR}$) $900$ ($9.0\%$) $2{,}450$ ($24.5\%$) $+15.5\text{ pp}$ (Huge win!)
    └─ Bad Abandonment ($\text{BAR}$) $2{,}300$ ($23.0\%$) $1{,}650$ ($16.5\%$) $-6.5\text{ pp}$ (Fewer frustrated exits!)
    Queries with Satisfied Click ($d \ge 30\text{s}$) $5{,}100$ ($51.0\%$) $4{,}750$ ($47.5\%$) $-3.5\text{ pp}$
    Queries with Pogo-Sticking ($\text{PSR}_Q$) $1{,}400$ ($14.0\%$) $920$ ($9.2\%$) $-4.8\text{ pp}$ ($-34.3\%$ relative)
    Net Query Success ($\text{SatClick} + \text{GAR}$) $5{,}100 + 900 = \mathbf{60.0\%}$ $4{,}750 + 2{,}450 = \mathbf{72.0\%}$ $+12.0\text{ pp}$ ($+20.0\%$ relative lift!)

    Takeaway: A naïve dashboard tracking only Query CTR ($68\% \to 59\%$) and Total Abandonment ($32\% \to 41\%$) would declare Treatment a catastrophic $-9\text{ pp}$ regression! Decomposing Abandonment into Good vs. Bad reveals that Good Abandonment rose by $+15.5\text{ pp}$, Bad Abandonment dropped by $-6.5\text{ pp}$, Pogo-Sticking dropped by $-34.3\%$, and Net Query Satisfaction jumped from $60.0\%$ to $72.0\%$.

    4. Reference Python Implementation: Good/Bad Abandonment & Pogo-Sticking

    import numpy as np
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from typing import List, Dict

@dataclass
class ClickLog:
    rank: int
    dwell_sec: float  # np.inf if terminal click (user never returned to SERP)

@dataclass
class SERPQueryLog:
    user_id: int
    query_id: int
    has_answer_card: bool
    answer_viewport_dwell_sec: float
    max_scroll_rank: int
    followed_by_same_intent_retry: bool
    clicks: List[ClickLog] = field(default_factory=list)

def classify_query_outcomes(
    logs: List[SERPQueryLog],
    short_dwell_sec: float = 10.0,
    sat_dwell_sec: float = 30.0,
    min_card_read_sec: float = 2.0,
    max_card_read_sec: float = 60.0,
) -> Dict[str, float]:
    """
    Decomposes a list of SERP query logs into:
      - Good Abandonment Rate (GAR)
      - Bad Abandonment Rate (BAR)
      - Satisfied Click Rate (SCR)
      - Query-level Pogo-Sticking Rate (PSR_Q)
      - Click-level Short-Dwell Bounce Share (SDBS)
      - Net Query Satisfaction Rate (SCR + GAR)
    """
    n_queries = len(logs)
    good_aband = 0
    bad_aband = 0
    sat_clicked_q = 0
    pogo_queries = 0
    total_clicks = 0
    pogo_clicks = 0

    for q in logs:
        m = len(q.clicks)
        if m == 0:
            # Zero-click query: classify Good vs Bad Abandonment
            card_read_ok = (
                q.has_answer_card
                and (min_card_read_sec <= q.answer_viewport_dwell_sec <= max_card_read_sec)
                and (q.max_scroll_rank <= 6)
                and (not q.followed_by_same_intent_retry)
            )
            if card_read_ok:
                good_aband += 1
            else:
                bad_aband += 1
        else:
            total_clicks += m
            has_sat = any(c.dwell_sec >= sat_dwell_sec for c in q.clicks)
            if has_sat:
                sat_clicked_q += 1

            q_has_pogo = False
            for j, c in enumerate(q.clicks):
                has_next_click = (j < m - 1)
                is_pogo = (c.dwell_sec < short_dwell_sec) and (
                    has_next_click or q.followed_by_same_intent_retry
                )
                if is_pogo:
                    pogo_clicks += 1
                    q_has_pogo = True
            if q_has_pogo:
                pogo_queries += 1

    return {
        "Total_Abandonment_Rate": (good_aband + bad_aband) / n_queries,
        "Good_Abandonment_Rate": good_aband / n_queries,
        "Bad_Abandonment_Rate": bad_aband / n_queries,
        "Satisfied_Click_Rate": sat_clicked_q / n_queries,
        "Net_Query_Success_Rate": (sat_clicked_q + good_aband) / n_queries,
        "Query_Pogo_Sticking_Rate": pogo_queries / n_queries,
        "Click_Short_Dwell_Bounce_Share": pogo_clicks / max(total_clicks, 1),
    }

    5. Self-Check & ML System Design Interview Exercises

    1. How do you distinguish between "Pogo-Sticking" (dissatisfied multi-click bouncing) and "Exploratory Comparison Shopping" (e.g., opening 4 hotel or laptop tabs to compare specs)?

    Two telemetry signals cleanly separate Pogo-Sticking from Exploratory Comparison:
    1. Dwell Time & Tab-Open Pattern: Pogo-sticking exhibits very short sequential dwell ($d(c_j) < 10\text{s}$) via back-button navigation before clicking $c_{j+1}$. Comparison shopping typically exhibits either multi-tab background opening (`Ctrl+Click` / middle-click / "Open in new tab", where cumulative tab dwell across opened tabs is high) or sequential visits with medium-to-long dwell ($20\text{s}\text{–}90\text{s}$) per item accompanied by image gallery / spec-table interactions.
    2. Query Category Priors: On navigational queries ("chase login"), clicking $2$ links is almost certainly an error; on broad shopping queries ("best mirrorless camera under 1500"), multi-click exploration is expected and healthy as long as individual page dwells exceed $\tau_{\text{short}}$.

    2. How should you train a Learning-to-Rank (LTR) model offline so that its predictions reduce Pogo-Sticking and increase Session Success online?

    Never train an LTR model on raw binary clicks $y_{\text{click}} \in \{0, 1\}$! Instead:
    • Assign zero or negative relevance grades to short-dwell pogo-stick clicks ($d(c) < 10\text{s}$), treating them identically to (or worse than) unclicked skipped impressions.
    • Assign graded positive labels proportional to log-dwell time $\ln(1 + d(c))$ and terminal conversion actions ($\text{ATC}$, download, purchase), or train a multi-task ranking head that predicts $\hat{p}_{\text{click}} \cdot \hat{p}(\text{dwell} \ge 30\text{s} \mid \text{click}) \cdot (1 - \alpha \hat{p}_{\text{pogo}})$.

    3. On mobile devices, users frequently close the search app or lock their phone screen right after reading a SERP snippet. How does this affect Good vs. Bad Abandonment measurement?

    On mobile, `visibilitychange` / app-backgrounding events provide exact foreground viewport timestamps. If a user submits a query, no answer card is present, the user scrolls rapidly for $3\text{s}$, and force-closes or backgrounds the app, that is a strong Bad Abandonment signal. Conversely, if an answer card (e.g., weather, flight status, local business hours) is centered in the viewport for $4\text{s}$ before the user locks their phone screen, the absence of subsequent search retries within the next $10\text{ minutes}$ confirms Good Abandonment.
