    Weight Initialization in Deep Learning: Reading Guide

    September 2026 · 2 min read + interactive labs

    A connected guide with step-by-step derivations, worked examples, figures, and interactive experiments.

    Weight initialization sets the starting point of optimization. Before a single gradient step is taken, it must break symmetry across hidden units, keep forward activations and backward gradients from vanishing or exploding across depth, and match the architecture’s activations.

    Reading order

    01
    Initialization: Break Symmetry and Keep Signals Alive

    See why identical hidden units stay identical, derive the per-layer second-moment multipliers $n_{\rm in}s_W^2$ (forward) and $n_{\rm out}s_W^2$ (backward), and watch scale compound through depth.

    02
    Xavier, LeCun, and He Initialization: Count the Inputs and Outputs

    Derive fan-in ($s_W^2=1/n_{\rm in}$), fan-out ($s_W^2=1/n_{\rm out}$), Xavier ($s_W^2=2/(n_{\rm in}+n_{\rm out})$), and He ($s_W^2=2/n_{\rm in}$) variances, and convert them to uniform bounds via $a=\sqrt{3}\,s_W$.

    Core notation at a glance

    • Layer widths: $n_{\rm in}$ and $n_{\rm out}$ count the inputs and outputs connected to each unit in $W_l\in\mathbb R^{n_{\rm out}\times n_{\rm in}}$.
    • Moments: Weight variance $s_W^2 = \operatorname{Var}(W_{l,ij}) = \mathbb E[W_{l,ij}^2]$ (standard deviation $s_W$), preactivation variance $\sigma_z^2 = \operatorname{Var}(z_{l,i}) = \mathbb E[z_{l,i}^2]$, activation second moment $q_l = \mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2] = \operatorname{Var}(h_{l,i}) + (\mathbb E[h_{l,i}])^2$ (or $q = \mathbb E[a_j^2]$), and backward gradient second moment $\tilde q_l = \operatorname{Var}(g_{l,i}) = \mathbb E[g_{l,i}^2]$ are tracked explicitly because non-centered activations like ReLU have $\mathbb E[h_{l,i}] > 0$.
