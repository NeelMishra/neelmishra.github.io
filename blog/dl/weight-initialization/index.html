    Weight Initialization in Deep Learning: Reading Guide

    September 2026 · 2 min read + interactive labs

    A connected guide with step-by-step derivations, worked examples, figures, and interactive experiments.

    Weight initialization sets the starting point of optimization. Before a single gradient step is taken, it must break symmetry across hidden units, keep forward activations and backward gradients from vanishing or exploding across depth, and match the architecture’s activations and residual paths.

    Reading order

    01
    Initialization: Break Symmetry and Keep Signals Alive

    See why identical hidden units stay identical, derive the per-layer second-moment multiplier $n_{\rm in}s_W^2$, and watch scale compound through depth.

    02
    Xavier and LeCun Initialization: Count the Inputs and Outputs

    Derive fan-in ($1/n_{\rm in}$), fan-out ($1/n_{\rm out}$), and Xavier ($2/(n_{\rm in}+n_{\rm out})$) variances, and convert them to uniform bounds.

    03
    He Initialization: Account for the Activation

    Derive the factor of $2$ for ReLU and $2/(1+a^2)$ for Leaky ReLU, and separate the second moment $\mathbb E[h^2]$ from centered variance $\operatorname{Var}(h)$.

    04
    Residual and Transformer Initialization: Start the Whole Architecture

    Derive $(1+\alpha^2)^L$ residual accumulation, $1/\sqrt{L}$ and zero-gate branch scaling, and $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ attention score normalization.

    Core notation at a glance

    • Layer widths: $n_{\rm in}$ and $n_{\rm out}$ count the inputs and outputs connected to each unit.
    • Moments: Weight variance $v = s_W^2 = \operatorname{Var}(W_{ij})$, activation second moment $q = \mathbb E[a^2]$, and centered variance $\operatorname{Var}(a) = \mathbb E[a^2] - (\mathbb E[a])^2$ are tracked separately because non-centered activations like ReLU have $\mathbb E[a] > 0$.
