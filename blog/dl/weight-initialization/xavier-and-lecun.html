    Xavier and LeCun Initialization: Count the Inputs and Outputs

    September 2026 · 4 min read + interactive lab

    Derive fan-in, fan-out, and Xavier variances from forward and backward sums, convert variance into uniform bounds, and count connections in dense and convolutional layers.

    Fan-based initialization sets a layer’s weight variance $v=\operatorname{Var}(W_{ij})$ by counting how many terms each matrix multiplication sums: LeCun preserves forward scale, fan-out preserves backward gradient scale, and Xavier (Glorot) balances the two.

    1. Forward sums ($n_{\rm in}$) vs. backward sums ($n_{\rm out}$)

    Consider a linear layer $z=Wa$ with $W\in\mathbb R^{n_{\rm out}\times n_{\rm in}}$, zero-mean independent weights of variance $v$, and uncorrelated inputs of second moment $q$:

    • Forward pass (LeCun / fan-in): Each output $z_i=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\rm in}}W_{ij}a_j$ sums $n_{\rm in}$ inputs, so $\mathbb E[z_i^2]=(n_{\rm in}v)\,q$. Setting $n_{\rm in}v=1$ gives LeCun initialization:
      $$v_{\rm fan\text{-}in}=\frac{1}{n_{\rm in}},\qquad\operatorname{std}(W_{ij})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{\rm in}}}.$$
    • Backward pass (fan-out): Each input gradient $g_{{\rm in},j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\rm out}}W_{ij}g_{{\rm out},i}$ sums $n_{\rm out}$ upstream gradients, so $\mathbb E[g_{{\rm in},j}^2]=(n_{\rm out}v)\,\mathbb E[g_{\rm out}^2]$. Preserving backward coordinate scale requires:
      $$v_{\rm fan\text{-}out}=\frac{1}{n_{\rm out}},\qquad\operatorname{std}(W_{ij})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{\rm out}}}.$$

    When $n_{\rm in}=n_{\rm out}$, both rules match. When a layer expands from $n_{\rm in}=64$ to $n_{\rm out}=256$, fan-in scaling ($v=1/64$) keeps forward scale at $1\times$ but multiplies backward gradient second moments by $256/64=4\times$; fan-out scaling ($v=1/256$) keeps backward scale at $1\times$ but shrinks forward moments to $64/256=0.25\times$.

    2. Xavier initialization balances both multipliers

    To compromise when $n_{\rm in}\ne n_{\rm out}$, Glorot and Bengio require the average of the forward multiplier $n_{\rm in}v$ and backward multiplier $n_{\rm out}v$ to equal $1$:

    $$\frac{n_{\rm in}v+n_{\rm out}v}{2}=1\implies v_{\rm Xavier}=\frac{2}{n_{\rm in}+n_{\rm out}},\qquad\operatorname{std}(W_{ij})=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{\rm in}+n_{\rm out}}}.$$

    Note that $v_{\rm Xavier}$ is the reciprocal of the average fan count (the harmonic mean of $1/n_{\rm in}$ and $1/n_{\rm out}$), not their arithmetic mean. On the $64\to 256$ layer, $v_{\rm Xavier}=2/320=0.00625$, giving forward multiplier $64(0.00625)=0.4$ and backward multiplier $256(0.00625)=1.6$, whose average is $(0.4+1.6)/2=1$.

    3. Converting variance to uniform bounds ($\sqrt{3}$ and $\sqrt{6}$)

    Why does Xavier uniform use $\sqrt{6}$ in the numerator while Xavier normal uses $\sqrt{2}$? A random variable $W_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(-a,a)$ has density $\frac{1}{2a}$ on $[-a,a]$, mean $0$, and variance

    $$v=\operatorname{Var}(W_{ij})=\frac{1}{2a}\int_{-a}^a w^2\,dw=\frac{1}{2a}\left[\frac{w^3}{3}\right]_{-a}^a=\frac{a^2}{3}\implies a=\sqrt{3v}.$$

    Multiplying any normal standard deviation $\sqrt{v}$ by $\sqrt{3}$ gives the variance-matched uniform bound $\pm a$:

    MethodTarget variance $v$Normal $\operatorname{std}=\sqrt{v}$Uniform bound $\pm a=\pm\sqrt{3v}$
    LeCun (fan-in)$1/n_{\rm in}$$1/\sqrt{n_{\rm in}}$$\pm\sqrt{3/n_{\rm in}}$
    Xavier (Glorot)$2/(n_{\rm in}+n_{\rm out})$$\sqrt{2/(n_{\rm in}+n_{\rm out})}$$\pm\sqrt{6/(n_{\rm in}+n_{\rm out})}$
    Fan-out$1/n_{\rm out}$$1/\sqrt{n_{\rm out}}$$\pm\sqrt{3/n_{\rm out}}$

    5. Fan counts for dense and convolutional layers

    • Dense layer (weight shape $[n_{\rm out}, n_{\rm in}]$): $n_{\rm in}$ is the second dimension and $n_{\rm out}$ is the first.
    • Conv2d layer (weight shape $[C_{\rm out}, C_{\rm in}/G, k_h, k_w]$ with $G$ groups): Each output pixel sums over a $k_h\times k_w$ spatial patch across $C_{\rm in}/G$ input channels, so
      $$n_{\rm in}=\frac{C_{\rm in}}{G}k_hk_w,\qquad n_{\rm out}=\frac{C_{\rm out}}{G}k_hk_w.$$

    6. Questions worth answering

    Is Xavier variance the arithmetic average of $1/n_{\rm in}$ and $1/n_{\rm out}$?

    No. Xavier averages the fan counts in the denominator—$v=2/(n_{\rm in}+n_{\rm out})$—which is the harmonic mean of $1/n_{\rm in}$ and $1/n_{\rm out}$ and guarantees $\tfrac12(n_{\rm in}v+n_{\rm out}v)=1$.

    Where does the $\sqrt{6}$ in Xavier uniform come from?

    Matching $\operatorname{Var}(\operatorname{Uniform}(-a,a))=a^2/3$ to $v_{\rm Xavier}=2/(n_{\rm in}+n_{\rm out})$ gives $a=\sqrt{3v_{\rm Xavier}}=\sqrt{6/(n_{\rm in}+n_{\rm out})}$.

    When is Xavier’s linear approximation inaccurate?

    Xavier assumes zero-centered activations with unit slope near zero ($\phi(z)\approx z$, such as linear or small-signal $\tanh$). For ReLU, which zeros out negative inputs, it loses half the signal energy per layer—handled by He initialization next.
