    He Initialization: Account for the Activation

    September 2026 · 4 min read + interactive lab

    Derive the $s_W^2=2/n_{\rm in}$ variance rule for ReLU and $s_W^2=2/((1+a^2)n_{\rm in})$ for Leaky ReLU, and see why preserving the second moment $q_l=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]$ differs from preserving variance $\operatorname{Var}(h_{l,i})$.

    Because ReLU zeros out negative preactivations, initializing a deep ReLU network with LeCun ($s_W^2=1/n_{\rm in}$) variance cuts the second moment $q_l$ in half at every layer. He (Kaiming) initialization doubles the weight variance to $s_W^2=2/n_{\rm in}$ to compensate for the discarded negative half.

    1. Derive He initialization step by step

    Consider layer $l$ with preactivation $z_l=W_lh_{l-1}$ and ReLU activation $h_l=\phi(z_l)=\max(0,z_l)$, where $W_l\in\mathbb R^{n_{\rm out}\times n_{\rm in}}$ has independent zero-mean weights $\mathbb E[W_{l,ij}]=0$ of variance $s_W^2=\operatorname{Var}(W_{l,ij})=\mathbb E[W_{l,ij}^2]$, independent of incoming activations $a_j=h_{l-1,j}$ with second moment $q_{l-1}=\mathbb E[h_{l-1,j}^2]$:

    • Step 1 — Linear sum scales $q_{l-1}$ by $n_{\rm in}\,s_W^2$: For each unit $i$, $z_{l,i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\rm in}}W_{l,ij}h_{l-1,j}$ has mean $\mathbb E[z_{l,i}]=0$ (even though $\mathbb E[h_{l-1,j}]>0$) and preactivation variance:
      $$\sigma_z^2=\operatorname{Var}(z_{l,i})=\mathbb E[z_{l,i}^2]=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\rm in}}\mathbb E[W_{l,ij}^2]\,\mathbb E[h_{l-1,j}^2]=(n_{\rm in}\,s_W^2)\,q_{l-1}.$$
    • Step 2 — ReLU halves the second moment of a symmetric preactivation: Because $W_{l,ij}$ is distributed symmetrically around $0$, $z_{l,i}$ has a symmetric density $p(z)=p(-z)$ around $0$. Since $h_{l,i}=\max(0,z_{l,i})$ equals $z_{l,i}$ for $z_{l,i}>0$ and $0$ for $z_{l,i}\le 0$, only the positive half survives:
      $$q_l=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]=\int_{-\infty}^0 0^2\,p(z)\,dz+\int_0^\infty z^2p(z)\,dz=\frac12\int_{-\infty}^\infty z^2p(z)\,dz=\frac12\,\mathbb E[z_{l,i}^2]=\left(\frac12\,n_{\rm in}\,s_W^2\right)q_{l-1}.$$
    • Step 3 — Match the per-layer multiplier to $1$: Requiring $q_l=q_{l-1}$ across depth ($q_L=(\frac12 n_{\rm in}s_W^2)^L q_0=q_0$) sets $\frac12\,n_{\rm in}\,s_W^2=1$, yielding He (Kaiming) fan-in initialization:
      $$\frac12\,n_{\rm in}\,s_W^2=1\implies s_{W,\text{He}}^2=\frac{2}{n_{\rm in}},\qquad s_W=\operatorname{std}(W_{l,ij})=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{\rm in}}},\qquad a_{\rm uniform}=\sqrt{3}\,s_{W,\text{He}}=\sqrt{\frac{6}{n_{\rm in}}}.$$

    Backward pass (He fan-out): For $g_{l-1,i}=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\rm out}}W_{l,ki}\,g_{l,k}\,\phi'(z_{l,k})$, the derivative $\phi'(z)=\operatorname{ReLU}'(z)=\mathbf 1_{z>0}$ is $1$ on the positive half ($z_{l,k}>0$) and $0$ on the negative half ($z_{l,k}\le 0$), so $\mathbb E[\phi'(z_{l,k})^2]=\int_0^\infty 1^2\,p(z)\,dz=\frac12$. Summing the $n_{\rm out}$ independent terms gives the backward gradient second moment $\tilde q_{l-1}=\mathbb E[g_{l-1,i}^2]$:

    $$\tilde q_{l-1}=\operatorname{Var}(g_{l-1,i})=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\rm out}}\mathbb E[W_{l,ki}^2]\,\mathbb E\bigl[\phi'(z_{l,k})^2\bigr]\,\mathbb E[g_{l,k}^2]=\left(\frac12\,n_{\rm out}\,s_W^2\right)\tilde q_l\implies s_{W,\text{He,fan-out}}^2=\frac{2}{n_{\rm out}}.$$

    2. Why post-ReLU variance is $0.682$, not $1$ (and why $q_l$ is still $\frac12\sigma_z^2$)

    If you measure h.var() after a He-initialized ReLU layer with unit-second-moment inputs ($q_{l-1}=1$), you get $\approx 0.682$ instead of $1.0$. Nothing is broken: He initialization preserves the uncentered second moment $q_l=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]=1$, which already includes the squared positive mean $(\mathbb E[h_{l,i}])^2$ alongside $\operatorname{Var}(h_{l,i})$.

    Specifically, because $z_{l,i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\rm in}}W_{l,ij}h_{l-1,j}$ sums $n_{\rm in}$ independent zero-mean terms, the Central Limit Theorem gives $z_{l,i}\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_z^2)$ with Gaussian density $p(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_z}e^{-z^2/(2\sigma_z^2)}$ (which is where $\pi$ enters). We can compute $\mathbb E[h_{l,i}]$ and $\operatorname{Var}(h_{l,i})$ explicitly:

    • 1. Positive mean $\mathbb E[h_{l,i}]$ and squared mean $(\mathbb E[h_{l,i}])^2$: Since $h_{l,i}=\max(0,z_{l,i})$ is $0$ for $z\le 0$, we integrate $z\,p(z)$ over $(0,\infty)$ using $u=\frac{z^2}{2\sigma_z^2}$ ($du=\frac{z}{\sigma_z^2}\,dz\implies z\,dz=\sigma_z^2\,du$, so $\sigma_z^2$ cancels one $\sigma_z$ in the denominator):
      $$\mathbb E[h_{l,i}]=\int_0^\infty z\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_z}e^{-z^2/(2\sigma_z^2)}\,dz=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_z}\int_0^\infty e^{-u}\bigl(\sigma_z^2\,du\bigr)=\frac{\sigma_z}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Bigl[-e^{-u}\Bigr]_0^\infty=\frac{\sigma_z}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\implies\bigl(\mathbb E[h_{l,i}]\bigr)^2=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sigma_z^2\approx 0.159\,\sigma_z^2.$$
    • 2. Centered variance $\operatorname{Var}(h_{l,i})$: Because the mean shifted from $0$ up to $\sigma_z/\sqrt{2\pi}$, subtracting $(\mathbb E[h_{l,i}])^2=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sigma_z^2$ from the uncentered second moment $q_l=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]=\frac12\sigma_z^2$ gives:
      $$\operatorname{Var}(h_{l,i})=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]-\bigl(\mathbb E[h_{l,i}]\bigr)^2=\frac12\sigma_z^2-\frac{1}{2\pi}\sigma_z^2=\left(\frac12-\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)\sigma_z^2\approx 0.341\,\sigma_z^2.$$
    • 3. How layer $l+1$ recombines both pieces ($q_l=1\to\sigma_{z,l+1}^2=2\to q_{l+1}=1$): Under He initialization ($s_W^2=2/n_{\rm in}$) with $q_{l-1}=1$, the preactivation variance is $\sigma_z^2=n_{\rm in}s_W^2q_{l-1}=2$. Plugging $\sigma_z^2=2$ into the two formulas above gives:
      $$\underbrace{q_l=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]}_{\frac{1}{2}(2)=1}=\underbrace{\operatorname{Var}(h_{l,i})}_{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)(2)=1-\frac{1}{\pi}\approx 0.682}+\underbrace{\bigl(\mathbb E[h_{l,i}]\bigr)^2}_{\frac{1}{2\pi}(2)=\frac{1}{\pi}\approx 0.318}.$$
      When layer $l+1$ multiplies $a_j=h_{l,j}$ by zero-mean weights $W_{l+1}$, its preactivation variance depends on the full second moment $\mathbb E[a_j^2]=\operatorname{Var}(a_j)+(\mathbb E[a_j])^2=0.682+0.318=1$:
      $$\sigma_{z,l+1}^2=\operatorname{Var}(z_{l+1,i})=n_{\rm in}\,s_W^2\Bigl(\operatorname{Var}(h_{l,j})+\bigl(\mathbb E[h_{l,j}]\bigr)^2\Bigr)=n_{\rm in}\left(\frac{2}{n_{\rm in}}\right)(1)=2\implies q_{l+1}=\frac12\,\sigma_{z,l+1}^2=1.$$

    3. Leaky ReLU and the slope-aware gain

    Leaky ReLU keeps negative values scaled by $a$: $\phi(z)=z$ for $z\ge 0$ and $\phi(z)=az$ for $z<0$. Integrating $\phi(z)^2$ over the negative and positive halves of a symmetric preactivation distribution $p(z)=p(-z)$ gives:

    $$q_l=\mathbb E[\phi(z_{l,i})^2]=\int_{-\infty}^0(az)^2p(z)\,dz+\int_0^\infty z^2p(z)\,dz=a^2\left(\frac12\sigma_z^2\right)+\frac12\sigma_z^2=\frac{1+a^2}{2}\sigma_z^2=\left(\frac{1+a^2}{2}n_{\rm in}\,s_W^2\right)q_{l-1}.$$

    Setting the per-layer multiplier $\frac{1+a^2}{2}n_{\rm in}\,s_W^2=1$ (and similarly $\mathbb E[\phi'(z_l)^2]=\frac{1+a^2}{2}$ in the backward pass) gives:

    $$s_W^2=\frac{2}{(1+a^2)n_{\rm in}},\qquad s_W=\sqrt{\frac{2}{(1+a^2)n_{\rm in}}},\qquad\text{gain}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+a^2}}.$$

    Check the two endpoints: at $a=0$ (ReLU), $\text{gain}=\sqrt{2}$ and $s_W^2=2/n_{\rm in}$; at $a=1$ (linear), $\text{gain}=1$ and $s_W^2=1/n_{\rm in}$ (LeCun scaling). Unlike ReLU and Leaky ReLU, smooth activations such as GELU and SiLU are not scale-homogeneous ($\phi(cz)\ne c\phi(z)$), so their exact moment ratio depends slightly on $\sigma_z^2$, though $\text{gain}\approx\sqrt{2}$ or architecture-level residual scaling is standard in practice.

    5. Implementation

    # He (Kaiming) fan-in normal initialization for a ReLU layer
torch.nn.init.kaiming_normal_(layer.weight, mode="fan_in", nonlinearity="relu")

# Leaky ReLU with negative slope a = 0.2 (gain = sqrt(2 / (1 + 0.2^2)))
torch.nn.init.kaiming_normal_(layer.weight, a=0.2, mode="fan_in", nonlinearity="leaky_relu")

    6. Questions worth answering

    Why does h.var() equal $\approx 0.68$ instead of $1.0$ after the first He-initialized ReLU layer?

    Because He initialization with $q_{l-1}=1$ produces preactivation variance $\sigma_z^2=2$ and activation second moment $q_l=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]=\frac12\sigma_z^2=1$, which splits into squared positive mean $(\mathbb E[h_{l,i}])^2=1/\pi\approx 0.318$ and centered variance $\operatorname{Var}(h_{l,i})=1-1/\pi\approx 0.682$.

    Why is the standard-deviation gain $\sqrt{2}$ when ReLU removes half the distribution?

    ReLU halves the second moment $q_l=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]$, which scales linearly with weight variance $s_W^2$. Doubling $s_W^2$ requires multiplying the standard deviation $s_W$ by $\sqrt{2}$.

    Should the final classification head after a ReLU trunk also use He gain $\sqrt{2}$?

    No. Gain $\sqrt{2}$ compensates for a ReLU applied to that layer’s output. A linear output head has no ReLU after it, so fan-in ($g=1$), Xavier, or small-logit initialization is appropriate.
