    He Initialization: Account for the Activation

    September 2026 · 4 min read + interactive lab

    Derive the $s_W^2=2/n_{\rm in}$ variance rule for ReLU and $s_W^2=2/((1+a^2)n_{\rm in})$ for Leaky ReLU, and see why preserving the second moment $q_l=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]$ differs from preserving variance $\operatorname{Var}(h_{l,i})$.

    Because ReLU zeros out negative preactivations, initializing a deep ReLU network with LeCun ($s_W^2=1/n_{\rm in}$) variance cuts the second moment $q_l$ in half at every layer. He (Kaiming) initialization doubles the weight variance to $s_W^2=2/n_{\rm in}$ to compensate for the discarded negative half.

    1. Derive He initialization in two steps

    Consider layer $l$ with preactivation $z_l=W_lh_{l-1}$ and ReLU activation $h_l=\max(0,z_l)$, where $W_{l,ij}$ are independent with mean $0$ and variance $s_W^2=\operatorname{Var}(W_{l,ij})=\mathbb E[W_{l,ij}^2]$:

    • Step 1 — ReLU halves the second moment of a symmetric preactivation: Because $W_l$ is symmetric around $0$, $z_{l,i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\rm in}}W_{l,ij}h_{l-1,j}$ has a symmetric distribution $p(z)=p(-z)$ with variance $\sigma_z^2=\operatorname{Var}(z_{l,i})=\mathbb E[z_{l,i}^2]=(n_{\rm in}\,s_W^2)\,q_{l-1}$. Since $h_{l,i}=z_{l,i}$ for $z_{l,i}>0$ and $0$ for $z_{l,i}\le 0$, only the positive half survives:
      $$q_l=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]=\int_0^\infty z^2p(z)\,dz=\frac12\int_{-\infty}^\infty z^2p(z)\,dz=\frac12\,\mathbb E[z_{l,i}^2]=\left(\frac12\,n_{\rm in}\,s_W^2\right)q_{l-1}.$$
    • Step 2 — Match the per-layer multiplier to $1$: Setting $\frac12\,n_{\rm in}\,s_W^2=1$ so $q_l=q_{l-1}$ yields He initialization:
      $$s_{W,\text{He}}^2=\frac{2}{n_{\rm in}},\qquad s_W=\operatorname{std}(W_{l,ij})=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{\rm in}}},\qquad a_{\rm uniform}=\sqrt{3}\,s_{W,\text{He}}=\sqrt{\frac{6}{n_{\rm in}}}.$$

    Backward pass (He fan-out): For $g_{l-1,i}=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\rm out}}W_{l,ki}\,g_{l,k}\,\phi'(z_{l,k})$, the derivative $\phi'(z)=\operatorname{ReLU}'(z)=\mathbf 1_{z>0}$ is $1$ on the positive half of the symmetric preactivation distribution and $0$ on the negative half, so $\mathbb E[\phi'(z_l)^2]=\frac12$. Summing the $n_{\rm out}$ independent terms gives the backward gradient second moment $\tilde q_{l-1}=\mathbb E[g_{l-1,i}^2]$:

    $$\tilde q_{l-1}=\operatorname{Var}(g_{l-1,i})=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\rm out}}\mathbb E[W_{l,ki}^2]\,\mathbb E\bigl[\phi'(z_{l,k})^2\bigr]\,\mathbb E[g_{l,k}^2]=\left(\frac12\,n_{\rm out}\,s_W^2\right)\tilde q_l\implies s_{W,\text{He,fan-out}}^2=\frac{2}{n_{\rm out}}.$$

    2. Why post-ReLU variance is $0.682$, not $1$

    If you measure `h.var()` after a He-initialized ReLU layer with unit-second-moment inputs ($q_{l-1}=1$), you get $\approx 0.682$ instead of $1.0$. Nothing is broken: He initialization preserves the uncentered second moment $q_l=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]=1$, whereas ReLU shifts the mean $\mathbb E[h_{l,i}]$ above zero.

    Specifically, for $z_{l,i}\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_z^2)$, integrating over the positive half gives:

    $$\mathbb E[h_{l,i}]=\int_0^\infty z\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_z^2}}e^{-z^2/(2\sigma_z^2)}\,dz=\frac{\sigma_z}{\sqrt{2\pi}},\qquad\operatorname{Var}(h_{l,i})=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]-\bigl(\mathbb E[h_{l,i}]\bigr)^2=\left(\frac12-\frac1{2\pi}\right)\sigma_z^2.$$

    Under He initialization ($s_W^2=2/n_{\rm in}$ and $q_{l-1}=1$), the preactivation variance is $\sigma_z^2=n_{\rm in}s_W^2q_{l-1}=2$, so $q_l=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]=\frac12\sigma_z^2=1$ splits into squared mean $(\mathbb E[h_{l,i}])^2=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sigma_z^2=\frac{1}{\pi}\approx 0.318$ and centered variance $\operatorname{Var}(h_{l,i})=\bigl(\frac12-\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigr)\sigma_z^2=1-\frac{1}{\pi}\approx 0.682$. When the next layer multiplies $a_j=h_{l,j}$ by zero-mean weights $W_{l+1}$, its preactivation variance $\operatorname{Var}(z_{l+1,i})=n_{\rm in}\,s_W^2\,\mathbb E[a_j^2]=n_{\rm in}\,s_W^2\bigl(\operatorname{Var}(a_j)+(\mathbb E[a_j])^2\bigr)$ recombines both pieces back into $q_l=1$.

    3. Leaky ReLU and the slope-aware gain

    Leaky ReLU keeps negative values scaled by $a$: $\phi(z)=z$ for $z\ge 0$ and $\phi(z)=az$ for $z<0$. Now the positive half contributes $\frac12\sigma_z^2$ and the negative half contributes $\frac{a^2}{2}\sigma_z^2$ to the second moment $q_l$:

    $$q_l=\mathbb E[\phi(z_{l,i})^2]=\frac{1+a^2}{2}\sigma_z^2=\left(\frac{1+a^2}{2}n_{\rm in}\,s_W^2\right)q_{l-1}\implies s_W^2=\frac{2}{(1+a^2)n_{\rm in}},\qquad\text{gain}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+a^2}}.$$

    Check the two endpoints: at $a=0$ (ReLU), $\text{gain}=\sqrt{2}$ and $s_W^2=2/n_{\rm in}$; at $a=1$ (linear), $\text{gain}=1$ and $s_W^2=1/n_{\rm in}$ (LeCun scaling). Unlike ReLU and Leaky ReLU, smooth activations such as GELU and SiLU are not scale-homogeneous ($\phi(cz)\ne c\phi(z)$), so their exact moment ratio depends slightly on $\sigma_z^2$, though $\text{gain}\approx\sqrt{2}$ or architecture-level residual scaling is standard in practice.

    5. Implementation

    # He (Kaiming) fan-in normal initialization for a ReLU layer
torch.nn.init.kaiming_normal_(layer.weight, mode="fan_in", nonlinearity="relu")

# Leaky ReLU with negative slope a = 0.2 (gain = sqrt(2 / (1 + 0.2^2)))
torch.nn.init.kaiming_normal_(layer.weight, a=0.2, mode="fan_in", nonlinearity="leaky_relu")

    6. Questions worth answering

    Why does `h.var()` equal $\approx 0.68$ instead of $1.0$ after the first He-initialized ReLU layer?

    Because $q_l=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]=1$ includes the squared positive mean $(\mathbb E[h_{l,i}])^2=1/\pi\approx 0.318$, leaving centered variance $\operatorname{Var}(h_{l,i})=1-1/\pi\approx 0.682$.

    Why is the standard-deviation gain $\sqrt{2}$ when ReLU removes half the distribution?

    ReLU halves the second moment $q_l=\mathbb E[h_{l,i}^2]$, which scales with weight variance $s_W^2$. Doubling $s_W^2$ requires multiplying the standard deviation $s_W$ by $\sqrt{2}$.

    Should the final classification head after a ReLU trunk also use He gain $\sqrt{2}$?

    No. Gain $\sqrt{2}$ compensates for a ReLU applied to that layer’s output. A linear output head has no ReLU after it, so fan-in ($g=1$), Xavier, or small-logit initialization is appropriate.
