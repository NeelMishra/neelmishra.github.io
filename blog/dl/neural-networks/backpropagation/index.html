    Backpropagation: Reverse-Mode Differentiation & Complete Derivations

    October 2026 · 6 min read

    If a neural network has $P = 10^9$ parameters and a scalar loss $\mathcal{L}(\theta) \in \mathbb{R}$, how do we compute all $10^9$ partial derivatives $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta_p}$? Perturbing one weight at a time via finite differences requires $O(P)$ full forward passes—a billion times slower than inference. Backpropagation (reverse-mode automatic differentiation) exploits dynamic programming on the multivariable chain rule to compute the entire gradient vector $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L} \in \mathbb{R}^P$ in a single reverse sweep costing only $\approx 2\times$ the FLOPs of one forward pass.

    1. Why $O(1)$ reverse sweeps beat $O(P)$ perturbations

    Because all $P$ parameters in an $L$-layer network funnel into a single scalar output $\mathcal{L} \in \mathbb{R}$, every weight $W_{ij}^{(\ell)}$ in layer $\ell$ shares the exact same downstream computation from layer $\ell$ to layer $L$. By defining the error sensitivity (adjoint) vector

    $$\delta^{(\ell)} \triangleq \nabla_{z^{(\ell)}} \mathcal{L} = \begin{bmatrix} \dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_1^{(\ell)}} & \cdots & \dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_{d_\ell}^{(\ell)}} \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell},$$

    and propagating $\delta^{(L)} \to \delta^{(L-1)} \to \dots \to \delta^{(1)}$ backward once from right to left, every weight gradient $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W_{ij}^{(\ell)}} = \delta_i^{(\ell)} a_j^{(\ell-1)}$ is obtained locally by multiplying the outgoing sensitivity $\delta_i^{(\ell)}$ from the backward pass with the cached incoming activation $a_j^{(\ell-1)}$ from the forward pass.

    2. Five Chapters: From Chain Rule to Numerical Verification

    01
    Computational Graphs, Forward (JVP) vs. Reverse Mode (VJP), Checkpointing & Hessians

    Derive the multivariable chain rule on general directed acyclic graphs, write the total Jacobian chain $J_f = J_L \cdots J_1$, prove via matrix-chain multiplication complexity why reverse-mode VJP is $O(P)$ faster than forward-mode JVP for scalar losses, and derive $O(\sqrt{L})$ activation checkpointing and Pearlmutter’s $O(P)$ Hessian-Vector Product ($Hv$).

    02
    The Four Fundamental Equations of Backpropagation (BP1–BP4): Scalar & Index Derivation

    Derive BP1, BP2, BP3, and BP4 component-by-component using explicit summation indices $z_i^{(\ell)} = \sum_{j=1}^{d_{\ell-1}} W_{ij}^{(\ell)} a_j^{(\ell-1)} + b_i^{(\ell)}$, showing why the matrix transpose $(W^{(\ell+1)})^\top$ and outer product $\delta^{(\ell)} (a^{(\ell-1)})^\top$ emerge directly from the chain rule.

    03
    Matrix Calculus & Mini-Batch Tensor Derivation: Differentials and the 3-GEMM Rule

    Derive the mini-batch backward pass for $Z^{(\ell)} = A^{(\ell-1)}(W^{(\ell)})^\top + \mathbf{1}_B (b^{(\ell)})^\top$ using matrix differentials $d\mathcal{L} = \langle G^{(\ell)}, dZ^{(\ell)} \rangle_F$, proving why every linear layer executes 1 GEMM forward ($2BP$ FLOPs) and 2 GEMMs backward ($4BP$ FLOPs).

    04
    Output-Layer Adjoints (BP1): MSE, BCE, the $C \times C$ Softmax Jacobian & Exponential Families

    Derive $\delta^{(L)} = \hat{y} - y$ for Linear+MSE, Sigmoid+BCE (showing the $\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})$ singularity cancellation), the full $C \times C$ Softmax Jacobian $\operatorname{diag}(\hat{p}) - \hat{p}\hat{p}^\top$ contracted with soft cross-entropy targets, and the general exponential-family cumulant theorem.

    05
    Complete Worked Numerical Backprop By Hand, Gradient Checking & NumPy Implementation

    Trace every floating-point number of a 2-layer network forward pass, backward pass, and 1-step parameter update by hand; derive the $O(\varepsilon^2)$ truncation vs. $O(\epsilon_{\text{mach}}/\varepsilon)$ roundoff tradeoff of centered finite-difference gradient checking; and run a self-contained NumPy verification script.
