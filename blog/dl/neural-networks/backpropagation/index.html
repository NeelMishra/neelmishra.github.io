    Backpropagation: Reverse-Mode Differentiation & Complete Derivations

    October 2026 · 4 min read

    Nudging one weight at a time to measure its effect on the loss takes one forward pass per parameter. Backpropagation applies the chain rule from the output back to the input, computing every weight gradient in a single reverse pass.

    1. How the backward pass reuses work

    Every weight in layer $\ell$ affects the scalar loss $\mathcal{L} \in \mathbb{R}$ through that layer’s pre-activations $z^{(\ell)}$. Defining the layer sensitivity

    $$\delta^{(\ell)} \triangleq \nabla_{z^{(\ell)}} \mathcal{L} = \begin{bmatrix} \dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_1^{(\ell)}} & \cdots & \dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_{d_\ell}^{(\ell)}} \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell},$$

    and stepping backward from $\delta^{(L)}$ to $\delta^{(1)}$ gives every weight gradient locally as $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W_{ij}^{(\ell)}} = \delta_i^{(\ell)} a_j^{(\ell-1)}$.
