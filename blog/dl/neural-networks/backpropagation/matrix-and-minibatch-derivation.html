    3. Matrix Calculus & Mini-Batch Tensor Derivation: Differentials, Outer Products, and the 3-GEMM Rule

    October 2026 · 16 min read

    In Chapter 2 we derived BP1–BP4 for a single vector input $x \in \mathbb{R}^{d_0}$ using scalar indices. Modern deep learning frameworks (PyTorch, JAX, TensorFlow) operate on row-major mini-batches $X \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_0}$. Rather than writing triple-index loops over batch index $b$ and spatial indices $(i, j)$, we can derive all mini-batch gradients in three lines of algebra using matrix differentials and the Frobenius inner product.

    1. The Frobenius inner product and the first-order matrix differential identity

    For any two matrices $U, V \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ of the exact same shape, their Frobenius inner product $\langle U, V \rangle_F$ is the sum of element-wise products, which equals the trace of $U^\top V$:

    $$\langle U, V \rangle_F \triangleq \sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=1}^n U_{ij} V_{ij} = \operatorname{Tr}\!\bigl(U^\top V\bigr) = \operatorname{Tr}\!\bigl(V^\top U\bigr).$$

    The Frobenius inner product satisfies three simple algebraic rules that make matrix calculus effortless:

    1. Cyclic trace / transpose adjoint identity: For conformable matrices $A, B, C$, $$\langle A, B C \rangle_F = \operatorname{Tr}\!\bigl(A^\top B C\bigr) = \operatorname{Tr}\!\bigl((B^\top A)^\top C\bigr) = \bigl\langle B^\top A, C \bigr\rangle_F = \bigl\langle A C^\top, B \bigr\rangle_F.$$
    2. Element-wise Hadamard identity: For matrices $A, B, C \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ of identical dimensions, $$\langle A, B \odot C \rangle_F = \sum_{i,j} A_{ij} B_{ij} C_{ij} = \langle A \odot B, C \rangle_F.$$
    3. Fundamental theorem of matrix differentials: If $\mathcal{L} : \mathbb{R}^{m \times n} \to \mathbb{R}$ is a differentiable scalar function of a matrix $M \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, then the total first-order differential $d\mathcal{L}$ for an arbitrary perturbation $dM \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ uniquely identifies the gradient matrix $\nabla_M \mathcal{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ (having the exact same shape as $M$): $$\boxed{d\mathcal{L} = \sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=1}^n \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial M_{ij}} \, dM_{ij} = \bigl\langle \nabla_M \mathcal{L}, \, dM \bigr\rangle_F.}$$

    This gives us a universal recipe: to find $\nabla_M \mathcal{L}$, take the differential $d\mathcal{L}$, use the transpose adjoint and Hadamard identities to isolate $dM$ on the right side of $\langle \,\cdot\,, dM \rangle_F$, and read off the left argument!

    2. Complete matrix-differential derivation of mini-batch backpropagation

    Recall from Feed-Forward Chapter 1 that for a mini-batch of $B$ examples, activations are stacked row-wise as $A^{(\ell-1)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_{\ell-1}}$ and pre-activations as $Z^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_\ell}$:

    $$Z^{(\ell)} = A^{(\ell-1)} \bigl(W^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top + \mathbf{1}_B \bigl(b^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_\ell}, \qquad A^{(\ell)} = \phi_\ell\!\bigl(Z^{(\ell)}\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_\ell},$$

    where $W^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}}$, $b^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}$, and $\mathbf{1}_B = [1, 1, \dots, 1]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 1}$. The empirical mini-batch loss is the average over the $B$ rows:

    $$\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{b=1}^B \ell\bigl(a_{b,:}^{(L)}, y_{b,:}\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}.$$

    Define the mini-batch pre-activation gradient matrix $G^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_\ell}$ at layer $\ell$:

    $$G^{(\ell)} \triangleq \nabla_{Z^{(\ell)}} \mathcal{L} = \begin{bmatrix} \bigl(\delta^{(1,\ell)}\bigr)^\top \\ \vdots \\ \bigl(\delta^{(B,\ell)}\bigr)^\top \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_\ell}, \qquad \text{so that } d\mathcal{L} = \bigl\langle G^{(\ell)}, \, dZ^{(\ell)} \bigr\rangle_F.$$

    Step 1: Taking the differential of the affine layer $Z^{(\ell)} = A^{(\ell-1)} (W^{(\ell)})^\top + \mathbf{1}_B (b^{(\ell)})^\top$

    By the product (Leibniz) rule of differentials, perturbing $A^{(\ell-1)}$, $W^{(\ell)}$, and $b^{(\ell)}$ simultaneously produces:

    $$dZ^{(\ell)} = dA^{(\ell-1)} \bigl(W^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top + A^{(\ell-1)} \bigl(dW^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top + \mathbf{1}_B \bigl(db^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top.$$

    Substitute $dZ^{(\ell)}$ into $d\mathcal{L} = \bigl\langle G^{(\ell)}, dZ^{(\ell)} \bigr\rangle_F$ and split by linearity of the inner product:

    $$d\mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\bigl\langle G^{(\ell)}, \, dA^{(\ell-1)} (W^{(\ell)})^\top \bigr\rangle_F}_{\text{(I) activation term}} + \underbrace{\bigl\langle G^{(\ell)}, \, A^{(\ell-1)} (dW^{(\ell)})^\top \bigr\rangle_F}_{\text{(II) weight term}} + \underbrace{\bigl\langle G^{(\ell)}, \, \mathbf{1}_B (db^{(\ell)})^\top \bigr\rangle_F}_{\text{(III) bias term}}.$$

    Now apply the cyclic trace / transpose adjoint identity $\langle X, Y Z^\top \rangle_F = \langle X Z, Y \rangle_F$ to each of the three terms:

    1. Term (I) — Gradient with respect to incoming activations $A^{(\ell-1)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_{\ell-1}}$: $$\bigl\langle G^{(\ell)}, \, dA^{(\ell-1)} \bigl(W^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top \bigr\rangle_F = \bigl\langle G^{(\ell)} W^{(\ell)}, \, dA^{(\ell-1)} \bigr\rangle_F \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \boxed{\nabla_{A^{(\ell-1)}} \mathcal{L} = G^{(\ell)} W^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_{\ell-1}}.}$$
    2. Term (II) — Gradient with respect to weight matrix $W^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}}$ (Mini-Batch BP4): $$\bigl\langle G^{(\ell)}, \, A^{(\ell-1)} \bigl(dW^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top \bigr\rangle_F = \operatorname{Tr}\!\Bigl(\bigl(G^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top A^{(\ell-1)} \bigl(dW^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top\Bigr) = \Bigl\langle \bigl(G^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top A^{(\ell-1)}, \, dW^{(\ell)} \Bigr\rangle_F \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \boxed{\nabla_{W^{(\ell)}} \mathcal{L} = \bigl(G^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top A^{(\ell-1)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}}.}$$
    3. Term (III) — Gradient with respect to bias vector $b^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}$ (Mini-Batch BP3): $$\bigl\langle G^{(\ell)}, \, \mathbf{1}_B \bigl(db^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top \bigr\rangle_F = \Bigl\langle \bigl(G^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top \mathbf{1}_B, \, db^{(\ell)} \Bigr\rangle_F \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \boxed{\nabla_{b^{(\ell)}} \mathcal{L} = \bigl(G^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top \mathbf{1}_B = \sum_{b=1}^B \bigl(G_{b,:}^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}.}$$

    Step 2: Propagating through the element-wise nonlinearity $A^{(\ell-1)} = \phi_{\ell-1}\bigl(Z^{(\ell-1)}\bigr)$ (Mini-Batch BP2)

    Because $\phi_{\ell-1}$ acts element-wise on $Z^{(\ell-1)}$, its differential is $dA^{(\ell-1)} = \phi_{\ell-1}'\bigl(Z^{(\ell-1)}\bigr) \odot dZ^{(\ell-1)}$. Substituting this into Term (I) and using the Hadamard identity $\langle U, V \odot W \rangle_F = \langle U \odot V, W \rangle_F$ gives:

    $$d\mathcal{L}\big|_{Z^{(\ell-1)}} = \bigl\langle \nabla_{A^{(\ell-1)}} \mathcal{L}, \, \phi_{\ell-1}'\bigl(Z^{(\ell-1)}\bigr) \odot dZ^{(\ell-1)} \bigr\rangle_F = \Bigl\langle \bigl(G^{(\ell)} W^{(\ell)}\bigr) \odot \phi_{\ell-1}'\bigl(Z^{(\ell-1)}\bigr), \, dZ^{(\ell-1)} \Bigr\rangle_F.$$

    Reading off the left side yields the mini-batch recursion BP2:

    $$\boxed{G^{(\ell-1)} = \nabla_{Z^{(\ell-1)}} \mathcal{L} = \bigl(G^{(\ell)} W^{(\ell)}\bigr) \odot \phi_{\ell-1}'\!\bigl(Z^{(\ell-1)}\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_{\ell-1}}.}$$

    3. Connecting single-sample outer products to the mini-batch GEMM $(G^{(\ell)})^\top A^{(\ell-1)}$

    How does the mini-batch matrix product $\nabla_{W^{(\ell)}} \mathcal{L} = (G^{(\ell)})^\top A^{(\ell-1)}$ relate to the single-sample outer product $\delta^{(\ell)}(a^{(\ell-1)})^\top$ from Chapter 2? Recall that multiplying a $d_\ell \times B$ matrix $(G^{(\ell)})^\top$ by a $B \times d_{\ell-1}$ matrix $A^{(\ell-1)}$ can be expanded as a sum of $B$ rank-1 outer products over the shared inner dimension $b \in \{1, \dots, B\}$:

    $$\nabla_{W^{(\ell)}} \mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\bigl(G^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top}_{d_\ell \times B} \underbrace{A^{(\ell-1)}}_{B \times d_{\ell-1}} = \sum_{b=1}^B \underbrace{\bigl(G_{b,:}^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top}_{d_\ell \times 1} \underbrace{A_{b,:}^{(\ell-1)}}_{1 \times d_{\ell-1}} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{b=1}^B \delta^{(b,\ell)} \bigl(a^{(b,\ell-1)}\bigr)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}},$$

    where $G_{b,:}^{(\ell)} = \frac{1}{B}\bigl(\delta^{(b,\ell)}\bigr)^\top$ includes the $\frac{1}{B}$ scaling from the mean loss $\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \ell_b$. Instead of materializing $B$ separate $d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}$ outer-product matrices in memory and summing them, the GPU executes one dense matrix-matrix multiplication (GEMM) $(G^{(\ell)})^\top A^{(\ell-1)}$ that accumulates the sum across the batch dimension inside Tensor Core registers!

    4. The 3-GEMM Rule: why the backward pass costs $2\times$ the forward pass

    Look at the dense matrix multiplications executed by layer $\ell$ across one complete training step (forward + backward):

    Pass Operation Matrix Formula Left Operand Shape Right Operand Shape Output Shape FLOPs
    1. Forward pass Compute pre-activations $Z^{(\ell)}$ $A^{(\ell-1)} \bigl(W^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top$ $B \times d_{\ell-1}$ $d_{\ell-1} \times d_\ell$ $B \times d_\ell$ $2 B d_{\ell-1} d_\ell$
    2. Backward (Data grad) Propagate sensitivity $\nabla_{A^{(\ell-1)}} \mathcal{L}$ $G^{(\ell)} W^{(\ell)}$ $B \times d_\ell$ $d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}$ $B \times d_{\ell-1}$ $2 B d_\ell d_{\ell-1}$
    3. Backward (Weight grad) Accumulate weight gradient $\nabla_{W^{(\ell)}} \mathcal{L}$ $\bigl(G^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top A^{(\ell-1)}$ $d_\ell \times B$ $B \times d_{\ell-1}$ $d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}$ $2 B d_\ell d_{\ell-1}$

    Every linear layer performs 1 GEMM during the forward pass ($2 B d_{\ell-1} d_\ell$ FLOPs) and 2 GEMMs during the backward pass ($2 B d_{\ell-1} d_\ell$ for the input-activation gradient + $2 B d_{\ell-1} d_\ell$ for the weight gradient = $4 B d_{\ell-1} d_\ell$ FLOPs). Summing over all layers $\ell = 1, \dots, L$:

    $$\boxed{\operatorname{FLOPs}_{\text{backward}} \approx 2 \times \operatorname{FLOPs}_{\text{forward}}, \qquad \operatorname{FLOPs}_{\text{train step}} = \operatorname{FLOPs}_{\text{forward}} + \operatorname{FLOPs}_{\text{backward}} \approx 3 \times \operatorname{FLOPs}_{\text{forward}} \approx 6 B P.}$$

    This $6BP$ rule ($2BP$ forward + $4BP$ backward per token/sample, where $P = \sum_\ell d_{\ell-1} d_\ell$ is the parameter count) is the universal compute-budget formula used across deep learning scaling laws (such as Kaplan et al. and Hoffmann et al. / Chinchilla).
