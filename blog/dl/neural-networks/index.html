    Neural Networks: Foundations, Feed-Forward Pass, and Backpropagation

    October 2026 · 4 min read

    A neural network stacks layers of linear weights and nonlinear activations. Training alternates two steps: a feed-forward pass that computes predictions and caches intermediate states, and a backpropagation pass that applies the chain rule in reverse to compute every weight’s gradient in one sweep.

    1. General $L$-Layer Architecture & Standard Notation

    An $L$-layer neural network maps an input vector $a^{(0)} = x \in \mathbb{R}^{d_0}$ through hidden layers $\ell = 1, \dots, L-1$ of widths $d_1, \dots, d_{L-1}$ to an output layer $\hat{y} = a^{(L)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_L}$:

    At any layer $\ell \in \{1, \dots, L\}$, the feed-forward pass computes pre-activations $z^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}$ and activations $a^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}$:

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{Scalar form:} &\quad z_i^{(\ell)} = \sum_{j=1}^{d_{\ell-1}} W_{ij}^{(\ell)} a_j^{(\ell-1)} + b_i^{(\ell)}, &\qquad a_i^{(\ell)} &= \phi_\ell\!\bigl(z_i^{(\ell)}\bigr), \\ \text{Vector form:} &\quad z^{(\ell)} = W^{(\ell)} a^{(\ell-1)} + b^{(\ell)}, &\qquad a^{(\ell)} &= \phi_\ell\!\bigl(z^{(\ell)}\bigr). \end{aligned}$$

    The backpropagation pass propagates the error sensitivity $\delta^{(\ell)} \triangleq \nabla_{z^{(\ell)}} \mathcal{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}$ from $\ell = L$ down to $\ell = 1$ to obtain every weight and bias gradient:

    $$\begin{aligned} \delta^{(L)} &= \nabla_{a^{(L)}} \mathcal{L} \odot \phi_L'\!\bigl(z^{(L)}\bigr), &\qquad \delta^{(\ell)} &= \Bigl(\bigl(W^{(\ell+1)}\bigr)^\top \delta^{(\ell+1)}\Bigr) \odot \phi_\ell'\!\bigl(z^{(\ell)}\bigr), \\ \nabla_{W^{(\ell)}} \mathcal{L} &= \delta^{(\ell)} \bigl(a^{(\ell-1)}\bigr)^\top, &\qquad \nabla_{b^{(\ell)}} \mathcal{L} &= \delta^{(\ell)}. \end{aligned}$$
