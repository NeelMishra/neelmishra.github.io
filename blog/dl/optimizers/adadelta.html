    Adadelta: Learning an Update Scale

    September 2026 · 5 min read + interactive lab

    Track two running averages—squared gradients and squared parameter updates—to adapt each coordinate’s step scale automatically.

    Adadelta was designed to fix two flaws in Adagrad ($w_t=w_{t-1}-\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^t g_k^2+\epsilon}}g_t$): first, summing squared gradients from $t=1$ forever forces the effective learning rate toward zero; second, dividing $g_t$ by its root-mean-square (RMS) leaves a dimensionless ratio that still requires a manual learning rate $\eta$ to restore parameter units.

    1. What Adadelta fixes: decaying rates and mismatched units

    Instead of summing all past $g_k^2$, Adadelta keeps an exponential moving average $v_t$ of recent squared gradients and a second exponential moving average $u_t$ of recent squared parameter updates ($v_0=0,\,u_0=0$, $0\le\rho<1$):

    $$\begin{aligned}v_t&=\rho v_{t-1}+(1-\rho)g_t^2,&\operatorname{RMS}[g]_t&=\sqrt{v_t+\epsilon},\\d_t&=-\frac{\operatorname{RMS}[d]_{t-1}}{\operatorname{RMS}[g]_t}g_t=-\frac{\sqrt{u_{t-1}+\epsilon}}{\sqrt{v_t+\epsilon}}g_t,\\u_t&=\rho u_{t-1}+(1-\rho)d_t^2,&\operatorname{RMS}[d]_t&=\sqrt{u_t+\epsilon},\\w_t&=w_{t-1}+d_t.\end{aligned}$$

    Every square, square root, and division is element-wise per coordinate. Notice the time indices in $d_t$: the denominator uses $v_t$ (which already includes today’s gradient $g_t$), whereas the numerator uses $u_{t-1}$ because $u_t$ depends on $d_t^2$ and cannot be computed until after $d_t$ is known. Having $\epsilon>0$ inside both square roots prevents division by zero and ensures the initial numerator $\sqrt{u_0+\epsilon}=\sqrt{\epsilon}$ is nonzero when starting from $u_0=0$.

    2. Why the ratio $\operatorname{RMS}[d]_{t-1}/\operatorname{RMS}[g]_t$ sets the step scale

    If you only normalize the gradient by its RMS, the gradient units $\bigl[\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}\bigr]=\frac{\text{loss}}{\text{param}}$ cancel completely, leaving a dimensionless number rather than a parameter displacement:

    $$\text{units}\!\left(\frac{g_t}{\operatorname{RMS}[g]_t}\right)=\frac{\text{loss}/\text{param}}{\text{loss}/\text{param}}=1\neq\text{units}(w).$$

    In a 1D second-order Newton step $\Delta w=-g_t/(\partial^2 L/\partial w^2)$, the inverse curvature converts gradient units into parameter units:

    $$\frac{1}{\partial^2 L/\partial w^2}=-\frac{\Delta w}{g_t}\implies\text{units}\!\left(\frac{\Delta w}{g_t}\right)=\frac{\text{param}}{\text{loss}/\text{param}}.$$

    Adadelta replaces that unknown scale with the ratio of recent RMS step size to recent RMS gradient magnitude, $\operatorname{RMS}[d]_{t-1}/\operatorname{RMS}[g]_t$, giving $d_t$ the exact units of $w$:

    $$\text{units}(d_t)=\text{units}\!\left(-\frac{\operatorname{RMS}[d]_{t-1}}{\operatorname{RMS}[g]_t}g_t\right)=\frac{\text{param}}{\text{loss}/\text{param}}\cdot\frac{\text{loss}}{\text{param}}=\text{param}.$$

    Spike damping: if $g_t$ suddenly spikes on step $t$, $v_t$ in the denominator increases immediately while $u_{t-1}$ in the numerator still reflects pre-spike step sizes, automatically damping the spike.

    4. Implementation, external lr, and memory state

    Although pure Adadelta sets $w_t=w_{t-1}+d_t$ with no global learning rate, PyTorch’s torch.optim.Adadelta exposes an external multiplier $\eta$ (lr=1.0 by default). Importantly, $u_t$ accumulates the unscaled step $d_t^2$ before $\eta$ is applied:

    $$u_t=\rho u_{t-1}+(1-\rho)d_t^2,\qquad w_t=w_{t-1}+\eta d_t.$$
    # Element-wise operations; v and u persist across batches.
v = rho * v + (1 - rho) * grad.square()
d = -(u + eps).sqrt() / (v + eps).sqrt() * grad
u = rho * u + (1 - rho) * d.square()
parameter += lr * d

optimizer = torch.optim.Adadelta(
    model.parameters(), lr=1.0, rho=0.9, eps=1e-6,
    weight_decay=0
)

    Optimizer state memory: Adadelta stores two extra numbers per model weight—the squared-gradient average $v_{t,i}$ and the squared-update average $u_{t,i}$. Every parameter tensor W of shape $(d_{\text{out}}, d_{\text{in}})$ requires two matching state tensors v and u of shape $(d_{\text{out}}, d_{\text{in}})$ ($+8\text{ bytes/parameter}$ in FP32, the same state footprint as Adam). Also note that $\epsilon$ sits inside both square roots ($\sqrt{v_t+\epsilon}$), unlike standard PyTorch RMSprop/Adam where $\epsilon$ sits outside ($\sqrt{v_t}+\epsilon$).

    5. Questions worth answering

    If $g_t=0$, does the update history $u_{t-1}$ keep the parameter moving?

    No. Because $d_t=-\frac{\sqrt{u_{t-1}+\epsilon}}{\sqrt{v_t+\epsilon}}g_t$ multiplies $g_t$ directly and stores squared magnitudes rather than signed momentum, $g_t=0$ immediately gives $d_t=0$.

    Why do alternating gradients $+2,-2,+2,-2$ produce the same $v_t$ as $+2,+2,+2,+2$?

    Because $v_t$ averages $g_t^2=4$ at every step, discarding the sign. The step direction comes purely from the current gradient’s sign $-g_t$.

    How does Adadelta differ from RMSprop?

    Both divide $g_t$ by $\sqrt{v_t+\epsilon}$, but RMSprop multiplies by a fixed scalar learning rate $\eta$, whereas Adadelta multiplies by the coordinate’s own running RMS update $\sqrt{u_{t-1}+\epsilon}$.
