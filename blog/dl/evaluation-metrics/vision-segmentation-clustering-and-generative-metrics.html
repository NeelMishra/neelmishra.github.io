    Vision, Segmentation, Clustering & Generative Metrics: IoU, GIoU/CIoU, Dice, COCO mAP, Panoptic PQ, PSNR/SSIM, FID, and Silhouette/ARI/NMI

    Evaluating structured spatial predictions, unsupervised representation clusters, and generative image distributions requires geometry-aware metrics. We derive spatial overlap metrics (IoU, Sørensen–Dice, GIoU/DIoU/CIoU, Hausdorff distance), COCO object detection $\text{mAP}@[\text{.50:.05:.95}]$, Panoptic Quality ($\text{PQ} = \text{SQ}\times\text{RQ}$), pixel and perceptual image fidelity (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), distributional generative metrics (Inception Score, Fréchet Inception Distance, CLIPScore), and internal/external clustering metrics (Silhouette, Davies–Bouldin, Calinski–Harabasz, Adjusted Rand Index, and Normalized Mutual Information).

    1. Spatial Overlap & Boundary Geometry: IoU, Dice, GIoU/DIoU/CIoU, and Hausdorff Distance

    1.1 Intersection over Union (IoU / Jaccard Index) and Sørensen–Dice

    Given a ground-truth region (pixel mask or bounding box) $A$ and a predicted region $B$, Intersection over Union ($\text{IoU}$), also known as the Jaccard Index, measures area overlap relative to their union:

    $$\text{IoU}(A, B) = J(A, B) = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|} = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A| + |B| - |A \cap B|} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP} + \text{FN}} \in [0, 1]$$

    The Sørensen–Dice Coefficient (identical to the pixel-wise $F_1$ score) normalizes intersection by the arithmetic mean of the two areas:

    $$\text{Dice}(A, B) = \frac{2|A \cap B|}{|A| + |B|} = \frac{2\,\text{TP}}{2\,\text{TP} + \text{FP} + \text{FN}} \in [0, 1]$$

    $\text{IoU}$ and $\text{Dice}$ are linked by a strictly monotonic bijection:

    $$\text{Dice} = \frac{2\,\text{IoU}}{1 + \text{IoU}}, \qquad \text{IoU} = \frac{\text{Dice}}{2 - \text{Dice}}, \qquad \text{IoU} \le \text{Dice}$$

    Although ranking two single masks by $\text{IoU}$ or $\text{Dice}$ gives the same order, averaging across a dataset differs: $\text{IoU}$ penalizes bad single-instance outliers more severely than $\text{Dice}$.

    1.2 GIoU, DIoU, and CIoU for Bounding-Box Regression

    When two bounding boxes do not overlap ($|A \cap B| = 0$), standard $\text{IoU}(A, B) = 0$ whether the predicted box is $1$ pixel away or $1000$ pixels away—yielding a vanishing gradient during training.

    • Generalized IoU (GIoU, Rezatofighi et al. 2019): Let $C$ be the smallest axis-aligned bounding box enclosing both $A$ and $B$. GIoU subtracts the fraction of empty space inside $C$:
      $$\text{GIoU}(A, B) = \text{IoU}(A, B) - \frac{|C \setminus (A \cup B)|}{|C|} \in (-1, 1]$$
    • Distance IoU (DIoU) & Complete IoU (CIoU, Zheng et al. 2020): GIoU still converges slowly when one box is contained inside the other ($|C \setminus (A \cup B)| = 0$). DIoU directly penalizes the normalized squared Euclidean distance $\rho^2(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{b}^{gt})$ between box centers divided by the diagonal length $c^2$ of $C$, and CIoU adds an aspect-ratio consistency penalty $\alpha v$:
      $$\text{CIoU}(A, B) = \text{IoU}(A, B) - \frac{\rho^2(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{b}^{gt})}{c^2} - \alpha v, \qquad v = \frac{4}{\pi^2}\left(\arctan\frac{w^{gt}}{h^{gt}} - \arctan\frac{w}{h}\right)^2$$

    1.3 Boundary Accuracy: Hausdorff Distance and $\text{HD}_{95}$

    Area-based metrics ($\text{IoU}$, $\text{Dice}$) are dominated by the interior of large organs or objects and can mencapai $\text{Dice} > 0.97$ even when a thin boundary leak extends several millimeters into critical surgical anatomy. Let $\partial A$ and $\partial B$ be the boundary point sets of masks $A$ and $B$. The symmetric Hausdorff Distance measures the worst-case boundary deviation:

    $$d_H(\partial A, \partial B) = \max\!\left\{\,\sup_{a \in \partial A}\inf_{b \in \partial B}\|a - b\|_2,\;\; \sup_{b \in \partial B}\inf_{a \in \partial A}\|a - b\|_2\,\right\}$$

    Because a single stray isolated false-positive pixel can blow up the $\sup$, medical imaging benchmarks (BraTS, MSD) report $\text{HD}_{95}$, which replaces the maximum ($\sup$) with the $95\text{th}$ percentile of directed boundary distances.

    2. Object Detection & Panoptic Segmentation: COCO mAP and Panoptic Quality (PQ)

    2.1 Greedy IoU Matching and COCO $\text{mAP}@[\text{.50:.05:.95}]$

    How do we evaluate an object detector that outputs a variable number of boxes $(B_i, c_i, s_i)$ with class $c_i$ and confidence score $s_i$? For each class $c$ and fixed IoU threshold $\tau$ (e.g., $\tau = 0.50$):

    1. Sort all predicted boxes of class $c$ across the dataset in descending order of confidence $s_i$.
    2. For each prediction, find the unmatched ground-truth box of class $c$ in the same image with the highest $\text{IoU}$.
    3. If $\text{IoU} \ge \tau$ and that ground-truth box has not yet been claimed, mark the prediction as a True Positive (TP) and lock the ground-truth box; otherwise (or if it is a duplicate detection of an already-matched object!), mark it as a False Positive (FP).
    4. Trace the cumulative Precision–Recall curve $P(R)$ as confidence decreases, apply the monotonic upper envelope $P_{\text{interp}}(r) = \max_{\tilde{r} \ge r} P(\tilde{r})$, and average over 101 recall checkpoints $r \in \{0.00, 0.01, \dots, 1.00\}$:
      $$\text{AP}_\tau^{(c)} = \frac{1}{101}\sum_{r \in \{0.00, 0.01, \dots, 1.00\}} \max_{\tilde{r} \ge r} P(\tilde{r})$$

    To reward precise localization, COCO mAP averages $\text{AP}_\tau^{(c)}$ over all classes $c \in \{1, \dots, C\}$ and over 10 IoU thresholds $\tau \in \{0.50, 0.55, 0.60, \dots, 0.95\}$:

    $$\text{mAP}@[\text{.50:.05:.95}] = \frac{1}{10\,C}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\sum_{j=0}^{9} \text{AP}_{0.50 + 0.05j}^{(c)}$$

    2.2 Panoptic Quality ($\text{PQ} = \text{SQ} \times \text{RQ}$)

    Kirillov et al. (2019) unified semantic segmentation ("stuff" like sky, road) and instance segmentation ("things" like cars, people) via Panoptic Quality ($\text{PQ}$). Because predicted panoptic segments are non-overlapping, a simple theorem guarantees that for any ground-truth segment $g$, at most one predicted segment $p$ can have $\text{IoU}(p, g) > 0.5$. This makes matching unique and threshold-free at $\text{IoU} > 0.5$:

    $$\text{PQ} = \frac{\sum_{(p, g) \in \text{TP}} \text{IoU}(p, g)}{|\text{TP}| + \frac{1}{2}|\text{FP}| + \frac{1}{2}|\text{FN}|} = \underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{|\text{TP}|}\sum_{(p, g) \in \text{TP}} \text{IoU}(p, g)\right)}_{\text{SQ (Segmentation Quality)}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{|\text{TP}|}{|\text{TP}| + \frac{1}{2}|\text{FP}| + \frac{1}{2}|\text{FN}|}\right)}_{\text{RQ (Recognition Quality / } F_1\text{)}}$$

    3. Image Reconstruction & Perceptual Fidelity: PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS

    3.1 Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR)

    For image denoising, super-resolution, compression, and NeRF / 3D Gaussian Splatting novel-view synthesis, PSNR expresses pixel-wise $\text{MSE}$ on a logarithmic decibel (dB) scale relative to the maximum possible pixel intensity $\text{MAX}_I$ ($255$ for 8-bit uint8 or $1.0$ for float $[0,1]$):

    $$\text{PSNR}(x, y) = 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\text{MAX}_I^2}{\text{MSE}(x, y)}\right) = 20\log_{10}(\text{MAX}_I) - 10\log_{10}(\text{MSE}(x, y)) \quad (\text{in dB})$$

    Every $+3.01\text{ dB}$ increase in PSNR corresponds to cutting pixel $\text{MSE}$ in half. However, because PSNR is a purely point-wise $\ell_2$ metric, translating an image by 1 pixel or slightly shifting contrast causes a massive PSNR drop, whereas blurry posterior-mean predictions achieve deceptively high PSNR.

    3.2 Structural Similarity Index (SSIM)

    Wang et al. (2004) designed SSIM to model human visual perception across local sliding windows (typically $11\times 11$ Gaussian-weighted patches) by factoring similarity into luminance $l(x,y)$, contrast $c(x,y)$, and structure $s(x,y)$:

    $$l(x, y) = \frac{2\mu_x\mu_y + C_1}{\mu_x^2 + \mu_y^2 + C_1}, \qquad c(x, y) = \frac{2\sigma_x\sigma_y + C_2}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2 + C_2}, \qquad s(x, y) = \frac{\sigma_{xy} + C_3}{\sigma_x\sigma_y + C_3}$$

    Setting $C_3 = C_2/2$ where $C_1 = (k_1 L)^2$ and $C_2 = (k_2 L)^2$ ($k_1=0.01, k_2=0.03$) cancels the $\sigma_x\sigma_y$ cross-term and yields the standard form:

    $$\text{SSIM}(x, y) = \frac{(2\mu_x\mu_y + C_1)(2\sigma_{xy} + C_2)}{(\mu_x^2 + \mu_y^2 + C_1)(\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2 + C_2)} \in [-1, 1]$$

    3.3 Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS)

    Zhang et al. (2018) showed that internal activations of deep convolutional networks (VGG, AlexNet, SqueezeNet) align remarkably well with human perceptual judgments. Given channel-normalized feature maps $\hat{\phi}_l(x), \hat{\phi}_l(y) \in \mathbb{R}^{H_l \times W_l \times C_l}$ at layer $l$, LPIPS computes the weighted $\ell_2$ distance:

    $$\text{LPIPS}(x, y) = \sum_{l} \frac{1}{H_l W_l}\sum_{h, w} \bigl\|\,w_l \odot \bigl(\hat{\phi}_l(x)_{h,w} - \hat{\phi}_l(y)_{h,w}\bigr)\,\bigr\|_2^2$$

    4. Generative Distribution & Alignment Metrics: Inception Score (IS), FID, and CLIPScore

    When evaluating unconditional or text-conditioned generative models (Diffusion models, Flow Matching, GANs, Autoregressive visual tokenizers), there is no pixel-aligned reference image for a newly sampled output $x \sim p_g$. Instead, we evaluate the distribution of generated samples $p_g$ against the real data distribution $p_r$.

    4.1 Inception Score (IS)

    Salimans et al. (2016) passed generated images $x \sim p_g$ through an ImageNet-pretrained Inception classifier $p(y\mid x)$ and observed two desiderata:

    1. Sharpness / Individual Quality: Each generated image should belong clearly to a recognizable class, so the conditional label distribution $p(y\mid x)$ has low entropy $H(Y\mid X)$.
    2. Mode Diversity: Across all generated images, the marginal class distribution $p(y) = \mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_g}[p(y\mid x)]$ should cover all classes uniformly, so $p(y)$ has high entropy $H(Y)$.

    Combining both via the expected Kullback–Leibler divergence (= mutual information $I(X; Y) = H(Y) - H(Y\mid X)$) gives the Inception Score:

    $$\text{IS}(p_g) = \exp\!\Bigl(\mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_g}\bigl[\,D_{\text{KL}}\bigl(p(y\mid x)\,\big\|\,p(y)\bigr)\,\bigr]\Bigr) = \exp\!\bigl(H(Y) - H(Y\mid X)\bigr) \in [1, C]$$

    Failure Mode of IS: IS never looks at real images $p_r$! A generator that memorizes and outputs one single training image per ImageNet class achieves maximum $\text{IS}$ despite zero intra-class diversity.

    4.2 Fréchet Inception Distance (FID)

    Heusel et al. (2017) resolved this by embedding both real images $x \sim p_r$ and generated images $\tilde{x} \sim p_g$ into the $2048$-dimensional `pool3` feature space of Inception-v3, modeling each feature distribution as a multivariate Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_r, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r)$ and $\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_g, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g)$, and computing their squared Fréchet ($2$-Wasserstein) distance:

    $$\text{FID} = W_2^2\bigl(\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_r, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r),\, \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_g, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g)\bigr) = \|\boldsymbol{\mu}_r - \boldsymbol{\mu}_g\|_2^2 + \text{Tr}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r + \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g - 2(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g)^{1/2}\right)$$

    Notice the two terms: $\|\boldsymbol{\mu}_r - \boldsymbol{\mu}_g\|_2^2$ measures mean feature drift, while the trace term $\text{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r + \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g - 2(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g)^{1/2})$ vanishes if and only if $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g$, penalizing both mode collapse ($\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g \prec \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r$) and noisy artifacts. Note that empirical $\widehat{\text{FID}}_N$ has a positive finite-sample bias $\mathcal{O}(d/N)$, so FID comparisons must always use the same sample count (conventionally $N = 50{,}000$ or $10{,}000$) and identical image resizing kernels.

    4.3 CLIPScore for Text-to-Image Alignment

    For text-to-image generation $(c, x)$ where $c$ is a text prompt and $x$ is the generated image, CLIPScore (Hessel et al. 2021) computes the scaled cosine similarity between the unit-normalized CLIP visual embedding $\mathbf{e}_x$ and text embedding $\mathbf{e}_c$ (with scaling constant $w = 2.5$):

    $$\text{CLIPScore}(x, c) = w \cdot \max\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{e}_x^\top \mathbf{e}_c}{\|\mathbf{e}_x\|_2\,\|\mathbf{e}_c\|_2},\; 0\right)$$

    5. Unsupervised Clustering Metrics: Silhouette, DBI, CHI, Adjusted Rand Index (ARI), and NMI

    Clustering metrics divide into internal metrics (which evaluate cluster geometry using only feature distances $d(x_i, x_j)$) and external metrics (which compare predicted clusters $U = \{U_1, \dots, U_R\}$ against ground-truth classes $V = \{V_1, \dots, V_C\}$ in a permutation-invariant way).

    5.1 Internal Metrics: Silhouette, Davies–Bouldin, and Calinski–Harabasz

    • Silhouette Coefficient (Rousseeuw 1987): For sample $i$ in cluster $U_k$, let $a(i) = \frac{1}{|U_k|-1}\sum_{j \in U_k \setminus \{i\}} d(i, j)$ be its mean intra-cluster distance (cohesion) and $b(i) = \min_{m \ne k} \frac{1}{|U_m|}\sum_{j \in U_m} d(i, j)$ be its mean distance to the nearest neighboring cluster (separation):
      $$s(i) = \frac{b(i) - a(i)}{\max\bigl(a(i),\, b(i)\bigr)} \in [-1, 1]$$
      Values near $+1$ indicate tight, well-separated clusters; $0$ indicates boundary overlap; negative values indicate sample $i$ is closer to another cluster than its own.
    • Davies–Bouldin Index (DBI, lower is better): Averages the worst-case ratio of within-cluster scatter $S_k = \frac{1}{|U_k|}\sum_{x \in U_k}\|x - \boldsymbol{\mu}_k\|_2$ to centroid separation $M_{k,j} = \|\boldsymbol{\mu}_k - \boldsymbol{\mu}_j\|_2$:
      $$\text{DBI} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} \max_{j \ne k}\left(\frac{S_k + S_j}{\|\boldsymbol{\mu}_k - \boldsymbol{\mu}_j\|_2}\right)$$
    • Calinski–Harabasz Index (CHI / Variance Ratio, higher is better): Ratio of between-cluster dispersion $\text{Tr}(\mathbf{B}_K)$ to within-cluster dispersion $\text{Tr}(\mathbf{W}_K)$ normalized by degrees of freedom:
      $$\text{CHI} = \frac{\text{Tr}(\mathbf{B}_K) / (K - 1)}{\text{Tr}(\mathbf{W}_K) / (N - K)}$$

    5.2 External Metrics: Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) and Normalized Mutual Information (NMI)

    Because cluster IDs are arbitrary up to label permutation, we cannot compute standard accuracy directly without Hungarian matching. Instead:

    • Adjusted Rand Index (ARI, Hubert & Arabie 1985): Let $n_{ij} = |U_i \cap V_j|$ be the contingency table between clusters $U_i$ and true classes $V_j$, with row sums $a_i = \sum_j n_{ij}$ and column sums $b_j = \sum_i n_{ij}$. Over all $\binom{N}{2}$ pairs of points, the raw Rand Index counts pairs that are either grouped together in both $U$ and $V$ or separated in both. Subtracting the hypergeometric expected value under random partitioning gives:
      $$\text{ARI} = \frac{\sum_{i,j}\binom{n_{ij}}{2} - \left[\sum_i\binom{a_i}{2}\sum_j\binom{b_j}{2}\right] \big/ \binom{N}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}\left[\sum_i\binom{a_i}{2} + \sum_j\binom{b_j}{2}\right] - \left[\sum_i\binom{a_i}{2}\sum_j\binom{b_j}{2}\right] \big/ \binom{N}{2}} \in [-1, 1]$$
    • Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) / V-Measure: Measures shared Shannon information $I(U; V) = \sum_{i,j}\frac{n_{ij}}{N}\log\frac{N\,n_{ij}}{a_i b_j}$ normalized by average entropy:
      $$\text{NMI}(U, V) = \frac{2\,I(U; V)}{H(U) + H(V)} \in [0, 1]$$

    6. Worked Numerical Examples

    6.1 Worked Box Overlap: IoU, Dice, and GIoU

    Suppose ground-truth box $A = [0, 0, 4, 4]$ (area $|A| = 16$) and predicted box $B = [2, 2, 6, 6]$ (area $|B| = 16$).

    • Intersection $A \cap B$: $[\max(0,2), \max(0,2), \min(4,6), \min(4,6)] = [2, 2, 4, 4]$, so $|A \cap B| = 2 \times 2 = 4$.
    • Union $|A \cup B|$: $16 + 16 - 4 = 28$.
    • IoU and Dice:
      $$\text{IoU}(A, B) = \frac{4}{28} = \frac{1}{7} \approx 0.1429, \qquad \text{Dice}(A, B) = \frac{2(4)}{16 + 16} = \frac{8}{32} = 0.2500$$
    • Smallest Enclosing Box $C$: $[\min(0,2), \min(0,2), \max(4,6), \max(4,6)] = [0, 0, 6, 6]$, so $|C| = 6 \times 6 = 36$, and $|C \setminus (A \cup B)| = 36 - 28 = 8$:
      $$\text{GIoU}(A, B) = \frac{1}{7} - \frac{8}{36} = \frac{1}{7} - \frac{2}{9} = -\frac{5}{63} \approx -0.0794$$

    6.2 Worked 2D Fréchet Inception Distance (FID)

    Suppose in a 2D feature space, the real distribution has $\boldsymbol{\mu}_r = [0, 0]^\top, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r = \text{diag}(4, 9)$ and the generated distribution has $\boldsymbol{\mu}_g = [1, 2]^\top, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g = \text{diag}(1, 4)$.

    • Mean shift squared: $\|\boldsymbol{\mu}_r - \boldsymbol{\mu}_g\|_2^2 = (0-1)^2 + (0-2)^2 = 1 + 4 = 5$.
    • Covariance term: Since diagonal matrices commute, $(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g)^{1/2} = \text{diag}(\sqrt{4\cdot 1}, \sqrt{9\cdot 4}) = \text{diag}(2, 6)$, so:
      $$\text{Tr}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r + \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g - 2(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g)^{1/2}\right) = (4 + 1 - 2\cdot 2) + (9 + 4 - 2\cdot 6) = 1 + 1 = 2$$
    • Total $\text{FID} = 5 + 2 = 7.0000$. (In general, for diagonal covariances, the trace term equals $\sum_d (\sigma_{r,d} - \sigma_{g,d})^2$.)

    7. Reference Python Implementation

    import math
from typing import Sequence, Tuple

Box = Tuple[float, float, float, float]  # (x1, y1, x2, y2)

def box_iou_dice_giou(a: Box, b: Box) -> Tuple[float, float, float]:
    area_a = max(0.0, a[2] - a[0]) * max(0.0, a[3] - a[1])
    area_b = max(0.0, b[2] - b[0]) * max(0.0, b[3] - b[1])
    ix1, iy1 = max(a[0], b[0]), max(a[1], b[1])
    ix2, iy2 = min(a[2], b[2]), min(a[3], b[3])
    inter = max(0.0, ix2 - ix1) * max(0.0, iy2 - iy1)
    union = area_a + area_b - inter
    iou = inter / union if union > 0 else 0.0
    dice = (2.0 * inter) / (area_a + area_b) if (area_a + area_b) > 0 else 0.0

    cx1, cy1 = min(a[0], b[0]), min(a[1], b[1])
    cx2, cy2 = max(a[2], b[2]), max(a[3], b[3])
    area_c = max(0.0, cx2 - cx1) * max(0.0, cy2 - cy1)
    giou = iou - (area_c - union) / area_c if area_c > 0 else iou
    return iou, dice, giou

def psnr(mse: float, max_val: float = 1.0) -> float:
    return 10.0 * math.log10((max_val ** 2) / mse)

def adjusted_rand_index(u_labels: Sequence[int], v_labels: Sequence[int]) -> float:
    n = len(u_labels)
    u_classes = sorted(set(u_labels))
    v_classes = sorted(set(v_labels))
    comb2 = lambda k: k * (k - 1) // 2

    contingency = {(u, v): 0 for u in u_classes for v in v_classes}
    for u, v in zip(u_labels, v_labels):
        contingency[(u, v)] += 1

    sum_comb_nij = sum(comb2(c) for c in contingency.values())
    sum_comb_a = sum(comb2(sum(contingency[(u, v)] for v in v_classes)) for u in u_classes)
    sum_comb_b = sum(comb2(sum(contingency[(u, v)] for u in u_classes)) for v in v_classes)
    total_pairs = comb2(n)
    expected = (sum_comb_a * sum_comb_b) / total_pairs
    max_idx = 0.5 * (sum_comb_a + sum_comb_b)
    return (sum_comb_nij - expected) / (max_idx - expected) if max_idx != expected else 1.0

if __name__ == "__main__":
    iou, dice, giou = box_iou_dice_giou((0, 0, 4, 4), (2, 2, 6, 6))
    print(f"IoU = {iou:.4f}, Dice = {dice:.4f}, GIoU = {giou:.4f}")
    u = [0, 0, 1, 1, 2, 2]
    v = [1, 1, 0, 0, 2, 1]
    print(f"ARI = {adjusted_rand_index(u, v):.4f}")

    8. Self-Check Questions & Summary

    Question 1: Why does training an image super-resolution network purely with pixel $\text{MSE}$ maximize $\text{PSNR}$ yet produce blurry textures with poor $\text{LPIPS}$ and $\text{FID}$?

    Answer: Under $\text{MSE}$ loss, the Bayes-optimal prediction is the conditional expectation $\mathbb{E}[Y \mid X]$, which averages over all plausible high-frequency texture realizations (e.g., individual blades of grass or brick pores). Averaging shifted high-frequency phases cancels them out into a smooth blur. Deep perceptual ($\text{LPIPS}$) and distributional ($\text{FID}$) metrics compare high-level feature statistics, penalizing missing high-frequency texture variance.

    Question 2: Why is unadjusted Mutual Information $I(U; V)$ or unadjusted Rand Index dangerous when selecting the number of clusters $K$?

    Answer: As the number of clusters $K$ increases toward $N$ (putting each data point into its own singleton cluster), the conditional entropy $H(V \mid U)$ trivially drops to $0$, so raw $I(U; V) = H(V) - H(V \mid U)$ reaches its maximum even though the clustering is useless! $\text{NMI}$ divides by $(H(U)+H(V))/2$ (where $H(U) = \log N$ explodes for singletons), and $\text{ARI}$ subtracts the hypergeometric chance expectation $\mathbb{E}[\text{RI}]$.
