    Marginal Vector Fields & the Continuity Equation

    September 2026 · 21 min read · MIT 6.S184, Lecture 2

    The marginalization trick. At a location $x$, average the conditional velocities according to how likely each data point $z$ is to have produced that location. The resulting field depends only on $x$ and $t$, yet it transports the full marginal distribution.

    The conditional field knows too much

    For each fixed endpoint $z$, the tractable field $u_t^{\mathrm{target}}(x\mid z)$ transports $p_t(\cdot\mid z)$. But generation begins from fresh noise and does not know a future data point $z$. The sampler needs one marginal field $u_t^{\mathrm{target}}(x)$.

    The marginal density is

    $$p_t(x)=\int p_t(x\mid z)p_{\mathrm{data}}(z)\,dz.$$

    The question is how to combine the corresponding conditional vector fields.

    At a fixed $x$, not every endpoint is equally plausible

    Bayes' rule gives a time-dependent posterior over the hidden data point:

    $$p_t(z\mid x)=\frac{p_t(x\mid z)p_{\mathrm{data}}(z)}{p_t(x)}.$$

    If $x$ lies near the conditional cloud aimed at one particular $z$, that endpoint receives a large weight. Far-away endpoints receive little weight. These weights sum to one:

    $$\int p_t(z\mid x)\,dz=1.$$

    Average conditional velocities with posterior weights

    Marginal vector field. $$\boxed{u_t^{\mathrm{target}}(x)=\int u_t^{\mathrm{target}}(x\mid z)\frac{p_t(x\mid z)p_{\mathrm{data}}(z)}{p_t(x)}\,dz}.$$ Equivalently, $$u_t^{\mathrm{target}}(x)=\mathbb E\!\left[u_t^{\mathrm{target}}(X_t\mid Z)\mid X_t=x\right].$$

    This is a local average. Two conditional fields may give conflicting directions at the same $x$; the posterior decides how much each direction contributes.

    See the posterior-weighted average

    In this one-dimensional example, $p_{\mathrm{data}}$ assigns equal probability to $z=-2$ and $z=+2$. The colored curves show the two conditional contributions to the marginal density.

    The continuity equation tracks probability mass

    For the ODE $\dot X_t=u_t(X_t)$, a density $p_t$ follows the flow exactly when

    $$\boxed{\partial_t p_t(x)=-\operatorname{div}\!\left(p_tu_t\right)(x)}.$$

    The product $J_t(x)=p_t(x)u_t(x)$ is the probability flux: density multiplied by velocity. In one dimension,

    $$\partial_t p_t(x)=-\partial_xJ_t(x).$$

    More inflow than outflow

    $-\operatorname{div}(p_tu_t)>0$, so probability accumulates and the local density rises.

    More outflow than inflow

    $-\operatorname{div}(p_tu_t)<0$, so probability leaves and the local density falls.

    Why the marginal field transports the marginal path

    Each conditional pair satisfies its own continuity equation:

    $$\partial_t p_t(x\mid z)=-\operatorname{div}\!\left(p_t(\cdot\mid z)u_t^{\mathrm{target}}(\cdot\mid z)\right)(x).$$

    Differentiate the marginal density and substitute the conditional equation:

    $$\begin{aligned} \partial_t p_t(x) &=\int \partial_t p_t(x\mid z)p_{\mathrm{data}}(z)\,dz\\ &=-\operatorname{div}\!\left(\int p_t(x\mid z)u_t^{\mathrm{target}}(x\mid z)p_{\mathrm{data}}(z)\,dz\right). \end{aligned}$$

    Multiply and divide the integral by $p_t(x)$:

    $$\begin{aligned} \int p_t(x\mid z)u_t^{\mathrm{target}}(x\mid z)p_{\mathrm{data}}(z)\,dz &=p_t(x)\int u_t^{\mathrm{target}}(x\mid z) \frac{p_t(x\mid z)p_{\mathrm{data}}(z)}{p_t(x)}\,dz\\ &=p_t(x)u_t^{\mathrm{target}}(x). \end{aligned}$$

    Therefore

    $$\partial_t p_t(x)=-\operatorname{div}\!\left(p_tu_t^{\mathrm{target}}\right)(x).$$

    The marginal field obeys the continuity equation for the marginal path. Starting the ODE at $p_0=p_{\mathrm{init}}$ therefore produces $X_t\sim p_t$ and ends at $X_1\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}$.

    What was proved

    Conditional paths and fields can be chosen analytically. Their posterior-weighted field is a valid marginal field for the full noise-to-data path, even though that marginal field is not available in closed form.

    The formula is correct but not directly computable

    The posterior weight contains $p_t(x)$, an enormous mixture over the unknown data distribution. Computing the marginal field explicitly is therefore impractical. Conditional flow matching avoids computing it while still learning it.
