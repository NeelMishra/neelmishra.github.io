    Conditional Flow Matching

    September 2026 · 20 min read · MIT 6.S184, Lecture 2

    The central result. Train on easy conditional velocities, but deploy a field that needs no condition. Squared-error regression automatically averages conflicting conditional targets and recovers the marginal vector field.

    The ideal flow-matching loss is inaccessible

    If samples from the marginal path and its target field were available, the direct objective would be

    $$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta)=\mathbb E_{\substack{t\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]\\X_t\sim p_t}} \left[\left\|u_t^\theta(X_t)-u_t^{\mathrm{target}}(X_t)\right\|^2\right].$$

    This asks the network to match the exact field that carries $p_{\mathrm{init}}$ along $p_t$ to $p_{\mathrm{data}}$. But both $p_t$ and $u_t^{\mathrm{target}}$ are generally intractable mixtures.

    Replace the unknown target with a conditional target

    The conditional construction provides a complete sampling recipe:

    $$t\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1],\quad Z\sim p_{\mathrm{data}},\quad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I_d),\quad X_t=\alpha_tZ+\beta_t\varepsilon.$$

    The associated conditional velocity is $\dot\alpha_tZ+\dot\beta_t\varepsilon$. This leads to

    $$\boxed{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CFM}}(\theta)= \mathbb E_{t,Z,\varepsilon}\!\left[ \left\|u_t^\theta(\alpha_tZ+\beta_t\varepsilon) -(\dot\alpha_tZ+\dot\beta_t\varepsilon)\right\|^2\right]}.$$

    Every random variable in this loss can be sampled, and every target can be calculated.

    Why conditional regression learns a marginal field

    Fix a random time and define

    $$U=u_t^{\mathrm{target}}(X_t\mid Z),\qquad m(X_t)=\mathbb E[U\mid X_t]=u_t^{\mathrm{target}}(X_t).$$

    The conditional velocity $U$ may differ for two training examples that happen to produce the same $X_t$. The marginal target $m(X_t)$ is their posterior-weighted mean.

    Write the conditional target as its mean plus a zero-mean residual:

    $$U=m(X_t)+\eta,\qquad \mathbb E[\eta\mid X_t]=0.$$

    For the network prediction $f(X_t)=u_t^\theta(X_t)$, expand the squared error:

    $$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E\|f-U\|^2 &=\mathbb E\|f-m-\eta\|^2\\ &=\mathbb E\|f-m\|^2+\mathbb E\|\eta\|^2 -2\mathbb E[(f-m)^\top\eta]. \end{aligned}$$

    The cross term is zero because $f-m$ depends only on $X_t$:

    $$\mathbb E[(f-m)^\top\eta] =\mathbb E\!\left[(f-m)^\top\mathbb E[\eta\mid X_t]\right]=0.$$

    Therefore

    $$\boxed{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CFM}}(\theta)=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta)+ \underbrace{\mathbb E\|\eta\|^2}_{\text{independent of }\theta}}.$$

    The two objectives differ only by irreducible variation among conditional targets. They have the same gradients with respect to $\theta$ and the same minimizers.

    Regression principle

    Under squared error, the best prediction from $X_t$ is the conditional mean of the label given $X_t$. That conditional mean is exactly the marginal vector field derived by the marginalization trick.

    Straight-line flow matching

    With $\alpha_t=t$ and $\beta_t=1-t$,

    $$X_t=(1-t)\varepsilon+tZ,\qquad \dot X_t=Z-\varepsilon.$$

    The training loss becomes

    $$\boxed{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CFM}}(\theta)= \mathbb E_{t,Z,\varepsilon}\left[ \left\|u_t^\theta\!\left((1-t)\varepsilon+tZ\right)-(Z-\varepsilon)\right\|^2 \right]}.$$

    The network receives the noisy interpolation $X_t$ and time $t$. It does not receive $Z$ or $\varepsilon$ separately; those variables are used only to construct the target.

    Build one training example

    The complete training algorithm

    Conditional flow matching with a straight-line path
    1Initialize neural vector field parameters $\theta$.
    2repeat for each optimization step:
    3Sample a minibatch $z\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}$.
    4Sample $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I_d)$ and $t\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$ independently.
    5Construct $x_t=(1-t)\varepsilon+tz$.
    6Construct target velocity $v=z-\varepsilon$.
    7Compute $\ell=\|u_t^\theta(x_t)-v\|^2$ and update $\theta$ using its gradient.

    Training pairs disappear at sampling time

    Once training is complete, the conditional variables are no longer used:

    Draw fresh noise$X_0\sim p_{\mathrm{init}}$
    →
    Solve learned ODE$\dot X_t=u_t^\theta(X_t)$
    →
    Return endpoint$X_1\approx p_{\mathrm{data}}$

    The network has learned the conditional average needed at each $(x,t)$, so it can guide samples without knowing which training example supplied any particular target.

    Implementation details that matter

    • Give time to the network. The correct velocity at the same $x$ can differ across $t$.
    • Sample time across the full interval. Otherwise parts of the path receive no supervision.
    • Use independent noise per example. The objective averages over the source distribution, not one fixed corruption.
    • Match the schedule and target. For a general $(\alpha_t,\beta_t)$, the target is $\dot\alpha_tz+\dot\beta_t\varepsilon$, not automatically $z-\varepsilon$.
