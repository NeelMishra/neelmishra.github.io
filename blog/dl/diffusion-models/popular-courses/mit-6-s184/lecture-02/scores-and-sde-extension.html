    Scores & the SDE Extension

    September 2026 · 20 min read · MIT 6.S184, Lecture 2

    Adding noise changes a probability path unless the drift is corrected. The score provides exactly that correction. It measures how log density rises in space, and it obeys the same conditional-to-marginal averaging rule as the vector field.

    A score points toward increasing log density

    Score function. For a differentiable density $p_t$, its score is $$s_t(x)=\nabla_x\log p_t(x).$$

    The score is a vector in data space. It points in the direction where log density increases most quickly. It does not include a time derivative and it is not the same as a velocity field:

    ObjectDefinitionQuestion answered
    velocity $u_t(x)$time derivative of sample motionWhere should this point move next?
    score $s_t(x)$$\nabla_x\log p_t(x)$Which spatial direction increases density?

    The Gaussian conditional score

    For $p_t(x\mid z)=\mathcal N(\alpha_tz,\beta_t^2I_d)$, ignore terms independent of $x$ and write

    $$\log p_t(x\mid z)=C-\frac{\|x-\alpha_tz\|^2}{2\beta_t^2}.$$

    Differentiating with respect to $x$ gives

    $$\boxed{\nabla_x\log p_t(x\mid z)=-\frac{x-\alpha_tz}{\beta_t^2}}.$$

    The vector points from $x$ back toward the Gaussian mean $\alpha_tz$. A smaller $\beta_t$ creates a sharper density, so the same displacement produces a larger score magnitude.

    Marginal scores use the same posterior weights

    Start from $p_t(x)=\int p_t(x\mid z)p_{\mathrm{data}}(z)\,dz$:

    $$\begin{aligned} \nabla_x\log p_t(x) &=\frac{\nabla_xp_t(x)}{p_t(x)}\\ &=\int \frac{\nabla_xp_t(x\mid z)}{p_t(x)}p_{\mathrm{data}}(z)\,dz\\ &=\int \underbrace{\frac{\nabla_xp_t(x\mid z)}{p_t(x\mid z)}}_{\nabla_x\log p_t(x\mid z)} \underbrace{\frac{p_t(x\mid z)p_{\mathrm{data}}(z)}{p_t(x)}}_{p_t(z\mid x)}\,dz. \end{aligned}$$

    Hence

    $$\boxed{\nabla_x\log p_t(x)= \mathbb E\!\left[\nabla_x\log p_t(X_t\mid Z)\mid X_t=x\right]}.$$

    Both marginal objects are posterior averages:

    Marginal velocity

    $$u_t(x)=\mathbb E[u_t(X_t\mid Z)\mid X_t=x].$$

    Marginal score

    $$s_t(x)=\mathbb E[s_t(X_t\mid Z)\mid X_t=x].$$

    The conditional quantities are analytic. The marginal quantities are complicated mixtures, but regression can learn either conditional mean without evaluating the mixture density.

    Why a stochastic sampler needs the score

    Suppose the deterministic ODE

    $$dX_t=u_t(X_t)\,dt$$

    already follows the desired path $p_t$. Now inject Brownian noise with any chosen scalar schedule $\sigma_t$:

    $$dX_t=b_t(X_t)\,dt+\sigma_t\,dW_t.$$

    Diffusion spreads probability mass, so setting $b_t=u_t$ would generally destroy the original path. The drift must counteract that spreading.

    The SDE extension theorem

    SDE extension. If $(p_t,u_t)$ satisfies the continuity equation, then for any nonnegative $\sigma_t$, the SDE $$\boxed{dX_t=\left[u_t(X_t)+\frac{\sigma_t^2}{2}\nabla_x\log p_t(X_t)\right]dt+\sigma_t\,dW_t}$$ has the same one-time marginal distributions $X_t\sim p_t$.

    The new drift is

    $$b_t(x)=u_t(x)+\frac{\sigma_t^2}{2}s_t(x).$$

    When $\sigma_t=0$, the correction vanishes and the SDE reduces to the original ODE. Larger diffusion requires a larger score correction.

    Proof by the Fokker–Planck equation

    The density of $dX_t=b_t(X_t)dt+\sigma_t dW_t$ evolves according to

    $$\partial_t p_t=-\operatorname{div}(p_tb_t)+\frac{\sigma_t^2}{2}\Delta p_t.$$

    Substitute $b_t=u_t+\frac{\sigma_t^2}{2}\nabla\log p_t$:

    $$\begin{aligned} \partial_t p_t &=-\operatorname{div}(p_tu_t) -\frac{\sigma_t^2}{2}\operatorname{div}(p_t\nabla\log p_t) +\frac{\sigma_t^2}{2}\Delta p_t. \end{aligned}$$

    Use the identity

    $$p_t\nabla\log p_t=\nabla p_t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{div}(p_t\nabla\log p_t)=\Delta p_t.$$

    The last two terms cancel, leaving

    $$\partial_t p_t=-\operatorname{div}(p_tu_t),$$

    which is exactly the original continuity equation. Brownian noise adds heat-like dispersion; the score term changes the drift by precisely enough to cancel that change at the distribution level.

    See the drift correction

    Same marginals does not mean same trajectories

    Preserved

    At every fixed time, both samplers have the same distribution $X_t\sim p_t$, including the same endpoint $p_1=p_{\mathrm{data}}$.

    Changed

    The ODE has one path per initial sample. The SDE has random trajectories because it receives fresh Brownian increments.

    Probability paths describe snapshots of the distribution. They do not uniquely determine how individual particles are coupled across time.
