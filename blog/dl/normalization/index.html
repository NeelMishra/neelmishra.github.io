    Normalization in Deep Learning: Reading Guide

    October 2026 · 6 min read

    Eight connected chapters covering sample-wise $L_p$ / Cosine / LRN / FRN normalizations and internal deep-network layers (BatchNorm, LayerNorm, RMSNorm, InstanceNorm, GroupNorm, Pre-LN vs. Post-LN, QK-Norm, AdaIN/FiLM/SPADE/AdaLN-Zero, and Weight & Spectral Normalization).

    For column-wise input preprocessing (Standardization, Min-Max, Robust IQR, MaxAbs) and non-linear feature transforms (Log, Box-Cox, Yeo-Johnson, Quantile), see the dedicated Feature Scaling and Feature Transformation subseries in Data Preparation.

    Eight chapters, one progression

    01
    Vector, Hyperspherical, and Divisive Normalizations: $L_p$ Unit Norms, Cosine Normalization, LRN, FRN, and Batch Renorm

    Switch from column-wise scaling to row-wise $L_1$, $L_2$, and $L_\infty$ unit-ball projections; derive Cosine Normalization and why temperature $\tau=1/s$ is mandatory on $\mathbb S^{d-1}$; and trace AlexNet’s Local Response Normalization (LRN), Filter Response Normalization (FRN + TLU), and Batch Renormalization.

    02
    Why Deep Network Normalization Works: Loss Smoothing, Scale Invariance, and Effective Learning Rates

    Compare Internal Covariate Shift with Santurkar et al.’s Lipschitz gradient smoothing, prove that $\operatorname{Norm}(\alpha Wx)=\operatorname{Norm}(Wx)$ makes gradients orthogonal to weights ($W^\top\nabla_W\mathcal L=0$), and derive the effective learning rate $\eta_{\text{eff}}\propto\eta/\|W\|_2^2$ under weight decay.

    03
    Batch Normalization: Forward Statistics, Running EMA Buffers, and the Exact Backward Pass

    Derive per-channel mini-batch normalization over $(N,H,W)$, track exponential moving average (EMA) buffers and Conv+BN inference folding, and work through the three-term orthogonal-projection backward gradient step by step.

    04
    LayerNorm and RMSNorm: Per-Token Normalization, Mean-Centering Invariance, and Backward Jacobians

    See why BatchNorm breaks on variable-length sequences and batch size $1$, derive LayerNorm’s per-token standardization and Jacobian projection, and show why RMSNorm drops the mean $\mu$ for a $7\%\text{–}40\%$ kernel speedup.

    05
    Spatial & Vision Normalizations: InstanceNorm, GroupNorm, and the $(N,C,H,W)$ Cube

    Unify BatchNorm, LayerNorm, InstanceNorm, and GroupNorm as four slices of an $(N,C,H,W)$ activation tensor, explain why GroupNorm stays stable at batch size $1\text{–}2$, and pair it with Weight Standardization.

    06
    Placement in Transformers: Post-LN vs. Pre-LN, Sandwich-LN, and QK-Norm

    Derive Xiong et al.’s expected gradient norms at initialization for Post-LN vs. Pre-LN residuals, diagnose residual variance growth $\operatorname{Var}(x_L)\propto L$ and Sandwich-LN fixes, and bound attention logits with QK-Norm.

    07
    Conditional Normalization: AdaIN, FiLM, Spatially-Adaptive SPADE, and DiT AdaLN-Zero

    Turn static affine parameters $(\gamma,\beta)$ into conditioning-driven functions for style transfer (AdaIN), multimodal feature modulation (FiLM), semantic synthesis (SPADE), and Diffusion Transformers (AdaLN-Zero).

    08
    Parameter Normalization: Weight Normalization, Weight Standardization, and Spectral Normalization

    Normalize weights instead of activations: decouple norm and direction via $w=g\,v/\|v\|_2$, standardize convolutional filters, and enforce $1$-Lipschitz layers via power-iterated Spectral Normalization $W/\sigma_{\max}(W)$.

    Notation that stays consistent

    For sample-wise vector normalizations (Chapter 01), $x \in \mathbb R^d$ is reduced across its $d$ coordinates. For deep network activation layers (Chapters 02–08), a normalization operator over an index set $\mathcal S$ of size $m=|\mathcal S|$ computes empirical mean $\mu=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}x_i$, variance $\sigma^2=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}(x_i-\mu)^2$, standardized activation $\hat x_i=\frac{x_i-\mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2+\varepsilon}}$, and affine output $y_i=\gamma\hat x_i+\beta$. For vision feature maps we use shape $(N,C,H,W)$, and for sequence models we use shape $(B,T,D)$.
