    Data Imbalance in Deep Learning: Reading Guide

    October 2026 · 4 min read

    Seven connected chapters on class imbalance and long-tailed learning: from gradient dominance and evaluation traps to resampling, SMOTE / Borderline-SMOTE / ADASYN, Focal Loss, decoupled two-stage training, Dice/Tversky losses, and prior-shift threshold calibration.

    Real-world datasets rarely have equal counts per class: fraud transactions, medical lesions, rare traffic hazards, and foreground segmentation pixels often make up less than $1\%$ of training examples. When standard cross-entropy is minimized on a skewed distribution, head classes dominate the mini-batch gradient, push tail decision boundaries inward, and inflate overall accuracy while minority recall collapses.

    Seven chapters, one progression

    01
    Why Accuracy Lies: Gradient Dominance, PR-AUC vs. ROC-AUC, and Minority Collapse

    Derive majority gradient dominance in mini-batch cross-entropy, solve for the skewed constant-logit optimum $b^*=\log(\pi_+/\pi_-)$, and prove on a $1{:}99$ table why ROC-AUC stays high while Precision and PR-AUC expose false alarms.

    02
    Resampling, Class-Balanced Sampling, and SMOTE / Mixup

    Unify instance-balanced, class-balanced, and square-root sampling via $p_k\propto n_k^q$, quantify duplicate memorization on rare classes, and compare feature-space SMOTE with ReMix and Manifold Mixup.

    03
    SMOTE, Borderline-SMOTE, and ADASYN: Geometry of $k$-NN Minority Synthesis

    Derive Chawla’s $k$-NN line-segment synthesis and covariance contraction, diagnose the Vanilla SMOTE cross-boundary bridge problem, and work through Borderline-SMOTE (Safe/Danger/Noise), ADASYN density weights, and Tomek/ENN cleaning.

    04
    Cost-Sensitive Cross-Entropy, Effective Number of Samples, and Focal Loss

    Derive Cui et al.’s effective number of samples $E_n=(1-\beta^n)/(1-\beta)$ from random subset coverage, and derive Focal Loss $-\alpha_t(1-p_t)^\gamma\log p_t$ and its exact logit gradient.

    05
    Two-Stage Decoupling: Representation Learning vs. Classifier Calibration (cRT and $\tau$-Normalization)

    Separate backbone learning from classifier calibration, inspect why head classifier weights have larger $\ell_2$ norms $\|w_k\|_2$, and compare cRT, LWS, and $\tau$-normalization.

    06
    Dense & Spatial Imbalance: Dice, Tversky, and Asymmetric Losses

    Derive Soft Dice Loss and its quotient-rule gradient for $99.8\%$ background segmentation, Tversky false-positive/false-negative weighting, and Asymmetric Loss (ASL) for multi-label tagging.

    07
    Post-Hoc Calibration, Cost-Optimal Thresholds, and Label-Shift Correction

    Derive the cost-optimal threshold $p^*=C_{\text{FP}}/(C_{\text{FP}}+C_{\text{FN}})$ and the exact Bayes prior-shift formula that recalibrates probabilities after oversampling or undersampling.

    Notation that stays consistent

    Across all seven chapters, a dataset of $N=\sum_{k=1}^K n_k$ examples has class counts $n_1\ge n_2\ge\cdots\ge n_K$ and empirical class priors $\pi_k=n_k/N$. The Imbalance Factor is $\text{IF}=n_1/n_K$. A backbone $f_\theta(x)\in\mathbb R^d$ feeds a linear classifier with weights $w_k\in\mathbb R^d$ and biases $b_k\in\mathbb R$ to produce logits $z_k=w_k^\top f_\theta(x)+b_k$ and softmax probabilities $p_k=e^{z_k}/\sum_{j=1}^K e^{z_j}$.
