    Data Imbalance in Deep Learning: Reading Guide

    October 2026 · 5 min read

    Eleven connected chapters on class imbalance and long-tailed learning: from the 99%-accuracy trap and imbalance evaluation to random oversampling, random undersampling, SMOTE, ADASYN, class weights, Effective-Number and Focal Loss, decoupled two-stage training, threshold moving, and prior-shift recalibration.

    Real-world datasets rarely have equal counts per class: fraud transactions, medical lesions, and rare traffic hazards often make up less than $1\%$ of training examples. When standard cross-entropy is minimized on a skewed distribution, head classes dominate the mini-batch gradient, push tail decision boundaries inward, and inflate overall accuracy while minority recall collapses.

    Eleven chapters, one progression

    The Imbalance Problem

    Understand class imbalance through a $99\%$-accurate baseline, clickable confusion counts, explicit error costs, and the separate roles of sampling, weights, and thresholds.

    Separate operating decisions, ranking, and calibration; explore prevalence interactively, compare ROC with PR curves, and compute tie-aware average precision.

    Follow source identities through sampling with replacement, see which rows are repeated, and understand why a larger resampled dataset does not add independent observations.

    Compare balanced subsets that retain different boundary evidence and calculate the probability of dropping an informative majority region.

    05
    SMOTE

    Choose minority parents, interpolate a synthetic point between them, and inspect domain constraints that valid endpoints satisfy while their midpoint violates.

    06
    ADASYN

    Use all-class neighbors to allocate a synthesis budget toward harder boundary seeds, then interpolate along minority-only neighbors.

    07
    Class Weights

    Calculate balanced class weights and vary their influence interactively.

    08
    Cost-Sensitive Cross-Entropy, Effective Number of Samples, and Focal Loss

    Derive Cui et al.’s effective number of samples $E_n=(1-\beta^n)/(1-\beta)$ from random subset coverage, and derive Focal Loss $-\alpha_t(1-p_t)^\gamma\log p_t$ and its exact logit gradient.

    09
    Two-Stage Decoupling: Representation Learning vs. Classifier Calibration (cRT and $\tau$-Normalization)

    Separate backbone learning from classifier calibration, inspect why head classifier weights have larger $\ell_2$ norms $\|w_k\|_2$, and compare cRT, LWS, and $\tau$-normalization.

    10
    Threshold Moving

    Tune a decision cutoff on fixed validation scores, inspect alert and error costs interactively, and derive the Bayes probability threshold $p^*=C_{\text{FP}}/(C_{\text{FP}}+C_{\text{FN}})$.

    11
    Post-Hoc Calibration and Label-Shift Correction

    Prove why oversampling, undersampling, and class weights warp predicted probabilities, and derive the exact Elkan/Saerens Bayes prior-shift recalibration formula.

    Notation that stays consistent

    Across the series, a dataset of $N=\sum_{k=1}^K n_k$ examples has class counts $n_1\ge n_2\ge\cdots\ge n_K$ and empirical class priors $\pi_k=n_k/N$. The Imbalance Factor is $\text{IF}=n_1/n_K$. A backbone $f_\theta(x)\in\mathbb R^d$ feeds a linear classifier with weights $w_k\in\mathbb R^d$ and biases $b_k\in\mathbb R$ to produce logits $z_k=w_k^\top f_\theta(x)+b_k$ and softmax probabilities $p_k=e^{z_k}/\sum_{j=1}^K e^{z_j}$.
