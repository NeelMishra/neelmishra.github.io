    Dropout and DropConnect: Learn with Randomly Missing Paths

    September 2026 · 4 min read + interactive lab

    Derive inverted dropout’s $1/q$ scaling, prove its exact equivalence to feature-weighted L2 in linear regression, and compare Activation Dropout with DropConnect.

    Dropout prevents hidden units from co-adapting by randomly zeroing out activations on each training pass. To keep expected activation magnitudes identical between training and evaluation without touching inference code, modern frameworks use inverted dropout.

    1. Inverted dropout: why surviving activations divide by $q=1-p$

    Let $p\in[0,1)$ be the drop probability and $q=1-p$ be the keep probability. For each activation $a$, draw a mask $m\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)$ ($\mathbb P(m=1)=q$, $\mathbb P(m=0)=p$). Inverted dropout outputs $\tilde a=\frac{m}{q}a$ during training and $a$ during evaluation:

    • Expectation (unbiased mean):
      $$\mathbb E_m[\tilde a]=q\left(\frac{a}{q}\right)+(1-q)(0)=a.$$
    • Variance (mask noise): Since $m^2=m$, the second moment is $\mathbb E_m[\tilde a^2]=q\left(\frac{a}{q}\right)^2=\frac{a^2}{q}$. Subtracting $(\mathbb E_m[\tilde a])^2=a^2$ gives:
      $$\operatorname{Var}_m(\tilde a)=\frac{a^2}{q}-a^2=\frac{1-q}{q}a^2=\frac{p}{q}a^2.$$

    For $a=3$ and $p=0.25$ ($q=0.75$), $\tilde a$ is $0$ with probability $0.25$ and $3/0.75=4$ with probability $0.75$, giving $\mathbb E[\tilde a]=3$ and $\operatorname{Var}(\tilde a)=\frac{0.25}{0.75}(9)=3$.

    2. Exact penalty in linear regression: feature-scaled L2

    What objective does averaging over dropout masks actually minimize? Take a linear predictor $s=\sum_{j=1}^d w_ja_j$ with noisy prediction $\tilde s=\sum_{j=1}^d w_j\tilde a_j$ under independent masks $m_j\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)$. Since $\mathbb E_m[\tilde s]=s$ and independent masks have zero cross-covariance, expanding the expected squared loss around the mean $s$ gives:

    $$\begin{aligned}\mathbb E_m\bigl[(y-\tilde s)^2\bigr]&=\mathbb E_m\bigl[((y-s)-(\tilde s-s))^2\bigr]\\&=(y-s)^2-2(y-s)\underbrace{\mathbb E_m[\tilde s-s]}_{0}+\operatorname{Var}_m(\tilde s)\\&=(y-s)^2+\frac{p}{q}\sum_{j=1}^d a_j^2\,w_j^2.\end{aligned}$$

    In linear squared-error regression, inverted dropout with drop rate $p$ is mathematically identical in expectation to L2 ridge regression with penalty $\lambda_j=\frac{p}{1-p}a_j^2$, penalizing weights most heavily on high-energy features.

    3. Activation Dropout vs. DropConnect: shared vs. independent noise

    For a layer $z=Wa$, compare masking the input vector vs. masking the weight matrix with $q=1-p$:

    • Activation Dropout: $\tilde z=W\left(\frac{m}{q}\odot a\right)$ with vector mask $m\in\{0,1\}^{n_{\rm in}}$. Because output rows $r$ and $s$ share the same dropped inputs $m_j$, their outputs co-vary across masks:
      $$\operatorname{Cov}_m(\tilde z_r,\tilde z_s\mid a)=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\rm in}}W_{rj}W_{sj}\operatorname{Var}_m(\tilde a_j)=\frac{p}{q}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\rm in}}W_{rj}W_{sj}a_j^2.$$
    • DropConnect: $\tilde z=\left(\frac{M}{q}\odot W\right)a$ with matrix mask $M_{rj}\stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)$. Each output row uses independent mask entries, so $\operatorname{Cov}_m(\tilde z_r,\tilde z_s\mid a)=0$ for $r\ne s$.

    5. Implementation: training vs. evaluation mode

    def inverted_dropout(a: torch.Tensor, p: float, training: bool) -> torch.Tensor:
    if not training or p == 0.0:
        return a
    q = 1.0 - p
    mask = (torch.rand_like(a) < q).to(a.dtype)
    return a * mask / q

    At evaluation (`model.eval()`), inverted dropout is the identity function. Only MC Dropout keeps dropout active at test time to average $K$ stochastic forward passes $\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K f(x;m^{(k)})$ as an approximate ensemble.

    6. Questions worth answering

    Why does inverted dropout scale surviving units up by $1/(1-p)$ if it is a regularizer?

    So that $\mathbb E_m[\tilde a]=a$ during training, allowing evaluation (`model.eval()`) to use the unmasked network with no rescaling. The regularization comes from the variance $\frac{p}{1-p}a^2$ and prevented co-adaptation, not from shrinking activation means.

    Why do Activation Dropout and DropConnect have the same mean $\mathbb E[\tilde z]=Wa$ but different covariances?

    In Activation Dropout, dropping input $a_j$ removes $W_{rj}a_j$ and $W_{sj}a_j$ simultaneously from outputs $r$ and $s$, coupling them with covariance $\frac{p}{q}\sum_j W_{rj}W_{sj}a_j^2$. DropConnect samples independent masks $M_{rj}$ and $M_{sj}$ for each edge.
