    Advantages and disadvantages at a glance

    Compare the property you need, rather than treating one activation as best for every layer. Each linked chapter gives the function-specific advantages and disadvantages.

    A quick comparison; these are properties and limitations, not a training-performance ranking.
    Function or familyAdvantagesDisadvantages
    Sigmoid / tanhBounded outputs: sigmoid suits binary probabilities or retention gates; tanh also allows signed values.Both tails saturate, weakening their local gradients. A bounded output does not guarantee calibration or zero mean.
    ReLU familySimple positive branch. ReLU gives exact zeros; leaky and learned variants retain a negative branch.ReLU can block negative-side gradients. Variants need a slope setting or learned parameters.
    Softplus / ELU / SELUOffer positive outputs, negative responses, or a self-normalizing recipe, respectively.Negative-tail gradients can be small. SELU's normalization claim depends on the network setup.
    GELUSmooth input weighting with a small negative region and a nearly linear positive tail.Its negative tail can still have tiny gradients. Exact and approximate implementations differ.
    SiLU / Swish / MishSmooth self-gating retains moderate negative inputs; Swish can tune the transition.Extra nonlinear computation, small negative-tail gradients, and no guaranteed training advantage.
    SwiGLUOne learned feature branch can control another through multiplication.Adds a projection at fixed width. Equal-budget comparisons need a narrower intermediate layer.
    SoftmaxNormalizes competing scores into a distribution across classes or attention positions.Forces competition, so it does not suit independent labels. Probabilities can still be poorly calibrated.
    Custom smooth leaky functionDirect control of the negative slope and transition width.Adds settings and computation; mathematical guarantees do not establish better training.

    Keep the symbols consistent

    $z$ is the input to a scalar activation, $f(z)$ its output, and $f^\prime(z)$ its slope. $\sigma$ denotes sigmoid, while $\Phi$ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. In the SwiGLU chapter, $x$ is a vector and $\odot$ means coordinate-by-coordinate multiplication.

    Hidden activations create features. Output activations enforce the form of a prediction. Softmax couples a vector of scores; SwiGLU combines learned branches. Those distinctions matter more than collecting a long list of names.
