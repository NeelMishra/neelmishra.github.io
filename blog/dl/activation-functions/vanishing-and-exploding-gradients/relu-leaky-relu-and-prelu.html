    2. ReLU, Leaky ReLU, and PReLU: Dead Neurons and Unbounded Forward Growth

    October 2026 · 7 min read

    The ReLU family was introduced to eliminate the positive-tail saturation of Sigmoid and Tanh by setting $f(z) = z$ with constant unit slope $f'(z) = 1$ for all $z > 0$. However, replacing smooth saturation with a hard hinge creates two new failure modes: irreversible vanishing gradients on the negative half-line (the Dying ReLU problem) and unbounded exploding activations and gradients on the positive half-line.

    1. Unified slope and variance formula for the ReLU family

    ReLU ($\alpha = 0$), Leaky ReLU (fixed small $\alpha > 0$, such as $\alpha = 0.01$), and PReLU (learned channel-wise $\alpha$) share the piecewise linear form

    $$f(z) = \begin{cases} z, & z > 0, \\ \alpha z, & z \le 0, \end{cases} \qquad f'(z) = \begin{cases} 1, & z > 0, \\ \alpha, & z < 0. \end{cases}$$

    In a layer of width $d$ with independent zero-mean weights of variance $\sigma_w^2$ and symmetric pre-activations ($P(z > 0) = P(z < 0) = 1/2$), the expected squared derivative is

    $$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(f'(z)\bigr)^{2}\bigr] = \frac{1}{2}(1)^{2} + \frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2} = \frac{1 + \alpha^{2}}{2}.$$

    Multiplying by the $d$ incoming connections, the backward gradient variance changes across each layer by the factor

    $$\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(\ell-1)}}\right) = \underbrace{\left(\frac{1 + \alpha^{2}}{2}\,d\,\sigma_w^{2}\right)}_{r} \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(\ell)}}\right).$$

    Whether gradients vanish ($r < 1$), stay stable ($r = 1$), or explode ($r > 1$) across $L$ layers is completely governed by this per-layer multiplier $r$ and by the sign of individual pre-activations $z_i$.

    2. Causes of vanishing gradients in the ReLU family

    Cause 2a: The Dying ReLU problem ($\alpha = 0$)

    For standard ReLU ($\alpha = 0$), any negative pre-activation $z_i = w_i^{\mathsf T}h + b_i < 0$ produces output $h_i^{\mathrm{next}} = 0$ and local derivative $\operatorname{ReLU}'(z_i) = 0$. By the chain rule, the gradients for that neuron's weight vector $w_i$ and bias $b_i$ are identically zero:

    $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial w_i} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h_i^{\mathrm{next}}}\cdot \operatorname{ReLU}'(z_i)\cdot h = 0, \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial b_i} = 0.$$

    Unlike Sigmoid—where $\sigma'(-5) \approx 0.0067$ is tiny but still nonzero so the neuron can eventually recover—a ReLU neuron whose pre-activation $z_i < 0$ across the entire training set receives exact zero gradient forever. A single overly large gradient step or negative bias update can knock a large fraction of a layer's neurons permanently offline.

    Cause 2b: Half-space variance decay under Xavier initialization

    Even when half of the neurons fire ($z > 0$), plain ReLU zeros out the other half, giving $\mathbb{E}[(\operatorname{ReLU}'(z))^2] = 1/2$. If a deep ReLU network is initialized with standard Xavier variance $\sigma_w^2 = 1/d$ (which assumes unit slope at the origin like Tanh), the per-layer variance factor is

    $$r = \frac{1 + 0}{2}\cdot d \cdot \frac{1}{d} = \frac{1}{2} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(0)}}\right) = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{L} \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L)}}\right).$$

    In a $30$-layer ReLU network initialized with Xavier weights, the gradient variance shrinks by $(1/2)^{30} \approx 9.31 \times 10^{-10}$, stalling training before it begins.

    Cause 2c: All-negative paths in Leaky ReLU and PReLU

    Leaky ReLU ($0 < \alpha \ll 1$, typically $\alpha = 0.01$) and PReLU replace the zero slope on $z < 0$ with $\alpha$, ensuring $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial w_i} = \alpha\,\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h_i^{\mathrm{next}}}\,h \neq 0$ so dead neurons can recover. However, along any path where $L$ consecutive layers have negative pre-activations, the gradient still attenuates exponentially as $\alpha^{L} = (0.01)^{L} = 10^{-2L}$.

    3. Causes of exploding gradients in the ReLU family

    1. Unbounded positive ray provides no saturation brake: Because $f(z) = z$ grows without bound as $z \to +\infty$ and $f'(z) = 1$ never decreases, positive activations cannot self-limit. Whenever the weight variance exceeds Kaiming/He scaling,
      $$\sigma_w^{2} > \frac{2}{(1 + \alpha^{2})\,d} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad r = \frac{1 + \alpha^{2}}{2}\,d\,\sigma_w^{2} > 1,$$
      both the forward activation norm $\lVert h^{(\ell)}\rVert_2$ and the backward gradient norm $\lVert \partial \mathcal{L}/\partial h^{(0)}\rVert_2$ grow exponentially as $r^{L/2} \to \infty$.
    2. Repeated multiplication in Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs): In a recurrent loop $h_t = \operatorname{ReLU}(W_{hh} h_{t-1} + W_{xh} x_t)$ sharing the same weight matrix $W_{hh}$ across $T$ time steps, if the largest eigenvalue of $W_{hh}$ on the active subspace exceeds $1$ ($\rho(W_{hh}) > 1$), both $h_T$ and $\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial h_1$ blow up as $\rho(W_{hh})^{T}$.
    3. Unregularized $\alpha > 1$ in PReLU: In PReLU, $\alpha$ is updated via $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \alpha} = \sum_{z_i < 0} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h_i^{\mathrm{next}}} z_i$. If $\alpha$ drifts above $1$ during training, the negative branch amplifies gradients by $\alpha > 1$ and raises the layer variance multiplier $r = \frac{1+\alpha^2}{2}d\sigma_w^2 > 1$.

    4. Cause, effect, and numerical comparison

    Summary of vanishing and exploding gradient mechanisms in ReLU, Leaky ReLU, and PReLU.
    PhenomenonMathematical causeObserved effect during training
    Dying ReLU$\operatorname{ReLU}'(z) = 0$ for all $z < 0$.Permanent zero columns in hidden representations; a large learning rate spike can kill $40\%+$ of units irreversibly.
    Xavier-initialized decay$\mathbb{E}[(\operatorname{ReLU}'(z))^2] = 0.5 \implies r = 0.5$ when $\sigma_w^2 = 1/d$.Forward variance and backward gradient variance halve at every layer; deep plain ReLU nets (>20 layers) fail to converge.
    Positive-ray explosion$f(z) = z$ is unbounded and $f'(z) = 1$ for all $z > 0$.If $\sigma_w^2 > \frac{2}{(1+\alpha^2)d}$ or learning rate is too high, activations and gradients blow up to Inf/NaN within a few steps.
    PReLU negative amplificationLearned $\alpha$ exceeds $1$, making $\frac{1+\alpha^2}{2} > 1$.Negative pre-activations amplify instead of contracting, destabilizing deep unnormalized blocks.

    Ten-layer variance check ($L = 10$). Compare the end-to-end gradient variance multiplier $r^{10} = \bigl(\frac{1+\alpha^2}{2}d\sigma_w^2\bigr)^{10}$ across $10$ layers:

    • ReLU ($\alpha = 0$) with Xavier init ($d\sigma_w^2 = 1$): $r = 0.50 \implies r^{10} = 0.50^{10} = \mathbf{9.77 \times 10^{-4}}$ (vanishes by $1{,}024\times$).
    • ReLU ($\alpha = 0$) with He init ($d\sigma_w^2 = 2$): $r = \frac{1}{2}(2) = 1.00 \implies r^{10} = \mathbf{1.00}$ (variance preserved).
    • ReLU ($\alpha = 0$) with over-scaled weights ($d\sigma_w^2 = 3$): $r = 1.50 \implies r^{10} = 1.50^{10} \approx \mathbf{57.67}$ (explodes).
    • PReLU with learned $\alpha = 1.2$ under standard He init ($d\sigma_w^2 = 2$): $r = \frac{1 + 1.44}{2}(2) = 2.44 \implies r^{10} = 2.44^{10} \approx \mathbf{7{,}471.8}$ (explodes unless initialized with $d\sigma_w^2 = \frac{2}{1+\alpha^2}$).
