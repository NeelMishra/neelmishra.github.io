    5. SwiGLU: Multiplicative Gate Coupling and Quadratic Outlier Explosion

    October 2026 · 7 min read

    Unlike scalar activations that apply a curve to a single pre-activation $z$, SwiGLU splits the input vector $x$ into two learned linear projections—a gate branch $u = W_{\mathrm{gate}}x$ and a value branch $v = W_{\mathrm{up}}x$—and multiplies them coordinate by coordinate:

    $$h = \operatorname{SiLU}(u) \odot v, \qquad h_i = \operatorname{SiLU}(u_i)\cdot v_i.$$

    Because the two branches multiply each other, SwiGLU changes the mathematics of both vanishing and exploding gradients: either branch can zero out the other's gradient when small, while large positive inputs make the output grow quadratically ($\propto \lVert x\rVert^2$) and the backward Jacobian grow linearly with activation magnitude.

    1. Coupled backward gradients via the product rule

    Given the upstream gradient $g_i = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h_i}$ arriving from the output projection $W_{\mathrm{down}}$, the partial derivatives with respect to the gate pre-activation $u_i$ and the value pre-activation $v_i$ are

    $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial u_i} = g_i \cdot v_i \cdot \operatorname{SiLU}'(u_i), \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial v_i} = g_i \cdot \operatorname{SiLU}(u_i).$$

    Combining both paths by the chain rule gives the total gradient back into the block's input $x$:

    $$\frac{\partial h_i}{\partial x} = v_i\,\operatorname{SiLU}'(u_i)\,(W_{\mathrm{gate}})_{i,:} + \operatorname{SiLU}(u_i)\,(W_{\mathrm{up}})_{i,:}.$$

    Notice that $v_i$ multiplies the gate gradient and $\operatorname{SiLU}(u_i)$ multiplies the value gradient.

    2. Causes of vanishing gradients in SwiGLU

    Cause 2a: Mutual gate-value starvation

    In a single-branch layer, only the activation slope $f'(z_i)$ can block the gradient. In SwiGLU, three separate conditions cause vanishing gradients in coordinate $i$:

    1. Closed negative gate ($u_i \ll -2$): When $u_i$ falls deep into SiLU's negative tail, both $\operatorname{SiLU}(u_i) \approx 0$ and $\operatorname{SiLU}'(u_i) \approx 0$. This simultaneously kills $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial v_i} = g_i\operatorname{SiLU}(u_i) \approx 0$ and $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial u_i} = g_i v_i \operatorname{SiLU}'(u_i) \approx 0$, freezing both row $i$ of $W_{\mathrm{up}}$ and row $i$ of $W_{\mathrm{gate}}$.
    2. Zero value branch ($v_i \approx 0$) or zero gate origin ($u_i = 0$): If $v_i = 0$, then $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial u_i} = 0$ even when $\operatorname{SiLU}'(u_i)$ is large. Conversely, if $u_i = 0$, then $\operatorname{SiLU}(0) = 0$, so $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial v_i} = 0$ completely blocks learning on $W_{\mathrm{up}}$.
    3. SiLU trough on the gate ($u_i \approx -1.2785$): At $u_i^* \approx -1.2785$, $\operatorname{SiLU}'(u_i^*) = 0$ blocks the gate gradient $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial u_i} = 0$, while $\operatorname{SiLU}(u_i^*) \approx -0.2785$ still lets a reduced gradient reach $v_i$.

    Cause 2b: Quartic variance shrinkage ($\sigma^4$) under small weight initialization

    Suppose $u_i$ and $v_i$ are independent zero-mean Gaussian pre-activations with small variance $\sigma^2 \ll 1$. Because $h_i = \operatorname{SiLU}(u_i)\,v_i$ is the product of two random variables, its variance is the product of their second moments:

    $$\operatorname{Var}(h_i) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(\operatorname{SiLU}(u_i)\bigr)^{2}\bigr] \cdot \mathbb{E}\bigl[v_i^{2}\bigr] \;\approx\; \frac{1}{4}\sigma^{2} \cdot \sigma^{2} = \frac{1}{4}\sigma^{4}.$$

    If weights are initialized too small ($\sigma^2 = 0.1$), a single SwiGLU block shrinks signal variance to $\frac{1}{4}(0.1)^2 = 0.0025$—ten times worse than a single-branch layer!

    3. Causes of exploding gradients in SwiGLU

    1. Quadratic forward activation growth ($h_i \propto \lVert x\rVert^2$): For large positive pre-activations $u_i, v_i \gg 1$, $\operatorname{SiLU}(u_i) \approx u_i$, so SwiGLU reduces to a quadratic form:
      $$h_i = \operatorname{SiLU}(u_i)\,v_i \;\approx\; u_i v_i = \bigl((W_{\mathrm{gate}}x)_i\bigr)\bigl((W_{\mathrm{up}}x)_i\bigr) \;\propto\; \lVert x \rVert_{2}^{2}.$$
      If an input token $x$ contains an outlier feature that is $10\times$ larger than typical tokens, both $u_i$ and $v_i$ grow by $10\times$, causing the intermediate activation $h_i$ entering $W_{\mathrm{down}}$ to spike by $10 \times 10 = 100\times$ (frequently overflowing FP16's $65{,}504$ ceiling in large Transformers).
    2. Unbounded linear growth of the local Jacobian ($\lVert \partial h_i / \partial x \rVert \propto \lVert x \rVert$): In any single-branch activation (Sigmoid, Tanh, ReLU, GELU, SiLU), the local derivative $|f'(z)|$ is bounded by a constant ($\le 1.13$) no matter how large $z$ gets. In SwiGLU, the partial derivatives with respect to $u_i$ and $v_i$ are $v_i\operatorname{SiLU}'(u_i) \approx v_i$ and $\operatorname{SiLU}(u_i) \approx u_i$, which grow linearly without bound as $|u_i|$ and $|v_i|$ increase! A $10\times$ input spike multiplies the backward Jacobian norm by $10\times$, amplifying gradients into earlier layers and weight matrices ($\nabla_{W_{\mathrm{down}}}\mathcal{L} = g\,h^{\mathsf T} \propto \lVert x\rVert^2$).

    4. Cause, effect, and numerical comparison

    Summary of vanishing and exploding gradient mechanisms in SwiGLU.
    PhenomenonMathematical causeObserved effect during training & inference
    Dual-branch starvation$\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial u_i \propto v_i\operatorname{SiLU}'(u_i)$ and $\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial v_i \propto \operatorname{SiLU}(u_i)$.If $u_i \ll -2$, both branches freeze at once; if $u_i = 0$ or $v_i = 0$, the partner branch receives zero gradient.
    Under-scaled $\sigma^4$ collapse$\operatorname{Var}(h_i) = \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{SiLU}(u_i)^2]\mathbb{E}[v_i^2] \propto \sigma^4$.Deep unnormalized SwiGLU networks vanish quadratically fast if initialized below unit pre-activation variance.
    Quadratic outlier spike$h_i \approx u_i v_i \propto \lVert x\rVert^2$ for $u_i, v_i > 0$.Produces massive activation outliers at the input to $W_{\mathrm{down}}$ (requiring BF16/FP32 training and AWQ/SmoothQuant channel scaling at inference).
    Unbounded Jacobian norm$\partial h_i / \partial u_i \approx v_i$ and $\partial h_i / \partial v_i \approx u_i$.Gradient spikes scale linearly with activation magnitude ($O(\lVert x\rVert)$) and weight gradients $\nabla_{W_{\mathrm{down}}}\mathcal{L}$ scale quadratically ($O(\lVert x\rVert^2)$).

    Numerical check: $10\times$ input spike in SiLU versus SwiGLU. Compare a single SiLU neuron $a = \operatorname{SiLU}(u)$ against a SwiGLU unit $h = \operatorname{SiLU}(u)\cdot v$ when pre-activations jump from $(u, v) = (1.0, 1.0)$ to $(10.0, 10.0)$ with upstream gradient $g = 1$:

    • At normal scale $(u, v) = (1.0, 1.0)$:
      • Standard SiLU: output $a = \operatorname{SiLU}(1) \approx 0.7311$, backward derivative $\partial a/\partial u = \operatorname{SiLU}'(1) \approx 0.9277$.
      • SwiGLU: output $h = 0.7311 \times 1.0 = \mathbf{0.7311}$, backward derivatives $(\partial h/\partial u,\, \partial h/\partial v) = (\mathbf{0.9277},\, \mathbf{0.7311})$.
    • At $10\times$ outlier scale $(u, v) = (10.0, 10.0)$:
      • Standard SiLU: output $a = \operatorname{SiLU}(10) \approx 9.9995$ ($13.7\times$), backward derivative $\partial a/\partial u = \operatorname{SiLU}'(10) \approx \mathbf{1.0004}$ (stays bounded near $1$!).
      • SwiGLU: output $h = 9.9995 \times 10.0 = \mathbf{99.995}$ ($136.8\times$ larger), backward derivatives $(\partial h/\partial u,\, \partial h/\partial v) = (\mathbf{10.004},\, \mathbf{9.9995})$ (more than $10\times$ larger!).
