    4. SiLU and Swish: Zero-Slope Troughs, Origin Halving, and Beta Sensitivity

    October 2026 · 6 min read

    SiLU and Swish multiply the input $z$ by its own logistic gate: $\operatorname{SiLU}(z) = z\sigma(z)$ and $\operatorname{Swish}_{\beta}(z) = z\sigma(\beta z)$. Because the function is smooth and non-monotonic, its derivative passes through three distinct regimes that directly govern vanishing and exploding gradients.

    1. The derivative of Swish and its critical points

    Differentiating $\operatorname{Swish}_{\beta}(z) = z\sigma(\beta z)$ with respect to $z$ by the product rule (using $\frac{d}{dz}\sigma(\beta z) = \beta\sigma(\beta z)(1 - \sigma(\beta z))$) yields

    $$\operatorname{Swish}'_{\beta}(z) = \sigma(\beta z)\Bigl(1 + \beta z\bigl(1 - \sigma(\beta z)\bigr)\Bigr).$$

    Notice that $\operatorname{Swish}'_{\beta}(z)$ depends only on the dimensionless product $u = \beta z$. Changing $\beta$ stretches or compresses the horizontal axis $z$, but the minimum slope ($-0.0998$), origin slope ($0.5$), and maximum slope ($+1.0998$) remain identical for every $\beta > 0$.

    2. Causes of vanishing gradients in SiLU and Swish

    Cause 2a: Exact zero slope at the trough ($\beta z^* \approx -1.2785$)

    Because $\sigma(u) > 0$ for all finite $u$, $\operatorname{Swish}'_{\beta}(z) = 0$ occurs precisely where the bracketed factor vanishes:

    $$1 + u\bigl(1 - \sigma(u)\bigr) = 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad 1 + u + e^{u} = 0.$$

    Solving this transcendental equation gives the unique root $u^{*} \approx -1.27846$. At $z^{*} = -1.2785/\beta$:

    $$\operatorname{Swish}_{\beta}(z^{*}) \approx -\frac{0.2785}{\beta}, \qquad \operatorname{Swish}'_{\beta}(z^{*}) = 0.$$

    Any neuron whose pre-activation lands near $-1.28/\beta$ multiplies its backward gradient by zero. Just to the left of this trough, the derivative turns negative, reaching $\min_z \operatorname{Swish}'_{\beta}(z) \approx -0.0998$ at $\beta z \approx -2.3994$.

    Cause 2b: Universal $0.5$ slope at the origin ($z = 0$)

    Substituting $z = 0$ into $\operatorname{Swish}'_{\beta}(z)$ gives

    $$\operatorname{Swish}'_{\beta}(0) = \sigma(0)\bigl(1 + 0\bigr) = \frac{1}{2}.$$

    No matter how large you make $\beta$, the slope right at $z = 0$ is always $0.5$. In a deep network where pre-activations start small ($|z| \ll 1/\beta$) and weights have unit scale, each layer halves the gradient magnitude, decaying across $L$ layers as $(0.5)^{L}$.

    Cause 2c: Exponential negative-tail saturation ($\beta z \ll -2$)

    As $z \to -\infty$, $\sigma(\beta z) \approx e^{\beta z} \to 0$, so the derivative decays exponentially:

    $$\operatorname{Swish}'_{\beta}(z) \approx (1 + \beta z)\,e^{\beta z} \to 0 \quad (\beta z \to -\infty).$$

    Increasing $\beta$ shrinks the negative tail $e^{\beta z}$ faster: at $z = -3$, standard SiLU ($\beta = 1$) has $|\operatorname{SiLU}'(-3)| \approx 0.090$, whereas $\operatorname{Swish}_{3}(-3)$ has $|\operatorname{Swish}'_{3}(-3)| \approx 0.00099$—nearly $100\times$ smaller.

    3. Causes of exploding gradients in SiLU and Swish

    1. Super-unit overshoot peak ($\operatorname{Swish}'_{\beta} \approx 1.0998 > 1$): On the positive half-line, the term $\beta z(1 - \sigma(\beta z)) > 0$ adds to $\sigma(\beta z)$, pushing the slope above $1$ for all $\beta z > 1.2785$. The slope peaks at $\beta z \approx +2.3994$:
      $$\max_{z}\operatorname{Swish}'_{\beta}(z) \approx 1.0998 > 1 \quad (\text{at } \beta z \approx 2.3994).$$
      Across $L$ layers with pre-activations near $2.4/\beta$, the activation slope alone amplifies gradients by $(1.0998)^{L}$.
    2. Unbounded positive ray and $\beta$-driven curvature spikes: Like ReLU, $\operatorname{Swish}_{\beta}(z) \approx z$ for large positive $z$, so weight matrices exceeding variance-preserving initialization ($\mathbb{E}[(\operatorname{SiLU}'(z))^2] \approx 0.355$ for $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, requiring $d\sigma_w^2 \approx 2.82$) cause exponential explosion. Furthermore, the second derivative at the origin scales linearly with $\beta$,
      $$\operatorname{Swish}''_{\beta}(0) = \frac{\beta}{2},$$
      and when $\beta$ is learned, its parameter gradient $\frac{\partial \operatorname{Swish}_{\beta}(z)}{\partial \beta} = z^{2}\sigma(\beta z)(1 - \sigma(\beta z))$ grows quadratically with $z$, making large-logit outliers destabilize $\beta$ updates.

    4. Cause, effect, and numerical comparison

    Summary of vanishing and exploding gradient mechanisms in SiLU and Swish.
    RegimeMathematical causeObserved effect during training
    Trough zero ($\beta z \approx -1.28$)$1 + \beta z(1 - \sigma(\beta z)) = 0 \implies f'(z) = 0$.Blocks gradient flow for units sitting at the bottom of the negative dip.
    Origin halving ($z \approx 0$)$\operatorname{Swish}'_{\beta}(0) = 0.5$ for all $\beta$.Small-variance initializations without residual connections attenuate gradients by $0.5^{L}$.
    Negative tail ($\beta z \ll -2$)$\operatorname{Swish}'_{\beta}(z) \approx (1 + \beta z)e^{\beta z}$.Large $\beta$ accelerates negative-tail saturation toward ReLU's dead zone.
    Positive overshoot ($\beta z \approx 2.40$)$\max_z \operatorname{Swish}'_{\beta}(z) \approx 1.0998 > 1$.Amplifies gradients along positive-shifted paths when weights are unnormalized.

    Ten-layer numerical check ($L = 10$, $\beta = 1$). Compare $\bigl|\operatorname{SiLU}'(z)\bigr|^{10}$ across $10$ layers with unit weights ($w = 1$) at four locations:

    • Negative tail ($z = -6$): $|\operatorname{SiLU}'(-6)| \approx 0.0123 \implies (0.0123)^{10} \approx \mathbf{8.11 \times 10^{-20}}$.
    • Negative trough ($z = -1.2785$): $|\operatorname{SiLU}'(-1.2785)| = 0 \implies \mathbf{0.0}$.
    • Origin ($z = 0$): $|\operatorname{SiLU}'(0)| = 0.50 \implies 0.50^{10} = \mathbf{9.77 \times 10^{-4}}$.
    • Overshoot peak ($z = +2.3994$): $|\operatorname{SiLU}'(2.3994)| \approx 1.0998 \implies 1.0998^{10} \approx \mathbf{2.59}$.
