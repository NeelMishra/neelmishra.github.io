    3. GELU, ELU, and SELU: Gaussian Tail Decay, Overshoot Peaks, and Fixed-Point Breakdown

    October 2026 · 7 min read

    GELU, ELU, and SELU replace ReLU's sharp corner at $z = 0$ with smooth negative branches that allow negative values to shift hidden representations toward zero mean. Yet each of these functions introduces specific gradient stability regimes: GELU has an exact zero-derivative trough and a super-unit slope peak ($f'(z) > 1$), while SELU relies on a fragile variance fixed point that explodes if paired with the wrong weight initialization.

    1. GELU: zero-slope trough, Gaussian tail decay, and the $\sqrt{2}$ overshoot peak

    Recall that $\operatorname{GELU}(z) = z\Phi(z)$, where $\Phi(z)$ is the standard Gaussian CDF and $\phi(z) = \Phi'(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-z^2/2}$ is the Gaussian density. Differentiating once and twice by the product rule (using $\phi'(z) = -z\phi(z)$) gives

    $$\operatorname{GELU}'(z) = \Phi(z) + z\phi(z), \qquad \operatorname{GELU}''(z) = \phi(z)(2 - z^{2}).$$

    Setting $\operatorname{GELU}'(z) = 0$ and $\operatorname{GELU}''(z) = 0$ pinpoints the exact causes of vanishing and exploding gradients in GELU:

    Causes of vanishing gradients in GELU

    1. Exact zero derivative at the negative trough ($z^* \approx -0.7518$): Because $\operatorname{GELU}(z)$ dips below zero before approaching $0$ as $z \to -\infty$, its derivative crosses zero where $\Phi(z^*) + z^*\phi(z^*) = 0$, which occurs at
      $$z^{*} \approx -0.7518, \qquad \operatorname{GELU}(z^{*}) \approx -0.1700, \qquad \operatorname{GELU}'(z^{*}) = 0.$$
      Any neuron whose pre-activation sits near $z \approx -0.75$ annihilates the backward gradient just like a dead ReLU.
    2. Origin halving ($\operatorname{GELU}'(0) = 0.5$): At $z = 0$, $\Phi(0) = 0.5$ and $0\cdot\phi(0) = 0$, so $\operatorname{GELU}'(0) = 0.5$. For small pre-activations clustered near the origin ($|z| \ll 1$), each uncompensated GELU layer cuts the gradient magnitude in half ($0.5^{L}$).
    3. Gaussian negative-tail decay ($z \ll -2$): For $z < -\sqrt{2} \approx -1.4142$ (where $\operatorname{GELU}'(z)$ reaches its most negative slope $-0.1288$), both $\Phi(z)$ and $z\phi(z)$ decay as $e^{-z^2/2}$—much faster than Sigmoid's $e^{-|z|}$. By $z = -4$, $\operatorname{GELU}'(-4) \approx -0.00050$.

    Causes of exploding gradients in GELU

    1. Super-unit derivative overshoot on $z \in (0.42, \infty)$: Because $\operatorname{GELU}''(z) = \phi(z)(2 - z^2) = 0$ at $z = +\sqrt{2} \approx 1.4142$, the slope $\operatorname{GELU}'(z)$ rises above $1$ and peaks at
      $$\operatorname{GELU}'(\sqrt{2}) = \Phi(\sqrt{2}) + \sqrt{2}\phi(\sqrt{2}) \approx 0.9214 + 0.2076 = 1.1289 > 1.$$
      Unlike Sigmoid, Tanh, or ReLU (whose slopes never exceed $1$), GELU itself amplifies gradients by up to $1.1289\times$ per layer when pre-activations sit near $z \approx 1.41$.
    2. Unbounded positive ray without variance calibration: For $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, $\mathbb{E}[(\operatorname{GELU}'(z))^2] \approx 0.425$. In deep stacks without normalization or residual scaling ($1/\sqrt{2L}$), weight variance exceeding $d\sigma_w^2 \approx 1/0.425 \approx 2.35$ triggers exponential blow-up across the unbounded positive branch.

    2. ELU and SELU: negative saturation versus the $\lambda > 1$ fixed-point trap

    ELU and SELU replace the negative half-line with a shifted exponential, where SELU multiplies both branches by a fixed scale $\lambda \approx 1.050701$ (with $\alpha \approx 1.673263$):

    $$\operatorname{SELU}(z) = \lambda\begin{cases} z, & z > 0, \\ \alpha(e^{z} - 1), & z \le 0, \end{cases} \qquad \operatorname{SELU}'(z) = \begin{cases} \lambda \approx 1.0507, & z > 0, \\ \lambda\alpha e^{z} \approx 1.7581\,e^{z}, & z \le 0. \end{cases}$$
    • Why ELU and SELU vanish in the negative tail: As $z \to -\infty$, the output saturates at the horizontal asymptote $-\lambda\alpha \approx -1.7581$, and the derivative $\lambda\alpha e^z \to 0$ decays exponentially. At $z = -6$, $\operatorname{SELU}'(-6) \approx 0.00436$.
    • Why SELU has $\lambda \approx 1.0507 > 1$ (and when it explodes): Klambauer et al. (2017) chose $(\lambda, \alpha)$ so that if layer $\ell-1$ has mean $0$ and variance $1$ and the weights use LeCun normal initialization ($d\sigma_w^2 = 1$), the positive slope $\lambda > 1$ exactly offsets the variance lost in the saturating negative tail ($\lambda\alpha e^z$). However, this balance breaks immediately in two common situations:
      1. Pairing SELU with He initialization ($d\sigma_w^2 = 2$): Because SELU already includes $\lambda \approx 1.0507$ and a deeper negative branch to preserve variance at $d\sigma_w^2 = 1$, doubling the weight variance to $d\sigma_w^2 = 2$ multiplies activation and gradient variance by roughly $2\times$ at every layer ($2^{L}$ explosion).
      2. Positive pre-activation drift ($z > 0$): Along any path where inputs stay positive ($z > 0$), $\operatorname{SELU}'(z) = \lambda \approx 1.0507 > 1$ is strictly expanding, so even unit-norm weights amplify gradients by $\lambda^{L}$.

    3. Cause, effect, and numerical comparison

    Summary of vanishing and exploding gradient mechanisms in GELU, ELU, and SELU.
    PhenomenonMathematical causeObserved effect during training
    GELU trough blocking$\operatorname{GELU}'(-0.7518) = 0$ and $\operatorname{GELU}'(z) < 0$ on $(-3.1, -0.75)$.Units near $z \approx -0.75$ pass zero gradient; units in $(-3, -0.75)$ flip the sign of the incoming gradient.
    GELU Gaussian tail$\operatorname{GELU}'(z) \sim z\phi(z) \propto |z|e^{-z^2/2}$ for $z \ll -2$.Pre-activations below $z < -3.5$ are strongly attenuated, though smooth curvature near $0$ prevents permanent death.
    GELU $\sqrt{2}$ overshoot$\max_z \operatorname{GELU}'(z) = 1.1289$ at $z = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.4142$.Amplifies gradients by up to $1.13\times$ per layer in unnormalized positive-shifted blocks.
    SELU + He init explosion$\operatorname{SELU}'(z) = \lambda \approx 1.0507$ expects $d\sigma_w^2 = 1$, not $d\sigma_w^2 = 2$.Rapid activation and gradient blow-up in deep SELU MLPs when standard ReLU/He initialization is used by mistake.

    Twenty-layer numerical check ($L = 20$). Compare the cumulative derivative product $\prod_{\ell=1}^{20}\bigl|f'(z)\bigr|$ across $20$ layers with unit weights ($w = 1$):

    • GELU at the trough ($z = -0.7518$): $\operatorname{GELU}'(-0.7518) = 0 \implies \mathbf{0.0}$ in a single layer.
    • GELU at the origin ($z = 0$): $\operatorname{GELU}'(0) = 0.50 \implies 0.50^{20} \approx \mathbf{9.54 \times 10^{-7}}$.
    • GELU at the overshoot peak ($z = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.4142$): $\operatorname{GELU}'(\sqrt{2}) \approx 1.1289 \implies 1.1289^{20} \approx \mathbf{11.31}$.
    • SELU on a positive path ($z > 0$) with LeCun weights ($w = 1$): $\lambda^{20} = 1.0507^{20} \approx \mathbf{2.69}$ (and with He weight scale $w = \sqrt{2}$, $(\sqrt{2}\lambda)^{20} \approx \mathbf{2{,}749.6}$).
