    6. Softmax: Logit-Gap Saturation, Rank-Deficient Jacobians, and Temperature Explosion

    October 2026 · 7 min read

    Softmax appears in two critical places: as the output probability layer of a classifier, and inside every self-attention head of a Transformer ($A = \operatorname{softmax}(QK^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt{d_k})$). Because Softmax couples all $K$ coordinates through a shared denominator, its $K \times K$ Jacobian matrix can either collapse entirely to zero (freezing attention Q/K weights or MSE classifiers) or blow up as $1/T$ when temperature is small or exponentials overflow.

    1. The Softmax Jacobian and backward gradient formula

    At temperature $T > 0$, Softmax maps logits $z = (z_1, \dots, z_K)^{\mathsf T}$ to probabilities $p_i = \frac{e^{z_i / T}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}e^{z_k / T}}$. As derived in the Softmax chapter, its partial derivatives form the symmetric $K \times K$ Jacobian matrix

    $$J_{ij} = \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j} = \frac{1}{T}p_i(\delta_{ij} - p_j), \qquad J = \frac{1}{T}\bigl(\operatorname{diag}(p) - p p^{\mathsf T}\bigr).$$

    Given an upstream gradient vector $g_i = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial p_i}$ with respect to the probabilities, the chain rule gives the gradient with respect to logit $z_j$:

    $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_j} = \sum_{i=1}^{K} g_i J_{ij} = \frac{1}{T}p_j\Bigl(g_j - \sum_{i=1}^{K} g_i p_i\Bigr).$$

    Every single logit gradient $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_j}$ is multiplied upfront by $p_j / T$ and by the centered upstream gradient $g_j - \langle g, p\rangle$.

    2. Causes of vanishing gradients in Softmax

    Cause 2a: Logit-gap saturation collapses the entire $K \times K$ Jacobian to zero

    Suppose one logit $z_c$ is larger than all competing logits by a gap $\Delta z = z_c - \max_{j \neq c} z_j \gg T$. Then the winning probability approaches $p_c = 1 - \varepsilon$ while all other probabilities sum to $\sum_{j \neq c} p_j = \varepsilon \approx e^{-\Delta z / T} \ll 1$. Inspect the entries of $J = \frac{1}{T}(\operatorname{diag}(p) - pp^{\mathsf T})$:

    • Winning diagonal ($i = j = c$): $J_{cc} = \frac{1}{T}p_c(1 - p_c) = \frac{1}{T}(1 - \varepsilon)\varepsilon = O(\varepsilon) \to 0$.
    • Losing diagonals ($i = j \neq c$): $J_{jj} = \frac{1}{T}p_j(1 - p_j) \le \frac{\varepsilon}{T} = O(\varepsilon) \to 0$.
    • All off-diagonals ($i \neq j$): $|J_{ij}| = \frac{1}{T}p_i p_j \le \frac{\varepsilon}{T} = O(\varepsilon) \to 0$.

    When Softmax saturates toward a one-hot vector, every single entry of its $K \times K$ Jacobian matrix vanishes exponentially as $e^{-\Delta z / T}$. This causes two famous failures:

    1. Unscaled dot-product attention (why Vaswani et al. divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$): If query $q$ and key $k$ have independent zero-mean, unit-variance entries, their dot product $z_j = q^{\mathsf T}k_j = \sum_{m=1}^{d_k} q_m k_{j,m}$ has mean $0$ and variance $d_k$ (standard deviation $\sqrt{d_k}$). For $d_k = 128$, typical score gaps exceed $\sqrt{128} \approx 11.3$, making $\varepsilon \approx e^{-11.3} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-5}$. Without scaling by $1/\sqrt{d_k}$, attention weights immediately saturate to one-hot vectors and the gradients into $W_Q$ and $W_K$ vanish!
    2. Softmax paired with MSE loss (wrong-class saturation trap): Suppose the true class is $1$ ($y = [1, 0]$), but the model confidently predicts class $2$ with $p = [\varepsilon, 1 - \varepsilon]$ and uses squared error $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}} = \frac{1}{2}\lVert p - y\rVert_2^2 = (1 - \varepsilon)^2$. Because the upstream gradient $g = p - y = [\varepsilon - 1,\, 1 - \varepsilon]$ is bounded, multiplying by $J$ gives
      $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}}}{\partial z_1} = -2\varepsilon(1 - \varepsilon)^{2} \to 0 \quad (\varepsilon \to 0).$$
      Even though the prediction is completely wrong, the gradient vanishes! By contrast, Cross-Entropy $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}} = -\log p_1$ has an unbounded upstream gradient $g_1 = -1/p_1 = -1/\varepsilon$, which explicitly cancels the vanishing $p_1 = \varepsilon$ factor in $J_{1j} = p_1(\delta_{1j} - p_j)$ to leave $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}}{\partial z_1} = p_1 - 1 = -(1 - \varepsilon) \approx -1$.

    Cause 2b: Rank deficiency along the constant vector ($J\mathbf{1} = 0$)

    Because $\sum_{j=1}^{K} p_j = 1$, multiplying the Jacobian $J$ by the all-ones vector $\mathbf{1} = (1, 1, \dots, 1)^{\mathsf T}$ always gives zero:

    $$(J\mathbf{1})_i = \frac{1}{T}p_i\Bigl(1 - \sum_{j=1}^{K}p_j\Bigr) = 0.$$

    Any upstream gradient component where all $g_i$ are equal ($g = c\mathbf{1}$) lies in the nullspace of $J$ and produces exact zero gradient on the logits.

    3. Causes of exploding gradients in Softmax

    1. Small temperature ($T \to 0$) or runaway QK norms at decision boundaries: When two competing classes (or two attention tokens) have equal scores ($p_1 = p_2 = 0.5$), the Jacobian along $z_1 - z_2$ has eigenvalue
      $$\lambda_{\max}(J) = \frac{1}{2T}.$$
      As $T \to 0$ (or as unregularized query and key norms $\lVert Q\rVert \cdot \lVert K\rVert$ grow large during Transformer pre-training, acting as an effective temperature $T_{\mathrm{eff}} \propto 1/(\lVert Q\rVert\lVert K\rVert) \to 0$), the gradient right at a tied attention boundary explodes as $1/(2T) \to \infty$, triggering attention-logit instability and loss spikes (mitigated in modern LLMs by QK-Norm or logit soft-capping).
    2. Exponential overflow ($e^{z_i} \to \infty$) and naive $\log(p_i)$ underflow: In FP16, $e^{z_i}$ overflows to Inf at $z_i > 11.09$ unless $\max_k z_k$ is subtracted first. Conversely, if a loss computes $\log(\operatorname{softmax}(z)_c)$ in two separate steps instead of fused log_softmax, a losing probability that underflows to $p_c = 0.0$ creates an infinite upstream derivative $-1/p_c = -\infty$.

    4. Cause, effect, and numerical comparison

    Summary of vanishing and exploding gradient mechanisms in Softmax.
    PhenomenonMathematical causeObserved effect during training
    Unscaled attention saturation$\operatorname{Std}(q^{\mathsf T}k) = \sqrt{d_k} \implies p \to \text{one-hot}$, $J \to 0$.Query and key projections $W_Q, W_K$ receive near-zero gradients while value projection $W_V$ passes signals through frozen routing.
    Softmax + MSE stallBounded $\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial p$ cannot cancel $p_i(1-p_i) \to 0$.Confident wrong predictions never recover during training.
    Boundary $1/T$ spike$\lambda_{\max}(J) = \frac{1}{2T}$ when two logits tie at $p_1 = p_2 = 0.5$.Gradient norm spikes in contrastive losses with tiny $\tau$ or Transformer layers with growing Q/K weight norms.
    Log-probability underflow$p_c$ rounds to $0.0$ in FP16/FP32 before $\log(p_c)$.Immediate -Inf loss and NaN backward pass unless fused logsumexp is used.

    Numerical check: Two-class Softmax at logit gap $\Delta z = 10$ ($z = [0, 10]$, true class $y = [1, 0]$). Here $p_1 = \frac{1}{1+e^{10}} \approx 4.54 \times 10^{-5}$ and $p_2 = 1 - p_1 \approx 0.9999546$:

    • Jacobian magnitude: $J_{11} = J_{22} = -J_{12} = p_1(1 - p_1) \approx \mathbf{4.54 \times 10^{-5}}$ (vanished by $5{,}500\times$ compared to $0.25$ at $z = [0, 0]$).
    • With MSE loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}} = \frac{1}{2}\lVert p - y\rVert_2^2 \approx 0.9999$): logit gradient is $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}}}{\partial z_1} = -2p_1(1-p_1)^2 \approx \mathbf{-9.08 \times 10^{-5}}$ (vanished!).
    • With Cross-Entropy loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}} = -\log p_1 = 10.00005$): logit gradient is $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}}{\partial z_1} = p_1 - 1 \approx \mathbf{-0.99995}$ (full unit gradient restored because $-1/p_1$ canceled $p_1$ in $J_{11}$!).
    • At tied logits $z = [0, 0]$ with tiny temperature $T = 0.01$: $p = [0.5, 0.5]$ and $J_{11} = \frac{0.25}{0.01} = \mathbf{25.0}$ ($100\times$ amplification across the decision boundary).
