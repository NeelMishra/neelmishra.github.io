    Vanishing and Exploding Gradient Problems Across Activation Functions

    October 2026 · 2 min read

    1. Forward pass between left layer $(l)$ and right layer $(l+1)$

    At left layer $(l)$ ($j = 1, \dots, n_{\rm in}$), pre-activation $z_j^{(l)}$ passes through activation function $f$ to give $a_j^{(l)} = f\!\bigl(z_j^{(l)}\bigr)$, which then feeds into right layer $(l+1)$ ($i = 1, \dots, n_{\rm out}$) via the right layer’s weights $W_{i,j}^{(l+1)}$:

    $$a_j^{(l)} = f\!\bigl(z_j^{(l)}\bigr), \qquad z_i^{(l+1)} = \sum_{j=1}^{n_{\rm in}} W_{i,j}^{(l+1)} a_j^{(l)} + b_i^{(l+1)}.$$

    2. Backward recurrence relation with $f'\!\bigl(z^{(l)}\bigr)$

    Let $\delta_j^{(l)} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_j^{(l)}}$ and $g_j^{(l)} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial a_j^{(l)}}$. By the chain rule:

    1. From right layer $(l+1)$ back to activation $a_j^{(l)}$:
      $$g_j^{(l)} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial a_j^{(l)}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\rm out}} \underbrace{\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_i^{(l+1)}}}_{\delta_i^{(l+1)}} \underbrace{\frac{\partial z_i^{(l+1)}}{\partial a_j^{(l)}}}_{W_{i,j}^{(l+1)}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\rm out}} W_{i,j}^{(l+1)} \delta_i^{(l+1)}.$$
    2. Multiply by the activation derivative $\frac{\partial a_j^{(l)}}{\partial z_j^{(l)}} = f'\!\bigl(z_j^{(l)}\bigr)$:
      $$\delta_j^{(l)} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_j^{(l)}} = g_j^{(l)} \cdot f'\!\bigl(z_j^{(l)}\bigr) = \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\rm out}} W_{i,j}^{(l+1)} \delta_i^{(l+1)}\right) f'\!\bigl(z_j^{(l)}\bigr).$$

    In a single-neuron-per-layer chain (with scalar weight $w^{(l+1)}$ and $h^{(l)} = a^{(l)}$), this recurrence is:

    $$\delta^{(l)} = w^{(l+1)}\, f'\!\bigl(z^{(l)}\bigr)\, \delta^{(l+1)}, \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(l)}} = w^{(l+1)}\, f'\!\bigl(z^{(l+1)}\bigr)\, \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(l+1)}}.$$

    3. Unrolling across $L$ layers and substituting the maximum bound $M$

    Unrolling from layer $L$ down to layer $0$ gives:

    $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(0)}} = \left(\prod_{l=1}^{L} w^{(l)}\, f'\!\bigl(z^{(l)}\bigr)\right) \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L)}}.$$

    With unit-scale weights ($|w^{(l)}| = 1$), replacing $\bigl|f'\!\bigl(z^{(l)}\bigr)\bigr|$ by its maximum bound $\bigl|f'(z)\bigr| \le M$ yields:

    $$\left|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(0)}}\right| = \left(\prod_{l=1}^{L} \bigl|f'\!\bigl(z^{(l)}\bigr)\bigr|\right) \left|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L)}}\right| \le M^L \left|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L)}}\right|.$$

    For Sigmoid, where $\sigma'(z) \le M = \frac{1}{4}$, this directly gives $\left|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(0)}}\right| \le \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{L}\left|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L)}}\right|$.
