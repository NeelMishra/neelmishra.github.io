    Vanishing and Exploding Gradient Problems Across Activation Functions

    October 2026 · 5 min read

    In our weight-initialization derivation, we tracked how gradients flow backward through a purely linear layer. Now we place a nonlinear activation function $f$ at every layer, derive the exact backward recurrence relation with the activation derivative $f'(z)$, and show how replacing $f'(z)$ by its maximum bound immediately produces the exponential vanishing and exploding gradient inequalities used across this series.

    1. Two-step forward pass between left layer $(l)$ and right layer $(l+1)$

    Write the layer index in the superscript—with left layer $(l)$ containing $n_{\rm in}$ neurons (indexed by $j = 1, \dots, n_{\rm in}$) and right layer $(l+1)$ containing $n_{\rm out}$ neurons (indexed by $i = 1, \dots, n_{\rm out}$). With an activation function $f$, each layer performs two sub-steps:

    1. Activation at the left layer $(l)$: Pre-activation $z_j^{(l)}$ passes through the nonlinearity $f$ to produce post-activation $a_j^{(l)}$ (often also written $h_j^{(l)}$):
      $$a_j^{(l)} = f\!\bigl(z_j^{(l)}\bigr), \qquad \frac{\partial a_j^{(l)}}{\partial z_j^{(l)}} = f'\!\bigl(z_j^{(l)}\bigr).$$
    2. Linear sum into the right layer $(l+1)$: Each neuron $i$ in the right layer sums the $n_{\rm in}$ activations from layer $(l)$ using the right layer’s weights $W_{i,j}^{(l+1)}$:
      $$z_i^{(l+1)} = \sum_{j=1}^{n_{\rm in}} W_{i,j}^{(l+1)} a_j^{(l)} + b_i^{(l+1)}, \qquad a_i^{(l+1)} = f\!\bigl(z_i^{(l+1)}\bigr).$$

    2. Deriving the backward recurrence relation with $f'\!\bigl(z^{(l)}\bigr)$

    Let $\delta_j^{(l)} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_j^{(l)}}$ be the gradient of the loss $\mathcal{L}$ with respect to pre-activation $z_j^{(l)}$ at layer $(l)$, and let $g_j^{(l)} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial a_j^{(l)}}$ be the gradient with respect to activation $a_j^{(l)}$. To express $\delta_j^{(l)}$ at the left layer $(l)$ in terms of $\delta_i^{(l+1)}$ at the right layer $(l+1)$, apply the chain rule in two steps:

    • Step 1 — Route gradients from right layer $(l+1)$ back to activation $a_j^{(l)}$ (using $W^{(l+1)}$): Because neuron $a_j^{(l)}$ in the left layer fed into all $n_{\rm out}$ pre-activations $z_1^{(l+1)}, \dots, z_{n_{\rm out}}^{(l+1)}$ of the right layer via the right layer’s weights $W_{i,j}^{(l+1)}$, its activation gradient $g_j^{(l)}$ sums over all $i = 1, \dots, n_{\rm out}$:
      $$g_j^{(l)} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial a_j^{(l)}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\rm out}} \underbrace{\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_i^{(l+1)}}}_{\delta_i^{(l+1)}} \underbrace{\frac{\partial z_i^{(l+1)}}{\partial a_j^{(l)}}}_{W_{i,j}^{(l+1)}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\rm out}} W_{i,j}^{(l+1)} \delta_i^{(l+1)}.$$
    • Step 2 — Multiply by the local activation derivative $f'\!\bigl(z_j^{(l)}\bigr)$ to get $\delta_j^{(l)}$: Because $a_j^{(l)} = f\!\bigl(z_j^{(l)}\bigr)$ depends only on $z_j^{(l)}$, chaining through the activation function multiplies $g_j^{(l)}$ by $f'\!\bigl(z_j^{(l)}\bigr)$:
      $$\delta_j^{(l)} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_j^{(l)}} = \underbrace{\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial a_j^{(l)}}}_{g_j^{(l)}} \cdot \underbrace{\frac{\partial a_j^{(l)}}{\partial z_j^{(l)}}}_{f'(z_j^{(l)})} = \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\rm out}} W_{i,j}^{(l+1)} \delta_i^{(l+1)}\right) f'\!\bigl(z_j^{(l)}\bigr).$$

    Equivalently, substituting $\delta_i^{(l+1)} = g_i^{(l+1)} f'\!\bigl(z_i^{(l+1)}\bigr)$ writes the exact same recurrence directly between activation gradients $g^{(l)} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial a^{(l)}}$ (or $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(l)}}$):

    $$g_j^{(l)} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial a_j^{(l)}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\rm out}} W_{i,j}^{(l+1)}\, f'\!\bigl(z_i^{(l+1)}\bigr)\, g_i^{(l+1)}.$$

    In a single-neuron-per-layer chain ($n_{\rm in} = n_{\rm out} = 1$ with scalar weight $w^{(l+1)}$), both recurrences take the compact form:

    $$\delta^{(l)} = w^{(l+1)}\, f'\!\bigl(z^{(l)}\bigr)\, \delta^{(l+1)}, \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(l)}} = w^{(l+1)}\, f'\!\bigl(z^{(l+1)}\bigr)\, \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(l+1)}}.$$

    And in a wide layer of $n_{\rm out}$ neurons with zero-mean independent weights $\mathbb{E}[W_{i,j}^{(l+1)}] = 0$ and $\operatorname{Var}(W_{i,j}^{(l+1)}) = s_W^2$, squaring $\delta_j^{(l)}$ and taking expectations (exactly as in our linear backward-pass derivation) multiplies the weight fan-out factor $n_{\rm out}\,s_W^2$ by the expected squared activation derivative $\mathbb{E}\bigl[f'(z^{(l)})^2\bigr]$:

    $$\operatorname{Var}\!\bigl(\delta_j^{(l)}\bigr) = \mathbb{E}\!\bigl[(\delta_j^{(l)})^2\bigr] = \Bigl(n_{\rm out}\,s_W^2\,\mathbb{E}\!\bigl[f'(z_j^{(l)})^2\bigr]\Bigr)\,\operatorname{Var}\!\bigl(\delta_i^{(l+1)}\bigr).$$

    3. Unrolling across $L$ layers and substituting the maximum derivative bound

    Unroll the single-step recurrence $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(l)}} = w^{(l+1)}\, f'\!\bigl(z^{(l+1)}\bigr)\, \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(l+1)}}$ (or $\delta^{(l)} = w^{(l+1)}\, f'\!\bigl(z^{(l)}\bigr)\, \delta^{(l+1)}$) step by step from the output layer $L$ all the way back to layer $0$:

    $$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L-1)}} &= w^{(L)}\, f'\!\bigl(z^{(L)}\bigr)\, \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L)}}, \\[4pt] \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L-2)}} &= w^{(L-1)}\, f'\!\bigl(z^{(L-1)}\bigr)\, \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L-1)}} = \bigl(w^{(L-1)} w^{(L)}\bigr)\,\bigl(f'(z^{(L-1)}) f'(z^{(L)})\bigr)\, \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L)}}, \\[4pt] &\;\;\vdots \\[4pt] \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(0)}} &= \left(\prod_{l=1}^{L} w^{(l)}\, f'\!\bigl(z^{(l)}\bigr)\right) \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L)}} \qquad \left(\text{and } \delta^{(0)} = \left(\prod_{l=1}^{L} w^{(l)}\, f'\!\bigl(z^{(l-1)}\bigr)\right)\delta^{(L)}\right). \end{aligned}$$

    Now suppose the weights are normalized to unit scale ($|w^{(l)}| = 1$, or $n_{\rm out}\,s_W^2 = 1$ under fan-out initialization) so the weight matrices themselves neither amplify nor shrink the backward signal. Then the gradient at layer $0$ is governed entirely by the product of the $L$ activation derivatives:

    $$\left|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(0)}}\right| = \left(\prod_{l=1}^{L} \bigl|f'\!\bigl(z^{(l)}\bigr)\bigr|\right) \left|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L)}}\right|.$$

    If the activation derivative is bounded everywhere by a maximum slope $\bigl|f'(z)\bigr| \le M$, replacing each $\bigl|f'\!\bigl(z^{(l)}\bigr)\bigr|$ in the product by $M$ immediately establishes the upper-bound inequality:

    $$\bigl|f'\!\bigl(z^{(l)}\bigr)\bigr| \le M \;\;\text{for all } l \quad\implies\quad \left|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(0)}}\right| \le M^L \left|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L)}}\right| \qquad \Bigl(\text{and } \bigl|\delta^{(0)}\bigr| \le M^L \bigl|\delta^{(L)}\bigr|\Bigr).$$
    • Example — Sigmoid ($M = \frac{1}{4}$): Because $\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)\bigl(1 - \sigma(z)\bigr) \le \frac{1}{4}$ for every $z \in \mathbb{R}$, substituting $M = \frac{1}{4}$ into the bound above immediately gives
      $$\left|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(0)}}\right| \le \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{L} \left|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L)}}\right|,$$
      proving that with unit-scale weights, gradients through $L$ Sigmoid layers must vanish at least as fast as $(1/4)^L$.
    • General vanishing regime ($\bigl|w^{(l)} f'(z^{(l)})\bigr| \le r < 1$): Whenever the per-layer factor stays below $1$—either because the maximum slope satisfies $M < 1$ (Sigmoid) or because pre-activations enter flat tails where $f'(z^{(l)}) \to 0$ (Tanh, ReLU for $z < 0$, GELU/SiLU for $z \ll 0$)—the product decays exponentially as $r^L \to 0$.
    • General exploding regime ($\bigl|w^{(l)} f'(z^{(l)})\bigr| \ge r > 1$): Whenever weights and slopes multiply to more than $1$—either because weights are over-scaled ($|w^{(l)}| > 1/M$) or because the activation slope itself exceeds $1$ ($f'(z) > 1$ in SELU, or the positive overshoot peaks of GELU and SiLU)—the product grows exponentially as $r^L \to \infty$.

    4. Apply this recurrence to every activation family

    01
    Sigmoid and Tanh: Tail Saturation, the $1/4$ Derivative Ceiling, and Mean Shift

    Plug $M = 1/4$ (Sigmoid) and tail decay $4e^{-2|z|}$ (Tanh) into the recurrence, and see how Sigmoid’s positive outputs shift pre-activations into saturation.

     02
    ReLU, Leaky ReLU, and PReLU: Dead Neurons and Unbounded Forward Growth

    Plug $f'(z) \in \{0, 1\}$ and $f'(z) \in \{\alpha, 1\}$ into the recurrence to see why $z < 0$ kills gradient paths (Dying ReLU) while $z > 0$ explodes without He scaling.

     03
    GELU, ELU, and SELU: Gaussian Tail Decay, Overshoot Peaks, and Fixed-Point Breakdown

    Plug GELU’s $[-0.129, 1.129]$ derivative and SELU’s $\lambda \approx 1.0507$ slope into the recurrence to see both zero-slope troughs and $>1$ slope amplification.

     04
    SiLU and Swish: Zero-Slope Troughs, Origin Halving, and $\beta$-Driven Sensitivity

    Trace SiLU’s $f'(0) = 1/2$ origin halving ($(1/2)^L$), its exact zero derivative at $z \approx -1.2785$, and its $1.10$ positive overshoot.

     05
    SwiGLU: Multiplicative Gate Coupling and Quadratic Outlier Explosion

    Extend the recurrence to two multiplied branches $\operatorname{SiLU}(u) \odot v$, exposing double-zero vanishing bottlenecks and linearly growing Jacobians.

     06
    Softmax: Logit-Gap Saturation, Rank-Deficient Jacobians, and Temperature Explosion

    Replace the scalar slope $f'(z)$ with the coupled $K \times K$ Softmax Jacobian $J = \frac{1}{T}(\operatorname{diag}(p) - pp^{\mathsf T})$ to analyze saturation and $1/T$ explosion.
