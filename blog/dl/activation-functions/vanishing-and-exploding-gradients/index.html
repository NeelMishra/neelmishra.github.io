    Vanishing and Exploding Gradient Problems Across Activation Functions

    October 2026 · 6 min read

    When a deep network learns by backpropagation, gradients travel backward from the loss through every layer by repeated multiplication. If each layer multiplies the gradient by a factor smaller than $1$, the signal decays exponentially toward zero (vanishing gradients). If each layer multiplies the gradient by a factor larger than $1$, the signal grows exponentially until weights overflow to NaN (exploding gradients).

    This subseries examines how every activation function in our series either causes, worsens, or mitigates vanishing and exploding gradients—and what happens to training when it does.

    1. Why depth turns local slopes into exponential decay or blow-up

    Consider an $L$-layer network where layer $\ell \in \{1, \dots, L\}$ computes pre-activations $z^{(\ell)}$ and post-activations $h^{(\ell)}$ from the previous layer's output $h^{(\ell-1)}$:

    $$z^{(\ell)} = W^{(\ell)} h^{(\ell-1)} + b^{(\ell)}, \qquad h^{(\ell)} = f\!\bigl(z^{(\ell)}\bigr).$$

    By the chain rule, the gradient of the loss $\mathcal{L}$ with respect to the first hidden representation $h^{(0)}$ is a product of $L$ layer-to-layer Jacobians:

    $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(0)}} = \left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{L} \bigl(W^{(\ell)}\bigr)^{\mathsf T} D^{(\ell)}\right) \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L)}},$$

    where $D^{(\ell)} = \operatorname{diag}\!\bigl(f'(z_1^{(\ell)}), \dots, f'(z_d^{(\ell)})\bigr)$ is the diagonal matrix of activation derivatives at layer $\ell$ (or the full Jacobian matrix $J^{(\ell)}$ for coupled activations like Softmax and SwiGLU).

    Even in a single scalar chain where each layer has weight $w$ and local activation slope $f'(z)$, the gradient at the first layer scales as

    $$\left|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(0)}}\right| = \prod_{\ell=1}^{L} \bigl|w^{(\ell)} f'\!\bigl(z^{(\ell)}\bigr)\bigr| \cdot \left|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L)}}\right| \;\approx\; \bigl(|w \cdot f'(z)|\bigr)^{L} \left|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{(L)}}\right|.$$
    • Vanishing regime ($|w \cdot f'(z)| < 1$): If the per-layer factor averages $r = 0.25$ (the best-case slope of Sigmoid with unit weights), across $L = 20$ layers the gradient shrinks by $0.25^{20} \approx 9.09 \times 10^{-13}$. Early layers receive virtually zero update.
    • Exploding regime ($|w \cdot f'(z)| > 1$): If the per-layer factor averages $r = 1.5$, across $L = 20$ layers the gradient multiplies by $1.5^{20} \approx 3{,}325.3$. Early-layer weight updates overshoot catastrophically.

    2. Six guides: one for every activation family

    01
    Sigmoid and Tanh: Tail Saturation, the $1/4$ Derivative Ceiling, and Mean Shift

    Why Sigmoid's $\le 0.25$ derivative guarantees vanishing gradients in deep plain nets, how its $(0,1)$ output shifts pre-activations into saturated tails, and when over-scaled weights cause explosion.

     02
    ReLU, Leaky ReLU, and PReLU: Dead Neurons and Unbounded Forward Growth

    Why $\operatorname{ReLU}'(z)=0$ for $z<0$ causes irreversible Dying ReLUs, why unbounded positive slopes ($f'(z)=1$) let activations and gradients explode without He scaling, and how Leaky ReLU and PReLU trade off negative-slope decay against explosion.

     03
    GELU, ELU, and SELU: Gaussian Tail Decay, Overshoot Peaks, and Fixed-Point Breakdown

    Locate GELU's exact zero-derivative trough ($z\approx -0.7518$) and $>1$ positive slope peak ($1.129$ at $z=\sqrt{2}$), and see why SELU's $\lambda \approx 1.0507$ multiplier explodes under He initialization.

     04
    SiLU and Swish: Zero-Slope Troughs, Origin Halving, and $\beta$-Driven Sensitivity

    Trace SiLU's two vanishing regimes (the exact zero slope at $z\approx -1.2785$ and the $0.5^L$ origin shrinkage) alongside its $1.10$ positive overshoot and large-$\beta$ curvature spikes.

     05
    SwiGLU: Multiplicative Gate Coupling and Quadratic Outlier Explosion

    See why multiplying two branches creates a double-zero vanishing bottleneck when either branch is small, and why positive inputs make SwiGLU grow quadratically ($\propto \lVert x\rVert^2$) with a linearly growing Jacobian.

     06
    Softmax: Logit-Gap Saturation, Rank-Deficient Jacobians, and Temperature Explosion

    Derive why large logit gaps or unscaled attention dot products collapse the entire $K\times K$ Softmax Jacobian to zero, why Cross-Entropy rescues the output gradient while MSE stalls, and why small temperatures $T\to 0$ explode gradients by $1/T$.
