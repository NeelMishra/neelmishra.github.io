    Numerical Optimization: Reading Guide

    September 2026 · 3 min read

    Connect update geometry, curvature, games, adaptive step lengths, and communication-efficient matrix optimizers.

    Reading order

    01
    Optimizer Geometry: From Gradient Descent to AdamW

    Understand curvature through a quadratic, derive momentum and Adam updates, and separate adaptive scaling from decoupled weight decay.

    02
    Newton and Gauss–Newton: Use Curvature Without Forming an Inverse

    Derive Newton and damped Gauss–Newton from local models, solve a numerical least-squares example, and understand singular curvature and acceptance tests.

    03
    GAN Optimization: Rotations, Fixed Points, and a Rank-One Preconditioner

    See why descent–ascent can rotate, derive the rank-one update in the Gauss–Newton GAN paper, and inspect the limits of its local model.

    04
    Angle-Based Step Lengths: Probe the Gradient Field

    Explain the perpendicular probe in angle-based gradient descent, derive its spherical example, and handle small angles and stochastic-gradient noise.

    05
    Muon: Why the Shape of a Weight Matrix Matters

    Derive the polar update from a spectral-norm constraint, distinguish it from elementwise sign, and understand practical orthogonalization and parameter groups.

    06
    SignMuon: Matrix Directions, Majority Votes, and the Actual Bytes Sent

    Trace the paper’s distributed algorithm, distinguish sign aggregation from averaging, and calculate communication without confusing one-bit information with an integer collective.

    How to use these notes

    Start with the worked example in each chapter. Calculate the answer before changing the interactive figure, then use the self-check to test the distinction the chapter teaches. The numerical examples are deliberately small enough to audit by hand; they are teaching examples, not production benchmarks.

    Follow the prerequisites when a concept is unfamiliar. Related notes link to existing foundations so each chapter can concentrate on its own question.
