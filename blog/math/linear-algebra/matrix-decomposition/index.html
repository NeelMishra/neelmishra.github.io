    Matrix decomposition: reading guide

    October 2026 · 2 min read

    Notes for the Matrix Decompositions playlist by FiveMinuteLectures. Each note follows one video, keeps the lecture's notation, and writes out the arithmetic the slides skip.

    Reading order

    01
    Eigenvalue decomposition: diagonalize a square matrix

    From \(Av=\lambda v\) to \(A=S\Lambda S^{-1}\), on the lecture's 3 by 3, including the row reductions and the Gauss-Jordan inverse.

    02
    Householder QR: reflect columns, then read off Q and R

    Derive the unit vector, correct the 2 by 2 denominator, and finish the linked 3 by 3 with an embedded reflector.

    03
    Householder matrices: a reflection that is its own inverse

    Prove symmetry and orthogonality from \(H=I-2uu^{\mathsf T}\), match the reflection picture, and see why a product of reflectors can be \(Q\).

    04
    Gram-Schmidt QR: orthonormal columns, then a triangular solve

    Orthonormalize the lecture's 3 by 2 columns, recover \(R=Q^{\mathsf T}A\), and back-substitute.

    05
    Gram-Schmidt: subtract the projections, then normalize

    Turn the three vectors into an orthogonal set, then unit length. The printed third unit vector is half as long as it should be.

    06
    Cholesky: square-root the pivots, then multiply

    Eliminate the lecture's 3 by 3, split the pivots, and multiply by their square roots. The opening picture puts 1s on the diagonal of G. The computed diagonal is 1, 1, 2.

    07
    Permuted LU: swap the zero pivot, then solve

    Record one row swap in P, and solve LUx = Pb. The step list writes Ly = b. That right-hand side does not solve the system.

    How to use the note

    Compute \(Av\) for each published eigenvector before you build \(S\). Then invert \(S\) yourself and compare with the fractions on the slide. The last check is the product \(S\Lambda S^{-1}\), which has to come back as \(A\).

    The lecture uses \(S\) and \(\Lambda\), not \(P\) and \(D\). Column order is part of the answer: eigenvalue \(j\) stays with column \(j\).

    For the Householder note, build \(u\) from \(a_1-\lVert a_1\rVert e_1\) and check that it has length 1 before forming \(H\). Then multiply \(HA\) and confirm the entry under the first pivot is 0.

    For the Cholesky note, eliminate \(A\) before reading \(D\) off \(U\). The product the lecture leaves unwritten is \(GG^{\mathsf T}\), and it has to come back as \(A\). The diagonal of \(G\) is the square roots of the pivots, not a row of 1s.

    For the permuted LU note, swap the zero pivot before you divide. The check is \(PA=LU\). The triangular solve uses \(Pb\), not the \(b\) written on the step list.
