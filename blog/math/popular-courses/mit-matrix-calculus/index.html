    Matrix Calculus for Machine Learning and Beyond

    A lecture-by-lecture companion · Alan Edelman & Steven G. Johnson

    A derivative tells you how a small change travels through a calculation. That idea works when your input is a number, a vector, an entire matrix, or something even bigger.

    Before starting: be comfortable with scalar derivatives, vectors, matrix multiplication, transposes, and dot products. MIT lists linear algebra and multivariable calculus as prerequisites. You do not need an automatic differentiation library to work through the first note.

    Where the course goes

    The official video sequence continues from derivatives as linear operators to Jacobians, vectorization, Kronecker products, finite differences, general inner products, optimization, adjoints, matrix inverses and determinants, automatic differentiation, differential equations, random functions, Hessians, and eigenproblems. This is a roadmap to the MIT material; the list above contains the available blog notes.

    A useful way to study

    1. Predict the shape. Write the input and output dimensions before differentiating.
    2. Perturb the input. Expand the changed expression and keep terms linear in the perturbation.
    3. Check a small example. Compare the exact change with the predicted change, then shrink the step.
    4. Explain the result. State what the derivative does to an input change in one sentence.
