    Sarsa: On-Policy TD Control

    August 2026 · 20 min read · Sample-based Learning Methods, Week 4

    From prediction to control. TD(0) learned $V_\pi$ for a fixed policy. Control needs action values so that the policy can compare choices and improve while learning continues.

    TD inside generalized policy iteration

    Generalized policy iteration alternates two pressures:

    • Evaluation: make $Q$ more accurate for the current policy.
    • Improvement: make the policy more greedy with respect to the current $Q$.

    Monte Carlo control waits until an episode ends before both operations. Sarsa performs an evaluation update after every transition and can improve the behaviour immediately.

    From state transitions to state-action transitions

    TD prediction moved from $S_t$ to $S_{t+1}$. TD control moves from the current pair $(S_t,A_t)$ to the next pair $(S_{t+1},A_{t+1})$:

    $$\begin{aligned}Q_\pi(s,a)=\mathbb E_\pi\big[&R_{t+1}+\gamma Q_\pi(S_{t+1},A_{t+1})\\&\mid S_t=s,A_t=a\big].\end{aligned}$$

    Use one sampled transition and one sampled next action to form the target:

    $$\boxed{R_{t+1}+\gamma Q(S_{t+1},A_{t+1})}.$$

    The Sarsa update

    $$\boxed{\begin{aligned}Q(S_t,A_t)\leftarrow{}&Q(S_t,A_t)+\alpha\big[R_{t+1}\\&+\gamma Q(S_{t+1},A_{t+1})-Q(S_t,A_t)\big]\end{aligned}}.$$

    The name records the five pieces used by the update:

    $$\begin{aligned}S_t,\ A_t,\ R_{t+1},\\S_{t+1},\ A_{t+1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{SARSA}.\end{aligned}$$

    The next action must be selected before the current update because its value appears inside the target.

    Why Sarsa is on-policy

    The policy that generates $A_{t+1}$ is the same policy whose action value appears in the target. If behaviour is $\varepsilon$-greedy, the learned value includes the consequences of occasional exploratory actions.

    Safety consequence. Sarsa does not evaluate an imaginary perfectly greedy future. It evaluates the future behaviour that will actually occur, exploration mistakes included.

    The complete control loop

    initialise Q(s,a), with Q(terminal,.) = 0

for each episode:
    initialise S
    choose A from an epsilon-greedy policy derived from Q

    until S is terminal:
        take A; observe R and S_next
        choose A_next epsilon-greedily from Q(S_next,.)

        target = R + gamma * Q(S_next,A_next)
        Q(S,A) += alpha * (target - Q(S,A))

        S = S_next
        A = A_next

return the epsilon-greedy policy derived from Q

    Policy improvement after every step

    After updating $Q(S_t,A_t)$, the $\varepsilon$-greedy policy at $S_t$ may change for the next time that state is visited. The already-selected $A_{t+1}$ is not changed retroactively. Evaluation and improvement are nevertheless interleaved at transition scale, a tighter form of GPI than episode-level Monte Carlo control.
