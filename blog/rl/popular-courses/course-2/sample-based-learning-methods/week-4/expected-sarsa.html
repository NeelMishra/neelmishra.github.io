    Expected Sarsa: Average the Next Action

    August 2026 · 20 min read · Sample-based Learning Methods, Week 4

    Remove one unnecessary sample. Once $S_{t+1}$ is known, the policy probabilities and all $Q(S_{t+1},a)$ values are already available. Expected Sarsa averages them exactly instead of sampling one next action for the target.

    From Sarsa to an expectation

    Sarsa's target is

    $$\begin{aligned}R_{t+1}+\gamma Q(S_{t+1},A_{t+1}),\\A_{t+1}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid S_{t+1}).\end{aligned}$$

    Conditioned on the observed next state, the expected next action value is

    $$\begin{aligned}\mathbb E_\pi[Q(S_{t+1},A_{t+1})\mid S_{t+1}]\\=\sum_a\pi(a\mid S_{t+1})Q(S_{t+1},a).\end{aligned}$$

    Substitute that exact expectation:

    $$\boxed{\begin{aligned}Q(S_t,A_t)\leftarrow{}&Q(S_t,A_t)+\alpha\Big[R_{t+1}\\&+\gamma\sum_a\pi(a\mid S_{t+1})Q(S_{t+1},a)\\&-Q(S_t,A_t)\Big]\end{aligned}}.$$

    A concrete next-state example

    Suppose the next-state action values are $(0,-1,2,1)$ and the target policy probabilities are $(0.1,0.1,0.7,0.1)$. Then

    $$\begin{aligned}\sum_a\pi(a\mid s')Q(s',a)&=0.1(0)+0.1(-1)\\&\quad+0.7(2)+0.1(1)\\&=1.4.\end{aligned}$$

    With $R=1$ and $\gamma=0.9$, Expected Sarsa's target is $1+0.9(1.4)=2.26$.

    Compare all three TD-control targets

    Why Expected Sarsa has lower target variance

    Sarsa may sample a low-valued exploratory action even when most policy mass lies on a better action. That single draw can move the estimate in the wrong direction for one update. Expected Sarsa integrates over the action distribution exactly, eliminating randomness from $A_{t+1}$.

    The transition to $S_{t+1}$ is still sampled, so the target is not deterministic overall. It is simply less noisy conditional on the next state.

    A template, not inherently on-policy

    Target policy inside expectationResult
    Same $\varepsilon$-greedy policy as behaviourOn-policy Expected Sarsa.
    Greedy policyThe expectation collapses to $\max_aQ(s',a)$: Q-learning.
    Different soft target policyOff-policy Expected Sarsa, provided behaviour supplies coverage.
    Softmax/Boltzmann policyA smooth target emphasizing high-valued actions.

    Why no importance sampling appears

    The expectation is computed analytically under the chosen target policy. There is no sampled next action from the behaviour policy inside the target, so there is no action-distribution mismatch to correct.

    The current transition still comes from behaviour, but conditioned on $(S_t,A_t)$ the environment's next-state distribution is policy-independent.

    The trade-off

    Sarsa needs one lookup for $Q(S_{t+1},A_{t+1})$. Expected Sarsa must inspect every available action. The extra $O(|\mathcal A|)$ computation buys a lower-variance target and often permits larger stable step sizes.
