    Models, Simulated Experience & Planning

    August 2026 · 19 min read · Sample-based Learning Methods, Week 5

    Learning versus planning. Direct RL updates from transitions produced by the real environment. Planning applies the same value update to transitions produced by a model.

    What an environment model predicts

    A model receives a state-action pair and predicts the reward and next state:

    $$M:(s,a)\longmapsto(r,s').$$

    It stores knowledge about dynamics. Querying the model asks “what would probably happen if action $a$ were taken in state $s$?” without paying the cost or risk of a real interaction.

    Sample models and distribution models

    ModelQuery resultStrengthCost
    Sample modelOne random $(r,s')$ drawn from the learned outcome distribution.Compact and directly usable by sample-based updates.Expected values require repeated queries.
    Distribution modelThe full probability of every possible $(r,s')$.Exact expectations and risk calculations.Can require enormous memory.

    One distribution, two interfaces

    A fair coin distribution model stores $P(H)=P(T)=0.5$. A sample model merely returns one random outcome per query. Repeated samples recover the expectation.

    Why full distributions can explode

    One six-sided die has 6 outcomes. Twelve dice have $6^{12}=2{,}176{,}782{,}336$ joint outcomes. Storing a full distribution over complex next states may be impossible even when sampling from it is easy.

    $$|\Omega_{\text{joint}}|=\prod_i|\Omega_i|.$$

    Planning is learning from imagined experience

    Choose $(s,a)$search control
    →
    Query modelsimulate $(r,s')$
    →
    Update valuesas if transition occurred

    The update can be exactly the Q-learning update:

    $$\begin{aligned}Q(s,a)\leftarrow{}&Q(s,a)+\alpha\big[r\\&+\gamma\max_{a'}Q(s',a')-Q(s,a)\big].\end{aligned}$$

    The equation does not care whether $(r,s')$ came from the world or a model. Only the source of experience changed.

    Random-sample one-step Q-planning

    repeat:
    choose a previously modelled state-action pair (S,A)
    query the sample model: (R,S_next) = Model(S,A)
    Q(S,A) += alpha * (R + gamma * max_a Q(S_next,a) - Q(S,A))

    The choice of which pair to simulate is called search control. Uniform random selection is simple; prioritised methods spend computation where values are changing most.

    Why planning improves sample efficiency

    Real experience is often slow, expensive, or dangerous. Computation is cheap by comparison. A single real transition can teach the model once, then support many simulated value updates.

    Planning does not create new facts. It extracts more learning from dynamics already stored in the model. If the model is wrong, planning can amplify the error; Week 5 Part 3 addresses that failure.
