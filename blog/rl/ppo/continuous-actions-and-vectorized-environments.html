    Continuous-Action PPO: Densities, Squashing, and Vectorized Rollouts

    September 2026 · 5 min read + experiments

    Build a Gaussian actor, sum the right log probabilities, account for action transforms, and preserve trajectory boundaries in parallel environments.

    See the ideaRead the explanationTry the calculation

    A Gaussian replaces the categorical head

    For a $d$-dimensional continuous action, a common actor outputs a mean vector $\mu_\theta(s)$ and log standard deviations. Set $\sigma=\exp(\log\sigma)$ to keep scales positive. With independent components, $u\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\operatorname{diag}(\sigma^2))$. PPO still needs one likelihood ratio per environment transition, so it uses the joint density of the entire action vector.

    $$\log p_\theta(u\mid s)=-\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left[\left(\frac{u_j-\mu_j}{\sigma_j}\right)^2+2\log\sigma_j+\log(2\pi)\right].$$

    With batch size $B$, a componentwise Normal distribution returns log probabilities of shape $[B,d]$. Sum over the final action dimension to get $[B]$. Averaging instead changes the likelihood ratio to a root of the intended joint ratio; leaving the dimension unsummed lets broadcasting create a different loss. PyTorch’s Independent(Normal(...), 1) can reinterpret the final batch dimension as an event dimension and perform this reduction. PyTorch distribution documentation.

    base = torch.distributions.Normal(mean, log_std.exp())
action_dist = torch.distributions.Independent(base, 1)
u = action_dist.sample()             # [B, action_dim]
old_logp = action_dist.log_prob(u)    # [B]
# Store u; do not replace it with a newly sampled action during training.

    A density is allowed to exceed one

    A narrow Normal can have density greater than one near its mean because probability is density integrated over a region. A point in a continuous distribution has zero probability mass. Ratios of densities are the continuous counterpart of discrete likelihood ratios. The log density may be positive without any probability rule being violated.

    The Gaussian’s unsquashed differential entropy is $\frac12\sum_j\log(2\pi e\sigma_j^2)$. It depends on action units and can be negative. Comparing its absolute numeric value with categorical entropy is usually meaningless. What matters operationally is how changing the distribution changes exploration and the chosen objective.

    Bounded actions require an explicit policy definition

    Suppose the environment accepts actions in $(-1,1)$. One choice is $a=\tanh(u)$ with a Normal latent $u$. Since this map compresses space near its limits, the transformed density needs a change-of-variables correction:

    $$\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)=\log p_\theta(u\mid s)-\sum_j\log(1-\tanh^2(u_j)).$$

    For arbitrary bounds $l_j,h_j$, define $c_j=(h_j+l_j)/2$ and $d_j=(h_j-l_j)/2$, then $a_j=c_j+d_j\tanh(u_j)$. Subtract an additional $\sum_j\log d_j$ from the log density. Save the pre-squash sample $u$ to avoid inverting values rounded onto the action boundary. Stable transform routines are preferable to directly evaluating $\log(1-a^2)$ near $|a|=1$.

    When the transform is fixed and invertible, its Jacobian is identical under the old and current policies for a stored action. It cancels in the log-probability difference used by PPO. That can simplify the ratio calculation. It does not justify using the base Normal’s entropy as if it were the executed action’s entropy: $H(a)=H(u)+\mathbb E[\log|\det da/du|]$, and that expectation changes with the policy.

    Clipping an action is a different construction

    Hard clipping $u$ into $[-1,1]$ is many-to-one: every $u>1$ maps to the same endpoint. A Normal density evaluated at the clipped endpoint is not the executed action’s probability law. One consistent approach treats the latent sample as the policy action and the clip as part of the environment transformation; then store the latent action and use its density. Another defines and handles the resulting mixed distribution explicitly. Do not silently alternate between latent and clipped actions when recording old and new likelihoods.

    The implementation study by Huang and colleagues documents several continuous-control choices, including action transformations and normalization, and shows why “PPO” alone is not a complete implementation specification. Use it as a comparison after deciding what your own policy distribution means. The 37 Implementation Details of PPO.

    Parallel environments add a time axis, not a new algorithm

    With $N$ environments, the rollout has shape $[T,N,\ldots]$. Compute GAE backward along time independently for each environment column. A termination in environment 7 must not reset environment 8’s trace. If a vector API automatically resets ended environments, retain the final pre-reset observation for bootstrapping external truncations, as established in the boundary chapter.

    Observation normalization also belongs to the policy’s effective input transformation. If running statistics change between collection and re-scoring, a frozen neural network can produce different action probabilities for the same stored raw observation. Store the normalized observations used for acting, or freeze the relevant statistics during an iteration and document how they update. Similar care applies to action rescaling, reward scaling, and evaluation: a saved network without its normalization state may not describe the trained policy.

    Recurrent actors add another state: hidden memory. Their batches must retain sequence order, initial hidden states, and reset masks. Arbitrarily shuffling individual transitions is appropriate for the feed-forward lab, but not sufficient to reproduce a recurrent actor’s conditioning context.

    Check your understanding: For a three-dimensional diagonal Normal, should the ratio be a length-three vector?

    No. One environment action is the whole vector. Sum component log densities first, then exponentiate the current-minus-old joint log density to obtain one ratio per transition.
