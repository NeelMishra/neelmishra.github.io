    GRPO: Group Rewards, One Clear Update

    September 2026 · 3 min read

    Four chapters connect same-prompt reward comparisons to a clipped policy update, with the notation introduced as you need it.

    Start hereSee the update

    Start with one question and several answers

    Ask the policy the same question four times and score each answer. Give answers above the group average a positive learning weight and answers below it a negative weight. The group supplies the comparison, so this update needs no separately trained critic.

    This is the starting idea of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), introduced in DeepSeekMath, Section 4.1. The full update combines those weights with token-level PPO clipping and a separate penalty for drifting from a reference model.

    Recognize three symbols

    SymbolMeaning here
    $G$Number of answers sampled for one prompt
    $r_i$Score assigned to answer $i$
    $\hat A_i$Group-relative learning weight for answer $i$

    A score can be binary, such as pass/fail, or give partial credit. What matters for the group’s reward-based signal is that the sampled scores differ. Chapter 1 introduces the mean and spread before using their equations.

    What this series covers

    We follow the paper’s outcome-supervised version: one score per answer, one group weight shared across its tokens, and token contributions averaged within each answer. Process rewards and alternative normalization rules are separate variants.

    You can start chapter 1 without PPO. If clipping is unfamiliar, read the PPO clipping explanation before chapter 2. For learning from already labeled chosen/rejected pairs, the DPO roadmap gives the corresponding path.
