    Keep the PPO Guardrail

    September 2026 · 4 min read + experiments

    GRPO changes the baseline, then reuses PPO’s most useful safety feature: compare the new policy with the one that collected the samples and clip the ratio.

    Follow the updateSee the two cases

    Keep one group weight, but compare probabilities token by token

    Chapter 1 gave answer $i$ a weight $\hat A_i$. In the outcome-supervised setup, that weight is shared by all its response tokens. The probability comparison is more local: GRPO compares old and current policies on each sampled token.

    Let $y_{i,t}$ be token $t$ in answer $i$, and $s_{i,t}$ its prompt plus preceding tokens. Write $\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$ for the policy that sampled the answers and $\pi_\theta$ for the trainable policy. The token ratio is:

    $$\rho_{i,t}=\frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid s_{i,t})}{\pi_{\mathrm{old}}(y_{i,t}\mid s_{i,t})}.$$

    A change from probability 0.25 to 0.30 gives a ratio of 1.2. The old probability remains fixed during this batch. The original GRPO objective clips these individual token ratios, as specified in DeepSeekMath, Equation 3.

    Apply the familiar clipping rule

    A positive group weight encourages the sampled token; a negative weight discourages it. Let $\epsilon$ be the clipping tolerance, such as 0.2. Compare the actual ratio with a copy restricted to $[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]$, multiply both by the group weight, and keep the smaller score:

    $$\ell_{i,t}^{\mathrm{clip}}=\min\!\left(\rho_{i,t}\hat A_i,\operatorname{clip}(\rho_{i,t},1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\hat A_i\right).$$

    This is a score to maximize. Its shape is the same as PPO’s clipped score; the group supplied the advantage instead of a critic.

    Two examples show where extra credit stops

    Group weightToken ratioScore at ε = 0.2
    +11.30min(1.30, 1.20) = 1.20
    −10.70min(−0.70, −0.80) = −0.80

    The first sample gets no extra credit for becoming still more likely. The second gets no extra credit for becoming still less likely. Clipping limits these incentives; shared parameters and finite optimizer steps can still move the actual ratios outside the interval.

    Add a separate reference penalty

    The old policy records who generated the batch. The reference policy is a longer-lived checkpoint used to discourage excessive drift. Keep the reference fixed within the training stage, while refreshing the old-policy snapshot for each newly collected batch.

    The original GRPO recipe subtracts a sampled policy-to-reference divergence penalty from each token’s clipped score. A positive coefficient $\beta$ controls its strength. Group advantages come from task rewards; the reference term is computed separately during optimization. DeepSeekMath, Equation 4, gives the sampled penalty, and the implementation chapter shows the calculation.

    For the paper’s reduction, average the valid token contributions within each answer, then average answers and prompts. Exclude prompt and padding positions. Other length reductions change the relative weight of responses, so record which one you use.

    Check whether the group supplied useful evidence

    We now have fixed group weights, current/old token ratios, and a separate reference penalty. Next, examine what happens when the rewards are identical, the group is too easy or too hard, or the verifier rewards the wrong behavior.
