    1. RTN: Symmetric vs. asymmetric grids, tie-breaking, and the $\Delta^2/12$ variance law

    October 2026 · 7 min read

    Given a continuous tensor $x$ and a bit-width $b$, Round-to-Nearest (RTN) places $2^b$ equally spaced points across $x$'s numerical range and snaps each entry $x_i$ to its nearest grid point. This post derives the two standard RTN grid parameterizations (symmetric and asymmetric), explains why IEEE 754 tie-breaking matters for unbiased neural network layers, and proves the classical $\Delta^2 / 12$ error variance law.

    1. Symmetric absmax vs. asymmetric min-max RTN

    A $b$-bit integer can represent $2^b$ distinct codes. How we align those $2^b$ integer codes with the floating-point interval of a tensor block determines whether the quantizer is symmetric or asymmetric.

    1a. Symmetric (absmax) RTN

    In symmetric RTN, floating-point $0.0$ is pinned to integer code $z = 0$ with no offset. To make the positive and negative ranges identical, we use the restricted signed integer range $q \in [-Q, +Q]$ where $Q = 2^{b-1} - 1$ (for example, $[-127, +127]$ for INT8 or $[-7, +7]$ for INT4, leaving the single most-negative two's-complement code $-2^{b-1}$ unused):

    $$\Delta_{\mathrm{sym}} = \frac{\max_i |x_i|}{2^{b-1}-1}, \qquad q_i = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\left\lfloor \frac{x_i}{\Delta_{\mathrm{sym}}} \right\rceil,\; -(2^{b-1}-1),\; +(2^{b-1}-1)\right), \qquad \widehat{x}_i = \Delta_{\mathrm{sym}}\cdot q_i.$$
    • Why kernels prefer symmetric RTN for weights: Because $z = 0$, dequantizing $\widehat{w}_i = \Delta_{\mathrm{sym}} q_i$ inside a fused W4A16 or W8A8 matrix-multiply kernel needs only a single multiplication per group and eliminates the cross-term $-z\sum_c x_c$ during accumulation.
    • Where symmetric RTN wastes codes: If a block of values is skewed (for instance, $[ -0.20,\, +1.40 ]$), symmetric RTN stretches the grid over $[-1.40, +1.40]$ so that almost all negative codes in $[-1.40, -0.20)$ sit completely empty.

    1b. Asymmetric (affine min-max) RTN

    Asymmetric RTN fits the grid tightly to the observed minimum $x_{\min} = \min_i x_i$ and maximum $x_{\max} = \max_i x_i$ (extended to include $0.0$ whenever padding or ReLU zeros must be represented exactly) across unsigned codes $q \in [0, 2^b - 1]$:

    $$\Delta_{\mathrm{asym}} = \frac{x_{\max} - x_{\min}}{2^b - 1}, \qquad z = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\left\lfloor \frac{-x_{\min}}{\Delta_{\mathrm{asym}}} \right\rceil,\; 0,\; 2^b - 1\right),$$
    $$q_i = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\left\lfloor \frac{x_i}{\Delta_{\mathrm{asym}}} \right\rceil + z,\; 0,\; 2^b - 1\right), \qquad \widehat{x}_i = \Delta_{\mathrm{asym}}\cdot (q_i - z).$$

    Rounding the zero-point $z$ to an integer ensures that real $0.0$ maps to the exact integer code $q = z$ with zero reconstruction error ($\widehat{0} = \Delta_{\mathrm{asym}}(z - z) = 0$).

    2. Rounding tie-breaking: why $\lfloor u \rceil$ uses round-half-to-even

    Inside the unclipped range, RTN maps $u = x / \Delta$ to the nearest integer $\lfloor u \rceil$. For any non-halfway value ($u \notin \mathbb{Z} + \tfrac{1}{2}$), the nearest integer is unique. Exactly halfway between two integers ($u = k + 0.5$), two tie-breaking rules appear in hardware and frameworks:

    • Round-half-away-from-zero (standard schoolbook rounding, C roundf()): maps $+0.5 \to +1$, $+1.5 \to +2$, $-0.5 \to -1$. If pre-quantized values hit half-integers with positive mean, rounding every tie upward introduces a systematic positive bias $\mathbb{E}[\widehat{x} - x] \neq 0$.
    • Round-half-to-even (banker's rounding) (IEEE 754 default, PyTorch torch.round(), NumPy np.round(), x86 ROUNDPS): rounds $k + 0.5$ to whichever of $k$ or $k+1$ is an even integer ($+0.5 \to 0$, $+1.5 \to +2$, $+2.5 \to +2$, $+3.5 \to +4$). Alternating the tie direction between even and odd intervals cancels tie bias in expectation, keeping $\mathbb{E}[\widehat{w} - w] \approx 0$ across a weight row so the downstream dot product $\sum_{c=1}^{d} \widehat{w}_c x_c$ does not drift.

    3. The $\Delta^2 / 12$ quantization noise law and $6.02\text{ dB/bit}$

    Let $x \in [x_{\min}, x_{\max}]$ be a continuous weight or activation that does not clip, and let $\varepsilon = \widehat{x} - x$ be its RTN quantization error. Because $\widehat{x}$ is the closest multiple of $\Delta$, the error $\varepsilon$ always lies in $[-\Delta/2, +\Delta/2]$:

    $$|\varepsilon| = |\widehat{x} - x| \le \frac{\Delta}{2}.$$

    When the step size $\Delta$ is small compared to the smooth variation of $x$'s probability density (Bennett's high-resolution assumption), the fractional position of $x/\Delta$ inside its quantization cell is approximately uniform, so $\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{U}\!\left[-\frac{\Delta}{2},\, +\frac{\Delta}{2}\right]$ with density $p(\varepsilon) = \frac{1}{\Delta}$. Its mean and mean-squared error (variance) follow immediately by integration:

    $$\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon] = \int_{-\Delta/2}^{+\Delta/2} \varepsilon\,\frac{1}{\Delta}\,d\varepsilon = 0, \qquad \mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{RTN}} = \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon^2] = \int_{-\Delta/2}^{+\Delta/2} \varepsilon^2\,\frac{1}{\Delta}\,d\varepsilon = \frac{1}{\Delta}\left[\frac{\varepsilon^3}{3}\right]_{-\Delta/2}^{+\Delta/2} = \frac{\Delta^2}{12}.$$

    For a symmetric $b$-bit quantizer over $[-M, +M]$ where $\Delta \approx \frac{2M}{2^b} = M\,2^{-(b-1)}$, substituting $\Delta$ into $\Delta^2 / 12$ shows how RTN error scales with bit-width $b$:

    $$\mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{RTN}}(b) = \frac{\Delta^2}{12} \approx \frac{M^2}{3}\,4^{-b}.$$

    Every additional bit ($b \to b + 1$) halves $\Delta$ and therefore cuts the mean-squared quantization error by a factor of $4$. In decibels, the Signal-to-Quantization-Noise Ratio ($\mathrm{SQNR} = 10\log_{10}(\sigma_x^2 / \mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{RTN}})$) improves by $10\log_{10}(4) \approx 6.02\text{ dB}$ per bit.

    4. Worked 4-bit hand calculation: symmetric vs. asymmetric RTN

    Take a group of $G = 4$ weights from a skewed channel:

    $$w = \bigl[-0.20,\; 0.00,\; +0.55,\; +1.40\bigr], \qquad b = 4\text{ bits}.$$

    Case A: 4-bit symmetric RTN ($q \in [-7, +7]$, $z = 0$)

    1. Step size: $M = \max_i |w_i| = 1.40$, so $\Delta_{\mathrm{sym}} = \frac{1.40}{7} = 0.20$.
    2. Quantize ($q_i = \lfloor w_i / 0.20 \rceil$):
      • $w_1 = -0.20 \;\Rightarrow\; -0.20 / 0.20 = -1.00 \;\Rightarrow\; q_1 = -1,\; \widehat{w}_1 = -0.20$ (error $0.00$).
      • $w_2 = 0.00 \;\Rightarrow\; 0.00 / 0.20 = 0.00 \;\Rightarrow\; q_2 = 0,\; \widehat{w}_2 = 0.00$ (error $0.00$).
      • $w_3 = +0.55 \;\Rightarrow\; 0.55 / 0.20 = 2.75 \;\Rightarrow\; q_3 = 3,\; \widehat{w}_3 = +0.60$ (error $+0.05$).
      • $w_4 = +1.40 \;\Rightarrow\; 1.40 / 0.20 = 7.00 \;\Rightarrow\; q_4 = 7,\; \widehat{w}_4 = +1.40$ (error $0.00$).
    3. Mean-squared error: $\mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{sym}} = \frac{1}{4}(0^2 + 0^2 + 0.05^2 + 0^2) = 0.000625$. Notice that codes $\{-7, -6, -5, -4, -3, -2\}$ are completely unused because the negative tail stops at $-0.20$.

    Case B: 4-bit asymmetric RTN ($q \in [0, 15]$)

    1. Step size and zero-point: $w_{\min} = -0.20$, $w_{\max} = +1.40$, so $\Delta_{\mathrm{asym}} = \frac{1.40 - (-0.20)}{15} = \frac{1.60}{15} \approx 0.10667$ (nearly $2\times$ finer resolution than $\Delta_{\mathrm{sym}} = 0.20$), and $z = \lfloor 0.20 / 0.10667 \rceil = \lfloor 1.875 \rceil = 2$.
    2. Quantize ($q_i = \lfloor w_i / \Delta_{\mathrm{asym}} \rceil + 2$) and dequantize ($\widehat{w}_i = \Delta_{\mathrm{asym}}(q_i - 2)$):
      • $w_1 = -0.20 \;\Rightarrow\; \lfloor -1.875 \rceil + 2 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \widehat{w}_1 = -2\Delta_{\mathrm{asym}} \approx -0.2133$ (error $-0.0133$).
      • $w_2 = 0.00 \;\Rightarrow\; \lfloor 0 \rceil + 2 = 2 \;\Rightarrow\; \widehat{w}_2 = 0\Delta_{\mathrm{asym}} = 0.0000$ (error $0.0000$).
      • $w_3 = +0.55 \;\Rightarrow\; \lfloor 5.156 \rceil + 2 = 7 \;\Rightarrow\; \widehat{w}_3 = 5\Delta_{\mathrm{asym}} \approx +0.5333$ (error $-0.0167$).
      • $w_4 = +1.40 \;\Rightarrow\; \lfloor 13.125 \rceil + 2 = 15 \;\Rightarrow\; \widehat{w}_4 = 13\Delta_{\mathrm{asym}} \approx +1.3867$ (error $-0.0133$).
    3. Mean-squared error: $\mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{asym}} = \frac{1}{4}\bigl((-0.0133)^2 + 0^2 + (-0.0167)^2 + (-0.0133)^2\bigr) \approx 0.000158$—nearly $4\times$ lower MSE than symmetric RTN on this skewed block, matching the theoretical $(\Delta_{\mathrm{asym}} / \Delta_{\mathrm{sym}})^2 = (0.1067 / 0.20)^2 \approx 0.284$ variance ratio.
    Why does asymmetric RTN slightly shift the endpoints $w_{\min}$ and $w_{\max}$?

    Because we round the zero-point $z = \lfloor -w_{\min}/\Delta \rceil$ to an exact integer so that $0.0$ reconstructs with zero error. That integer snap shifts the grid alignment by at most $\Delta / 2$ at the two boundaries.
