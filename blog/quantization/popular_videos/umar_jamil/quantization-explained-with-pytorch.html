    Quantization explained with PyTorch - Post-Training Quantization, Quantization-Aware Training

    October 2026 · Umar Jamil's video

    Based on Umar Jamil's video. The figures are his actual accompanying slides, reproduced under CC BY-NC 4.0. Click a figure to view it at full size.

    A 7B model in FP32 ($4\text{ bytes/param}$) needs $28\text{ GB}$ just for weights; 8-bit quantization cuts that to $7\text{ GB}$. An 8-bit integer maps its $2^8=256$ patterns uniformly to $[0,255]$ or $[-128,127]$, whereas IEEE 754 FP32 uses 1 sign, 8 exponent (bias $127$), and 23 fraction bits to decode $(-1)^\sigma 2^{E-127}(1+f)$—for example, $0.15625 = 1.25\times 2^{-3}$ ($E=124$, $f=0.25$):

    Standard 16-bit FP16 (1 sign, 5 exponent, 10 fraction; max $65{,}504$) narrows the exponent and risks overflow. BFloat16 (bf16) keeps FP32's full 8-bit exponent ($\pm 3.4\times 10^{38}$ range) and truncates the fraction to 7 bits:

    NormalFloat4 (nf4) replaces sign/exponent/mantissa fields with a 4-bit index ($k\in\{0,\dots,15\}$) into $16$ fixed $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ quantiles $Q[k]\in[-1,1]$ scaled by the block absmax $s$ (for $s=1$, $0.15625$ maps to $k=9=\texttt{1001}_2$ with $Q[9]=+0.1609$):

    Integer quantization maps a real interval onto a uniform integer grid for the inputs, weights, and outputs of $Y=XW+B$:

    With integer code $q$, step scale $s$, and zero point $z$ (the integer code for real $0$), dequantization reconstructs $\widehat{x}=s(q-z)$. Asymmetric grids map $[\beta,\alpha]\to [0,2^b-1]$; symmetric grids map $[-A,A]\to [-Q,Q]$ with $z=0$:

    Asymmetric quantization ($b$ unsigned bits, $2^b-1$ steps):

    $$s=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2^b-1},\qquad z=\operatorname{round}(-\beta/s),\qquad q=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\operatorname{round}(x/s)+z,\ 0,\ 2^b-1\right).$$

    With $\beta=-44.93$ and $\alpha=43.31$, $s=88.24/255\approx0.3460$ and $z=130$, so $43.31 \mapsto q=255 \mapsto s(255-130)\approx43.25$ and real $0 \mapsto z=130$.

    Symmetric quantization ($A=\max|x|$, $Q=2^{b-1}-1=127$, $z=0$):

    $$s=\frac{A}{Q},\qquad q=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\operatorname{round}(x/s),-Q,Q\right),\qquad \widehat{x}=sq.$$

    Here $s=44.93/127\approx0.3538$, mapping $43.31 \mapsto q=122 \mapsto \widehat{x}\approx43.16$ (from-scratch notebook). Activation ranges are estimated at runtime (dynamic) or via observers on unlabeled calibration data (static), and INT8 products accumulate in INT32:

    With $q_b=\operatorname{round}(b/(s_xs_w))$, the INT32 accumulator $a$ and rescaled output code $q_y$ are (gemmlowp derivation):

    $$a=\sum_i(q_{x,i}-z_x)(q_{w,i}-z_w)+q_b,\qquad q_y=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{s_xs_w}{s_y}a\right)+z_y,\ q_{\min,y},q_{\max,y}\right).$$

    A single outlier ($1000$) stretches a $[-50,150]$ min-max grid ($s\approx0.78$) to $[-50,1000]$ ($s\approx4.12$). Percentile ($0.01\text{st}$–$99.99\text{th}$, notebook) or MSE clipping preserves resolution for bulk values, and per-channel scales (axis $0$ of [out_features, in_features]) prevent wide channels from coarsening narrow ones:

    Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) attaches observers to a pretrained model, calibrates on unlabeled inputs, and converts modules to INT8 without retraining (PTQ notebook):

    import torch
from torch.ao import quantization as quant

torch.backends.quantized.engine = "qnnpack"
prepared = net_with_stubs.cpu().eval()
prepared.qconfig = quant.get_default_qconfig("qnnpack")
prepared = quant.prepare(prepared, inplace=False)

with torch.no_grad():
    for images, _ in calibration_loader:
        prepared(images.cpu())

int8_model = quant.convert(prepared, inplace=False)

    Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) inserts fake-quantize nodes (quantize $\to$ dequantize in float) during training, using the Straight-Through Estimator (STE) ($\partial\widehat{x}/\partial x = 1$ in-range, $0$ outside) to update FP32 master weights (QAT notebook):

    qat_model = net_with_stubs.cpu().train()
qat_model.qconfig = quant.get_default_qat_qconfig("qnnpack")
qat_model = quant.prepare_qat(qat_model, inplace=False)

optimizer = torch.optim.Adam(qat_model.parameters(), lr=0.001)
for images, labels in training_loader:
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    logits = qat_model(images.cpu())
    loss = torch.nn.functional.cross_entropy(logits, labels.cpu())
    loss.backward()
    optimizer.step()

qat_model.eval()
qat_int8 = quant.convert(qat_model, inplace=False)
