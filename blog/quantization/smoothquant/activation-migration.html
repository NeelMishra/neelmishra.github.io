    SmoothQuant: move the activation difficulty into weights

    October 2026 · 5 min read

    SmoothQuant targets weight-and-activation quantization. Its starting point is an imbalance: weights are often easier to quantize than activations containing persistent large channels. Instead of routing exceptional features to a separate floating-point multiplication, it rebalances the operands before quantizing both. The floating-point model remains equivalent; only the later approximation changes.

    The same identity, a different objective

    Keep the $y=Wx$ orientation from the AWQ note. With a positive diagonal channel scale $S$,

    $$W'=WS,\qquad x'=S^{-1}x,\qquad W'x'=Wx.$$

    Dividing a large activation channel by its scale shrinks its range while multiplying the matching weight column expands that column's range. This does not erase the outlier's contribution. It moves representational difficulty into weights, which are fixed and can be transformed offline. Both AWQ and SmoothQuant use equivalent scaling, but AWQ seeks better low-bit weights with floating activations; SmoothQuant seeks a workable balance for W8A8.

    A channelwise balance rule

    Let $a_j$ be the maximum absolute activation in channel $j$ across calibration samples, and let $b_j$ be the maximum absolute weight in column $j$. SmoothQuant defines a family of scales of the form

    $$s_j=\frac{a_j^\alpha}{b_j^{1-\alpha}},\qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq1.$$

    At $\alpha=0.5$, the transformed activation and weight maxima both become $\sqrt{a_jb_j}$. A larger $\alpha$ transfers more of the burden to weights. Zero or degenerate channels need guarded scale handling in code; the formula is not a license to divide by zero. The appropriate balance depends on the model and quantization configuration rather than a universal best $\alpha$.

    Move a range of 100 into two ranges of 1

    Take two input channels with activation maxima $(100,1)$ and weight-column maxima $(0.01,1)$. At $\alpha=0.5$, the scales are $(100,1)$. The transformed activation maxima are $(1,1)$, and transformed weight maxima are also $(1,1)$. Before quantization, an input $x=(100,1)$ and output row $w=(0.01,1)$ give $2$; afterward, the equivalent pair $x'=(1,1)$ and $w'=(1,1)$ still give $2$.

    For a smaller input $x=(100,0.1)$, a single symmetric eight-bit activation scale set by 100 has step $100/127\approx0.7874$. The second activation rounds to zero. After smoothing, the activation scale set by 1 has step $1/127$, and $0.1$ reconstructs as $13/127\approx0.10236$. With this toy weight row exactly represented after scaling, the output becomes about $1.10236$ rather than $1$. Real tensors contain many values and may use different scale granularities, so this is a mechanism example, not a promised error reduction.

    Offline smoothing is not static activations

    Calibration estimates the channel maxima used to construct $S$. A separate decision determines whether activation quantization uses dynamic scales during execution or fixed scales derived offline. SmoothQuant's scaling transformation does not itself mean every activation scale is static. Read the exact deployment configuration before attributing its accuracy or overhead to the algorithm.

    Outliers outside the calibration distribution can still overflow or clip on a fixed grid. Even a dynamic scale can lose resolution when unexpected extremes arrive. Evaluate representative prompts and held-out tasks, and check context lengths beyond the calibration samples. Smoothing is a preconditioning step, not a guarantee about all future activations.

    The W8A8 path must exist

    Useful deployment requires efficient eight-bit matrix multiplication, suitable scale application, and wider accumulation where needed. Normalization, residual additions, and some other operations may remain floating point. Compatible preceding layers can absorb smoothing scales, but residual branches and shared inputs must be handled consistently. Benchmark prefill and decode independently: moving work to INT8 does not imply the same benefit for every matrix shape or device.

    Is SmoothQuant a weight-file format?

    No. It is a numerical transformation and quantization approach. A runtime still needs a concrete tensor layout, scale representation, and supported kernels to execute the result.
