    SmoothQuant: move the difficult range

    October 2026 · 5 min read

    LLM.int8 sends troublesome feature dimensions to a higher-precision branch. SmoothQuant instead makes the activation range easier to quantize by transferring some of its difficulty into the weights. The transformation itself preserves the floating-point layer; rounding afterward does not.

    An exact channel transformation

    Again use $Y=XW$, with $X$ shaped $T\times C$ and $W$ shaped $C\times D$. Choose positive channel factors $s_c$ and write $S=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_C)$. Then

    $$\widetilde{X}=XS^{-1},\qquad \widetilde{W}=SW,\qquad \widetilde{X}\widetilde{W}=XW.$$

    Divide activation channel $c$ by $s_c$ and multiply weight row $c$ by the same number. This is an input-channel transformation. Under PyTorch's stored weight layout, it scales columns of the stored weight tensor, not rows.

    Choose how much difficulty to move

    Let $A_c=\max_t|X_{tc}|$ on calibration data, and $B_c=\max_d|W_{cd}|$. SmoothQuant proposes

    $$s_c=\frac{A_c^\alpha}{B_c^{\,1-\alpha}},\qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq1.$$

    For positive ranges, the transformed maxima are $A_c/s_c=(A_cB_c)^{1-\alpha}$ and $s_cB_c=(A_cB_c)^\alpha$. At $\alpha=0.5$ they match within each channel. Increasing $\alpha$ shifts more of the burden into the weights; it does not guarantee lower end-to-end error. Implementations need guards for zero ranges to avoid division by zero.

    A two-channel check

    Take $x=[100,1]$ and $w=[0.01,1]^\top$. Their dot product is $2$. With $A=[100,1]$, $B=[0.01,1]$, and $\alpha=0.5$, choose $s=[100,1]$. The transformed activation is $[1,1]$, and the transformed weight is $[1,1]^\top$. Their dot product is still $2$.

    The large activation range has disappeared in this example. This is an exact rescaling check, not an accuracy benchmark: a real weight row contains many values and shares quantization parameters with other values. Rounding both transformed tensors still introduces error.

    From the identity to W8A8

    1. Collect representative activation-channel ranges with the trained model.
    2. Choose the smoothing strength and construct the channel factors.
    3. Fold inverse scaling into compatible preceding operations where possible, and scale the linear weights. Shared consumers and residual branches must retain equivalent behavior.
    4. Calibrate the transformed model's quantizers as required by the selected static or dynamic scheme, then export to a runtime with supported W8A8 kernels.
    5. Check held-out quality and measure prefill and decode separately.

    The paper explores multiple quantization granularities and runtime scaling choices. “SmoothQuant” alone does not specify a single per-token or per-tensor deployment configuration. Nor does it mean every attention operation or the KV cache has become INT8.

    When smoothing is not enough

    Calibration maxima can miss unseen outliers. Too much migration can make weights hard to quantize; too little can leave activations hard to quantize. An equivalent floating-point graph does not imply an equivalent quantized graph. The useful claim is that this representation can make W8A8 accurate and efficient under a supported configuration, not that rescaling removes all error.

    Why not keep increasing every scale?

    Activation values shrink, but weight values grow. The product is preserved before rounding, while the weight quantizer's range and step size may become worse.
