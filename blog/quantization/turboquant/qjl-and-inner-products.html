    2. TurboQuant: why MSE quantizers bias inner products, and how 1-bit QJL fixes them

    October 2026 · 8 min read

    In Part 1, $\textsc{TurboQuant}_{\mathrm{mse}}$ rotated a unit vector $x \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ by a random orthogonal matrix $\Pi$ and snapped each coordinate $u_j = (\Pi x)_j$ to its 1D Lloyd-Max centroid. That procedure minimizes the expected Euclidean reconstruction error $\mathbb{E}[\lVert x - \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} \rVert_2^2]$.

    Attention layers and vector databases, however, rarely consume $\tilde{x}$ by itself. They compute inner products $\langle y, \tilde{x} \rangle$ between an unquantized query $y$ and quantized keys or embeddings. A surprising property of Lloyd-Max quantization is that minimizing Euclidean MSE guarantees a systematic multiplicative bias on inner products. This post proves that bias law, derives the asymmetric 1-bit Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss (QJL) estimator, and shows how two-stage $\textsc{TurboQuant}_{\mathrm{prod}}$ combines both to achieve unbiased, exponentially decaying inner-product error.

    Why every Lloyd-Max quantizer shrinks inner products by $(1 - D_{\mathrm{mse}})$

    Let $U = (\Pi x)_j$ be a single rotated coordinate of a unit vector $x \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$, and let $\tilde{U} = Q(U)$ be its quantized value in a Lloyd-Max codebook with Voronoi cells $I_1, \dots, I_{2^b}$ and centroids $c_1, \dots, c_{2^b}$. Because each centroid is the conditional expectation of its cell, $c_k = \mathbb{E}[U \mid U \in I_k]$, the quantization error $U - \tilde{U}$ has zero mean inside every cell and is therefore orthogonal to $\tilde{U}$:

    $$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tilde{U}(U - \tilde{U})\bigr] = \sum_{k=1}^{2^b} c_k \int_{I_k} (t - c_k)\,f_X(t)\,dt = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbb{E}[U\tilde{U}] = \mathbb{E}[\tilde{U}^2].$$

    Now expand the total MSE distortion across all $d$ coordinates, using $d\,\mathbb{E}[U^2] = \mathbb{E}[\lVert u \rVert_2^2] = 1$:

    $$D_{\mathrm{mse}} = d\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[(U - \tilde{U})^2\bigr] = d\bigl(\mathbb{E}[U^2] - 2\mathbb{E}[U\tilde{U}] + \mathbb{E}[\tilde{U}^2]\bigr) = 1 - d\,\mathbb{E}[\tilde{U}^2] = 1 - d\,\mathbb{E}[U\tilde{U}].$$

    Because $\Pi$ is orthogonal, $\langle x, \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} \rangle = \langle u, \tilde{u} \rangle = \sum_{j=1}^d U_j \tilde{U}_j$, so its expectation is $\mathbb{E}[\langle x, \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} \rangle] = d\,\mathbb{E}[U\tilde{U}] = 1 - D_{\mathrm{mse}}$. Furthermore, because the distribution of $\Pi$ is rotationally invariant around $x$, any component of $\tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}}$ orthogonal to $x$ cancels in expectation, yielding the exact vector expectation and inner-product shrinkage law:

    $$\mathbb{E}_{\Pi}[\tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}}] = (1 - D_{\mathrm{mse}})\,x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbb{E}_{\Pi}\bigl[\langle y, \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} \rangle\bigr] = (1 - D_{\mathrm{mse}})\,\langle y, x \rangle.$$

    At $b = 1$ bit in the high-dimensional Gaussian limit, the Lloyd-Max centroids are $\pm\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi d}}$ and $D_{\mathrm{mse}} = 1 - \frac{2}{\pi} \approx 0.3634$. Substituting into the law above gives:

    $$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle y, \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} \rangle\bigr] = \frac{2}{\pi}\,\langle y, x \rangle \approx 0.6366\,\langle y, x \rangle.$$

    Every inner product is attenuated by more than $36\%$ at $1$ bit (and by $D_{\mathrm{mse}} \approx 11.75\%$ at $2$ bits). In transformer attention $\operatorname{softmax}(q k^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt{d})$, scaling logits down by $(1 - D_{\mathrm{mse}})$ artificially raises the softmax temperature, leaking probability mass from sharp attention peaks into irrelevant context tokens.

    The 1-bit Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss (QJL) transform

    To estimate inner products without multiplicative bias at $1$ bit per coordinate, Zandieh, Daliri, and Han (AAAI 2025) introduced the Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss (QJL) transform. Let $S \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ have i.i.d. standard normal entries $S_{i,j} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$. For any vector $r \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with stored scalar norm $\gamma = \lVert r \rVert_2$, QJL stores only the $d$ sign bits of $S r$ and reconstructs asymmetrically:

    $$Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}(r) = \operatorname{sign}(S r) \in \{-1, +1\}^d, \qquad Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}^{-1}(z) = \frac{\sqrt{\pi / 2}}{d}\,\gamma\,S^{\mathsf T} z.$$

    Note the scaling factor $\frac{\sqrt{\pi/2}}{d} \approx \frac{1.2533}{d}$ instead of the MSE-optimal centroid $\frac{\sqrt{2/\pi}}{d} \approx \frac{0.7979}{d}$. Why does replacing $\sqrt{2/\pi}$ by its reciprocal $\sqrt{\pi/2}$ make inner products with any query $y \in \mathbb{R}^d$ strictly unbiased?

    Proof of unbiasedness and variance via orthogonal Gaussian decomposition

    By linearity in $\gamma = \lVert r \rVert_2$, assume without loss of generality that $\lVert r \rVert_2 = 1$. Let $s_i^{\mathsf T}$ denote the $i$-th row of $S$, so the inner-product estimate is the average of $d$ i.i.d. terms:

    $$\bigl\langle y, Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}^{-1}(Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}(r)) \bigr\rangle = \frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^d \alpha_i, \qquad \alpha_i = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\langle y, s_i \rangle\,\operatorname{sign}\bigl(\langle s_i, r \rangle\bigr).$$

    Decompose the query $y$ into its projection along the unit vector $r$ and its orthogonal complement $y^{\perp}$:

    $$y = \langle y, r \rangle\,r + y^{\perp}, \qquad \langle y^{\perp}, r \rangle = 0.$$

    Because $s_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$ is an isotropic Gaussian vector, its projections onto the orthogonal vectors $r$ and $y^{\perp}$,

    $$g_1 = \langle s_i, r \rangle \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \quad\text{and}\quad g_2 = \langle s_i, y^{\perp} \rangle \sim \mathcal{N}\bigl(0, \lVert y^{\perp} \rVert_2^2\bigr),$$

    Have zero covariance $\mathbb{E}[g_1 g_2] = r^{\mathsf T} I_d y^{\perp} = \langle r, y^{\perp} \rangle = 0$, and jointly Gaussian variables with zero covariance are strictly independent. Substituting $\langle y, s_i \rangle = \langle y, r \rangle g_1 + g_2$ into $\alpha_i$ and taking expectations gives:

    $$\mathbb{E}[\alpha_i] = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\Bigl(\langle y, r \rangle\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[g_1 \operatorname{sign}(g_1)\bigr] + \mathbb{E}[g_2]\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\operatorname{sign}(g_1)\bigr]\Bigr) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\langle y, r \rangle\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lvert g_1 \rvert\bigr] = \langle y, r \rangle,$$

    because $\mathbb{E}[g_2] = 0$ and the half-normal mean of $g_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ is $\mathbb{E}[\lvert g_1 \rvert] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \lvert t \rvert \frac{e^{-t^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,dt = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}$. For the variance, since $\operatorname{sign}(g_1)^2 = 1$ almost everywhere, $\alpha_i^2 = \frac{\pi}{2}\langle y, s_i \rangle^2$. Because $\langle y, s_i \rangle \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \lVert y \rVert_2^2)$ has second moment $\lVert y \rVert_2^2$, averaging $d$ independent rows yields Lemma 5 of TurboQuant (restoring $\gamma = \lVert r \rVert_2$):

    $$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle y, Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}^{-1}(Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}(r)) \rangle\bigr] = \langle y, r \rangle, \qquad \operatorname{Var}\bigl(\langle y, Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}^{-1}(Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}(r)) \rangle\bigr) \le \frac{\pi}{2d}\,\lVert y \rVert_2^2\,\lVert r \rVert_2^2.$$

    During KV-cache decoding, $Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}^{-1}(z)$ does not even need to be materialized: projecting the query once as $\tilde{y} = S y$ reduces each key-query inner product to a hardware-friendly bitwise XNOR popcount between $\tilde{y}$ and the packed sign bits $z \in \{-1, +1\}^d$.

    Two-stage $\textsc{TurboQuant}_{\mathrm{prod}}$: MSE first, 1-bit QJL on the residual

    Why not simply repeat 1-bit QJL $b$ times with $b d$ random Gaussian rows when given $b$ bits per coordinate? Averaging $b d$ independent sign projections reduces the variance only linearly in $b$, as $\frac{\pi}{2bd}\lVert x \rVert_2^2$, whereas the information-theoretic distortion-rate function decays exponentially as $\frac{1}{d \cdot 4^b}$.

    Notice, however, that QJL's variance bound $\frac{\pi}{2d}\lVert y \rVert_2^2 \lVert r \rVert_2^2$ is proportional to the squared norm $\lVert r \rVert_2^2$ of the vector being sketched. $\textsc{TurboQuant}_{\mathrm{prod}}$ exploits this by splitting a $b$-bit budget into two stages:

    1. Stage 1 ($(b - 1)$-bit MSE quantization): Quantize $x$ using $\textsc{TurboQuant}_{\mathrm{mse}}$ with bit-width $b - 1$ to obtain index vector $\mathrm{idx} = Q_{\mathrm{mse}}(x)$ and reconstruction $\tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} = Q_{\mathrm{mse}}^{-1}(\mathrm{idx})$. Form the residual vector
      $$r = x - \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}}, \qquad \gamma = \lVert r \rVert_2,$$
      whose expected squared norm is already shrunk exponentially by Theorem 1: $\mathbb{E}[\lVert r \rVert_2^2] = D_{\mathrm{mse}}(b - 1) \le \frac{\sqrt{3}\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{4^{b-1}}$.
    2. Stage 2 ($1$-bit QJL on the residual): Quantize the residual $r$ into $1$ bit per coordinate via $z_{\mathrm{qjl}} = \operatorname{sign}(S r) \in \{-1, +1\}^d$ and store the scalar residual norm $\gamma = \lVert r \rVert_2$.
    3. Combined reconstruction: Sum the deterministic MSE reconstruction and the asymmetric QJL residual estimate:
      $$\tilde{x}_{\mathrm{prod}} = \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} + \frac{\sqrt{\pi / 2}}{d}\,\gamma\,S^{\mathsf T} z_{\mathrm{qjl}}.$$

    Conditioned on the Stage-1 outcome $\tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}}$, the QJL stage is an unbiased estimator of $\langle y, r \rangle = \langle y, x - \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} \rangle$. By the law of total expectation, the Stage-1 bias cancels identically:

    $$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle y, \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{prod}} \rangle \,\big|\, \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}}\bigr] = \langle y, \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} \rangle + \mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{qjl}}\bigl[\langle y, \tilde{r} \rangle \,\big|\, r\bigr] = \langle y, \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} \rangle + \langle y, x - \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} \rangle = \langle y, x \rangle.$$

    Taking the expectation of the conditional QJL variance over the Stage-1 residual distribution yields Theorem 2 of TurboQuant:

    $$D_{\mathrm{prod}}(Q_{\mathrm{prod}}) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[\lvert \langle y, x \rangle - \langle y, \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{prod}} \rangle \rvert^2\bigr] \le \frac{\pi}{2d}\,\lVert y \rVert_2^2\, D_{\mathrm{mse}}(b - 1) \le \frac{\sqrt{3}\pi^2\,\lVert y \rVert_2^2}{d}\cdot\frac{1}{4^b}.$$

    Worked 4D numerical check: repairing the 1-bit inner-product shrinkage

    Return to the 4D unit key $x = [0.9, -0.3, 0.3, 0.1]^{\mathsf T}$ from Part 1 and pair it with the unit query $y = [0.7, -0.5, 0.5, 0.1]^{\mathsf T}$ ($\lVert y \rVert_2^2 = 0.49 + 0.25 + 0.25 + 0.01 = 1.00$). The exact inner product is:

    $$\langle y, x \rangle = 0.7(0.9) + (-0.5)(-0.3) + 0.5(0.3) + 0.1(0.1) = 0.63 + 0.15 + 0.15 + 0.01 = 0.94.$$

    Now run two-stage $\textsc{TurboQuant}_{\mathrm{prod}}$ at a total budget of $b = 2$ bits per coordinate ($1$ bit for $\textsc{TurboQuant}_{\mathrm{mse}}$ plus $1$ bit for QJL):

    1. Stage 1 ($1$-bit $\textsc{TurboQuant}_{\mathrm{mse}}$): From Part 1, rotating $x$ by $\Pi$ gives $u = [0.5, 0.7, -0.1, -0.5]^{\mathsf T}$, which snaps to the 1-bit Gaussian centroids $\tilde{u}_{1\mathrm{bit}} = [0.398942, 0.398942, -0.398942, -0.398942]^{\mathsf T}$ and reconstructs to $\tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} = \Pi^{\mathsf T}\tilde{u}_{1\mathrm{bit}} = [0.797885, 0, 0, 0]^{\mathsf T}$. Using $\tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}}$ alone yields inner product $\langle y, \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} \rangle = 0.7 \times 0.797885 = 0.558519$, under-predicting the true $0.94$ by $-0.381481$.
    2. Residual and norm: Subtracting $\tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}}$ from $x$ leaves the residual vector and scalar norm
      $$r = x - \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.102115 \\ -0.300000 \\ 0.300000 \\ 0.100000 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \gamma = \lVert r \rVert_2 = \sqrt{0.200428} = 0.447691.$$
    3. Stage 2 ($1$-bit QJL on $r$): Using the concrete $4 \times 4$ projection matrix $S_{\mathrm{demo}}$ in tools/check_turboquant.py,
      $$S_{\mathrm{demo}} r = \begin{bmatrix} 1.1 & -0.4 & 0.3 & 0.2 \\ 0.8 & -0.9 & -0.5 & 0.4 \\ -0.7 & 0.6 & -0.8 & 0.1 \\ 0.5 & 1.2 & 0.7 & -0.6 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 0.102115 \\ -0.300000 \\ 0.300000 \\ 0.100000 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.342327 \\ 0.241692 \\ -0.481481 \\ -0.158942 \end{bmatrix} \;\Longrightarrow\; z_{\mathrm{qjl}} = \begin{bmatrix} +1 \\ +1 \\ -1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}.$$
      Scaling $S_{\mathrm{demo}}^{\mathsf T} z_{\mathrm{qjl}} = [2.1, -3.1, -0.1, 1.1]^{\mathsf T}$ by $\frac{\sqrt{\pi/2}}{4}\gamma = 0.140275$ gives the residual reconstruction $\tilde{r} = [0.294576, -0.434851, -0.014027, 0.154302]^{\mathsf T}$ and two-stage vector $\tilde{x}_{\mathrm{prod}} = \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{mse}} + \tilde{r} = [1.092461, -0.434851, -0.014027, 0.154302]^{\mathsf T}$, whose inner product with $y$ is $\langle y, \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{prod}} \rangle = 0.990565$ (cutting the absolute error from $0.381481$ down to $0.050565$).
    4. Monte Carlo check across random $S \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)^{4 \times 4}$: Averaging over $40{,}000$ independent Gaussian matrices in tools/check_turboquant.py gives $\mathbb{E}[\langle y, \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{prod}} \rangle] = 0.94080$ (matching the exact $0.94000$) and empirical inner-product MSE $0.042795$, well inside the theoretical upper bound $\frac{\pi}{2d}\lVert y \rVert_2^2 \lVert r \rVert_2^2 = 0.078708$.
    Does $\tilde{x}_{\mathrm{prod}}$ also have smaller Euclidean MSE $\lVert x - \tilde{x}_{\mathrm{prod}} \rVert_2^2$ than $\tilde{x}_{2\mathrm{bit}}$?

    No — and it is not meant to. Because $Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}^{-1}$ scales by $\sqrt{\pi/2}$ to make inner products with an unquantized query $y$ unbiased, its Euclidean norm is inflated by $\mathbb{E}[\lVert \tilde{r} \rVert_2^2] \approx \frac{\pi}{2}\gamma^2$. If your downstream task reconstructs vectors in $\ell_2$ norm, use $b$-bit $\textsc{TurboQuant}_{\mathrm{mse}}$; if it computes dot products $\langle y, x \rangle$, use $\textsc{TurboQuant}_{\mathrm{prod}}$.
