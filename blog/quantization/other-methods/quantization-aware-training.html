    QAT: train with the error in the loop

    October 2026 · 5 min read

    PTQ constructs a compressed approximation after training. If its error is unacceptable and training data and compute are available, quantization-aware training (QAT) lets trainable parameters adapt while the forward pass experiences simulated quantization. QAT is a family of training strategies, not a single checkpoint format.

    Fake quantization in the forward pass

    Reuse the affine quantizer from Part 1. Its fake-quantized output is

    $$\operatorname{FQ}(x)=s\left[\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\operatorname{round}(x/s)+z,q_{\min},q_{\max}\right)-z\right].$$

    The result is a floating-point tensor constrained to reconstructed quantization values. Fake quantization simulates rounding and clipping; it does not automatically pack the training tensor or run an integer kernel. Master weights and optimizer states can remain floating point.

    Rounding needs a surrogate gradient

    Rounding is piecewise constant, with a derivative of zero almost everywhere. A common straight-through estimator (STE) treats fake quantization approximately as the identity inside the representable range, while clipping can block gradients outside it. This is a chosen surrogate, not the true derivative of rounding. Other QAT variants can learn scales or use different gradient estimators.

    Suppose a scalar weight is $0.26$ and the scale is $0.1$. Its forward value is $0.3$. If an STE update moves the master weight to $0.24$, the next forward value becomes $0.2$. The master weight can accumulate small updates until it crosses a grid boundary, even though the quantized forward value stays constant between boundaries. This simplified example keeps the scale fixed.

    A practical training sequence

    1. Define the deployment target first: bit widths, granularity, supported operators, rounding, clipping, and required graph fusions.
    2. Initialize from a trained model and insert the matching fake-quantization operations for the tensors to be quantized.
    3. Collect ranges using observers or initialize learnable quantizer parameters. Use a deliberate schedule for enabling fake quantization and stabilizing ranges.
    4. Fine-tune on the relevant training task. Preserve a held-out evaluation set and compare against both the original model and a PTQ baseline.
    5. Freeze the needed parameters, convert or export the graph, and evaluate the actual low-precision artifact in the intended runtime.

    Observer updates and fake quantization are different controls. Continuously changing ranges can make the training target unstable; freezing them too early can lock in a poor range. The right schedule depends on the model and quantizer rather than a universal number of steps.

    Why export can change the answer

    A simulator can use different rounding, scales, fusions, or excluded operators than the deployment kernel. It may also omit accumulator overflow or other hardware effects. Good fake-quantized validation is therefore necessary but not sufficient. Check output agreement between the simulated model and exported artifact before attributing a quality drop to low bit width itself.

    QAT consumes training compute and can overfit or damage general capabilities if the training data is narrow. It also cannot make an unsupported kernel appear. If the hardware only offers efficient execution for particular shapes or formats, that constraint belongs in the training design.

    Return to the choice

    LLM.int8 handles outliers with a mixed-precision branch. SmoothQuant migrates activation range to weights for W8A8. AWQ protects weight channels through activation-aware scaling. NF4 defines a nonuniform storage codebook, while QLoRA trains adapters on a frozen quantized base. QAT changes trainable parameters under simulated quantization. Compare them under the same quality and runtime contract, not just the same nominal bit count.

    Does QAT mean training entirely with integers?

    No. Fake quantization commonly runs in floating point with floating-point master weights and surrogate gradients. Integer execution is the deployment target, not an automatic property of training.
