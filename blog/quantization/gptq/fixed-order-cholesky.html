    GPTQ: fixed order, lazy batches, and Cholesky

    October 2026 · 4 min read

    Part 2 derived the single-row compensation step $\delta_F = -\frac{a}{(G_F)_{qq}}(G_F)_{:,q}$ and the rank-one inverse downdate $G_{\mathrm{next}} = G_{R,R} - \frac{G_{R,q}G_{q,R}}{G_{qq}}$. Below is the exact full-layer GPTQ algorithm (Algorithm 1 in Frantar et al.), followed by the three ideas that make it work.

    Algorithm 1: Quantize $W$ with GPTQ

    Algorithm 1 — Quantize $W \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d}$ given inverse-Hessian $G = (2A^{\mathsf T}A + \lambda I_d)^{-1}$ and block size $B$
    Input: weight matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d}$, calibration activations $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ (or $X = A^{\mathsf T}$), block size $B = 128$, damping $\alpha = 0.01$
    Output: quantized weight matrix $\widehat W \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d}$
    1:$\widehat W \leftarrow 0_{m \times d},\quad E \leftarrow 0_{m \times B}$// quantized output & block error buffer
    2:$H \leftarrow 2A^{\mathsf T}A = 2XX^{\mathsf T},\quad \lambda \leftarrow \alpha\frac{\operatorname{tr}(H)}{d},\quad G \leftarrow (H + \lambda I_d)^{-1}$// damped inverse Hessian
    3:$U \leftarrow \operatorname{Cholesky}(G)^{\mathsf T}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad U^{\mathsf T}U = G$// upper-triangular Cholesky factor of $G$
    4:for $i = 0, B, 2B, \dots$ do $\quad\bigl($with $b = \min(i + B, d)\bigr)$// loop over blocks of $B$ columns
    5:for $q = i, i+1, \dots, b - 1$ do// sequential columns inside block
    6:$\widehat W_{:,q} \leftarrow \operatorname{quant}(W_{:,q})$// snap column $q$ across all $m$ rows
    7:$E_{:,q-i} \leftarrow \dfrac{W_{:,q} - \widehat W_{:,q}}{U_{qq}}$// normalized error $a / U_{qq}$
    8:$W_{:,q:b} \leftarrow W_{:,q:b} - E_{:,q-i}\cdot U_{q,q:b}$// intra-block rank-1 compensation
    9:end for
    10:$W_{:,b:d} \leftarrow W_{:,b:d} - E_{:,0:(b-i)}\cdot U_{i:b,\,b:d}$// deferred inter-block GEMM update
    11:end for
    12:return $\widehat W$

    1. Fixed column order across all $m$ rows

    As shown in Part 2, quantizing columns in a single fixed order $q = 0, 1, \dots, d-1$ across all $m$ output rows gives every row the same suffix of remaining free columns $F = \{q, \dots, d-1\}$ and the same active inverse $G_F = H_F^{-1}$:

    • Complexity drop: instead of running $m$ separate $O(d^3)$ inverse-downdate sequences ($O(md^3)$ in greedy OBQ), GPTQ prepares the inverse directions once in $O(d^3)$ and applies weight updates in $O(md^2)$.
    • Static permutation (actorder): because $H_F$ only depends on calibration inputs $A$, we only need the column order to be shared across rows, not necessarily $0, 1, \dots, d-1$. The actorder option sorts columns once by descending Hessian diagonal $H_{qq}$ so high-variance input features are rounded first while the most free weights remain to compensate them.

    2. Damp the Hessian and factor its inverse ($U^{\mathsf T}U = G$)

    Collinear or rarely active input features can make $H = 2A^{\mathsf T}A$ nearly singular, causing repeated rank-one subtractions to accumulate floating-point error. GPTQ first adds diagonal damping $\lambda$ (with default $\alpha = 0.01$):

    $$\lambda=\alpha\frac{\operatorname{tr}(H)}{d},\qquad H_{\mathrm{damped}}=H+\lambda I_d,\qquad G=H_{\mathrm{damped}}^{-1}.$$

    Adding $\lambda I_d$ to $H$ adds an $\ell_2$ penalty $\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\lVert w-w^{(0)}\rVert_2^2$ to Part 2's reconstruction cost $C(w)$, discouraging large compensating weight shifts along weakly observed input directions. (Columns with $H_{qq} = 0$ before damping are inactive on calibration data and zeroed directly.)

    Next, GPTQ computes the upper-triangular Cholesky factor $U \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ of the inverse $G$ (rather than running Part 2's rank-one downdate $G_{\mathrm{next}} = G_{R,R} - \frac{G_{R,q}G_{q,R}}{G_{qq}}$ in a loop $d$ times, which is both slower on GPUs and prone to floating-point drift):

    $$U^{\mathsf T}U=G=H_{\mathrm{damped}}^{-1}.$$

    How does factoring $U^{\mathsf T}U = G$ secretly perform Part 2's rank-one downdate? Write out $U^{\mathsf T}U = G$ at the first step ($q = 0$, with remaining indices $R = \{1, \dots, d-1\}$). Because $U$ is upper triangular, its bottom-left block below $U_{00}$ is $0$:

    $$\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} U_{00} & 0^{\mathsf T} \\[0.3em] U_{0,R}^{\mathsf T} & U_{R,R}^{\mathsf T} \end{bmatrix}}_{U^{\mathsf T}} \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} U_{00} & U_{0,R} \\[0.3em] 0 & U_{R,R} \end{bmatrix}}_{U} = \begin{bmatrix} U_{00}^2 & U_{00}\,U_{0,R} \\[0.4em] U_{0,R}^{\mathsf T}U_{00} & U_{0,R}^{\mathsf T}U_{0,R} + U_{R,R}^{\mathsf T}U_{R,R} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} G_{00} & G_{0,R} \\[0.4em] G_{R,0} & G_{R,R} \end{bmatrix}.$$

    Matching the blocks on the left and right sides reveals three facts at once:

    1. Top-left scalar ($1\times 1$): $U_{00}^2 = G_{00} \implies U_{00} = \sqrt{G_{00}}$.
    2. Top-right row ($1\times |R|$): $U_{00}\,U_{0,R} = G_{0,R} \implies U_{0,R} = \dfrac{G_{0,R}}{U_{00}} = \dfrac{G_{0,R}}{\sqrt{G_{00}}}$.
    3. Bottom-right block ($|R|\times |R|$): solve $U_{0,R}^{\mathsf T}U_{0,R} + U_{R,R}^{\mathsf T}U_{R,R} = G_{R,R}$ for $U_{R,R}^{\mathsf T}U_{R,R}$ and substitute $U_{0,R} = G_{0,R}/\sqrt{G_{00}}$:
      $$U_{R,R}^{\mathsf T}U_{R,R} = G_{R,R} - U_{0,R}^{\mathsf T}U_{0,R} = G_{R,R} - \frac{G_{R,0}\,G_{0,R}}{G_{00}} = G_{\mathrm{next}} = (H_{R,R})^{-1}.$$

    Look at equation (3): subtracting $U_{0,R}^{\mathsf T}U_{0,R}$ inside Cholesky factorization is literally Part 2's rank-one downdate $G_{\mathrm{next}} = G_{R,R} - \frac{G_{R,0}G_{0,R}}{G_{00}}$, so the remaining submatrix $U_{R,R}$ is the Cholesky factor of the reduced inverse $G_{\mathrm{next}}$! Repeating this step-by-step for $q = 0, 1, \dots, d-1$ (where $F = \{q, \dots, d-1\}$ is the active free set at step $q$) means row $q$ of $U$ always satisfies:

    $$(G_F)_{qq}=U_{qq}^2,\qquad (G_F)_{q,F}=U_{qq}\,U_{q,q:}.$$

    Substituting these two identities into Part 2's single-row update $\delta_F^{\mathsf T} = -\frac{a}{(G_F)_{qq}}(G_F)_{q,F}$ (with $a = w_q - t$) cancels one power of $U_{qq}$:

    $$\delta_F^{\mathsf T} =-\frac{a}{(G_F)_{qq}}(G_F)_{q,F} =-\frac{a}{U_{qq}^2}\bigl(U_{qq}U_{q,q:}\bigr) =-\frac{a}{U_{qq}}U_{q,q:}.$$

    Thus a single GPU Cholesky call precomputes all $d$ downdated inverse rows at once: row $q$ of $U$ is the compensation direction for step $q$, and $U_{qq}$ is the normalizer.

    3. Lazy batching: update inside block $B$ now, after block $B$ once

    Updating all remaining columns $q:d$ of $W \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d}$ after every single column $q$ would bottleneck on memory bandwidth ($d$ full-matrix reads/writes). Because rounding column $q$ only depends on corrections from earlier columns $0, \dots, q-1$, GPTQ partitions columns into blocks of size $B = 128$ and delays updates to columns outside the current block:

    1. Round column $q$ and normalize its error across all $m$ rows: stack the normalized discrepancies $a / U_{qq}$ into the column vector $E_{:,q} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times 1}$:
      $$\widehat W_{:,q}=\operatorname{quant}(W_{:,q}),\qquad E_{:,q}=\frac{W_{:,q}-\widehat W_{:,q}}{U_{qq}}.$$
    2. Immediate intra-block compensation ($q:b$): if the current block ends at column $b$, immediately update the remaining columns inside the block via the rank-one outer product:
      $$W_{:,q:b}\leftarrow W_{:,q:b}-E_{:,q}U_{q,q:b}\qquad\bigl(\text{shape } m\times(b-q)\bigr).$$
      On column $q$ itself, $U_{q,q}/U_{qq} = 1$, so the first column subtracts $W_{:,q} - \widehat W_{:,q}$ and lands $W_{:,q}$ on $\widehat W_{:,q}$. Columns $q+1, \dots, b-1$ receive their compensation before their turn to be rounded.
    3. Deferred inter-block update ($T = b:d$): once all $B$ columns in block $J$ finish, stack their error vectors into $E_{:,J} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times B}$ and update all later columns $T$ in a single level-3 matrix multiply (GEMM):
      $$W_{:,T}\leftarrow W_{:,T}-E_{:,J}U_{J,T}\qquad\bigl(\text{shape } (m\times B)\cdot(B\times |T|)\bigr).$$

    Lazy batching changes when memory is written, not the math: every column $q$ still receives the exact same total compensation from columns $0, \dots, q-1$ before it is quantized.

    4. Worked example: tracing Algorithm 1 end-to-end

    Let us trace every line of Algorithm 1 on a $2\times 4$ layer ($m = 2$ output rows, $d = 4$ input columns) with block size $B = 2$ and integer rounding $\operatorname{quant}(x) = \lfloor x + 0.5\rfloor \in \mathbb{Z}$:

    $$W^{(0)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.4 & -0.6 & 0.3 & 0.8 \\ -0.6 & 1.2 & -0.4 & 0.4 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \widehat W_{\mathrm{RTN}} = \operatorname{quant}(W^{(0)}) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \mathbf{-1} & 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}.$$

    Lines 1–3: Setup and Cholesky factor $U$

    Suppose the damped Hessian $H_{\mathrm{damped}}$, its inverse $G = H_{\mathrm{damped}}^{-1}$, and the upper Cholesky factor $U$ ($U^{\mathsf T}U = G$) are:

    $$H_{\mathrm{damped}} = \begin{bmatrix} 4.5 & 4 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 6 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 & 2 & 2 \\ 3 & 2 & 2 & 4 \end{bmatrix}, \quad G = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -0.5 & 0 & -0.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.5 & -0.25 & 0.25 \\ 0 & -0.25 & 1.25 & -0.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.25 & -0.5 & 0.75 \end{bmatrix}, \quad U = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{1} & -0.5 & 0 & -0.5 \\ 0 & \mathbf{0.5} & -0.5 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \mathbf{1} & -0.5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \mathbf{0.5} \end{bmatrix}.$$

    Block 1 ($i = 0,\, b = 2$: columns $q \in \{0, 1\}$)

    • Column $q = 0$ (Lines 6–8): snap $W_{:,0}$, divide the error by $U_{00} = 1$, and update columns $0:2$ inside Block 1 using $U_{0,0:2} = [1,\; -0.5]$ (leaving columns $2:4$ untouched for now):
      $$\widehat W_{:,0} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad E_{:,0} = \frac{1}{1}\left(\begin{bmatrix} 1.4 \\ -0.6 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}\right) = \begin{bmatrix} 0.4 \\ 0.4 \end{bmatrix},$$
      $$W_{:,0:2} \leftarrow \begin{bmatrix} 1.4 & -0.6 \\ -0.6 & 1.2 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 0.4 \\ 0.4 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -0.5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \mathbf{-0.4} \\ -1 & 1.4 \end{bmatrix}.$$
      Notice $W_{0,1}$ shifted from $-0.6$ to $-0.4$ (crossing $-0.5$), so it will now round to $0$ instead of $-1$.
    • Column $q = 1$ (Lines 6–8): snap the updated $W_{:,1} = [-0.4,\; 1.4]^{\mathsf T}$ and divide by $U_{11} = 0.5$:
      $$\widehat W_{:,1} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0} \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad E_{:,1} = \frac{1}{0.5}\left(\begin{bmatrix} -0.4 \\ 1.4 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\right) = \begin{bmatrix} -0.8 \\ 0.8 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad W_{:,1:2} \leftarrow \begin{bmatrix} -0.4 \\ 1.4 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} -0.8 \\ 0.8 \end{bmatrix}[0.5] = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}.$$
    • Deferred inter-block GEMM update (Line 10, on columns $2:4$): now that Block 1 is done, apply both columns' accumulated errors $E = \begin{bmatrix} 0.4 & -0.8 \\ 0.4 & 0.8 \end{bmatrix}$ to the later columns $2:4$ in one matrix multiply with $U_{0:2,2:4} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -0.5 \\ -0.5 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$:
      $$W_{:,2:4} \leftarrow \begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & 0.8 \\ -0.4 & 0.4 \end{bmatrix} - \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 0.4 & -0.8 \\ 0.4 & 0.8 \end{bmatrix}}_{E} \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 0 & -0.5 \\ -0.5 & 0 \end{bmatrix}}_{U_{0:2,\,2:4}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & 0.8 \\ -0.4 & 0.4 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 0.4 & -0.2 \\ -0.4 & -0.2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -0.1 & 1.0 \\ 0.0 & \mathbf{0.6} \end{bmatrix}.$$
      Notice $W_{1,3}$ shifted from $0.4$ to $0.6$ (crossing $0.5$), so it will later round to $1$ instead of $0$.

    Block 2 ($i = 2,\, b = 4$: columns $q \in \{2, 3\}$)

    • Column $q = 2$ (Lines 6–8): snap $W_{:,2} = [-0.1,\; 0.0]^{\mathsf T}$, divide by $U_{22} = 1$, and update columns $2:4$ using $U_{2,2:4} = [1,\; -0.5]$:
      $$\widehat W_{:,2} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad E_{:,0} = \frac{1}{1}\begin{bmatrix} -0.1 \\ 0.0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -0.1 \\ 0.0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad W_{:,2:4} \leftarrow \begin{bmatrix} -0.1 & 1.0 \\ 0.0 & 0.6 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} -0.1 \\ 0.0 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -0.5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0.95 \\ 0 & 0.6 \end{bmatrix}.$$
    • Column $q = 3$ (Lines 6–8): snap the final column $W_{:,3} = [0.95,\; 0.6]^{\mathsf T}$ with $U_{33} = 0.5$:
      $$\widehat W_{:,3} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \mathbf{1} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad E_{:,1} = \frac{1}{0.5}\left(\begin{bmatrix} 0.95 \\ 0.6 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\right) = \begin{bmatrix} -0.1 \\ -0.8 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad W_{:,3:4} \leftarrow \begin{bmatrix} 0.95 \\ 0.6 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} -0.1 \\ -0.8 \end{bmatrix}[0.5] = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}.$$

    Line 12: Result vs. naive round-to-nearest (RTN)

    $$\widehat W_{\mathrm{RTN}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \mathbf{-1} & 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \widehat W_{\mathrm{GPTQ}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \mathbf{0} & 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 & \mathbf{1} \end{bmatrix}.$$

    Evaluating the quadratic reconstruction cost $\frac12(\widehat w - w^{(0)})^{\mathsf T}H_{\mathrm{damped}}(\widehat w - w^{(0)})$ on each row shows the effect of those two compensated flips ($\widehat W_{0,1}: -1 \to 0$ and $\widehat W_{1,3}: 0 \to 1$):

    • Row 0 cost: drops from $1.61$ (RTN) to $0.41$ (GPTQ) — nearly a $4\times$ reduction.
    • Row 1 cost: drops from $1.12$ (RTN) to $0.72$ (GPTQ).
    • Total layer cost: drops from $2.73$ (RTN) to $1.13$ (GPTQ).
