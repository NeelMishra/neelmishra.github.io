    Connectivity & Cut Bottlenecks: SCCs, Bridges & Articulation Points

    October 2026 · 5 min read · Handwritten Notes

    1-to-1 reading roadmap across 4 handwritten competitive coding notes: Tarjan's Bridges (G-55), Kosaraju's Directed SCCs, Articulation Points (G-56), and the Bridges Revision Playbook

    Plain DFS tells you whether vertex $u$ can reach vertex $v$. Advanced DFS connectivity algorithms go a step deeper and uncover the cycle structure and single-point failure bottlenecks of a graph in a single $\mathcal{O}(V + E)$ linear budget:

    • In Undirected Graphs — Bridges & Articulation Points: Every edge is already bidirectional, so every connected component is trivially mutually reachable. Instead, we look for critical edges (Bridges) and critical vertices (Articulation Points) whose removal splits a connected component into two or more pieces — solved via Tarjan's DFS discovery/low-link arrays (tin[] and low[]).
    • In Directed Graphs — Strongly Connected Components (SCCs): Two vertices $u, v$ belong to the same SCC iff $u \leadsto v$ and $v \leadsto u$. Contracting each SCC into a supernode collapses every directed graph into an acyclic Condensation DAG. Kosaraju's algorithm extracts every SCC cleanly using a finish-time stack and graph transposition.
