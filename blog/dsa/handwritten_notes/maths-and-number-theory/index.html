    Maths & Number Theory

    October 2026 · 5 min read · 3 Parts (11 Handwritten Pages)

    Expanded from 6 handwritten OneNote notebooks (IIIT Math Material & TUF Number Theory notes)

    Competitive programming number theory revolves around one recurring trade-off: when can you answer a single query in $O(\sqrt{n})$ or $O(\log n)$ time on the fly, and when do $Q \approx 10^5$ queries force you to invert your loops and precompute a table across $[1, N]$ in $O(N \log N)$ or $O(N \log \log N)$ time? These three guides transcribe and expand 11 handwritten pages covering divisibility proofs, factor-pair symmetry, harmonic-sum divisor counting, Euclidean GCD/LCM, and the Sieve of Eratosthenes.

    Reading Roadmap

    Follow the three parts in order—Part 1 establishes the mathematical proofs and multi-query harmonic tricks, Part 2 drills single-number primality, divisor enumeration, GCD, and LCM from brute force to optimal, and Part 3 builds $O(1)$ primality lookup via the Sieve of Eratosthenes.

    01
    Part 1: IIIT Math Material — Division, $\sqrt{n}$ Factoring, CSES 1713 & Euclidean Proof

    Euclid's division lemma ($a = bq + r$), unique prime factorization, contradiction proof for $\sqrt{n}$ factor pairs, Codeforces T-Primes ($x = p^2$), CSES 1713 Counting Divisors in $O(N \log N)$ via the harmonic sum $H_N$, prime-exponent $\min/\max$ views of GCD/LCM, and the divisor-set invariance proof of $\gcd(a,b) = \gcd(b, a \bmod b)$.

     02
    Part 2: Primes, Divisors, GCD & LCM — Brute Force to Optimal

    Checking primality via the $n = 36$ factor-pair turnaround table, collecting sorted divisors without duplicating $\sqrt{n}$, forward vs. backward vs. Euclidean GCD dry runs ($\gcd(7,2) \to 1$ and why $\gcd(a,0)=a$), and overflow-safe LCM computation.

     03
    Part 3: Sieve of Eratosthenes — $O(1)$ Primality Precomputation

    Eliminating the per-query $\sqrt{N}$ cost by crossing out composite multiples on $N = 30$, proving why the inner loop starts at $i \times i$ and the outer loop stops at $i \times i \le N$, and contrasting the prime harmonic sum $O(N \log \log N)$ with full $O(N \log N)$ divisor sieving.
    Companion Note — Parity-Optimized Prime Factorization For a dedicated standalone walkthrough of extracting all prime factors (with multiplicity) by stripping $2$s first and stepping through odd divisors $i = 3, 5, 7, \dots$ up to $\sqrt{n}$, see Program to Find All Prime Factors.
