    Bipartite Graphs: 2-Coloring, König's Odd-Cycle Theorem, BFS & DFS

    October 2026 · 5 min read · 2-Note Series (3 Handwritten Pages)

    Expanded 1-to-1 from handwritten competitive coding study notes: 01_Striver_g_17_bfs.pdf & 02_Striver_g_18_dfs.pdf

    Many competitive programming disguises—"split $N$ people with dislike edges into two conflict-free groups" (LC 886), "partition vertices into two independent sets" (LC 785), or "test whether an undirected graph is odd-cycle-free"—reduce to the exact same question: is $G$ 2-colorable (bipartite)? Replacing the standard boolean visited[] array with a -1-initialized color[] array solves the problem in $\mathcal{O}(V + 2E)$ via either BFS or DFS.

    BFS vs. DFS 2-Coloring at a Glance

    Comparing the two linear-time bipartite checks.
    Dimension 01 · BFS 2-Coloring (G-17) 02 · DFS 2-Coloring (G-18)
    Color Meaning Equals distance_from_source % 2 layer by layer Alternates col ↔ 1 - col along each plunge path
    Conflict Geometry Two same-depth nodes connected by a horizontal odd-cycle edge Back-edge from odd-depth descendant to even-depth ancestor
    Time / Space $\mathcal{O}(V + 2E)$ / $\mathcal{O}(V)$ $\mathcal{O}(V + 2E)$ / $\mathcal{O}(V)$
    Stack-Overflow Risk? Zero (iterative FIFO queue) Zero with explicit stack $S$; recursive needs $V \le 10^4$ in Python
